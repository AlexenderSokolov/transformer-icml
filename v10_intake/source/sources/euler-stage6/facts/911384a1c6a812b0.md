## 2. Sufficiency and extraction of the bounded subsequence

    G/M>=8(4M+1)/M=32+8/M.

    lambda_j=G_(M_j)/M_j -> lambda,   32<=lambda<=L<infinity.       (2.1)

For example the required subsequence can be obtained by repeated bisection of the compact interval [32,L], retaining a closed half containing infinitely many terms and choosing increasing indices; the nested interval lengths tend to zero.     z0<=lambda<=z0+1,   z0>=lambda-1>=31.           (2.3)

## 3. Choosing one rational parameter with no floor boundary or cancellation

Set I=(1/10,1/6). On its closure define
d1(v)=2-2v and d2(v)=4-7v. Their ranges are respectively [5/3,9/5] and [17/6,33/10]. For the fixed finite lambda in (2.1), both functions lambda/d1(v), lambda/d2(v) are continuous and strictly increasing. Their images are bounded intervals, containing only finitely many integers; each integer has at most one preimage for each function. Thus the set of v in I at which either ratio is an integer is finite.

Deleting these finitely many points leaves a nonempty open subinterval J of I on which both integer floors are constant. Write these constants as

    z1=floor(lambda/d1(v)), z2=floor(lambda/d2(v)), v in J.

In particular z1>=17 and z2>=9, so all z0,z1,z2 are positive. Define on J

    f(v)=(-2+11v)/(z2(4-7v))
         +(3-17v)/(z1(2-2v))+(-1+6v)/z0.           (3.1)

Multiplying by the strictly positive denominator z0 z1 z2(4-7v)(2-2v) gives the polynomial

    P(v)=z0 z1(-2+11v)(2-2v)
         +z0 z2(3-17v)(4-7v)
         +z1 z2(-1+6v)(4-7v)(2-2v).               (3.2)

The three polynomial factors, written in ascending powers of v, are
(-4,26,-22), (12,-89,119), and (-8,70,-146,84), respectively. In particular the coefficient of v^3 in P is 84z1z2>0; the first two terms have degree at most two. Thus P is a nonzero cubic, with at most three real roots, by repeated application of the factor theorem.

Every nonempty open interval contains infinitely many rationals. We can therefore choose a single rational v in J avoiding these at most three roots. Fix this v for the rest of the proof. It satisfies simultaneously

    lambda/d1(v) and lambda/d2(v) are nonintegers,
    f(v)!=0.                                       (3.3)

This is a finite algebraic existence argument, not numerical sampling or independent choices for four heads. The parameter depends only on the already selected lambda,z0 and hence may depend on the public configuration sequence. It will never vary with j. The choice is a witness-source selection, not hidden information supplied to the predictor.

## 4. The fixed legal source and exact terminal probability

For this fixed rational v, put t=v/(1-3v). Since 1/10<v<1/6, one has 0<t<1 (in fact 1/7<t<1/3). Define on symbols 0,1

    theta_00=(1-t,t), theta_01=delta_1,
    theta_11=delta_0, theta_10=delta_0,
    mu(00)=1-3v, mu(01)=mu(11)=mu(10)=v.            (4.1)

    00 -> 00 with probability 1-t,

## 5. Finite flow and martingale second moments give joint frequencies

For a length-M departure segment of a trajectory from (4.1), write
A=sum_(i=0)^(M-1)1{Y_i=00},
B=C_001,
C=C_011, D=C_110, E=C_100.
Here C_abc counts the indicated observed triple among precisely i=0,...,M-1. Only 000,001,011,110,100 can occur. Remove 000 self loops from the chronological departure-edge list. What remains is a finite segment of the cyclic word 001,011,110,100, possibly starting or ending within a cycle. Every complete four-edge block has one of each type and its leftover segment has at most one of each type. Consequently

    |C-B|<=1, |D-B|<=1, |E-B|<=1,
    M=A+C+D+E=A+3B+rho_M, |rho_M|<=3.              (5.1)

This includes arbitrary supported initial pair and finite prefixes, including a list with no nonself edges.

Let F_i be the sigma field generated by X_1,...,X_(i+2), so Y_i is F_i-measurable, and define the bounded difference

    xi_i=1{Y_i=00}(1{X_(i+3)=1}-t).

The second-order transition rule gives E[xi_i|F_i]=0, |xi_i|<=1, and xi_i is F_(i+1)-measurable. For i<k, xi_i is F_k-measurable; hence
E[xi_i xi_k]=E[xi_i E[xi_k|F_k]]=0.
Therefore, writing Delta_M=sum_i xi_i=B-tA,

    E[Delta_M^2]=sum_i E[xi_i^2]<=M.                (5.2)

No independent row stacks or conditional iid assertion is used.

Eliminate A from (5.1) and B-tA=Delta_M:
(1+3t)B=tM+Delta_M-t rho_M.
Since t/(1+3t)=v, this gives the explicit L2 estimate

    ||B/M-v||_2
       <=(sqrt(M)+3t)/((1+3t)M) ->0.               (5.3)

Here the triangle inequality for the L2 norm and |rho_M|<=3 justify the bound. Then (5.1) gives C/M,D/M,E/M->v in L2, while A/M->1-3v and C_000/M=(A-B)/M->1-4v in L2. In particular

    (C_000,C_001,C_011,C_110,C_100)/M
        ->(1-4v,v,v,v,v) jointly in probability.    (5.4)

Joint convergence follows from the finite union bound applied to the five coordinate estimates. These are frequencies on the original trajectory, with its actual endpoints and all M departures retained.
