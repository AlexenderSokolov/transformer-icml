## 1. Risk targets and uniformity

Keep current D1-D3, N>=3, Q=2^b>=2. For one source-independent predictor q
define A(q)=sup_(u',t) R_N(u',t;q) and, for an evaluation value0<u<1/4,
B_u(q)=sup_t R_N,00(u,t;q). The prime on u' only distinguishes the global
supremum defining average risk from the particular u at which conditional
risk is evaluated. The attaining branch program is not supplied with either.

Let desired risks be0<epsilon_A,epsilon_C<=1. The C1/C2 candidates imply
the following **necessary** conditions for any positive-grid predictor with
A(q)<=epsilon_A and B_u(q)<=epsilon_C:

    N+6 >= 3/(16384 epsilon_A),
    Q >= 1/(3 epsilon_A),
    1+(N-2)u >= 3/(16384 epsilon_C),
    Q >= 1/sqrt(2 epsilon_C).

Proof: every individual predictor's appropriate worst-case risk is at least
the infimum defining M_N,Q or C_N,Q(u). Apply each term of the corresponding
lower maximum separately, and use delta_Q>=1/Q. No single source is asserted
to attain the sum, and no exact minimizer or optimal constant is inferred.
If a displayed lower threshold is below the domain N>=3 or Q>=2, that
threshold adds no further restriction. These are minimax necessities, not
lower bounds at every fixed t or on a prescribed sampled history.

For the single C2 branch program the following are **sufficient**:

    N >= 12000000/epsilon_A,
    Q >= 6/epsilon_A,
    1+Nu >= 2000000/epsilon_C,
    Q^2 >= 6/epsilon_C.

Proof: each of the two terms6000000/N and3/Q is then at most epsilon_A/2;
each of1000000/(1+Nu) and3/Q^2 is at most epsilon_C/2. Thus the same
program meets both targets, including the constants in the pending theorem.
Integers may be increased to satisfy the displayed inequalities; the C2
all-N/Q branches already cover the original parameter domain.

The exact dyadic output lower condition can be written

    b >= max{1,ceil log2(1/(3epsilon_A)),
                 ceil[(1/2)log2(1/(2epsilon_C))]}.

A sufficient b is the smallest integer>=1 whose Q satisfies both Q>=6/epsilon_A
and Q^2>=6/epsilon_C. For public rational tolerances it can be generated by
doubling Q from2 and comparing integer products after clearing denominators.
No real logarithm or square-root oracle is needed. This is a statement about
output precision b; internal h_A and the full resource ledger remain separate.

Consequently average accuracy alone needs output precision of order
log2(1/epsilon_A), while fixed-u conditional00 accuracy alone needs order
(1/2)log2(1/epsilon_C), up to additive constants. When both tolerances are
equal, the average requirement supplies the stronger output-grid constraint.
The half coefficient is about the asymptotic output-bit threshold, not
half the total storage, arithmetic cost or neural width.

## 2. Unknown u and the impossibility of one uniform conditional guarantee

The sufficient condition1+Nu>=2000000/epsilon_C is an evaluation condition.
The attaining program knows N and b but not u; it cannot choose a law-dependent
stopping time by evaluating that inequality. For a given public N it states
which laws have enough expected rare-context information for this bound.
No public positive lower bound on u was introduced.

In fact, define the global conditional minimax value over unknown u by

    U_N=inf_q sup_(0<u<1/4,1/4<=t<=3/4) R_N,00(u,t;q).

For each q and every u, the C1 lower proof (even for the stronger comparator
that knows u) gives

    sup_t R_N,00(u,t;q) >= 3/[16384(1+(N-2)u)].

Taking the supremum as u decreases to0 at each fixed N, and then the infimum
over q, proves U_N>=3/16384. The bound holds for unrestricted outputs, so
increasing Q or changing internal arithmetic cannot remove this statistical
obstruction. In particular no finite N can guarantee a conditional risk
below3/16384 uniformly over the entire allowed u-domain. The supremum does
not insert the excluded u=0 source into D1.

This is compatible with every fixed positive-u law having vanishing
conditional expected KL as N,Q increase. It also matches the explicit
triangular-family statement of C1, which gives a fixed nonzero lower for
u_N=1/(N+1) and chooses t_N from two fixed interior values. No single
law's inconsistency, or almost-sure convergence/failure, follows.
