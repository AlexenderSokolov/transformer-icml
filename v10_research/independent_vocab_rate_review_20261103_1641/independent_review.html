1. Mean absolute deviation

For L≥4, μ∈[0,L], and weights exp(−(j−μ)²/L), the normalizer is ≥√L/(2e) by the candidate's endpoint-inclusive argument. For all real x, |x|e^(−x²/L)≤√L e^(−x²/(2L)), since y e^(−y²/2)≤1. The translated lattice sum is at most 1+√(2πL): more generally, the sum of translates of any nonnegative unimodal function is at most its integral plus its maximum. This follows by integrating the lengths of its superlevel intervals and noting an interval of length a contains at most a+1 integer lattice points.

Consequently E|J−μ|≤2e[1/√L+√(2π)]√L<17√L for L≥4. The argument holds uniformly in μ, including both boundaries.

2. Geometric-mean estimate

Jensen gives G≤EJ≤μ+17√L and J≥1 gives G≥1. For μ≤2√L the absolute deviation is at most 17√L. For μ>2√L, write the negative logarithmic contribution as an integral-free sum: when μ/2≤J<μ, ln(μ/J)≤2(μ−J)/μ, so its expectation is at most 34√L/μ. On J<μ/2, each unnormalized weight is ≤exp(−μ²/(4L)). Since x↦ln(μ/x) is positive and decreasing on (0,μ/2], the discrete sum is bounded by the integral, which is (μ/2)(1+ln 2)<μ. After the normalizer bound the far contribution is at most

2e(μ/√L) exp(−μ²/(4L)) ≤ 8√L/μ.

The last step follows by putting x=μ/√L and using max x² exp(−x²/4)=4/e. Positive log contributions for J>μ can only improve E ln(J/μ). Therefore E ln(J/μ)≥−42√L/μ, and G≥μ exp(−42√L/μ)≥μ−42√L. Together with the Jensen upper bound this proves |G−μ|≤42√L. The μ=0 endpoint is handled by the small-μ branch, which does not divide by μ or take its logarithm.
