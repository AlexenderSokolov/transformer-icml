# Izzo, Nichani & Lee, ICLR 2026: primary-source audit

Audit date: 2026-11-03. Scope: §§3–5, theorem proof structure and appendix B.3 of the conference paper, compared with the v10 reference experiment and existing project evidence (see ../../provenance/t1t2_stage1_20261103/INDEX.html). The source is the conference PDF, not the earlier 2025 workshop version: https://openreview.net/pdf?id=TLSUIyBIfs . Conference metadata: https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/4a765a1bbf8332154e58bf452fbf806c-Abstract-Conference.html . No numerical tests, RNG, or external writes were used. This is an audit, not independent proof verification or an exhaustive novelty search.

## The strongest conclusion

The distinction between training-length extrapolation and estimation from a random second-order trajectory is sound; the comparison also includes a direct in-context k-gram connection. Izzo's main bounds compare *two specified limit transformers* on short strings versus all long strings. They do not, by themselves, give an unknown-Markov-kernel prediction-risk bound, a finite-trajectory minimax bound, or an N-versus-inference-depth tradeoff. Their k-gram example explicitly addresses empirical conditional distributions, but its rate calculation is explicitly heuristic and assumes a typical sequence with linearly many matches; it is not a uniform rare/zero-context theorem. This comparison is necessary but not sufficient to establish novelty.

## Architecture and exact length quantifiers

- Finite alphabet Σ; causal limit transformer, input x of arbitrary length T, outputs at the last position; token embeddings, periodic absolute positions, position bias, attention, residual and a single-hidden-layer MLP with 1-Lipschitz activation, then linear unembedding (§3.1, equations on p.3). No LayerNorm is part of this stated architecture. For finite-precision results, absolute position embedding is Δ-periodic and position bias is translation invariant and τ-local. Inputs and outcomes are arbitrary strings rather than a particular underlying trajectory model. Source: https://openreview.net/pdf?id=TLSUIyBIfs#page=3 .
- Their theorem N is a **short-string comparison threshold**, usually interpreted as a required maximal training sequence length: hypothesis assumes closeness on *all strings of every length up to N*. Their arbitrary evaluation length T is not the same quantity. This is a deterministic, very strong hypothesis, not a statement that observing a finite stochastic training sample, running SGD, or minimizing average training loss gives the hypothesis.
- Paper uses L for an aggregate parameter-norm constant in Theorem 4.1, not network depth. The v10 comparison uses L for depth; use C_norm for the paper's constant to avoid ambiguity. Neither inference FLOPs nor memory nor a budget-optimal depth frontier is provided. Parameter sensitivity does not itself yield a lower bound or an N–depth tradeoff.

## Theorem 4.1, one layer, finite precision

For τ-local, Δ-periodic, translation-invariant single-layer f,g with the specified p-bit rounding model and minimum nonzero logit margin γ=min(γ(f),γ(g)), define C_norm=L_f+L_g as in paper. There exists a cutoff

    N = O(max{2^(p/γ), C_norm² Δ⁷ |Σ|⁶ τ² / ε²})

such that `||f(x)-g(x)|| ≤ ε` for all strings `|x| ≤ N` implies `||f(x)-g(x)|| = O(ε)` for every string x (Theorem 4.1, pp.5–6). Norm is over model outputs; it is not directly a TV loss against the unknown oracle. The theorem claims a sufficient cutoff, not necessity or a lower bound.

The special precision semantics matter: all quantities of absolute magnitude ≤2^-p are rounded to zero at each intermediate calculation; before exponentiation logits are max-shifted. Finite-precision LT scales logits by log |x|. Beyond 2^(p/γ), nonmaximal exponentials are truncated and attention is uniform over argmax ties (§3.2). Hardmax is not an automatic consequence of generic p-bit implementation, fixed finite attention logits, or arbitrary rounding/accumulation precision. This is distinct from v10's separate logit, state, accumulator, and readout error budget. Source pp.3–5: https://openreview.net/pdf?id=TLSUIyBIfs#page=4 and #page=5 .

Proof A.2 constructs a bounded-length simulation z of arbitrary x preserving, simultaneously for f and g, the relevant hardmax token-type proportions, periodic position and local suffix information. Since both models agree on z by hypothesis, triangle inequalities imply agreement on x. This is an expressivity/simulation argument; it does not estimate Markov transition probabilities from dependent visits.

## Theorem 4.2, average-case caveat

Conditional on a token probability vector P, symbols are i.i.d.; P itself has Dirichlet prior with positive coordinates α_s. If the mean model discrepancy at **every** short length through an explicit N₀ is ≤ε, then at every T the mean discrepancy is O(√ε), with paper's N₀ approximately ε^(-2-2/α₀) up to other factors and logs, α₀=min α_s. This is not a Markov trajectory theorem, and the √ε loss in the conclusion matters. Authors explicitly say a Markov extension via concentration might be possible but leave its conditions for future work. The short-string distribution cannot be arbitrary: the simulation proof must control how much long-sequence probability maps to improbable short strings. See pp.6–7 and Appendix A.3: https://openreview.net/pdf?id=TLSUIyBIfs#page=6 .

## Definition 5.1, Theorem 5.2, infinite precision and depth 2

Class F_τ: depth two, τ-local, translation invariant, nonnegative positional scores, no second-layer positional information, unit-bounded token embeddings; H first-layer heads, one second-layer head as written in the definition. Infinite precision throughout. Position contribution in attention is `log(i) φ(j,i)` while content logits are unscaled, specifically to balance local suffix and global token contributions. This differs from both their finite-precision model and ordinary fixed-bias attention. With α denoting the positive positional margin γ(f),γ(g) defined from distinct maxima of each first-layer head's positional-score set including 1, and C(f) the paper's norm-based complexity, there is

    N ≲ (max{C(f),C(g)}/ε) ^ max{1/γ(f),1/γ(g),3},

so agreement to ε on **all** strings up to N gives O(ε) agreement at all lengths. C involves exponential dependence on first-layer norm factors (and other norm factors inside the exponential), and polynomial dependence on remaining quantities as defined. This is a one-versus-two-layer comparison across separately constrained models, not a theorem for arbitrary depth or a depth lower bound. See https://openreview.net/pdf?id=TLSUIyBIfs#page=7 .

Proof architecture: Lemma B.1 represents layer-1 state as bounded Lipschitz function of the current local suffix, prefix unigram histogram, and position. Lemma 5.3 constructs a short subsequence approximating averages of bounded Lipschitz functions of suffixes and evolving prefix histograms, error O((G+Lip)(τ+1)n^-1/3). Its proof uses a two-state *auxiliary selection chain* to sample long contiguous blocks of an arbitrary fixed input string; this is not a hypothesis that the data are Markov or a finite-sample stochastic Markov risk theorem. In B.2 the same short sequence simulates f and g; stated final intermediate bound is C(f)n^-min(1/3,γ(f)). See https://openreview.net/pdf?id=TLSUIyBIfs#page=32 and #page=37 .

## In-context k-gram example and experimental overlap

Definition 5.4 targets the empirical distribution of tokens following occurrences of the terminal (k−1)-token context, not the true transition table. Thus k=3 corresponds to a binary second-order context; it overlaps directly with v10's same-history statistical reference, not automatically its oracle target. Its ratio is undefined if the terminal context has zero historical occurrences unless an added fallback is specified.

Paper credits Nichani et al. (2024) for a two-layer approximation with k−1 first-layer heads and τ=k−1. Appendix B.3 sketches heads copying lagged tokens and a second head using score β times matching features. It bounds deviation from empirical k-gram by a quantity proportional to `T/(exp(β) * match_count)`. It simplifies to O(exp(-β)) and concludes C(f)=ε^-Θ(k²) **heuristically on “typical” strings where match_count=Θ(T)**. The hidden constant can depend on context frequency, and no uniform positive occupancy for the v10 rare-state family follows. In particular, it cannot settle zero counts or all-string approximation of the empirical target merely by quoting β≈log(1/ε). Check all details independently before treating this sketch as an implementation theorem. Source https://openreview.net/pdf?id=TLSUIyBIfs#page=38 .

Experiments draw a random k-gram transition tensor, sample a trajectory, then replace an interior block with the terminal context to force a past match. They train small two-layer models against the empirical k-gram target, not the true oracle TV; this modified distribution is not automatically the stationary, unaltered Markov trajectory of v10. Training experiments support behavior empirically, not generic SGD attainability or finite-sample oracle-risk guarantees (§6 and Appendix C, pp.8,38–39).

4. A valid reduction from Izzo to v10 would require proving both models belong to the same F_τ and numerical model; an oracle or same-history reference is representable in that class with bounded costs; the extremely strong all-short-strings agreement hypothesis holds; and output-vector error transfers to TV. Its theorem could then transfer **model discrepancy**, not replace the separate statistical risk and rare-context analysis. None of these steps is automatic. A learned model's finite observed training loss is not all-short-string agreement.
