## Reconstruction and evidence

1. **Kernel and stationary law.** For 0<u<1/4, w=(1-u)/3, k=3u/(1-u)<1 and h=kt<3/4. Each permitted transition is positive and each row sums to one. πP=(u(1-t)+wh, ut+w(1-h),w,w)=(u,w,w,w). Thus π is common to all t at fixed u. Under stationarity P(X_next=1)=ut+w/2+w(1-h)+w/2=2w. Irreducibility is valid for every u>0; u=0 itself is excluded, not silently included.
2. **Exact common-atom mixing and certificate distinction.** Direct multiplication gives stated P²; its 11 entries t/2,1/4,(1-h)/2,1/4 are ≥1/8. Equality at t=1/4 in the first row shows sharpness of this particular common-atom constant. Therefore P²≥(1/8)δ11 and the Dobrushin contraction gives ‖μP^m−π‖TV≤(7/8)^floor(m/2). The stronger assertion of an m=2 stationary-measure minorization uniformly over u is false: P²(10,01)/π(01)=ut²/w²→0 at fixed t. Distinction is correctly made.
3. **Four-step stationary-measure certificate.** P⁴(x,·)≥(1/8)P²(11,·). The latter row is (h/2,(1-h)/2,1/4,1/4). Its ratios to π are t/(2w), (1-h)/(2w), 1/(4w),1/(4w), respectively ≥3/8,3/8,3/4,3/4, using t≥1/4, h<3/4, w<1/3. Thus P⁴≥(3/64)π uniformly. This is a four-step certificate and does not satisfy an assumption requiring the same minorization at exactly two steps.
4. **Full path KL.** There are n=N−2 transitions of pair states in X₁:N. Stationary initial KL is u log(u/v)+3w_u log(w_u/w_v)=kl(u‖v). Forward transition row 00 contributes u kl(t‖t′) and row 10 contributes w_u kl(h‖h′); rows 01 and 11 are common. For fixed u, initial KL vanishes; Pinsker gives ≥2nuΔ². The χ² bound gives kl(t‖t′)≤(16/3)Δ², kl(kt‖kt′)≤16kΔ² since t′≥1/4 and 1−kt′≥1/4, so total ≤(64/3)nuΔ². Taylor's integral remainder and second derivative 1/[x(1-x)]≥4/(3k) on the interval between kt and kt′ give w kl(kt‖kt′)≥(2/3)uΔ², including Δ=0 by continuity.
5. **Actual terminal event and reverse orientation.** Time reversal is R(i,j)=π(j)P(j,i)/π(i), giving the stated P*. Condition on S_N=00 and traverse n reverse transitions. Chain rule weights reverse row KL by the occupation distribution under the FIRST parameter, yielding sum_j[A_j kl(t‖t′)+B_j kl(h‖h′)]. No unconditional/conditional interchange was made. R² fourth-column entries t/2,(1-h)/2,1/4,1/4 give the same 1/8 minorization and invariant π. Since each singleton discrepancy is bounded by TV, A_j≤u+(7/8)^floor(j/2), hence Σ_{j=0}^{n-1}A_j≤nu+2/(1−7/8)=nu+16; ΣB_j≤n. With k<4u, this gives [(208/3)nu+256/3]Δ²≤(256/3)(1+nu)Δ².
