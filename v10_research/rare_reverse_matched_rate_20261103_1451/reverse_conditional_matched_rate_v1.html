Rare terminal-state minimax rate via reversed transition counts

3 November 2026. Mathematical candidate and explicit structured-mask realization; no novelty, formal-verification, training, standard-dense-decoder or full finite-bit claim. An independent analyst has checked the main proof and constants; the exact final artifact and realization require a separate hash-bound review. No random sampling, enumeration, numerical test, external write or source overwrite was used.

Scope: public exact u and the one-parameter family are known; t is unknown. The risk conditions on the actual terminal state 00 of the same stationary path. The rate is statistical and depends on the known family; it is not an architecture-wide lower bound or a claim about estimating an unrestricted unknown second-order transition table.

1. Model, estimator, and precise result

Let N≥2 and M=N−2. Given 0<u<1/4, w=(1−u)/3 and unknown t∈[1/4,3/4], the binary order-two source has pair chain S_i=(X_{i−1},X_i), stationary pair law π=(u,w,w,w) and successor-1 probabilities θ=(t,1/2,1−ut/w,1/2), in order 00,01,10,11. The observed path is X₁:N with S₂∼π; the event S_N=00 has probability u>0. Its next-1 target is t. No X_(N+1) is observed.

For M≥1, count complete historical triples, all ending at or before X_N:

H_abc = Σ_(i=3)^N 1{(X_(i−2),X_(i−1),X_i)=(a,b,c)},
K=H_001,  J=H_001+H_101.

For M=0 let J=K=0. Define the source-independent-in-t, public-u estimator

t̂ = clip_[1/4,3/4] ((w/u)·K/J)  if J>0,
     1/2                           if J=0.

For every allowed u,t,N,

E_t[ |t̂−t|  | S_N=00 ] ≤ min{1/2, 145 / √(1+Mu)}.          (1)

For the same public u and actual-terminal conditional experiment, the earlier full-history two-point lower bound states that for every one common parameter-independent prediction rule, with

t± = 1/2 ± 1/[32√(1+Mu)],
max_(t∈{t−,t+}) E_t[ TV(p̂,Bernoulli(t)) | S_N=00 ]
  ≥ (1−1/√6)/[32√(1+Mu)].                                  (2)

Binary TV equals absolute error in probability assigned to 1. Hence the conditional minimax risk, over all common full-path rules that know u but not t, is Θ((1+Mu)^−1/2), with explicit unequal constants in (1)–(2). This is an order statement, not a claim of optimal constants or a standard attention lower bound. For N=2 the sole input on the conditional event is 00, the estimator returns 1/2, and its worst error is 1/4.

2. Reversed process and observed counts

Put h=ut/w and condition on S_N=00. By stationary time reversal, Z_j=S_(N−j), j=0,…,M, is a homogeneous Markov chain started deterministically at Z₀=00, with transition matrix

R = [ 1−t   0    t    0  ]
    [  h    0   1−h   0  ]
    [  0   1/2   0   1/2 ]
    [  0   1/2   0   1/2 ],     states 00,01,10,11.

In particular, R(01,00)=h. The reverse-time departure and success counts are exactly

J=Σ_(j=0)^(M−1) 1{Z_j=01},
K=Σ_(j=0)^(M−1) 1{Z_j=01,Z_(j+1)=00}.

Indeed a reverse 01→00 edge is a forward 001 triple; every reverse departure from 01 corresponds to a forward triple ending 01, namely 001 or 101. The terminal query is not independent of the history: conditioning is handled by the deterministic reversed-chain initial state, not by replacing the query with an independent sample.

3. Split proof: concentration of reverse opportunities

Each entry of R²(s,11) is at least 1/8. Since R(11,01)=1/2, R³(s,01)≥1/16 for every state s. For M≥6 let L=⌊(M−1)/3⌋≥M/6 and B_ℓ=1{Z_(3ℓ)=01}, ℓ=1,…,L. All sampled states occur at indices ≤M−1, so J≥ΣB_ℓ. Conditioning on the reversed history up to time 3(ℓ−1),

P(B_ℓ=1 | past) ≥ 1/16.

The variables need not be independent. Their conditional exponential moment is at most that of independent Bernoulli(1/16) variables for a nonpositive exponent. Applying the multiplicative Chernoff bound to this supermartingale moment gives

P(J<M/192 | S_N=00) ≤ exp(−L/128) ≤ exp(−M/768).       (3)

One explicit derivation chooses λ=1: each conditional factor is at most 1−p+pe^−1, p=1/16, and Markov's inequality bounds P(ΣB≤Lp/2) by exp[Lp(e^−1−1/2)]≤exp(−Lp/8), using e≥8/3. Since Lp/2=L/32≥M/192, (3) follows. This proof does not assume independent block indicators.

4. Split proof: self-normalized reverse martingale

Let the reverse filtration contain Z₀,…,Z_j and define

D=K−hJ=Σ_(j=0)^(M−1) 1{Z_j=01}(1{Z_(j+1)=00}−h).

This is a sum of martingale differences under the already-conditioned reversed law, even though Z₀ is not stationary. Orthogonality and h(1−h)≤h imply

E[D² | S_N=00] = h(1−h) E[J | S_N=00] ≤ hM.           (4)

No random denominator is treated as independent of D. On G={J≥M/192}, J>0 and clipping is nonexpansive because t∈[1/4,3/4], so by Cauchy–Schwarz,

E[|t̂−t|·1_G | S_N=00]
 ≤ (192w/(uM)) E|D|
 ≤ 192√(wt)/√(Mu) ≤ 96/√(Mu).                              (5)

The last inequality uses w≤1/3 and t≤3/4. On Gᶜ the clipped loss is at most 1/2. For M≥6, (3) and (5) yield

E[|t̂−t| | S_N=00] ≤ min{1/2, 96/√(Mu)+(1/2)e^(−M/768)}.  (6)

For x=Mu≥1, exp(−M/768)≤√(384/(eM))<12/√M≤12/√x, and therefore (6) is at most 102/√x≤145/√(1+x). For x<1 clipping alone is enough for (1). When M<6 the same clipping bound suffices for (1) because 1+Mu<9/4; M=0 uses the declared fallback. There is no empirical-Bayes prior or iid-context premise.

5. Finite-triple comparison and paid model-R realization

The usual same-history forward add-one estimator for the rare row is (H_001+1)/(H_000+H_001+2). Its earlier conditional upper has an explicit logarithmic term and was separately reviewed; bound (1) does not prove that this add-one estimator fails to attain the same rate. Here the known stationary family supplies an additional useful reverse experiment: J=H_001+H_101 counts visits to reverse 01, which has stationary mass w, while the corresponding success h is O(u). Both procedures use exactly the same observed binary word and no independent query. A streaming implementation can accumulate K,J in one pass and evaluate one division with O(log M) bits per exact counter, plus storage/precision for public u, w and final output; it is a required computational baseline.

For M≥1, the estimator has the following ideal real-arithmetic structured-mask implementation, not yet an end-to-end fixed-bit result. First block: two singleton-mask copy heads place X_(i−2),X_(i−1) beside the current X_i at each i≥3; early positions have harmless self masks. Tokenwise ReLU gates form

f_K(i)=ReLU(X_i−X_(i−2)−X_(i−1)) = 1{001},
f_J(i)=ReLU(X_i−X_(i−1))              = 1{ending in 01}.

A single second-block zero-score head, with terminal mask {3,…,N} and two value coordinates, emits (K/M,J/M). Its exact probability weights are 1/M, with no dependence on the future query in history-side values. The terminal pair is retained separately. At M=0 bypass this empty attention mask and emit 1/2.

For fixed M and public u, a finite terminal ReLU lookup can compute the ratio without assuming a free neural division. Multiply the two ideal averages by M to obtain integers K,J. For each 0≤j≤M form a tent ψ_j(z)=ReLU(1−|z−j|), and for 0≤k≤j≤M form the gate ReLU(ψ_j(J)+ψ_k(K)−1). On valid integer counts exactly one pair gate is one. Give that gate coefficient clip_[1/4,3/4]((w/u)k/j) for j>0; give (0,0) coefficient 1/2. Summing these gates returns t̂ exactly. The lookup uses O(M²) gates and stored coefficients and a constant number of pointwise ReLU stages; whether those stages add Transformer blocks depends on the declared implementation. To obtain a full next-bit output, gate on exact terminal S_N=00 and use any valid fallback (for example 1/2) elsewhere, then output exact complement (1−p,p). Thus two first-block copy-head instances plus one uniform aggregation head suffice in this explicit model-R construction. The full unconditional risk of that arbitrary off-00 fallback is not claimed to obey (1).

Optional narrow precision interface: if the two averaged head coordinates are each within ε<1/(4M), and multiplication by M and subsequent gates are exact, replace ψ_j by the plateau ReLU(2−4|z−j|)−ReLU(1−4|z−j|). The true integer gate is then exactly one and all others zero. If lookup coefficients are stored with absolute error ≤η and clipping/complement are exact, the prediction error increases by at most η. These are assumed output/weight error ports, not a derivation of attention, exp, score, copy, accumulator, u-encoding, ReLU or fixed-point errors. O(M²) coefficients need separate storage and preparation; an executor evaluating the lookup at every token may spend O(NM²) operations, while terminal-only sparse execution costs O(M²) after aggregation. Structural masks, head widths, positions, output accuracy and any dense-causal compilation must be charged explicitly. No same-budget architecture separation is asserted.

6. Evidence, lineage and review limits

The prior rare-family derivation and independent review establish the reverse matrix, common atom and two-point conditional lower. The original direct add-one conditional bound and structured attention analysis remain separate antecedents; the present reverse-count estimator exploits the parameterized stationary family and public u. Generic reverse-time martingales, Bernoulli concentration, Euler/transition counts, and empirical conditional estimates are classical tools. This note makes no novelty claim and does not assert that its estimator or rate was absent from existing work. The previous Route B two-scalar-port obstruction does not apply to the joint feature head used here; no full-network lower follows.

Proof obligations separated above are: conditional reverse-law identity; M=0 and positive-count mapping; occupancy concentration from the 3-step atom; martingale variance and clipping; minimax comparison with the pre-existing lower; and the optional paid model-R interface. Independent review of the statistical proof has reported no counterexample, subject to a separately bound final-artifact review. No inference is made about training dynamics, arbitrary u unknown to the network, finite-precision lower bounds, or external peer acceptance.
