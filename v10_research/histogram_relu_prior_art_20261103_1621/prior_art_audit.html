Source audit: histogram ReLU obstruction and prior work

3 November 2026. Targeted primary-source comparison, not an exhaustive review or a novelty certification. No random or numerical experiment was performed.

Assessment. The restriction of finite ReLU functions to finitely many affine pieces, quantitative curvature-based approximation lower bounds, and even a fixed-depth integral L1 lower bound for the reciprocal 1/x are already in the literature. Efficient finite-accuracy ReLU approximation of rational functions is also known. The studied calculation connects these well-known ingredients to a specific actual-terminal Markov prediction experiment with a restricted normalized histogram interface and explicit finite-M mixing correction. The sources inspected do not by themselves establish that exact bridge is new, nor does the bridge imply a full-Transformer lower bound.

Target: a fixed or bounded-width/depth scalar clipped ReLU readout sees only an eight-coordinate normalized pair-context/successor count bank, a bounded terminal encoding, and optionally a vanishing BOS mass. The source family has u=π₀₀, v=uθ₀₀, and population bank affine in (u,v), while θ₀₀=v/u. An addendum takes a source prior on a compact rectangle and derives a fixed-architecture prior-risk lower bound, extending to length-varying readouts only under a uniform Lipschitz bound. The mandatory separate optional-BOS correction changes the finite-M numerator from +12 to +13 if that extra coordinate is used. No other attention, division oracle, unrestricted features or unbounded length input is admitted.

1. Reciprocal L1 lower bound: especially close prior

Telgarsky, Neural Networks and Rational Functions, ICML 2017, Proposition 1.5 already proves an integral L1 approximation lower bound for f(x)=1/x on [1/2,3/4]: for its stated layer/node bounds, the integral error exceeds ε. This is directly analogous to the present restriction of v/u to a fixed-v line, and significantly stronger overlap than merely citing generic piecewise-linearity. The source proposition concerns deterministic function approximation, not by itself the stationary source prior, the actual terminal event, normalized count fluctuations, or an L-Lipschitz finite-M extension. Changes of interval and a positive fixed multiplier do not make the reciprocal mechanism new.

The same paper’s Theorem 1.1(1) gives ReLU approximations of polynomial ratios p/q when q is bounded away from zero, with complexity polylogarithmic in inverse target error for fixed degree and denominator conditions. Thus there is no contradiction between a strictly positive gap for one fixed finite architecture and arbitrarily small fixed error using architectures chosen for the desired accuracy. It does not give an N-uniform standard-Transformer implementation, nor a zero-error finite ReLU representation of division.

2. Linear regions and curvature are established tools

Mehrabi, Tchamkerten and Yousefi, Bounds on the Approximation Power of Feedforward Neural Networks, ICML 2018, Theorem 1 and Corollary 3 relate the number of affine pieces along a line to width/depth and lower bound approximation of curved functions; their setup allows non-neighbor-layer connections. Their stated lower bound uses a uniform approximation criterion and their own smoothness/Hessian hypotheses. The current elementary second-difference proof translates curvature into a specific integrated L1 constant. The generic line-piece/curvature argument is not novel.

Yarotsky, Error bounds for approximations with deep ReLU networks, 2017, Theorem 6 gives a fixed-depth complexity lower bound for uniformly approximating a non-affine C² function and proves it by restriction to a strictly convex or concave line segment. Its quantifiers and sup-norm loss differ from the present source-averaged terminal loss. Serra, Tjandraatmadja and Ramalingam, Bounding and Counting Linear Regions of Deep Neural Networks, 2018 is a primary antecedent for finite ReLU linear regions. None is a statement that a causal Transformer with extra attention must fail this prediction task.

3. Transformer and Markov-probability antecedents

Rajaraman et al., Transformers on Markov Data: Constant Depth Suffices, arXiv v1, Theorems 4.4–4.5 and Remark 4.2 already give attention-only conditional k-gram representations with their specified depth/head and positional mechanisms, and state an O(log T)-bit approximation qualification. Their empirical estimate assumes a positive matching count; the constructions and precision semantics are not the same as a fixed normalized histogram followed by a fixed finite ReLU ratio readout. These positive constructions do not contradict the restricted obstruction and greatly narrow any claim about general architectures.

D’Angelo et al., Induction Heads Interpolate N-Grams, arXiv v1, Proposition 3.1, equation (8), and Corollary 4.1 already give mask-weighted transition counts and limiting BOS add-α smoothing. Softmax normalization itself is a nonlinear ratio; the pure ReLU-bank restriction intentionally forbids using another such attention stage or division oracle. Makkuva et al., Attention with Markov, arXiv version, Theorem 1 give an explicit single-layer global population-loss minimizer that matches a fixed binary first-order Markov kernel, with parameters depending on that fixed source. Their quantifier ∀ source ∃ parameters differs from requiring one source-independent rule for the whole family and should not be mistaken for either a proof or a refutation of the current claim.

Weiss, Goldberg and Yahav, Thinking Like Transformers, arXiv v2 provide RASP histogram constructions; Behrens, Biggio and Zdeborová, Counting in Small Transformers, arXiv v3, §4.1 and Proposition 4.1 study attention/BOS counting under their stated dimensions. Histogram access is an established idea, not a new representational mechanism.

4. Length-generalization comparison

Izzo, Nichani and Lee, Quantitative Bounds for Length Generalization in Transformers, arXiv v1, Theorem 5.2, Definition 5.4 and Appendix B.3 analyze two-layer infinite-precision limit transformers under their τ-local, translation-invariant positional assumptions. Theorem 5.2 is a worst-case finite-length simulation/generalization implication between two functions in the declared class; Appendix B.3 gives a heuristic k-gram construction, with zero-match behavior unrestricted. Their Theorem 4.2 average-case distribution is an iid categorical/Dirichlet mixture; the authors discuss extending average-case results to Markov sequences as future work. These are strong length-generalization neighbors but not the present same-history order-two, actual-terminal conditional-TV population bound, and they do not settle the restricted bank question. Their theorem is not an unconditional guarantee for arbitrary position encodings, finite hardware, or a trained network.

5. The specific bridge and remaining uncertainty

The following are mathematical reductions in the studied note, not independent discoveries of the underlying methods: (i) construct a well-mixing stationary order-two family where the normalized bank converges to an affine surface and the 00 target restricts to reciprocal curvature; (ii) use a source prior over a rectangle and the exact terminal probability u to convert an integrated approximation gap into population next-bit TV; (iii) use edge-function covariance decay to transfer that gap to finite histories without making terminal state and bank independent; (iv) control length-dependent readouts by a uniform Lipschitz constant, rather than interchanging a limit and an infimum. The optional BOS correction must accompany the finite-M statement.

The proof assigns source parameters after fixing the rule, or averages against a declared prior; it does not exhibit one source that defeats every different rule. The finite-M prior lower bound can be negative until history is long enough. It makes no claim for a final-softmax quantitative constant, unconstrained length-dependent weights, more attention, added input channels, or all Transformers. Targeted searches covered reciprocal approximation, affine-region lower bounds, Markov probability estimation, attention counting and length generalization. No systematic search over all nonlinear approximation, ergodic prediction, coding and Transformer literatures was performed; failure to locate an equivalent theorem is not evidence of novelty.
