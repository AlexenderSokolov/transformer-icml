## 8. NEW tolerance corollary: separate fresh proof

Let r=sqrt M≥8, e=e_K≥1/M, x=1/(er), f=1+log(1+x), T=r/f. Since x≤r and `1+log(1+r)≤r` for r≥8 (positive at 8 and increasing difference), 1≤f≤r and **1≤T≤r**.

For real u>0, `g(u)=u[1+log(1+1/(eu))]` has derivative `1+log(1+v)−v/(1+v)>0`. Therefore it suffices to check u=T. At this value,

`F(T)/s = [1+log(1+fx)]/f ≤ [f+log f]/f ≤ 2`,

using 1+fx≤f(1+x) and log f≤f. Every integer 1≤k≤floor T satisfies the sufficient bound; k=0 has F=0 by definition. Since T≥1, floor T≥T/2, so rounding retains an order-sized positive budget. No branch evaluates 1/k at k=0.

Conversely F(k)≤As implies k≤Ar because F≥k/M. With A'=max(A,1), k≤A'r, so the logarithmic bracket is at least `1+log(1+x/A')`. Concavity at factor 1/A' plus 1≥1/A' makes this at least f/A'. Hence `k f/(A'M)≤As`, or **k≤AA'T**. This also covers 0<A<1, where A'=1; there is no unjustified assumption A≥1.

The manuscript does **not** assert an iff with the same multiplier. For 0<c≤1, the further displayed inequality `F(cT)≤c[2+log(1/c)]s` follows by 1+fx/c≤(f/c)(1+x). Decreasing c handles any desired positive fixed profile multiplier; if cT<1, only k=0 is guaranteed after rounding. This explicitly resolves the potentially problematic positive-integer feasibility case.

To pass from profile order to risk order, apply the minimax lower cK min(1,max(s,F)). Along M→∞, a fixed-multiple statistical-risk upper eventually lies strictly below the lower bound's cap. Thus F=O(s), and the converse follows from the upper. Finitely many remaining horizons are absorbed into constants; this is an order statement, not an exact finite-multiplier inverse. For positive unprotected budgets, the endpoint profile yields precisely k=O(sqrt M) and δ=O(M^-1/2) in the same order sense. Zero budget imposes no δ condition.

At β=1/M, binary e=M^-1/2 and f=1+log2, whereas nonbinary e=M^-1 and f=1+log(1+sqrt M). At β=0 both use e=M^-1. These examples and the Chinese guide's order-only statements agree. No clean-reconstruction, practical safety, or arbitrary-small-multiplier threshold is claimed.
