## Learned-position smoke

One initialization, five conditions and 200 Adam updates per condition on the shared 12,800 trajectories alternated M=64 and 128; evaluation also included held-out M=256. Each attack selects CE against the clean history's B=(C+1)/(J+2) over identity and nine finite proposals, with the endpoint protected. The same clean histories are paired across models but each model chooses its own edit, so attacked inputs need not be identical. B equals the conditional predictive probability under the specified independent-row prior, not the true transition probability of every fixed stress source. The panel used three sources, two starts and 256 trajectories per source/start at each length; its selected maxima and pointwise standard errors are descriptive, not worst-source bounds, simultaneous inference or tests over the full Hamming ball.

The smoke did not demonstrate useful learned positional protection: learned weights moved, but learned edit training did not clearly outperform the tested count-only or weak frozen-random control; its normalized stress-panel maximum was worse at M=256. The random control was nearly uniform (M=128 half masses 0.50393 and 0.49960, greatest pointwise relative departure about 2.72%), not a broad test of random features. Replacing learned profiles by uniform profiles improved measured scores in this combined intervention, but changes features and attack selection and can induce distribution shift; this does not isolate causal harm from learned positions. The supplied fixed-half control did not show clear benefit in that short training, but contains information needed for the half count and was not directly taught the gate; no inability or impossibility follows. An independent internal audit reproduced outputs and checked legality, but reused forward/proposal/statistic code; it is not an independent mathematical implementation or formal verification.

A can return 0 or 1 and has infinite-CE ties: among 15,360 nonzero-budget A candidate sets, 4,009 had infinite maxima, 3,440 multiple infinite maxima, and first-index tie-breaking selected identity in 3,226. A's selected candidate failed to maximize true-source TV within proposals in 5,855 sets, as a post-hoc oracle diagnostic. The source was **not** used to select the operational attack, and using it would change the threat model. CE maximization is not generally source-TV maximization. Original smoke and calibration files are retained; smoke checkpoints have model weights, not Adam moments, so they cannot by themselves resume the interrupted optimizer state.

## Fixed-half Q-supervised calibration

At M=64, a fixed 353-parameter, width-32 tanh readout received supplied fixed pooling features and explicit teacher Q=0.75A+0.125. Four collision words used in training received half of the empirical objective; there was no attack or natural-label feature-discovery experiment. Count-scaled and normalized inputs have the same fixed-M representable function class and identical initial predictions, but raw Adam updates are not invariant to the reparameterization. Three initializations shared one frozen training sample, validation sample and minibatch stream. All three count-scaled fits passed the declared finite screen (mean validation KL≤0.01 and supplied-collision max error≤0.05); all normalized fits failed under that budget. This supports a finite parameterization-sensitive optimization contrast, not normalized-readout impossibility or a diagnosis of the original smoke's sole cause.

The four collision words are training examples, hence this is an **in-sample** collision screen. Independent train/validation draws overlapped in 9 distinct strings, representing 25 of 2,048 validation trajectories; they are not word-disjoint. Count-scaled validation maximum absolute errors remained 0.401–0.434, with 13–14 of 2,048 errors above 0.1 per fit. All scaled fits therefore fail any claim of small uniform teacher approximation, despite good mean KL. Independent literal recounting and checkpoint reconstruction support the reported metrics; a replay reused the same training implementation and environment. The audit also measured high tanh saturation, a diagnostic rather than proof of a particular mechanism or improved conditioning.

## First rare-weight ablation: exploratory, exposed, and incomplete original raw data

The sealed first study paired corrected empirical-prior and rare-boost weighting at shared initial weights and shared sample/index streams. On the eight low-J feature vectors in seven held-out groups, boosted/corrected mean absolute-error ratios were 0.317, 0.459 and 0.448; the finite composite screen passed for all three paired initializations. Group selection followed earlier analyses, so it was not historically blind. The conditional rare-heldout sample's mean error fell, but its boosted maxima remained 0.541–0.594; on the 11-feature structural slice boosted maxima remained 0.330–0.385. Natural-prior mean KL fell in each pair, whereas natural-prior mean absolute error **increased** in every pair. The natural unconditioned sample, conditional rare/group holdout and equally weighted finite structural panel have different distributions; they cannot be merged into one population risk. Collision words were in-sample. Three paired initializations share one dataset and minibatch stream, not three independent data replications. Reweighting changes gradient noise as well as the objective; no uniform approximation, source-risk, capacity, general optimization or robustness theorem follows.

A serious preservation promise was breached. Of 8,192 train candidates, 1,633 rejected and 4,511 accepted-overflow words were not retained; of 122,880 conditional-holdout candidates, 121,783 rejected and 73 accepted-overflow words were not retained. Thus **128,000 original candidate words** were discarded: 123,416 rejected and 4,584 accepted overflow. Latent source tables and transition uniforms for all 139,264 generated trajectories, including the 8,192 saved natural words, were not stored. Each *retained* word's initial pair is recoverable as its first two bits, but starts of discarded words are unavailable in retained arrays. No missing original candidate or latent record is restored by seed replay. The run retained selected words and Q targets, all sampled indices, 12,000 update-summary rows, 24 parameter checkpoints, final predictions, metrics, console and sealed code, but not full gradient tensors or Adam moments. The completed data were exposed; they cannot be presented as a pristine unseen confirmatory set. The detected preservation defect was not shown to trigger a failed runtime check before completion; the attempted stop was too late.

A separate retained-data independent audit found no discrepancy in reconstructed groups, targets, checkpoint predictions or finite screen, but did not replay every optimizer update; the loss/metric calculations depend on the saved word data, not the missing latents, so the loss of latents alone does not demonstrate numerical bias. It does prevent a complete contemporaneous archive and a full source-level reconstruction. An independent compatible seed regeneration is retrospective consistency evidence, not an original record or proof of the unobserved execution. The omission is neither cured nor concealed by subsequent study.

## Second rare-weight study: fresh data, nonblind, independently reconciled

The later six-fit follow-up used fresh training/evaluation draws but was designed after earlier results were exposed, so is **nonblind**. All three paired boosted/corrected rare-conditional mean-error ratios were 0.727, 0.662 and 0.676 and passed the predeclared computational screen; natural-prior mean KL changes were +0.000245, −0.000227 and +0.000409, within the +0.002 allowance. Natural-prior mean absolute error nevertheless increased in all three pairs; boosted rare-heldout maxima remained 0.604–0.738. The 20-vector panel comprised groups absent from earlier input/evaluation arrays, but identities and Q targets were known and the design informed by earlier results: **input-novel, not historically blind**. Its boosted mean errors improved for every pair, while maximum error rose in two and remained 0.380–0.403. The unconditioned natural sample shared 15 distinct strings with training; the conditional rare sample followed a different hash-and-reserve law. Each trio again shares the same datasets and index stream. None establishes uniform error, adversarial/source risk or natural next-token discovery.

For this completed run all 14 committed candidate batches were retained, 114,688 candidates total, including rejects and overflow, tables, starts, uniforms, flags, states and corresponding pre-draw intents. The frozen indices, 12,000 update summaries, 12,000 full-gradient/proposed-parameter write-ahead records, 24 checkpoints with Adam states, predictions, metrics and console were retained. Independent completion audit reconstructed the candidate generation, every recorded gradient/Adam update, checkpoints and metrics, and reconciled the journal with CSV and console; it reports no discrepancy within its checks. Journal entries alone are proposals, not proof of applied updates: completion here is supported by reconciliation. An intent without a committed batch would not preserve values left only in RAM. Cross-platform bitwise replay and the earlier missing records are not established. These independent LLM audits and local hashes are not external peer review or trusted timestamps.
