# Correction and checked deterministic baseline

2026-11-03. This file supersedes the **Sharp baseline** paragraph in AUDIT.md and the recommendation there to report an unconditional matching 7/8 algorithm. The earlier paragraph falsely said every normalized clause of length at most three is satisfied by a uniform assignment with probability at least 7/8. A one-literal clause has probability 1/2 and a two-literal clause 3/4. Duplicate literal occurrences may create such clauses when normalized. The hardness proofs' normalization still preserves every clause's truth value and the imported gap; this correction concerns the proposed matching policy, not that reduction.

## What can be proved constructively

Given the original list of nonempty clauses with up to three literal occurrences, normalize each independently: remove repeated occurrences of the same signed literal; if both signs of a variable occur, mark the clause always true. Do not delete, merge, pad, or change the multiplicity of clauses. The tautology count is t, and let r_j be the number of distinct variables in a non-tautological normalized clause. Thus 1≤r_j≤3. Use expectation *only as a mathematical averaging device*, without random sampling or RNG calls.

Start from the exact average E0 = t + sum_j(1-2^(-r_j)) of the number of satisfied clauses over the uniform Boolean assignments. Given a partial assignment, each already true or tautological clause contributes 1; each unsatisfied clause with r≥1 unassigned distinct variables contributes 1-2^(-r); and a clause whose literals are all false contributes 0. Sum these rational values with multiplicity. At variable i compute the two conditional averages for setting 0 and 1; choose a larger one, breaking ties with 0. Their mean is the previous average, so this never decreases it. Each clause has at most three variables, denominators divide 8, and both scores are computable by a single scan of the m clauses, so O(nm) simple operations suffice. At the end the conditional average equals the actual integer S(x), proving S(x)≥E0.

For every normalized nonempty clause E0≥m/2, and so this algorithm achieves at least 1/2 of the global optimum S*≤m. If *every non-tautological normalized clause contains exactly three distinct variables*, E0=t+(7/8)(m-t)≥7m/8 and the algorithm satisfies at least (7/8)S*. Tautologies, duplicate *clauses*, and unused variables cause no difficulty. More generally, any input whose computed E0≥7m/8 enjoys the same bound, but no such inequality is asserted for arbitrary normalized inputs. No inference is made that Håstad's E3 promise silently excludes repeated occurrences: Definition 2.3 explicitly says clause length exactly three; no distinctness condition was located in the inspected definitions. Establishing a no-repeated-variable hardness restriction would require a separate cited theorem or proof.

For the 0945 model, q-p=S/(MP), and for the 0954 model q-p=S/(2P). Fix the constructed assignment independently of the clean history, replace the first n bits and leave all other bits fixed. There are at most n edits, the protected pair is unchanged, and on every history the resulting utility is exactly the displayed scale times S(x). Consequently the 7/8 policy factor applies to these restricted encodings only; the half-factor applies to all encodings meeting the nonempty-clause assumptions. The analogous 0939 model has a different positive floor and already has its own stronger 3/5 baseline; do not substitute this derivation without its affine expression. These are not algorithms for arbitrary neural predictors or general attacker objectives.

## Primary sources and evidence

[Håstad, Some Optimal Inapproximability Results](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/max3satl.pdf), Definitions 2.2–2.4, Theorem 2.15 and proof (PDF pp. 6–8), and Theorem 6.5 (p. 37): the primary source explicitly describes the conditional-expected-values method for its Max-CSP random-assignment bound and states the 7/8+epsilon satisfiable E3 gap. Its stated literal-count definition does not explicitly settle repeated-variable clauses, and its general k-tuple claim is not used as authority for treating repeated occurrences as independent here. [Raghavan 1988](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022000088900037) is primary antecedent on deterministic conversion; only metadata/abstract were accessible (the publisher page denied full text), and the proof above is self-contained.

`check_conditional.py` is a deterministic finite diagnostic, not the proposed polynomial algorithm: it enumerates all completions for five small fixed cases, records original clauses, each branch's exact conditional average, chosen bit, final optimal score, a complete fixed history and legal edits, and both risk scalings in the exclusively created `conditional_cases.jsonl`. These include repeated same-sign literals, two distinct literals after normalization, tautology, duplicate clauses, all eight distinct-variable clauses, and all-distinct clauses. All five passed. This script's completion enumeration is exponential and used only for independent small-instance checking; the analytic clause scan described above is polynomial. No RNG, sampling, external writes or prior artifacts were used.
