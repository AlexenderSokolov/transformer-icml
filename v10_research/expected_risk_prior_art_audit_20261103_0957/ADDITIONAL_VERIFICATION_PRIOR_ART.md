# Additional exact-verification comparators

2026-11-03. These direct-source comparisons supplement AUDIT.md and do not alter the separate correction. A parallel reviewer inspected the original theorem text and proof sections as noted; my own OpenReview opens returned an anti-bot page, so I have not independently checked the two OpenReview proofs. No bypass was attempted.

* [Complexity of Formal Explainability for Sequential Models](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=73lu1yw6At), ICLR 2024 anonymous submission, Proposition 1 and Appendix D pp. 20–21 as inspected by the reviewer: existence of a counterfactual within Hamming distance k for transformers is NP-complete. The inspected reduction uses an unrestricted encoding function to contain a hard MLP, so do not misdescribe this as showing hardness of a standard embedding plus a constrained softmax head. The objective is pointwise 0–1 output change, not true next-symbol TV. A point-mass clean distribution would make pointwise 0–1 robustness equal expected robustness if that paper's architecture and such distributions were allowed; that observation does not subsume the present iid source, protected endpoint, calibrated predictor output, or value-approximation gap. It is a very strong antecedent against claiming that discrete adversarial transformer verification hardness itself is new.
* [Sälzer, Alsmann and Lange, Transformer Encoder Satisfiability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18548), ICLR 2025, Theorem 3 and Section 5 as inspected by the reviewer: bounded-word encoder satisfiability is NP-complete with unary length for classes containing the specified T_udec construction and NEXPTIME-complete with binary length. Its model uses hardmax and expressive scoring/positional machinery. This is acceptance of any bounded-length word, not the same fixed Hamming neighborhood or TV expected objective. The theorem is an independent strong formal-reasoning precursor, not literal architecture-matched subsumption.
* [Baluta et al., Quantitative Verification of Neural Networks and Its Security Applications](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~prateeks/papers/NPAQ.pdf), Theorem 2.3, Definition 2.4 and §3 robustness application, read directly: #P-hard counting of properties of binarized networks, with an example counting class-changing words in a Hamming ball. Their count/probability is over perturbed candidate words; it is not the same quantifier as expectation over clean histories of a maximized loss. The paper's no-FPRAS remark is for multiplicative count approximation, not constant-additive normalized risk. Weighted counting is described as an extension, not claimed as a theorem proved in the inspected source.
