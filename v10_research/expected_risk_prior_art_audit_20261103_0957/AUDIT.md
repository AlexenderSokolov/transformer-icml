## Assessment

**Main negative result:** The new expected-risk problems are mathematically transparent embeddings of classical Max-3SAT, not a new stochastic hardness mechanism. The k=n budget can overwrite every formula-bearing bit from every clean history, so every history has exactly the same pointwise optimization problem; the expectation, known iid source, and source-independent policy impose no extra optimization constraint. The architecture-specific encoding and precise source/protection interface still must be checked and are not literally supplied by the prior theorems, but the hardness is task-specific packaging of familiar ingredients. Uniform attention carries an average of clause contributions and is not the source of the difficulty; the position encoding and growing clause width allow the formula to be hard-wired. On the evidence checked, this does not appear to be a standalone top-venue novelty claim. Do not claim a new robust-prediction lower bound, computational hardness of learning, or generic Transformer verification breakthrough.

**Sharp baseline:** For every such normalized instance, assign variables sequentially by the method of conditional expectations under a uniform random assignment. Each clause of at most three distinct literals is satisfied with probability at least 7/8; tautologies contribute 1 and multiplicity is preserved. The resulting deterministic polynomial-time assignment satisfies at least 7m/8 clauses. Rewriting the n positions gives a source-independent legal policy with risk at least (7/8)R* on *every* history because S*≤m. Thus the >7/8 hardness in the 0945 construction is the familiar sharp Max-E3-SAT threshold in a new interface. This claim is about that clause-satisfaction family, not all predictors. The 0954 constant additive gap is obtained by replacing 1/(MP) by 1/(2P); this loses uniform vanishing error. Normalizing the 0945 risk by M is equivalent to asking for original-risk accuracy O(1/M), not a constant additive gap at the TV scale.

**DP:** The fixed-feature recurrence is a direct finite-state product with multidimensional resource-sum dynamic programming and minimum-cost dominance, with useful task-specific charging of precision, protected endpoints, exact readout and masks. Fixed-bank flow accounting gives a narrower polynomial bound. Neither is a new generic automata, knapsack, or attention algorithm. Growing feature dimension creates the standard product-of-ranges blowup; the Max-SAT construction illustrates an instance with growing *value channels* and clause width, not a lower bound for fixed-dimensional attention or one standard Transformer block.

## Evidence and exact comparison

1. [Håstad, Some Optimal Inapproximability Results](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/max3satl.pdf), Theorem 6.5 (PDF p. 37), states the satisfiable versus at-most-7/8+epsilon satisfiable E3-CNF promise. This is the precise source of the gap, including perfect completeness. The local algebra and normalization are separate reductions. The theorem is directly applicable once the per-instance architecture is shown to reproduce the clause objective; it does not itself discuss attention, TV, or iid histories.

2. [Sälzer and Lange, Reachability Is NP-Complete Even for the Simplest Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.13179), §§3–4, especially Theorems 2–5: NP-hard neural reachability even with one hidden ReLU layer, one output and simple specifications; they encode 3SAT and also restrict weights. This is a strong direct antecedent against generic claims about small-depth neural verification. Their inspected theorem concerns real-input reachability, not the exact padded discrete edit/expected-TV/source interface or this approximation gap; do not assert literal theorem subsumption without a mapping.

3. [Ataei, Conic Certification of Transformer Attention](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00585v1#S3), Theorems 5–6 and Remark 3. Theorem 5 establishes NP-hardness of maximizing a linear head output with two box-varying tokens including the query and constant values; Theorem 6 gives 1/200 output-range additive inapproximability at specified constant score widths. Remark 3 gives a polynomial epsilon-approximation for one head with independently listed token candidates by enumerating query candidates and solving separable fractional optimization. The hardness is continuous-box single-head attention; the positive result does not itself handle a global Hamming budget or post-head multilayer objective. Neither theorem is an automatic subsumption of discrete fixed-source expected TV. Together they are a strong warning against both broad novelty and broad intractability claims. Preprint dated October 2026; inspect the version carefully before citation.

4. [Shi et al., Robustness Verification for Transformers](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.06622), ICLR 2020, formulation and §§3–5: certifies lower bounds for continuous embedding perturbations, including bounded numbers of perturbed positions; its practical word-substitution enumeration is discussed in §5. It is an algorithmic verification antecedent, not an NP-hardness theorem for the present architecture or expected prediction TV. [Zhang et al., categorical adversarial assessment](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BmJV7kyAmg), ICLR 2022, §2 Eq. 1 and §3.3, studies expected adversarial risk and categorical edits and describes the inner search as NP-hard; its Theorem 1 is an information-theoretic lower bound and its later guarantees concern a greedy method. It does not, in the theorem passages checked, state the local exact source/architecture or Håstad value gap. Cite as task context rather than asserting theorem-level subsumption.

5. [Diochnos, Mahloujifar and Mahmoody, Adversarial Risk and Robustness](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/file/3483e5ec0489e5c394b028ec4e81f3e1-Paper.pdf), §1–2: standard expected adversarial risk and Hamming perturbations under uniform product distributions predate this work. [Gourdeau et al., On the Hardness of Robust Classification](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/file/8133415ea4647b6345849fb38311cf32-Paper.pdf), Definitions 1–2 and Theorems 5, 9–11: learning impossibility and sample/computational reductions under distribution-free and uniform/log-Lipschitz laws. These are not the present supplied-predictor numerical-TV value theorem. [Mahloujifar and Mahmoody, Can Adversarially Robust Learning Leverage Computational Hardness?](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v98/mahloujifar19a/mahloujifar19a.pdf), Theorems 7 and 9, gives polynomial-time product-distribution tampering/evasion under explicit initial-event-probability, oracle and perturbation-size assumptions. It does not contradict a narrowly targeted Max-SAT optimal-value gap: finding an error when one already has constant error is different from achieving 7/8+epsilon of a numerical maximum under this particular hard budget. Check formal theorem and constants before making any direct comparison.

6. [Dutta and Rajasree, Efficient reductions and algorithms for variants of Subset Sum](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.11020), Appendix B.1–B.2, presents the standard Bellman recurrence and the k-dimensional product table O(n∏(t_i+1)). This directly anticipates the range-product feature bank; adding a finite automaton state and keeping least Hamming cost is a standard layered shortest-path/resource DP composition, though this paper does not spell out the present protected pair/readout. It is a cleaner literal antecedent than asserting a theorem in a weighted-automata paper not inspected in full. [Mohri, Edit-Distance of Weighted Automata](https://research.google/pubs/edit-distance-of-weighted-automata-general-definitions-and-algorithms/) supplies relevant primary metadata; its bearing theorem was not available from the inspected landing page, so no exact subsumption is asserted.

## Scope, gaps, and recommendation

Read theorem statements/definitions or stated sections as listed, not complete proofs of all papers. Search is not comprehensive; no result found by this rapid pass is a certificate of priority, nor does nonmatch prove novelty. A further search should include approximation hardness of robust *expected losses* for discrete classifiers and weighted automata with counters, and independent checking of the exact dyadic model as a realization of a named Transformer architecture. If retained, put the result in a limitations/complexity appendix: “Even for a public iid source, evaluating worst-case attack risk of a supplied growing-width predictor can encode Max-3SAT; a fixed number of bounded-range features admits a standard pseudo-polynomial finite-state DP.” Report the 7/8 matching baseline and uniform vanishing-error versus absolute-gap tradeoff adjacent to the theorem. The research-facing core remains the source-class adaptive robust-prediction theorem subject to the separate central prior-art assessment, not this computational embedding.
