# Source-independent adversarial learning from grouped next-token labels

Status: initial candidate, author-derived 2026-11-03, not independently reviewed; no claim of research novelty. These observations concern a meta-distribution average, not the worst source or budget. All estimates and optimization costs below are explicit; the construction is not an efficient-SGD theorem. No existing frozen files were changed.

## Decision and antecedents

The more valuable next question is learning positional protection, not another gate-readout construction. The accepted BCJ finite-bank theorem and FPA construction already pay for positional aggregation, Boolean/ReLU gates, arithmetic, precision, and a classical same-information comparator; see `../central_prior_art_20261103/INTERNAL_ANTECEDENT_RECONCILIATION.md`, `../positional_manuscript_consolidated_20261103/IMPLEMENTATION_INTEGRATION_NOTES.md`. Earlier clean-objective results show mean clean loss does not imply the required robust worst-case; see `../learning_objective_stage_20261103/RESEARCH_SUMMARY.zh.md`. This note instead asks what a legal supervised adversarial objective actually estimates. The general robust-learning literature, including Attias, Kontorovich and Mansour (JMLR 2022, https://jmlr.org/papers/v23/20-1353.html), already develops generalization bounds for adversarial learning; the proof below uses basic sufficiency and Hoeffding and should not be marketed as a new generic learning principle. Anwar et al. (2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05189) study adversarial training for in-context linear regression, and Kumano et al. (2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14042) study adversarial pretraining for ICL. Abstract-level comparison only; no comprehensive novelty audit performed.

## 1. Experiment and quantifiers

M>=1 is fixed. An episode first draws an arbitrary latent pair (theta, mu) from a fixed but otherwise unknown distribution P. Here theta is a homogeneous binary order-two transition table and mu is an arbitrary initial pair distribution, possibly dependent on theta. It then draws H=(X_0,...,X_{M+1}) from that chain and Y=X_{M+2}. Write q=theta_{X_M X_{M+1}}(1). Episodes are iid from this joint law; a fixed source and initial distribution is a special case. At evaluation, an attack map a transforms H into z=a(H), editing at most k positions in {0,...,M-1} and leaving the final pair fixed. The map receives neither theta nor Y; it may be chosen after the fixed learned predictor and the population law are specified, but takes only H as episode-specific input. Any randomized attack must be a kernel of H and any fixed training data and predictor only; its fresh randomness is conditionally independent of the current (theta, mu, Y) given H and the fixed training data. Deterministic maps suffice for the supremum. For every word h, write A_k(h) for the permitted variants and B=sum_{j=0}^{min(k,M)} binom(M,j).

A deterministic predictor f maps edited words to [0,1]. Let

 L(f) = sup_a E[(f(a(H))-q)^2].

The supremum is over one legal history-only map used for the whole population, not over a new oracle using the hidden theta or Y for each occurrence. Since the word space and A_k(h) are finite, the supremum decomposes as a maximum separately at each word h, after taking conditional expectation over latent source and Y. A mixture of maps cannot improve this linear objective. A family of fixed maps can be large; a population-optimal map may be constructed using knowledge of the population law, but does not inspect the current latent source or next label. This upper-bounds more restricted attackers.

Let p(h)=E[q|H=h]=P(Y=1|H=h), with arbitrary values at null words. Then

 L(f)=E_H max_{z in A_k(H)} (f(z)-p(H))^2 + C,
 C=E_H Var(q|H).

The second term is independent of f and the attack. Indeed E[(f(z)-q)^2|h]=(f(z)-p(h))^2+Var(q|h), and the same h-wise max is used above. Supervised square loss has the identical ranking, since E[(f(z)-Y)^2|h]=(f(z)-p(h))^2+p(h)(1-p(h)). In particular label noise and latent-source variation are distinct constants, both independent of the attack at fixed h. Nothing here says min_f sup_a equals sup_a min_f.

## 2. A legal loss can rank predictors differently from label-aware training

Take M>=1, k>=1, and an iid Bernoulli(1/5) source. Consider two predictors f1(z)=1/10 if z_0=0 and 3/10 if z_0=1, and f2(z)=2/5. Both edits of z_0 are legal and do not change the protected pair. For f1 either action has supervised mean square loss 17/100, because 4/5*(1/10)^2+1/5*(9/10)^2=17/100 and 4/5*(3/10)^2+1/5*(7/10)^2=17/100. For f2, it is 1/5. Thus legal expected attack risk ranks f1 ahead. Corresponding true-source squared errors are 1/100 and 1/25, respectively.

If instead the training procedure separately chooses the edit *after seeing Y*, f1 has loss (4/5)*(3/10)^2+(1/5)*(9/10)^2=117/500, whereas f2 remains 1/5, so the ranking reverses. This label-aware threat is stronger and may be a meaningful conservative design, but is not the history-only evaluation game. This is an explicit example, not an assertion that ordinary adversarial training always chooses badly. An empirical max over a shared history-to-edit map can still memorize random labels when exact words are mostly unique; finite-sample guarantees for it need to account for the size of that attacker class.

## 3. Polynomial number of clean predictive groups

For clean h, define T(h) as its initial pair, terminal pair, and the eight triple transition counts N_abc(h), a,b,c in {0,1}. There are at most S=16(M+1)^4 possible values (usually much fewer), by the explicit flow reconstruction below. For fixed (theta,mu), the likelihood of h is

 mu(h_0,h_1) product_{a,b,c} theta_ab(c) ^ N_abc(h).

Hence any two words with the same T have exactly the same likelihood for every (theta,mu), including degenerate transition probabilities using the usual 0^0=1 convention. Bayes' rule yields the same posterior on (theta,mu), and the same p(h)=E[theta_terminal(1)|H=h], within each positive-probability group. This permits arbitrary latent correlations between theta and the initial law; it does not assume a uniform prior or a Markov process after averaging over theta. For the sharper count, write (a,b,c,d,e,f,g,h)=(N000,N001,N010,N011,N100,N101,N110,N111). For pair u define delta_u=1{terminal=u}-1{initial=u}. Flow balance gives e-b=delta_00, b+f-c-d=delta_01, d-g=delta_11, while the fourth balance is redundant. Thus, once initial/terminal pairs and a,b,c,d are specified, e=b+delta_00, f=delta_01-b+c+d, g=d-delta_11, and h=M-(a+b+c+d+e+f+g). Each of the four free counts belongs to {0,...,M}; infeasible choices only make the upper bound looser. This saves a factor O(M^4) compared with treating all counts as independent.

The eight counters are for the clean, pre-attack history. The robust predictor still receives only the edited history and protected endpoint; the learner's estimation of conditional loss is a separate offline cost.

## 4. Finite-class candidate theorem

Let F be any specified finite set of [0,1]-valued prediction maps, with cardinality N, fixed independently of the training samples. It can include finite-precision Transformer maps and a classical gate; no result below says gradient descent finds the minimizer or even that any chosen network contains that gate. Draw n1>=1 independent labeled clean episodes and estimate each p_t from the mean of Y over episodes whose T=t; use zero when the group is unobserved. Draw n2>=1 further independent clean episodes, and define

 Vhat(f) = (1/n2) sum_i max_{z in A_k(H_i)} ( f(z)-phat_{T(H_i)} )^2.

Choose any fhat in F whose Vhat is at most xi>=0 above its minimum (xi=0 is exact ERM). For delta1,delta2 in (0,1), with probability at least 1-delta1-delta2 over both independent samples,

 L(fhat) <= min_{f in F} L(f) + 4 sqrt(2S/(n1+1))/delta1
            + 2 sqrt(log(2N/delta2)/(2n2)) + xi.       (1)

The bound holds for every fixed meta-distribution P of the type above and the same algorithm; confidence is over its training draw, not simultaneous worst-case control of fhat over every source in P's support. It uses k in offline attack enumeration; no simultaneous unknown-budget guarantee is claimed.

Proof. Let pi_t=P(T=t), pt=P(Y=1|T=t), Ct the first-sample count, and A=sum_t pi_t |phat_t-pt|. Conditional on Ct>0, the group labels are iid Bernoulli(pt), so E|phat_t-pt| <= 1/(2 sqrt(Ct)); when Ct=0 the error is <=1. For Ct~Bin(n1,pi_t), E[1/(Ct+1)] <= 1/((n1+1)pi_t), and 1/(Ct or 1) <=2/(Ct+1). Cauchy-Schwarz gives E|phat_t-pt| <=sqrt(2/((n1+1)pi_t)). Summing and using sum sqrt(pi_t)<=sqrt(S) yields E A <=sqrt(2S/(n1+1)); Markov gives A at most this expression divided by delta1.

For any f and h, the max of (f(z)-phat_t)^2 differs from the max of (f(z)-pt)^2 by at most 2|phat_t-pt|. Therefore, conditional on the first sample, their population means differ by at most 2A uniformly over f, with no union bound over attack maps. The bounded second-sample functions lie in [0,1]; Hoeffding and a union bound over N predictors give uniform sampling error sqrt(log(2N/delta2)/(2n2)). The empirical minimizing inequality contributes twice these deviations, plus xi. Add the common C from Section 1. QED.

This Markov-inequality form has poor delta dependence and is deliberately conservative; no optimality is claimed. The number of trajectories is polynomial in M for fixed target accuracy and confidence if log N is polynomial, but S is very large, and a bound may be vacuous. Importantly this result counts independently drawn full episodes, not tokens from one trajectory, and does not prove a polynomial dependence on 1/beta or a uniformly optimal statistical rate. A sharper occupancy analysis may improve confidence dependence without changing the scientific scope.

For this comparator transfer only, assume M>=64 and that for P-almost every latent source theta there is a stationary pair law Pi and certificate beta>=1/M for its pair-chain transition kernel P_theta, satisfying P_theta^2(u,v)>=beta Pi(v) for every pair u,v, including unsupported states. The episode may still start from the arbitrary initial law mu, which need not be stationary. Let f0 be exactly the protected-endpoint half-gated comparator of `../approximate_gate_statistics_20261103/CANDIDATE.md`, with exact clean/edited-word statistics and exact output, translated from probability of its selected symbol d_u to probability of 1. The candidate file has a historical unreviewed header, but `../approximate_gate_statistics_20261103/ROOT_ACCEPTANCE.json` records later separate source-bound review and root replay of its exact candidate hash; this is internal review, not external peer review. Its e=xi=0 bound gives sup_a E|f0(a(H))-q| <= r_M(k) = min(1,131072/sqrt(M)+6433056 k/M), for each source satisfying those full assumptions and thus the mixture. Since square error <= absolute error on [0,1], L(f0)<=r_M(k). If f0 belongs to F, (1) and Cauchy-Schwarz imply sup_a E|fhat(a(H))-q| <= sqrt(r_M(k)+estimation/optimization terms). This transferred TV result loses a square root and can be vacuous. The very large inherited constants make finite-M illustrations especially uninformative. Output approximation, finite precision and resource costs must be composed separately from the comparator construction; no new fixed-width architecture is implied.

## 5. Accounting and open points

Computing group keys for both samples takes O((n1+n2)M) symbol operations. A dense table of S group counts and positive-label sums uses O(S log(n1+1)) bits; sparse dictionaries may use less but require indexing overhead. The probability estimates are exact rationals with O(log(n1+1)) bits (or must incur separately analyzed rounding).

In a unit-cost bounded-predictor/evaluation and exact-arithmetic-oracle model only, exhaustive offline evaluation takes up to O(N n2 B) predictor calls, O(N n2 B) loss comparisons, and O(N n2 B M) naive word-construction symbol operations; this is not a bit-complexity claim. Predictor descriptions, indexing F, evaluating their architecture, its weights and activations, and all rational operations are additional. A generator can stream B edits for each history; B is polynomial only for fixed k and can be exponential for k proportional to M. Second-sample histories cost O(n2(M+2)) bits if stored for repeated traversal; alternatively an external sample store or exactly reproducible replay is required, plus its storage and I/O, while a single pass over histories can maintain N accumulators at an additional cost. Explicitly indexing all F may itself be exponentially costly.

As an example of a possible exact accounting, suppose each predictor oracle returns a rational in [0,1] with numerator and positive denominator representable in at most b bits and supplies an exact evaluation, and group probabilities are exact count ratios. Each loss comparison then uses rationals of O(b+log(n1+1)) bits; a naive exact accumulated sum over n2 examples can have numerator and denominator bit lengths O(n2(b+log(n1+1)+1)) before simplification. Multiplication, division, comparison, integer-representation, and actual predictor-evaluation costs must be charged as functions of those lengths; no unit-cost arithmetic or rounding-free implementation is implied. Finite-precision approximations to these steps need a separate error analysis and can enlarge xi. ERM xi is an assumed achieved optimization tolerance, not an SGD convergence result. A source-specific trained predictor from many episodes may store information about the fixed source; this differs from the earlier one-context predictor and must be compared against a classical learner using the same meta-training data and compute.

The remaining central questions are (i) can one obtain non-vacuous instance-dependent rates or sharp TV rates without estimating all count groups, (ii) can a bounded-width/precision Transformer learn positional features with a feasible algorithm, (iii) can the same model adapt to every unknown k and all eligible sources, and (iv) can robust training beat a same-information classical baseline rather than just represent it? Neither this candidate nor existing representation results answers these. Validate the labels-only sufficiency and game distinction independently before use.
