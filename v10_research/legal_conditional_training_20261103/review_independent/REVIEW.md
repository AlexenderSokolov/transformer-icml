## Quantifiers, sufficiency and legal game

The attack is a single possibly randomized history-only kernel, with fresh randomness conditionally independent of the current latent source, initial law and label, given H and fixed training information. Its population-optimal action may use the known population law and fixed predictor but not the current latent realization or future label. Because words and legal edit sets are finite, conditional expectation precedes the per-history maximum and a deterministic active action suffices. Conditioning on the independently drawn training data makes the same reasoning valid for a trained predictor. This is not an interchange of min and sup, nor a hidden-source or label-aware attacker.

For any deterministic edited-word prediction r, conditional square loss decomposes as E[(r−q)²|H=h]=(r−p(h))²+Var(q|H=h). The supervised analogue replaces the additive conditional variance by p(h)(1−p(h)). Both additive terms are independent of the chosen edit and predictor, so the rankings agree under this legal game. They differ if the choice of edit is allowed to see Y.

For arbitrary possibly theta-dependent initial law, a word's conditional likelihood is its initial-pair mass times the product of its eight triple-transition factors. A fixed T containing initial pair, terminal pair and all triple counts therefore gives identical likelihood at every (theta,mu), including zeros with 0^0=1. On positive-mass groups the posterior, hence the terminal predictive mean, is identical. Null words/groups can be assigned arbitrary values and omitted from divisions by pi_t. No averaged process need itself be Markov.

The four-count flow compression is correct. For b_u=1{initial=u}−1{terminal=u}, given start/end and free counts n000,n001,n010,n011, one reconstructs n100=n001−b00, n101=n010+n011−n001−b01, n110=n011+b11, and n111=M−sum(other seven). Thus at most 16(M+1)^4 groups; infeasible tuples only reduce this count. This uses three independent pair-state balance constraints and the fixed total M. Exhaustive independent enumeration for M=1 through 11 confirms the reconstruction and injective keys, with group counts 8, 16, 32, 60, 106, 176, 280, 426, 626, 892, 1238. Finite enumeration supports but does not replace the algebraic proof.

## Exact counterexample and independent diagnostic

For Bernoulli(1/5), first-coordinate editable, f1's two possible values 1/10 and 3/10 each give legal supervised square loss 17/100, below f2=2/5 with loss 1/5. When the edit sees Y, f1's loss becomes 117/500, above f2. Their true-source squared risks are respectively 1/100 and 1/25. An exact independent Fraction calculation reproduces these values.

A separate exact M=5, k=2 check uses a three-source mixture with source-dependent initial laws, including transition probabilities zero and one. Across all 128 words and 106 positive-mass groups, with 20 colliding groups, it verifies exact conditional predictive sufficiency, equality of the legal direct source-risk maximum with the conditional-mean expression plus latent variance, the analogous supervised expression, and the larger label-aware maximum. It also verifies the binomial reciprocal-moment identity on 20 exact cases. These finite tests do not establish the general theorem; they exercise the edge cases independently of the author's implementation.

## Finite-class bound and comparator

Conditioning the first sample on each occupancy count m>0 gives a Bernoulli sample-mean error at most 1/(2 sqrt(m)); the unobserved-group error is at most one. For positive pi, E[1/(C+1)]=(1−(1−pi)^(n1+1))/((n1+1)pi), and the stated reciprocal and Cauchy–Schwarz bounds yield E A≤sqrt(2S/(n1+1)). Markov supplies A≤sqrt(2S/(n1+1))/delta1 with probability at least 1−delta1. The clean-label calibration affects each bounded squared loss by at most 2A uniformly over all predictors and legal edits. Conditional Hoeffding/union bound over the fixed data-independent finite class contributes epsilon=sqrt(log(2N/delta2)/(2n2)); approximate ERM then contributes 4A+2epsilon+xi. The common source variance cancels in comparisons. This establishes the displayed bound as a conservative distribution-specific high-probability statement, not a simultaneous guarantee over sources in the mixture or over unknown budgets.

For the optional comparator transfer, the revised note now correctly specifies M≥64, arbitrary start law, and a stationary pair law satisfying the all-state P_theta²≥beta Pi certificate with beta≥1/M for almost every latent source. At exact count and output precision, the source-bound ACG candidate gives the stated 131072/sqrt(M)+6433056 k/M bound; its hash matches ROOT_ACCEPTANCE.json, which records internal separate review and replay, not external peer review. Averaging the per-source bound over the latent mixture is valid; squared error≤absolute error and Cauchy–Schwarz yield the stated square-root loss in the transfer. Inclusion of that comparator in F is an additional explicit assumption, not a learned-attention or resource claim.

The final precision-only revision resolves the remaining formal suggestions: it states k is a nonnegative integer and N, n1, n2 are positive integers, and conditions the sample-mean inequality explicitly on C_t=m for each m≥1. A previous revision separately distinguished ACG's exact output tolerance from the learning algorithm's possibly positive ERM tolerance. The correction diff matches the previous audited candidate exactly and makes no substantive change to the theorem.

## Compute, precision and prior art

The revised accounting explicitly charges O((n1+n2)M) group computation, dense-group and sample storage, O(N n2 B) predictor calls and comparisons, and naive word construction. It correctly labels these as oracle/symbol-operation counts rather than a general bit-complexity bound. The rational-output example accounts for bit growth; exact construction of arbitrary F, predictor computation, finite-width/precision, rounding and approximate-ERM guarantees remain separate assumptions/costs. B is O(M^k) only for fixed k and can be 2^M. No claim follows that SGD solves the ERM or learns positional features.

The generic sufficiency and adversarial generalization ingredients have clear antecedents; do not market them as novel. Primary-source checks support the descriptions of [Attias, Kontorovich and Mansour](https://jmlr.org/papers/v23/20-1353.html), the [2024 Anwar et al. arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05189v1), and [Kumano et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14042). The [2002 Fortini et al. paper](https://didattica.unibocconi.it/mypage/upload/49041_20150620_094039_2002MARKOV_RIV.PDF) confirms the corrected third author is Petris. The [Diaconis–Freedman Stanford report](https://statistics.stanford.edu/technical-reports/partial-exchangeability-and-sufficiency) is correctly attributed. This is a scoped abstract/metadata check, not a theorem-by-theorem literature review or proof that the particular game distinction is novel.
