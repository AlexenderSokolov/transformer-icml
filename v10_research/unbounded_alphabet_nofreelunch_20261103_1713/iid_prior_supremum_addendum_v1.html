Stronger classical iid baseline

3 November 2026. This is a separate supplement to the unbounded-alphabet reduction; it does not modify that file. It gives a simpler and stronger obstruction to unrestricted distribution-free TV prediction, while the paired-sign construction remains useful for its quantitative A³ dependence and genuinely second-order, fast-mixing witness.

1. Existing source

Han, Jiao and Weissman, “Minimax Estimation of Discrete Distributions under ℓ₁ Loss,” arXiv v3, Theorem 2 and the discussion on page 2, give finite-sample minimax ℓ₁ distribution-estimation lower bounds, including inconsistency when n=o(S). TV is half ℓ₁. All transition rows equal to p give stationary iid samples and a homogeneous order-at-most-two process whose actual-terminal next-observation law is always p; hence this is already an applicable no-free-lunch baseline, not a distinct independent-query reduction. Such a source is generally not strictly second order.

2. Explicit supremum-one reduction

For each fixed sample size n≥1, let the observation space be ℕ and allow all finite-support iid distributions. For any randomized n-sample predictor Q, its worst-source expected TV risk is exactly 1 as a supremum. Consequently the minimax risk is 1. This is not a claim that the supremum is attained or that one fixed source is hard for all n.

Proof. Pick deterministic integers n<K<L and a uniformly random K-subset S of {1,…,L}; let pS be uniform on S. The sampling law and Q's random seed are independent conditional on S. Given the observed sample and the set D of d≤n distinct observed labels, the unobserved support U=S∖D is a uniformly random (K−d)-subset of {1,…,L}∖D. This remains true after conditioning on the estimator's independent seed. Therefore

E[Q(U) | sample, seed] = (K−d)/(L−d) · Q({1,…,L}∖D) ≤ K/(L−n),

whereas pS(U)=1−d/K≥1−n/K. By the event form of TV, TV(pS,Q)≥pS(U)−Q(U). Averaging yields Bayes risk at least 1−n/K−K/(L−n). For any fixed n take K/n arbitrarily large, then L/K arbitrarily large. Thus every predictor has supremum risk at least 1; TV≤1 gives equality. This remains true if the predictor is told n,K,L, since S remains unknown. All choices here are analytical, not experiments.

If desired, insist instead that each iid source have full support on ℕ: replace pS by (1−ε)pS+εr for one fixed known full-support distribution r on ℕ. A coupling changes the n-sample law by at most nε, and the target TV by at most ε; bounded loss and the triangle inequality change risk by at most (n+1)ε. Let ε↓0. The resulting iid sources are also order-at-most-two, but the previous note's strict-order-two and uniform stationary pair-mixing example is a different construction.

3. Pointwise is not uniform

Berend and Kontorovich, “On the Convergence of the Empirical Distribution,” Theorem 4 and Lemma 9, analyze distribution-dependent ℓ₁ convergence for countable iid distributions. In particular, the empirical distribution is TV-consistent for every fixed countable p. This is compatible with supremum risk 1 for each n: the difficult p can vary with n. Their result on arbitrarily slow decay of their bound is not here asserted as an all-estimator pointwise-rate impossibility. More generally, finite-state stationary Markov predictors converge per fixed source by applying an ergodic theorem to the finitely many recurrent contexts, even when the source class has no uniform rate.

For higher-order models, Han, Jana and Wu, Theorems 5 and 22, establish same-trajectory stationary prediction minimax KL rates, including A³ for order two in the stated alphabet/sample regime. Their KL lower is not a TV lower by reversing Pinsker. The finite-alphabet ℓ₁ source and the simple reduction above are directly TV-relevant. Search coverage is not exhaustive; no originality claim is made.
