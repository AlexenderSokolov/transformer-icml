Why a uniform prediction problem needs complexity control

3 November 2026. This note reduces a previously analyzed finite-alphabet family to a nonconvergence statement. It is a statistical result, not a Transformer architecture, compute, training, or precision lower bound. No numerical tests or formal proof verification were performed.

1. The quantifiers matter

Observe X₁,…,XN from an unknown homogeneous order-two source and output any possibly randomized probability measure QN based on that same history. The target is its actual conditional next-observation law given XN−1, XN. Risk is expected total variation (TV), with the terminal state and estimator randomness included. One estimator must work for all sources in the class; randomness is independent of the source. Let RN be infimum over estimators followed by supremum over sources.

The prior finite-alphabet construction and proof, independently reviewed here, gives for every A≥2 and N≥2

RN(A) ≥ (1/288) min{1, √(A³/N)}.

This remains true for stationary pair-chain initialization, positive probability of every alphabet symbol after every context, uniform stationary distribution on the A² contexts, and forward and reverse two-step Doeblin constant at least 1/4. Taking AN=max{2,⌈N1/3⌉}, the minimum is 1, so the union over all finite alphabets has RN≥1/288 for every N≥2. Even an estimator told AN cannot improve this lower bound. The worst source and its alphabet may change with N. Thus this does not exhibit one fixed source with nonvanishing error, and does not conflict with pointwise consistency for each fixed finite alphabet. The former is an inf–sup statement, not a lower bound for each source. A stationary all-source add-one upper and the older arbitrary-initial upper are stated with their different scopes in the linked construction.

2. What “real-valued” does and does not imply

Merely identifying the symbols with distinct real numbers places these examples in a common real observation space, provided arbitrary discrete conditional probability laws and probability-measure outputs are allowed. That law is full support on its finite active alphabet, not on ℝ. If an output is restricted to a Lebesgue density while the target is atomic, their TV is 1; this mismatch is a separate trivial obstruction, not the finite-alphabet argument. Conversely, an assumption of continuous observations, a common dominating law, or strictly positive real densities requires a separate reduction.

3. Reduction to positive real densities

For each A, fix a known partition ℝ=I₁∪⋯∪IA into A disjoint intervals, each with positive standard Gaussian mass, for instance I₁=(−∞,1), I₂=[1,2), …, IA=[A−1,∞). Let gc be the standard Gaussian density conditioned on Ic, zero off Ic, and write bA(x)=c when x∈Ic. Partition boundary values may be defined arbitrarily. These emissions and the partition are public and do not depend on the unknown source parameter σ.

Take the finite A-source θσ from the linked proof, and conditionally on its label path draw Yi independently with density gXᵢ. The real process has conditional density

fσ(y | y−1,y₀)=∑c=1A θσ(c | bA(y−1),bA(y₀)) gc(y).

Since θσ≥(1−η)/A and gc is positive in its interval, f is positive Lebesgue-almost everywhere on ℝ. Thus the real law has full topological support and is a stationary homogeneous order-two process; for this paired-sign family η>0 and at least one predecessor-dependent row exists, so it is not merely presented as order two while actually being first order. Its one-coordinate stationary density is μA=A−1∑gc, and the stationary pair density is μA⊗μA. Since each legal label transition is ≥(1−η)/A, its forward pair kernel satisfies, for every starting pair, P²≥(1−η)² μA⊗μA≥¼ μA⊗μA. The same follows for the reversal by the uniform pair stationary law and the identical lower bound on the reverse label transitions. The constant is uniform over A and σ, though the minorizing measure μA changes with A.

Now let Q be any real-valued predictive probability measure, with no density or parametric restriction. Push it forward to the A labels, bA#Q. TV cannot increase under a measurable map, and the pushforward of the true terminal conditional real law is θσ(· | XN−1,XN). Moreover a label-history estimator can simulate all the Yᵢ from the known gXᵢ, run the alleged real-history estimator, then push forward its output. This simulated experiment has exactly the law of the real one for every σ, including the actual terminal query. Hence any real estimator with risk below 1/288 on the induced class would contradict the finite-alphabet lower bound. The bound holds even if the learner knows A, the partition and all emissions, and can use arbitrary randomized computation or output any probability measure.

This construction uses piecewise transition densities whose boundaries and dominating stationary density vary with A. It does not establish the same statement under a single fixed dominating reference measure, a uniform density ratio to that measure, a fixed smoothness/Lipschitz bound, a fixed finite-dimensional transition parameterization, or other real-space structural restrictions. Its TV statement is not automatically a KL statement.

4. Prior art and scope

Han, Jana and Wu, Theorems 5 and 22 obtain stationary finite-alphabet higher-order same-trajectory prediction minimax rates for KL loss, including the A³ complexity for order two in their stated sample regime. Their KL lower bound alone cannot be reversed into this TV lower through Pinsker. Cohen, Kontorovich and Wolfer discuss the absence of distribution-free TV-risk rates without tail restrictions for infinite-support iid distribution learning, a different but related no-free-lunch setting. This reduction uses standard finite-alphabet information bounds, simulation and TV data processing; no novelty or exhaustive literature claim is made.
