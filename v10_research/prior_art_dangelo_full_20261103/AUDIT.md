## Actual model and quantifiers

Paper §§2–3 and proof §§3.2.1–3.2.3: finite token set V, sequence x_1:T, k fixed; one-hot symbols; causal softmax with additive relative positional bias RPE (one scalar per distance and head); no MLP; concatenation rather than additive residual, attention values equal preceding layer's entire representation. A single full content-score matrix W_A per head stands for bilinear Q/K (no explicit head dimension/precision account). First layer k heads, second one; hidden dimension d_1=(k+1)|V| and d_2=2(k+1)|V|; RPE table length T per head. Direct final linear projection extracts the normalized attention-weighted one-hot average as the probability vector. No output softmax is applied in the construction (although §2 calls the linear map logits), and no LayerNorm/RMSNorm is analyzed. Do not silently add an output softmax or cite this as a finite-realized standard decoder with normalization.

The empirical study samples one latent transition table for each trajectory from independent symmetric Dirichlet or a hierarchical suffix Dirichlet prior; initial k tokens uniformly distributed, not assumed stationary. Last-position log-loss training and KL evaluation; k=2, |V|=5 in experiments. The construction's estimator identity is stated for every input sequence; it is pathwise and does not require the Dirichlet prior or stationarity. It implicitly needs a valid history (without BOS, T≥k+1) or an independently specified fallback: otherwise its no-BOS denominator is zero. The result is representational, not an estimator-risk theorem uniform over all kernels and initial laws.

## Exact statements and proof checks

1. [Definition 3.1 and Proposition 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02800v1#S3.SS1): for valid candidate s, M_s={r: x_{T-r+1}=x_{s-r}}; N_M(m) counts successors with exact match mask M. For any real β_r and BOS score κ, claimed output is

    P(m) = [exp(κ)/|V| + Σ_{M⊆[k]} exp(Σ_{r∈M} β_r) N_M(m)] / [exp(κ)+Σ_M exp(Σ_{r∈M} β_r) N_M],

with BOS terms absent without BOS. Thus non-contiguous as well as contiguous partial matches are explicitly included. Relative β may be negative, but positive-mixture interpretation using γ=e^β−1 below requires γ≥0; algebraic polynomial identity itself works for any finite β, γ>−1. This is a score-family parametrized by k scalars, not arbitrary 2^k independent mask weights.

2. [Constructive proof](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02800v1#S3.SS2): the kth first-layer lag-copier achieves exactly one-hot attention only in δ^(1)→∞. To exclude early keys lacking full context, δ_1^(2)→∞ is also used, although at a fixed terminal position exact masks could be considered a different granted primitive. Remaining valid-key scores sum lag-specific equality indicators; BOS embedding uniform across V contributes fixed κ and uniform values. The selected block is already a probability vector, and the decoder is the identity projection onto it. Consequently the advertised finite β means finite **matching scores**, not that all local routing and masking logits are finite. Paper does not provide a bounded-score, finite-word/precision, rounding-aware, end-to-end implementation or a bound on its error/cost.

The arXiv paper's proof, in its displayed denominator and next displayed aggregate [PDF p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02800v1#page=7), writes I{e^(2)_{T1}>0}; immediately preceding it defines e^(2)_{T1}=κ for BOS, with κ arbitrary real. Read literally that indicator incorrectly drops the BOS when κ≤0. It therefore does not correctly encode the BOS-presence test for all allowed κ. The preceding piecewise softmax calculation supports the intended BOS-presence indicator, not this printed sign condition. This is a localized discrepancy of exposition/algebra, not by itself a refutation of the claimed construction. Document rather than silently correct.

3. [Corollary 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02800v1#S4.SS1), proof Appendix D.1: symmetric α>0, β_r=β and κ=kβ+log(α|V|); in β→∞, (N_full(m)+α)/(N_full+α|V|). At finite β, BOS adds uniform pseudo-counts to **soft weighted** counts, rather than being exactly the full-context add-α estimator. Nonuniform Dirichlet prior is introduced in §2, but this stated BOS result specifically realizes symmetric α using the uniform BOS value; do not enlarge it without a construction.

4. [Definition 4.1, Lemma 4.1, Corollary 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02800v1#S4.SS2), proofs D.2: K_M(m)=Σ_{S⊇M} N_S(m) counts all matches containing M. With no BOS and equal β, let γ=e^β−1; then P(m) = Σ_M γ^|M| K_M(m) / Σ_M γ^|M| K_M. The binomial/product expansion is exact, including γ=0 via 0^0=1; normalized order and mask coefficients are valid convex weights for β≥0 (where zero-count components require conventions). They discuss context-dependent smoothing, low-count backoff, and when exact matches are absent. Their order-i model averages all size-i masks, unlike ordinary contiguous suffix-only interpolation. The risk benefit in a rare context is discussed/experimentally illustrated; no asserted nonasymptotic frequentist risk bound follows just from this identity.

5. [Lemma 4.2 and Appendix D.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02800v1#A4.SS3): approximate formula for β matching the expectation of a data-dependent pseudo-count to α; proof also motivates via first-order Taylor approximation to ratios and uses approximate expectation counts assuming uniformly distributed and independent contexts. Appendix explicitly warns concentration and omitted second-order error are needed and independence is coarse for higher orders. Footnote 2 changes the candidate range to s∈[k′,T−1], excluding the query, whereas Definition 3.1 uses [k′,T]; for k=1 without BOS, this shifts the exact empty-mask candidate count from T−k to T−k−1. Thus the β heuristic should not be transplanted as the exact expectation for the original closed range without reconciling the switch. This is not an exact optimizer, finite-N risk bound, theorem controlling dependence, or a guaranteed optimal scaling. Avoid calling it a nonasymptotic optimal-β theorem.

5a. [Lemma D.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02800v1#A4.SS3) gives the first-order Taylor approximation E[X/(c+Y)] ≈ E[X]/(c+E[Y]); its displayed proof cancels the linear term. The lemma supplies no remainder bound or guarantee that the denominator is bounded away from zero. It is not an exact equality or a concentration/risk theorem.

6. [Theorem E.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02800v1#A5.SS3): label-symmetric sequence distribution and parameter initialization invariant under all permutations; population gradient flow preserves permutation-fixed parameters and predictor equivariance. Proof uses invariance of population loss and gradient equivariance, then tangency of the gradient-flow vector field. It is not convergence to a desired model nor a finite-step SGD/optimization rate. [Lemma E.1 and Corollary E.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02800v1#A5.SS4): invariant token comparison blocks are aI+b11ᵀ, with additive score shifts canceled by softmax. With layer-1 copying preserved throughout and prescribed successor-token value/readout, generalized score compares every pair of query/key lags and yields mask M⊆[k]×[k] with e^{sum β_ab} weights. Again constrained flow, not arbitrary trained Transformer and not a training-success theorem.

7. [§5 and Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02800v1#S5): three model families include analytical/frozen-copy construction, trainable disentangled model and a standard 2-layer d_model=64 model with learned embeddings, residual, LayerNorm and MLP. Adam 10^6 steps, lr 10^-3, batch 32; stated observations of near-Bayes and recovered mechanisms are empirical under selected priors/tasks, not proof for standard architecture or uniform recovery. [Appendix A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02800v1#A1.SS1) explicitly limits theoretical guarantees to the two-layer disentangled and synthetic Markov setting. Comparisons with risk and rare-context must distinguish prior-averaged last-token cross-entropy/empirical KL from v10's unconditional terminal-query expected TV.

## Claim-to-claim derivations and nonimplications

### A: source-matched finite soft aggregate versus recovery

For exactly one equality test over candidate contexts s, no BOS, with n legal candidate positions, q_s(m)=Σ_j [1+γ I{c_j=s}]1{y_j=m}/[n+γ n_s] = [r(m)+γ u_s p̂_s(m)]/[1+γu_s], where r is overall successor average, u_s=n_s/n and γ=e^β−1. On u_s>0 and γ≠0, p̂_s=q_s+(q_s−r)/(γu_s). u_s=0 or β=0 precludes this inversion. The mixture is the paper's k=1 formula, and the rearrangement is elementary algebra; it is not independently eligible as a new mechanism. Paper does **not** state that one real causal network simultaneously exports q_s,r,u_s and the reciprocal with controlled precision/risk, nor that such a circuit is impossible. Even if q_s and r are accessible as two heads, u_s still needs its query-matched count extracted from permissible history-side values and retained; charge the complete channel.

Crucial qualification: an indicator for an entire two-symbol state is NOT the paper's native k=2 aligned score Σ_r β_r I{coordinate r matches}: its one-coordinate matches have positive weights intermediate between full and zero match. The claim that the A expression is a special case is valid algebraically for a pair-token representation: encode z_i=(x_i,x_{i+1}) as one symbol in an enlarged alphabet K², set paper k=1, then project value z_{j} to its appropriate continuation coordinate, with correct causal alignment and legal window. But this introduces cost and indexing obligations for forming z_i in real causal networks; ideal supplied composite tokens are not free. Neither the algebraic equivalence nor a limit construction establishes finite-cost A in the original input model.

### B: product and Walsh spectrum

For binary a,c∈{−1,+1}, βac=2β I{a=c}−β; thus β1ac+β2bd has identical attention weights to the paper's k=2 aligned equality model with its coefficients (2β1,2β2); the additive −β1−β2 cancels. Under a uniform distribution over the four contexts, row-normalized kernel factorizes, with Walsh eigenvalues 1, tanh β1, tanh β2, tanh β1 tanh β2. This eigenvalue statement follows by a short independent algebraic computation; paper contains the product overlap weighting but we did not find it explicitly stated as this Walsh spectral formula. The product attenuation itself is a direct kernel consequence, not sufficient for a novelty claim; crucially the uniform-population kernel need not describe finite-history random denominators, biased occupancy, or all surviving information in residuals and other heads. Theorem E.1/Corollary E.1 expand the comparison beyond aligned matches.

### Risk, resources, and source comparison

The research scope already forbids treating either identity or product eigenvalue as novelty and correctly keeps causal provenance, joint trajectory fluctuations, zero counts, all available ports, resource comparison and rare-context random terminal query as unresolved candidate obligations. Our audit supports those cautions. The paper does not establish the targeted general finite-N Markov-dependent terminal TV risk upper/lower, fast-mixing rare-context finite-channel lower bound, complete N/L/width/head/bit frontier, query-specific inversion stability, or architecture-wide lower bound. This is a paper-specific finding, not a literature-wide novelty or impossibility claim; other references need audit. Nor does their β heuristic solve the v10 finite-random-history problem.

Internal current consolidated positional manuscript `v10_research/positional_manuscript_consolidated_20261103/main.tex`, abstract, §§2–3 and related-work paragraphs (lines 42–54, 77, 147–190, 280–303), studies a different binary order-two single trajectory experiment: arbitrary-start upper vs stationary lower, expected terminal unsquared TV, all-state two-step minorization certificate 1/M, strict source edits, exact triple counts and boundary windows, budget-blind map, finite masked-attention/Boolean-ReLU and twelve-coordinate rounded bank; it expressly limits lower bounds to the stated observation and disclaims network-wide precision laws or learned performance. D’Angelo's propositions do not state this edit-adaptation/restricted-observation theorem. The manuscripts' computations should not be equated: the positional manuscript uses structural singleton masks and an explicitly accounted bank; D’Angelo uses global context RPE and ideal limits; neither by itself subsumes the other. Its related-work attention paragraph cites other Markov constructions but currently omits this directly relevant D’Angelo work; recommend adding a narrowly scoped citation distinguishing the two without changing scientific claims. This is editorial advice, not a performed manuscript edit.

Prior internal audit `v10_research/prior_art/comparison.md` §D already records Proposition 3.1, the limiting copy/early mask, no finite precision, and Appendix E's distinction from convergence; this report extends it with the exact pair-token condition, sign indicator discrepancy, theorem-by-theorem scope, and training/approximation details.
