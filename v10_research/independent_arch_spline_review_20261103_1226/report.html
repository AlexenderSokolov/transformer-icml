Counterexample: exact zero-count output is not a finite-spline property

Take N=3, word 001, K=1, and exact ports. The terminal pair is 01, while the only historical pair is 00, so J=0, C=0, C_all=1, q=r=1, h=0, and the add-one target is 1/2. For K=1, S_1(x)=x, so P_1 is identically zero. The finite decoder has Aclip=0, z=1/3, H_1(0)=1, and outputs clip((3/2) P_1(1/3,1),0,1)=0. Its error is 1/2, within (2), whose uncapped value here is 99/32. This does not refute (2): section 2's rational expression is exactly 1/2, whereas section 4 approximates it. Recommend stating that distinction where the zero-count output is discussed.

Alignment, masks, and information channels

Candidate position i=3,...,N supplies key (X_{i−2},X_{i−1})=S_{i−1} and value X_i, corresponding precisely to the counts indexed by S_2,...,S_{N−1}. The query at N is (X_{N−1},X_N)=S_N. Singleton backward-copy masks are causal; first two positions can use self-only masks because block 2 excludes them. No X_{N+1} or BOS is used. One-hot Boolean indicators are exactly ReLU(b1+b2−1), with complements as affine functions of bits.

Head A has weights 2 on matching keys and 1 on others, hence output (C_all+C)/(m+J); head B uniformly averages successor values and each of four one-hot keys, giving r=C_all/m and h_a=J_a/m. The selected h is exactly Σ_a ReLU(h_a+Q_a−1), since Q is one-hot and every h_a∈[0,1]. Thus (1+h)q−r=C/m, including J=0. The separate retained q,r,h and query channels are essential: no isolated-head lower bound follows. Value dimensions may be padded to a common head size if required by an implementation.

Seven-block dependencies and width

  1. Two singleton-copy heads, followed by Boolean key/query features.
  2. Finite match head A and uniform head B in parallel; then select h by tokenwise ReLU.
  3. Compute S_K((h+q)/2), S_K(h/2), S_K(q/2), and H_K(h) in parallel; combine the square splines into A0.
  4. Compute lower=ReLU(A0).
  5. Compute Aclip=lower−ReLU(lower−h), then z=(m Aclip+1)/(m+2).
  6. In parallel evaluate the three S_K terms defining P_K(z,H_K(h)) and combine them.
  7. Apply clip(p,0,1)=ReLU(p)−ReLU(p−1), producing (1−p,p).

Each spline is an affine term plus K−1 hinges. All affine combinations, retained residual coordinates, and fixed-dimensional selectors can be implemented by constant additional hidden units and state coordinates. Thus O(K) hidden width and O(K) breakpoint/coefficient storage suffice, with exact-real weights. Block 7's two ReLUs are parallel; the variable-upper-endpoint clamp genuinely needs the two sequential stages in blocks 4–5. Zero-value attention in later blocks is consistent with the ledger, assuming an architecture that permits it and has no compulsory LayerNorm or output softmax. The block-2 query and required residual data must actually be retained.

Constants and error accounting

Linear interpolation on mesh 1/K has error at most sup|f″|/(8K²). Therefore the square error is at most 1/(4K²), the three-term P_K error is at most 3/(2K²), and H″(h)=(m²/2)(1+mh/2)−3 implies the H_K error at most m²/(16K²). Because H_K linearly interpolates endpoint values in [0,1], it also lies in [0,1]. Aclip∈[0,h′] ensures z∈[0,1].

Writing h′q′−hq=h(q′−q)+(h′−h)q′ gives A0 error at most (1+h)e_q+e_r+e_h+3/(2K²). Projection to [0,h′] introduces at most another e_h relative to A∈[0,h]. The fraction's changed denominator introduces one further e_h using (mA+1)/(mh+2)∈[0,1]. These yield (2)'s 3e_h coefficient and denominator 2+m(h−e_h)_+. The second line follows from the product error and |z|≤1. Final projection cannot increase error relative to B∈[0,1].

On the stated high-count event, h≥u/2 and e≤u/4 imply m/[2+m(h−e)_+]≤4/u. Since (1+h)e+e+3e≤6e, the first line of (2) is at most 24e/u+6/(uK²). The stated e and K choices split the three terms of (6), except ζ, into at most ε/3 each. Conditioning on the actual terminal pair is not assumed independent in this deterministic comparison: every bound holds pathwise. No assertion is made here about the imported reversed-chain certificate or the probabilistic estimate (3).

Numeric and computational boundaries

The TV perturbation bound follows from likelihood ratios in [e−2a,e2a], and total variation controls differences in expectations of [0,1]-valued coordinates. Its use assumes separate port/gate errors are included in e. The b_score formula addresses only an already-established absolute logit error ≤2−b_score; it does not establish that a b-bit arithmetic system achieves that error, or account for coefficient representation, copy thresholding, accumulation, exp, attention normalization, all hidden stages, or final rounding. Ideal log 2 and coefficients are not finite-bit implementations.

Under the allowed explicit masks a sparse executor uses O(N) attended edges, O(NK) pointwise FFN work, and O(N) token-state storage, plus O(K) coefficients and the encoding of masks/position logic. A dense implementation may require O(N²+NK). The ordinary four-row streaming add-one comparator needs O(N) updates, O(log N) bits per fixed-number counter, and a final division. “Two extra heads” in §8 should be qualified: the construction has four total heads across two blocks, with two block-2 heads; relative to a single direct q head in the same copy-feature backbone, the corrected readout adds one uniform head, whereas a streaming counter has no attention heads. No general efficiency advantage follows.

Independent exact finite test

The accompanying check_exact.py independently enumerates all 248 words for N=3,...,7, of which 78 have J=0, and verifies the exact head identity and zero-count rational comparator. For K∈{1,2,3,4}, it checks (2) using exact rational arithmetic on all 27 combinations of perturbations −1/10, 0, +1/10 applied to q,r,h and clipped to [0,1], plus one separately included exact-port case. That is 27,776 checks including the duplicate all-zero case. No violation occurred. The maximum observed error-to-bound ratio was 2912/4987, at word 01101, K=4, shifts (−1,+1,0)/10. This finite search is not a proof for arbitrary N, K, or errors, and it does not validate the probability, prior art, or bit-complexity claims.
