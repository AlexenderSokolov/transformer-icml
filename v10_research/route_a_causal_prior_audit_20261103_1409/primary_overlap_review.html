Route A finite-causal construction: primary-source overlap

2026-11-03. Focused adversarial comparison, not an exhaustive literature search, formal verification or a claim of a fully compiled implementation. No source overwritten, RNG or external write.

Decision: No strict novelty gap is established by replacing infinite lag-copy logits with finite causal logits, nor by approximating a same-history add-one estimator with positive finite score. Rajaraman already claims finite-precision approximation of two-layer empirical k-gram; D’Angelo already gives the finite-score soft match and BOS add-one limit; a direct calculation below gives its finite-β all-word error bound. A viable gap would have to be a substantially stronger fully specified no-BOS, same-information, same-cost, finite-computation guarantee, not just a different presentation. Whether such a gap is important remains unresolved.

Exact construction comparison

Rajaraman et al. arXiv:2407.17686v1, Definition 2.1, Theorems 4.4 and 4.5, Remark 4.2, Appendix A.1 eqs. (15)–(23), Appendix B. Architecture 0.1 is causal softmax attention with relative position added to keys/values, residual and no nonlinear FFN. For binary S=2, k=2, Theorem 4.4 uses L=2, k=2 heads in the first layer and one effective matching head in the second, embedding dimension d=(k+2)S+k+1=11. Theorem 4.5 gives L=ceil(log₂(k+1))=2 and one head/layer, d≤2k(S+1)+S=14, with relative positions. Its first-layer hard-copy and second-layer empirical-count proof take κ→∞; finite logits must be approximated. Remark 4.2 asserts O(log T) bits suffice for O(1/T) additive error for the constructions, but the statement does not expose a complete rounding, exponent, accumulation and numerical stability ledger. Definition 2.1 requires J>0 and targets the empirical conditional distribution C/J, not add-one B=(C+1)/(J+2). Theorem 5.1 is a different 3-layer construction using layer normalization.

D’Angelo et al. arXiv:2607.02800v1, Proposition 3.1 eq. (8), §3.2.1, §3.2.2, Corollary 4.1 eq. (10), Appendix D.1. Their two-layer disentangled RPE construction uses k first-layer copy heads and one second-layer match head, with lag logits +δ(1) versus 0, and takes δ(1)→∞. For valid candidate j, finite-score match logit is ∑r=1kβr1{query coordinate r matches candidate r}. Early positions are suppressed via δ1(2)→∞. For binary k=2 this is already finite-β soft matching, but weights partial matches differently from the two-level full-pair q of v3; exact q follows instead by a composite-token mapping with separately paid binding. Add-one uses BOS with κ=kβ+ln(α|V|), then β→∞. BOS is a special sequence-independent uniform value and positional access in their construction; it is an additional input/resource relative to the no-BOS v3 model, not a free conversion.

Izzo, Nichani & Lee. ICLR 2026, Appendix B.3: lag-copy heads are postulated to attend fully using large positional scores; second-layer finite β compares coordinatewise contexts, with value picking the next token and both MLPs zero. They display an error bounded by a constant times T/[eβJ], then simplify to O(e−β) for a “typical” J=Θ(T), with hidden dependence on context frequency. This is a heuristic illustrative k-gram construction within their positional/infinite-precision setting, not an all-word uniformly small error when J can vanish, nor a finite-bit or true-θ conditional-risk theorem. Footnote 3 to Definition 5.4 permits arbitrary output at J=0; there is an apparent definition/appendix context-index mismatch, not resolved here.

Derived finite-score BOS baseline, including J=0

This calculation is an elementary specialization of D’Angelo, not a formula asserted to appear verbatim in the paper. Take k=2, binary values, α=1, β≥0, κ=2β+ln 2, and assume the ideal exact copies and candidate boundary set used in Proposition 3.1. BOS has weight 2e2β and value 1/2; J full matches each have weight e2β. Their normalized combined contribution is precisely B=(C+1)/(J+2). Every nonmatch has weight at most eβ; there are at most m of them and all values lie in [0,1]. Thus the finite-β output P obeys on every word:

|P−B| ≤ m e^(−β)/(J+2) ≤ (m/2)e^(−β).

Consequently β≥max{0, log(m/(2η))} suffices for |P−B|≤η for every word, including J=0, conditional on ideal copying and boundary suppression. Unlike Izzo’s typical-frequency simplification this bound includes scarce and zero contexts, though it is conservative. With finite copying and finite boundary scores, additional errors and exact token/position accounting must be proved. Compose any valid uniform pathwise implementation bound with v3’s separate true-θ conditional statistical bound; the composition itself does not prove its assumptions or a new statistical theorem.

Finite-logit bridge and its actual cost

For a lag-copy head with one eligible target, causal softmax scores δ on that target and 0 on at most T−1 alternatives, total off-target mass is (i−1)/(eδ+i−1)≤(T−1)e−δ. For values in [0,1], the absolute copy error is at most that mass; δ≥log((T−1)/p) is sufficient for error p. There is no eligible target for i≤h; those positions need explicit boundary treatment, not fabricated lag symbols. This elementary calculation is a bridge, not by itself a fully audited standard-causal implementation.

For the D’Angelo baseline, approximate query/key copies perturb subsequent β-scaled match scores, so δ depends on β and target accuracy as well as length. For example, with probability-vector copies within TV p and exact current symbols, an inner-product match changes by at most 2p per compared pair, giving a crude ≤4βp score change for k=2 valid candidates. An attention-distribution perturbation bound for sup score error a is ≤(e2a−1)/2 in TV. Finite suppression of early positions, distinguished BOS handling, score/embedding scaling, separate output and normalization errors, and all per-head accumulation and exponential precision must also be charged. These inequalities indicate a route to a finite compilation; they do not assert an end-to-end theorem, exact existing-paper costs, or that ordinary fixed sinusoidal positions simulate the required lookup/boundary logic.

v3’s explicit original model has two singleton-mask copy heads, two second-block heads, then five FFN-only blocks, total L=7 and O(K) ReLU hinge width, and its numerical denominator/reciprocal approximation is charged separately. Singleton and candidate masks are more powerful access than a standard dense-causal mask; compiling them with relative or absolute positions changes the assumption and resource ledger. Even when standard causal finite-logit compilation is proved, comparison to the BOS alternative must acknowledge BOS access and whether the final probability vector or a final output softmax is required, as well as positional-encoding table size, logits/magnitudes, weights, head/value dimensions, real arithmetic, exponentials and accumulated bit errors.

Scope and source status

Prior papers establish representation and finite-β/precision overlap, not the v3 same-trajectory terminal-conditioned frequentist θ-risk bound, and do not prove the same exact no-BOS architecture under identical resource rules. Those narrower differences are real but are not themselves evidence of a novel or nonroutine theorem. A strict separation needs a matched resource definition and a lower bound for the competing family, not a comparison against an uncorrected single head. R011 already covers unconditional actual-terminal add-one risk; rare-row conditional loss must not be conflated with it.
