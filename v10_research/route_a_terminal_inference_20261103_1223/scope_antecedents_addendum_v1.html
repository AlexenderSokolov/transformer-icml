Finite-spline boundary and antecedent clarification

2026-11-03. Additive correction to unchanged Route A v1; output projection and baseline accounting addenda also apply. This is a provisional candidate, not a novelty judgment or complete fixed-bit implementation.

Zero-count fallback: ideal decoder versus finite spline

Section 2's exact output 1/2 at J=0 describes the ideal rational reconstruction, not the implemented finite-K ReLU spline network. For a concrete boundary case take N=3, X=001, K=1, and zero port errors. The terminal row 01 has J=C=0 and the ideal comparator B=1/2. Because S1(x)=x on [0,1], P1(x,y)=0, so the implemented spline network returns 0. This is within the conservative finite-spline error bound (2); it does not contradict the risk bound. Exact fallback in the final network would require an additional paid gate/branch or another proof and is not asserted.

Probability-vector implementation

After all effects already bounded by ζ, apply the exact or otherwise range-preserving saturation described in the projection addendum. Emit one scalar p in [0,1] and define the other output coordinate as the exact complement 1−p, or implement a separate normalization with its error and cost explicitly bounded. Two independently rounded coordinates cannot simply be assumed to sum to one. The ideal (1−p,p) representation already has exact normalization.

Internal statistical antecedents

R011, R011_global_rates_and_constant_adaptation_v1.md, already proves an unconditional expected actual-terminal TV upper bound for the full-history add-one estimator, including the β=0 class and arbitrary initial laws; its proof hash aaafd3823ab11e20ffdeff2164115c6c91db5efea6e2dffd9c3c78da0b7fbbbf matches its local acceptance receipt. Thus unconditional add-one consistency is not new here. Its loss is averaged over the actual terminal state; that does not by itself give the row-00 conditional bound at the rate written here.

SM001, v10_research/strong_mixing_precision/SM001_strong_mixing_half_log_precision_v1.md, already conditions a stationary path on the terminal state and uses the reversed chain to study reciprocal occupation. Its §3 assumes P²(s,t)≥βπ(t) for all states. The rare-family audit establishes a two-step common atom and a four-step π-minorization but warns that a uniform two-step π certificate cannot be substituted. The proof method of reverse conditioning is therefore an existing internal antecedent; the different certificate and risk scope are not by themselves a novelty certificate. SM001's inspected source marks it author candidate and independent review pending; this note makes no stronger acceptance claim for it.

Exact finite-softmax mask aggregation and add-alpha limiting baselines from D’Angelo and the paid positional-bank work noted in v1 remain essential. The numerical spline, rare-row conditioned risk and their conjunction require further independent literature and internal subsumption checks before any originality claim.
