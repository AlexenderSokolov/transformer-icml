ROUTE A: ACTUAL-TERMINAL FINITE-PREFIX RISK AND A PAID SOFT ATTENTION DECODER

1. Setup and exact target
Let N≥3, m=N−2, X_1:N be a stationary binary homogeneous order-2 path and S_i=(X_{i−1},X_i). For every pair s let J_s=∑_{i=2}^{N−1}1{S_i=s}, C_s=∑_{i=2}^{N−1}1{S_i=s}X_{i+1}, and C_all=∑_{i=2}^{N−1}X_{i+1}. The terminal query is s=S_N, drawn from and correlated with this very path; X_{N+1} is not input. θ_s=P(X_{i+1}=1|S_i=s). For binary distributions TV is absolute difference of probabilities. The comparator, on exactly the same X_1:N, is B_s=(C_s+1)/(J_s+2), defined even for J_s=0.

For the rare fast-mixing family use 0<u<1/4, w=(1−u)/3, t∈[1/4,3/4], π=(u,w,w,w), θ=(t,1/2,1−ut/w,1/2) in 00,01,10,11 order. Set v=mu. The network parameters below depend on N and a chosen spline resolution K≥1, not on t, θ, the realized query or u. A lower bound u≥u0 may be used to set a uniform numerical budget; no u is fed to the network. An expected conditional risk for S_N=00 is meaningful because u>0. For v<60 the only assertion here is risk≤1.

2. Exact finite attention computation, including zero counts
Two attention blocks have the following allowed explicit causal structural masks. With d_k=4, take matching-head W_Q=2 log(2) P_query and W_K=P_key, so the scaled score equals log(2) times the one-hot inner product. Its W_V selects the successor bit. The uniform head takes W_Q=W_K=0 and W_V selecting the successor bit and all four one-hot historical key coordinates. The two singleton-copy heads take W_Q=W_K=0, W_V selecting the raw bit, and separate output projections to the two lag coordinates. In block 1, at position i≥3, two singleton heads copy X_{i−1}, X_{i−2}; at i=1,2 use a harmless self-only mask. The shared tokenwise ReLU feature map forms (i) one-hot historical key K_i=e_(X_{i−2},X_{i−1}) and historical successor value Y_i=X_i, and (ii) one-hot query Q_i=e_(X_{i−1},X_i). Both are retained in distinct coordinates even at i=N. For binary inputs a 2-bit pattern indicator is ReLU(b1+b2−1), where b_j is the chosen bit or its complement. Thus the history keys and terminal query have different alignment and no future token enters any value.

In block 2 the two terminal heads have exactly the mask {3,…,N}; for all other queries they can be self-only. Head A has score log(2)·<Q_N,K_i>, value Y_i; its matching score is log 2, its nonmatching score zero, and all logits are finite. Head B has zero scores and value (Y_i,K_i), dimension five. Thus their terminal outputs are
  q=(C_all+C_s)/(m+J_s),   r=C_all/m,   (h_00,h_01,h_10,h_11)=(J_00,…,J_11)/m.
The tokenwise decoder selects h=h_s using ∑_a ReLU(h_a+Q_N,a−1), exact for one-hot query. Every mask and value above is explicit and contains no BOS token. Head outputs and residual channels are not treated as hidden: q,r,all four h_a, Q_N and any residual input are visible. This is an upper construction; it asserts no loss of information in the whole network.

On every word, including J_s=0,
 A=(1+h)q−r=C_s/m,  and  B_s=[mA+1]/[mh+2].
When J_s=0, A=0 and the ideal rational decoder gives 1/2. The finite-K ReLU spline need not: for N=3, word 001, K=1 and exact ports it outputs 0 although the comparator is 1/2; equation (2) allows the error. This is a pathwise finite-softmax construction; it does not require the current query to appear in the past. General γ=e^β−1>0 has A=((1+γh)q−r)/γ. These algebraic relations are known mechanisms and are not a new contribution by themselves.

3. Perturbation with all three actual ports
For general γ>0, suppose q',r',h'∈[0,1] have errors at most e_q,e_r,e_h relative to q,r,h. Define A'=((1+γh')q'−r')/γ and project the decoded scalar [mA'+1]/[mh'+2] onto [0,1]. Then the difference from B_s is at most
  m/[2+m(h−e_h)_+] · [(1/γ+h)e_q+e_r/γ+2e_h].                 (1)
Proof: |A'−A|≤(1/γ+h)e_q+e_r/γ+e_h by writing h'q'−hq=h(q'−q)+(h'−h)q'. The true numerator mA+1 is between zero and its denominator mh+2. A common-denominator expansion bounds the fraction difference by m(|A'−A|+|h'−h|)/(mh'+2). Lower-bound mh'+2 by 2+m(h−e_h)_+; projection cannot enlarge distance to B_s∈[0,1]. This bound is pointwise: arbitrary dependence of port errors, the path and the terminal query is permitted. It does not say that a single head's attenuation is a full-network lower bound.

4. An explicit finite ReLU approximation to the readout
Fix γ=1. For K≥1 and x∈[0,1], let S_K(x) be the linear interpolant of x² on the grid {0,1/K,…,1}. The exact error is ≤1/(4K²); S_K is a continuous piecewise-linear function represented by K−1 ReLU hinges plus an affine term. Define
 P_K(x,y)=2[S_K((x+y)/2)−S_K(x/2)−S_K(y/2)].
For x,y∈[0,1], |P_K(x,y)−xy|≤3/(2K²). Let H_K(h') be the linear interpolant on the same grid of H(h')=(1+mh'/2)^{−1}. Since sup |H''|≤m²/2, |H_K−H|≤m²/(16K²). H_K remains in [0,1]. Both interpolants use O(K) hinges.

For observed clipped q',r',h', compute A0=q'−r'+P_K(h',q'), Aclip=clip(A0,0,h'), z=(m Aclip+1)/(m+2), and output clip((m+2)P_K(z,H_K(h'))/2,0,1). The clamp is ReLU(x)−ReLU(x−h') and is applied after ReLU(x), so it has two visible piecewise-linear stages. Since 0≤Aclip≤h'≤1, z,H_K(h')∈[0,1]. By projection stability under change of the upper endpoint,
 |Aclip−C_s/m|≤(1+h)e_q+e_r+2e_h+3/(2K²).
A further denominator comparison and product interpolation give the pathwise bound
 |network−B_s| ≤ m/[2+m(h−e_h)_+]·[(1+h)e_q+e_r+3e_h+3/(2K²)]
                    + (m+2)/(2K²)·[3/2+m²/16].                    (2)
The first line applies to every word and every h, including h=0. The second line is the explicit decoder spline error; it is deliberately conservative. If the pre-saturation numerical output differs from the ideal clipped scalar by at most ζ, exact final saturation onto [0,1] adds no error beyond ζ. If the saturation instead has range [0,1] but differs from exact saturation by at most ξ uniformly, the additive error is ζ+ξ. Mere range preservation does not imply such accuracy. The other probability coordinate is the exact complement, or a separately analyzed simplex normalization must be supplied.

One realization uses block 1 for copying and Boolean features, block 2 for both heads and h selection, then blocks 3–7 as FFN-only blocks (zero attention values) for parallel square/reciprocal splines, lower clamp, upper clamp/z, product spline, and final [0,1] clamp. The ideal output is the probability vector (1−p,p), not an assumed output softmax. A backend with a final numerical error must additionally execute the explicitly costed post-error saturation and preserve the exact complement, or analyze separate saturation/normalization errors. The added saturation is one scalar clip operation or one further ReLU block if counted as a neural layer. No LayerNorm or unpriced external division is used. Exact real coefficients and nonlinear softmax are the ideal arithmetic model; their digit-level implementation is a distinct obligation.

5. Terminal-conditioned statistical statement
For the rare family, v=mu≥60 and any δ∈(0,1), the same fixed predictor above satisfies
 E[|B_00−t| | S_N=00] ≤ min{1, δ+1220/v+√[log(2(m+1)/(uδ))/v]+1/(v/2+2)}. (3)
The next lemma proves this directly and does not treat S_N as independent.

First, condition on S_N=00. The m preceding states evolve according to the stationary reversed chain P*, whose two-step rows all put at least 1/8 on 11 (the supplied rare-family v3 report verifies the matrix). Let ρ=7/8. A common-atom coupling yields TV distance from stationarity at lag j at most ρ^floor(j/2), and ∑_{j≥1}ρ^floor(j/2)=15. Therefore |E[J_00|S_N=00]−v|≤15 and E[J_00|S_N=00]≤v+15.

For reversed indicators I_i,I_j at i<j, their conditional covariance is at most 2ρ^floor((j−i)/2) E I_i. This follows by subtracting the two lag-(j−i) state distributions and applying the triangle inequality about π. Summing covariances and variances yields Var(J_00|S_N=00)≤61(v+15). As v≥60, E[J]−v/2≥v/4, so Chebyshev gives
 P(J_00<v/2 | S_N=00) ≤ 61(v+15)/(v/4)² ≤ 1220/v.              (4)
The bound may exceed one and is then trivially capped.

Second, Z=C_00−tJ_00 is a sum of predictable row indicators times Bernoulli martingale differences. Conditional Hoeffding's lemma gives E exp(λZ−λ²J_00/8)≤1 for each fixed λ. For any integer j≥1 choose λ separately on the event J_00=j. A union bound over j=1,…,m and both signs yields
 P(|Z|>√[(J_00/2) log(2(m+1)/(uδ))]) ≤ uδ.                    (5)
At J_00=0, Z=0. Divide (5) by P(S_N=00)=u to condition; no martingale property after conditioning is invoked. On the simultaneous good events J≥v/2 and (5)'s complement,
 |B_00−t|=|Z+1−2t|/(J+2) ≤ √[log(2(m+1)/(uδ))/v]+1/(v/2+2).
On either bad event, TV≤1. This proves (3). The logarithm is a consequence of the conservative conditioning/union bound, not asserted optimal. For overall stationary risk, combine the other rows separately; a rare-row conditional result is not itself an average-risk result.

6. Combined statistical/computational risk with finite port errors
Assume e_q,e_r,e_h≤e≤u/4 on every path, and that §4's FFNs are otherwise exact real computations except pre-saturation final scalar error ≤ζ, followed by exact final saturation and exact complement. On J_00≥v/2, h≥u/2 and h−e≥u/4. From (2), the full-network excess over the same-path B_00 is at most
  24e/u + 6/(uK²) + (m+2)[3/2+m²/16]/(2K²) + ζ.               (6)
Therefore the conditional actual-terminal risk of the network is bounded by the sum of the right side of (3) *before its min with 1* and (6), capped at 1. This is a sufficient upper bound for this explicit R-model network. The low-count event is already paid in (4); no empty denominator or query-independence assumption is hidden.

One finite-accuracy interpretation of e: if every valid softmax logit differs from its ideal by ≤a, each value coordinate differs by ≤v_err, and the implemented attention probability vector differs in TV by ≤s_err from the exact softmax of its perturbed logits, the head coordinate error is at most v_err+s_err+min{1,(exp(2a)−1)/2}. Indeed each likelihood ratio is in [exp(−2a),exp(2a)], and values are clipped to [0,1]. Require the numerical gate and the q,r,h readout errors also within this declared e; this assumption is NOT yet derived from an end-to-end floating-point implementation. Singleton copied bits can be decoded robustly with a clipped piecewise-linear threshold when their errors are <1/4. To make (6), excluding ζ, at most ε∈(0,1], it is sufficient to impose e≤u0 ε/72 and
 K²≥max{18/(u0 ε), 3(m+2)[3/2+m²/16]/(2ε)}
for a public u0≤u. The first condition also implies e≤u/4. Under approximate rather than exact final saturation, add its separately bounded ξ to the final risk; independent coordinate rounding must not be silently treated as exact normalization. A score rounding error bound a≤½log(1+2e/3), value error ≤e/3 and softmax probability-TV error ≤e/3 suffice for each head output, prior to separate gate errors. If score rounding has a known absolute error bound 2^(−b_score), b_score≥ceil(log2(1/a)) is sufficient for that component only. This is not a complete bit-complexity theorem for weights, exp, hidden state, all FFNs or accumulators.

7. Comparison with the directly read soft head
For fixed u and t=1/2, the stationary unconditional mean successor is 2w. Ergodicity gives, along a long path, q→(2w+u/2)/(1+u) while θ_00=1/2. Conditioning on S_N=00 preserves this convergence in probability because that event has constant probability u>0; explicitly P(A_N|S_N=00)≤P(A_N)/u. Thus the direct q head has limiting conditional absolute bias
  (1−4u)/[6(1+u)] > 0.                                      (7)
The same-prefix add-one comparator has risk tending to zero for fixed u by (3), e.g. δ=1/m. The paid q,r,h decoder can track it as numerical budgets shrink. This proves a difference between the direct q output and the augmented corrected computation in this specified example. It is NOT an impossibility for an unrestricted Transformer: the extra head, residuals or other channels may retain the needed information. At t=2w the direct population bias vanishes; no universal fixed nonzero bias is claimed.

8. Explicit resource ledger and limits
Two singleton-copy heads in block 1, two heads in block 2, then five zero-value-attention FFN blocks: L=7 total; at most H=2 heads per attention block, four-dimensional one-hot query/key and up to five value coordinates. A constant number of model-state coordinates suffices; the largest pointwise ReLU hidden width is O(K) for spline hinges, with O(K) stored coefficients/breakpoints; additional parameters describe the finite masks and public position logic. Singleton masks and exclusion of first two candidate positions are extra model-R access, NOT standard dense-causal masking. Arbitrary dense-score head equivalence is unproved. A sparse executor touches O(N) attention edges plus O(NK) tokenwise FFN arithmetic and O(N) token-state storage, excluding any training/backpropagation retention of O(NK) intermediate FFN activations; a dense implementation can cost O(N²+NK). Fixed model weights depend on m,K and include the ideal score log 2; their own bit lengths and exp/softmax arithmetic remain uncharged as complete bit costs. Input has N bits; positions/candidate masks and any table representation must be accounted for in the chosen encoding. Comparator: an ordinary same-history streaming add-one counter tracks all four row/successor counts; on seeing X_N it performs the last transition update, forms S_N, and then reads the appropriate row. It requires O(N) updates, O(log N) bits per fixed-number counter plus a paid final division. Compared with a direct-q network sharing the same two-copy-head block, this construction adds one uniform head plus selection and O(K)-width arithmetic; in total it uses four head instances across the two attention blocks. Against a streaming counter it uses four head instances where the counter uses none. It establishes no efficiency improvement, necessary depth or N–L lower bound.
