The proposed result is valid under the stated stationary model, with θ interpreted as the probability of the next bit being 1, γ > 0, δ ∈ (0, 1), and the estimates clipped to [0, 1].

For the reverse chain, write a = ut/w. In state order 00, 01, 10, 11, its transition rows are

- (1 − t, 0, t, 0)
- (a, 0, 1 − a, 0)
- (0, 1/2, 0, 1/2)
- (0, 1/2, 0, 1/2).

The two-step Dobrushin coefficient is at most 3/4, so the claimed 7/8 contraction is conservative. Consequently, the sum of the claimed mixing bounds over positive lags is 15, giving the stated conditional mean bound.

The conditional variance can in fact be bounded by 31(v + 15): each covariance is bounded using the reverse-chain contraction, and the positive-lag bounds sum to 15. Thus 61(v + 15) is safe. Chebyshev then gives the stated 1220/v bound when v ≥ 60; the sharper variance estimate would give 620/v.

For the martingale step, apply the exponential supermartingale at the kth visit to 00 and union-bound over possible positive visit counts. The unconditional failure probability is at most uδ. Dividing by P(S_N = 00) = u gives the conditional δ bound, without requiring the query to be independent of the path. The J = 0 case has Z = 0.

The decoder inequality also follows. Expand the numerator difference using the true h and the clipped estimated q. The product perturbation contributes e_h, while the denominator perturbation contributes at most another e_h because the true add-one prediction lies in [0, 1]. Projection onto [0, 1] cannot increase error, and the estimated denominator is at least 2 + m(h − e_h)₊.
