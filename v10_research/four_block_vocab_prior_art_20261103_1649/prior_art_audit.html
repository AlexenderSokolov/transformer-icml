Prior-art audit: fixed-parameter Markov prediction with vocabulary softmax

Search and comparison, 3 November 2026. This is a targeted primary-source audit, not a comprehensive novelty determination or a formal peer review. No tests or research programs were run and no source file was overwritten.

Finding. Conditional k-gram computation, positional copying, BOS smoothing, attention-based mixtures, Markov universal prediction, and length-generalization analyses all have substantial prior art. In the primary results examined, we did not verify a theorem with the full combination of one finite fixed parameter set for every length; causal full-history inputs with paid unbounded absolute positions; an ordinary vocabulary-softmax output; and a uniform O(N−1/2) expected TV guarantee at the actual terminal context over every binary order-two kernel and arbitrary initial law. This is a bounded search result, not evidence of novelty. Some papers are close enough that their constructions, variants and version histories deserve further checking before any priority claim.

1. Comparison target and evidence status

The comparison is against the specific four-block candidate v1 (SHA-256 bf7f6c3ab19472d83658a3dc5cf24b90514df96e145b578706504526045c14c8) and its rate addendum (SHA-256 d21062d3d1bf660a955bba10df9c466cf0eabaa61e57251236b8a870e15f9ad7). The candidate uses fixed matrices, two BOS tokens, width 128, four blocks, full causal masks, and paid positional inputs (1,i,i²,ln i). Its query-specific logit magnitudes grow even though the matrices are fixed. The standard linear-logit vocabulary softmax follows the final Gaussian/log-position heads. The stated all-word representation error is O(N−1/2); the source-uniform statistical conclusion additionally relies on the separately inherited R011 theorem, not solely on the architecture argument. The input and masks grow with N, and the model is not trained by the construction.

The specialized four-block integer backend is identified by SHA-256 e532e78809c4edf7d4e47ec39ad548c06ad339c55e9f56b8d75a85ea86a7143b; its independent analytic review is SHA-256 3edee0b42c69903da41faa7f2434dcca9568595726fabb95d72d3548abf0245a. The review filename is not treated as a trusted timestamp. The backend is an analytically specified, specialized growing-precision integer model; it was not run or certified on standard floating-point hardware. Earlier provenance and corrections to the three-block backend remain separate records and must not be obscured. Hashes identify documents; they do not replace accessible linked proof sources.

3. What is and is not matched

  • Same problem component, known: the empirical query-context row and induction-head circuits are explicit in Rajaraman/Ekbote; D’Angelo directly relates BOS and smoothed count formulas. A claim that these components first appear here would be incorrect.
  • Same rate in a different statistical quantity: Basu et al. state O(|S|/n) expected convergence in their own trained-estimator test cross-entropy setting. Its quantity, initial-law assumptions and architecture quantifiers must not be converted to the target rate without a separate proof.
  • Same order for the full bundle: no examined theorem was verified to have all target quantifiers and the paid integer backend. This is not an impossibility theorem or proof of priority; cited empirical-ratio constructions and classical universal coding may imply close variants after nontrivial work.
  • Backend: charging ln-position generation, integer score magnitude, max-shift, truncation, normalization, final softmax, gate margins, and length-growing precision is a separate model/implementation contribution to assess against numerical-analysis literature; no exhaustive arithmetic literature search was completed here. No benchmark or IEEE-float result follows.

4. Search limitations and next checks

Searches combined fixed weights, all lengths, Markov/online universal prediction, endpoint risk, conditional k-grams, positional encodings, vocabulary softmax and precision; the audit read the cited theorem statements and relevant construction passages, not every version, appendix or citing paper. Search coverage is incomplete, and a source's silence on the target bundle in the inspected passages does not establish its negation. Prior-art review should next trace papers citing Ekbote/Rajaraman, check whether their large-temperature or relative-position schemes admit one length-independent fixed-weight specialization with growing positions, compare classical minimax online Markov prediction under matching last-time TV rather than cumulative regret, and independently verify the inherited R011 theorem with actual source materials. The separately reviewed candidate and backend remain mathematical research artifacts, not formal certification.
