Prior-art audit: fixed parameters, positions and Markov prediction

3 November 2026. Targeted primary-source audit, not exhaustive novelty clearance. No random or numerical experiment was performed.

Reading guide. Two-layer one-head conditional k-gram constructions, positional copying, and softmax count-ratios already have substantial direct antecedents. The precise question here is narrower: one finite set of source- and horizon-independent weights, a specified causal graph, possibly unbounded absolute positional coordinates, finite attention scores at each position, and next-token error tending to zero as the same Markov trajectory grows. The sources inspected do not immediately certify this whole conjunction; this is not evidence that it is new or that any proposed new construction works.

Primary paper and theoremWhat is shown, and model/length/temperature qualificationsRelation to the fixed-parameter question
Ekbote et al., What One Cannot, Two Can, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv v2, §4, Appendix B and Remark 2Very close direct precedent: two-layer one-head-per-layer representation of conditional k-grams using relative position, ReLU, layer normalization and skip paths. Appendix B selects the previous k positions by relative key biases i log 3+κ for the k desired offsets and 0 otherwise, then takes κ→∞. The final match softmax also takes a score scale to infinity. Their discussion distinguishes a finite number of early contaminated positions whose contribution vanishes as T grows under irreducibility, but this is after the displayed ideal attention limits. Appendix I lists positional parameters of size proportional to T; Remark 2 states Ω(log T+k) bits per parameter suffice for O(1/T) additive approximation. The limited training analysis uses additional assumptions and a staged/simplified model.The architecture and empirical k-gram target are prior, not structurally new. The cited limits and length-indexed position table do not by themselves show that one finite fixed-parameter, generated-absolute-position model with no length-specific retuning has vanishing error. No claim of such an impossibility follows either. Positive matching counts and early-position treatment must be compared explicitly.
D’Angelo et al., Induction Heads Interpolate N-Grams, arXiv v1, Proposition 3.1, equation (8), §§3.2 and 4Two-layer disentangled transformer, k copy heads then one match head, relative position encodings. Its mask-count output is an explicit finite-β soft interpolation with BOS pseudo-count. First-layer exact copying is shown by sending the lag RPE score δ¹→∞; early-position suppression is another ideal limit. With fixed finite matching β, nonmatching masks retain positive weight; hard exact n-gram and BOS add-α arise in a separate match-temperature limit. Lemma 4.2 discusses length-dependent calibration under a prior-average pseudo-count criterion.Strong direct antecedent for count ratios and partial matching, not automatically one finite model whose interpolation bias vanishes with N. Do not call interpolation, BOS smoothing or fixed scalar-port logarithmic scaling new. Fixed β has source-dependent population bias in general.
Rajaraman et al., Transformers on Markov Data: Constant Depth Suffices, arXiv v1, Theorems 4.4–4.5, Remark 4.2 and §6Attention-only conditional k-gram representation with two layers/k heads or O(log k) layers/one head and relative positions; higher-depth construction uses nonlinearities. The proof describes concentrated position and context matching through large/limiting attention scales. Remark 4.2 asserts O(log T)-bit approximation up to O(1/T); §6.1 states lower bounds under explicit pattern restrictions, while Theorem 6.1 concerns one layer, ternary symbols, conditional 3-gram and O(1/T) worst-case accuracy.Strong prior representation and precision claims. It is not automatically a same-fixed-weights, all-length finite-softmax result with (1,i,i²), nor a generic binary-order-two average-risk lower. Treat the precision remark as its stated claim, not as a verified floating-point end-to-end backend.
Izzo, Nichani and Lee, Quantitative Bounds for Length Generalization in Transformers, arXiv v1, §3.3, Theorem 5.2 and Appendix B.3The limit-transformer model uses specific τ-local translation-invariant positional-logit rules and an index-dependent log i scaling, rather than generic bounded absolute vectors. Theorem 5.2 is a worst-case finite-length simulation result between fixed two-layer models in that class at infinite precision. Appendix B.3 sketches a k-gram construction: ideal local-copy behavior and match scale β≈log(1/ε), with typical-sequence error O(e^−β); the paper calls it heuristic. Theorem 4.2 average-case result is for an iid categorical/Dirichlet setup; the paper names broader Markov-data average-case extension as open.Very close prior on index-growing positional scores and length-generalizing k-gram mechanisms. Fixed β at fixed target ε gives a fixed error allowance, not a displayed proof that one fixed model has error→0 with N. Theorem 5.2 compares functions inside its class and does not imply actual-terminal Markov TV consistency for an arbitrary candidate.
He et al., Two Stones Hit One Bird: Bilevel Positional Encoding for Better Length Extrapolation, ICML 2024, Appendix A.1The appendix uses absolute position i and quadratic features to construct q=(1,i²,i), k_j=(−j²,−1,2j), so q·k_j=−(i−j)²; it invokes an attention-concentration lemma for copying a designated position. The coordinates grow unbounded as i grows. The paper studies position schemes and automata/length extrapolation, not the Markov conditional-TV setting.Quadratic position-based localization is an established mechanism. A finite score gap alone is not exact hardmax; whether a complete implementation has errors tending to zero across an unbounded history requires its own scale and normalization proof. This source does not directly establish the complete next-token consistency conjunction.
Makkuva et al., Attention with Markov, arXiv version, Theorem 1For each fixed binary first-order chain, there exist network parameters that attain the global population cross-entropy optimum and reproduce that chain’s transition law.The order of quantifiers is ∀ chain ∃ source-specific parameters, not one source-independent model estimating an unknown kernel from arbitrarily long same-history prompts. This is not the sought uniform in-context estimator.
Ding et al., Optimality and NP-Hardness of Transformers in Learning Markovian Dynamical Functions, NeurIPS 2025, Theorems 3.3–3.5Their theoretical loss-landscape analysis concerns a one-layer linear self-attention model, supervised in-context initial-state/transition pairs and population objectives; the paper also studies longer chain representations and parameter-reconstruction hardness under its own setup. The global-minimum formulas depend on problem/sample parameters.Useful comparison for training and Markov ICL, not a theorem for finite softmax causal attention with one fixed generated-position rule and same-path actual-terminal TV consistency.

Evaluation checklist and limitations

For any candidate, separately verify: (1) finite fixed matrices and fixed formula for positions, not a length-indexed table of learned parameters; (2) whether generated positions or logits grow and their representation/precision costs; (3) causal indexing and boundary terms; (4) absolute rather than fixed target-ε approximation as N→∞; (5) source assumptions, actual-terminal conditioning versus average or independent test queries, and the zero-count case; (6) fixed network output interpreted as probabilities rather than logits requiring an additional fixed softmax; and (7) whether proof assumes hard attention or a score limit. Fixed weights with unbounded positional inputs do not imply bounded activations, bounded scores or standard fixed-word implementation.

Searches covered Markov n-gram induction heads, position-based copying, fixed-parameter length generalization, causal attention and training dynamics. Only named primary texts/sections above were inspected. Broad prior-art research, exact paper-version reconciliation, and independent audit of any candidate are still required; absence of a discovered direct match is not a novelty conclusion.
