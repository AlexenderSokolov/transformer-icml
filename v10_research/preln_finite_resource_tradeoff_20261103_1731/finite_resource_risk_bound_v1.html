Finite-N risk bound for a budgeted pre-LN model

3 November 2026. Candidate quantitative elaboration of the fixed-source pre-LN obstruction, together with its input/quantifier and score-interface clarifications. Independent audit is required. The conservative constants below are not claimed sharp, novel, or equivalent to a hardware lower bound. No tests, enumeration or overwrites.

1. Model

There are L full-causal pre-LN blocks with Hℓ heads. Each score is q·k/√dh plus a deterministic uniformly bounded bias independent of the source and token sequence; all causal keys are present. Each Q/K/V is an affine map of that position's LayerNorm; attention output passes through fixed head-output projections and a residual. A second positive-ε LayerNorm feeds a positionwise one-hidden-layer ReLU FFN with residual. Fixed source- and token-independent position inputs may be unbounded and N-dependent; the input is their sum with a fixed token embedding and finitely many fixed special tokens. No token×position preprocessing, hidden token bypass, unbounded score bias, dropout or non-Lipschitz normalized readout is allowed. N is the number of data tokens; the number of eligible keys at the data terminal is at least N. All constants below are evaluated for the particular finite model at that N, so the derived inequality also holds for an N-dependent family when its parameters and bounds are explicitly recomputed.

2. Explicit per-layer constants

For each attention LayerNorm define Baℓ=√d||γaℓ||∞+||baℓ||₂ and Gaℓ=||γaℓ||∞/√εaℓ. Define Bf,Gf similarly for FFN LayerNorm. For head h, use spectral operator norms and Euclidean vector norms:

Q=||WQ|| Bᵃ+||bQ||,  K=||WK|| Bᵃ+||bK||,  V=||WV|| Bᵃ+||bV||,
aQ=||WQ|| Gᵃ,       aK=||WK|| Gᵃ,       aV=||WV|| Gᵃ,
S=Q K/√dₕ + sup |deterministic score bias|.

All quantities carry layer/head indices. Every score has magnitude ≤S and every key weight ≤e2S/N. Let oℓh=||WOℓh||. For FFN x↦W₂ReLU(W₁x+b₁)+b₂ define

Fℓ = 1+||W₂|| ||W₁|| Gᶠ,
Uℓ = ||W₂|| (||W₁|| Bᶠ+||b₁||)+||b₂||,
Gℓ = ||bO|| + Σₕ oℓₕ Vℓₕ.

Let D₀=||embed(0)−embed(1)||₂ and set Dℓ=D₀+2∑r<ℓ(Gr+Ur). For words differing only in the penultimate token, the norm of the representation difference at that position before layer ℓ is at most Dℓ. This follows from the residual triangle inequality and the bounded value of each attention/FFN branch, even when positional residuals are unbounded. It is a conservative bound, not an iid claim.

With all symbols in the following head formula carrying the same ℓ,h indices, define

cℓₕ = 2V(K aQ+Q aK)/√dₕ + aV,
tℓₕ = exp(2S) [ 2V·min{2QK/√dₕ, Q aK Dℓ/√dₕ} + min{2V,aV Dℓ} ],
Aℓ = Fℓ [1+Σₕ oℓₕ cℓₕ],       Bℓ = Fℓ Σₕ oℓₕ tℓₕ.

Thus the ledger pays width d, head dimension, every head and layer, LayerNorm scale and ε, affine biases, Q/K/V/O and FFN norms, score bias and token-embedding difference. Softmax, ReLU and exact normalization are mathematical operations here; a finite-arithmetic implementation must additionally pay approximation and rounding errors.

3. Recurrence and proof

Compare two length-N histories with identical prefix and last symbol but opposite penultimate symbol. All positions before the changed token agree at every layer by causality. Write δℓ for the last-position residual difference after ℓ blocks, δ₀=0. For each head, first change the terminal query/key/value while holding the other keys and values from one history fixed. All score changes are at most (K aQ+Q aK)δℓ/√dh. Along the softmax interpolation, its L1 change is at most twice the maximum score change, so the bounded output change is at most cℓhδℓ; the aV term pays terminal value change. The score bias does not change between histories.

Then change the penultimate key/value. The key norm bound and LayerNorm Lipschitz bound give its score difference ≤min{2QK/√dh,Q aK Dℓ/√dh}, and its value difference ≤min{2V,aV Dℓ}. Changing only one score changes its softmax mass by at most (e2S/N) times that score difference: the derivative p(1−p)≤p and every interpolated score stays within the same ±S bounds. Since any two bounded values/averages are at distance ≤2V, this step is bounded by tℓh/N. Summing heads, applying output maps, then the second normalized FFN and both residuals gives

δℓ₊₁ ≤ Aℓ δℓ + Bℓ/N,   δL ≤ [Σℓ Bℓ ∏ₖ₌ℓ₊₁ᴸ Aₖ]/N.

The construction of each positive-ε LayerNorm and the softmax stability inequality are standard; compare Hahn, Lemma 5, and the causal prior-art audit. This derivation is explicitly conservative.

4. Fixed-source risk and necessary capacity

Let Rout be a global Lipschitz constant from terminal hidden vector to predicted Bernoulli probability. For fixed affine logits w₀,w₁ followed by binary softmax, Rout≤||w₁−w₀||/4; if a final positive-ε LayerNorm precedes this map, multiply by its Lipschitz constant. Define

C_model = R_out Σℓ Bℓ ∏ₖ₌ℓ₊₁ᴸ Aₖ.

For the fixed binary source θ₀₀(1)=3/4, θ₁₀(1)=1/4, θ₀₁(1)=θ₁₁(1)=1/2, and any initial pair law, the 00 and 10 continuations of any prefix of length N−2 have probability at least 1/16 each. Their targets differ by 1/2, whereas their predicted probabilities differ by at most Cmodel/N. Hence for every N≥4

 E TV(Q_N,θ_(Xₙ₋₁,Xₙ)) ≥ max{0, 1/32 − C_model/(16N)}.

In particular, for a required risk r<1/32, the necessary condition for any model at this N is Cmodel≥N(1/2−16r). If a public budget class bounds Cmodel by Cbudget(N), the same condition applies with Cbudget(N); if its budget is independent of N, no member can achieve r at sufficiently large N. This is a capacity condition obtained from an upper bound on information flow, not an assertion that increasing Cmodel is sufficient, or that the exponent/constants are optimal. The infimum over each bounded-budget class is controlled at that N. If parameters or budgets grow with N, one must use their N-dependent constants; the fixed-model asymptotic and the unrestricted finite-N infimum must not be exchanged.

5. Coarse common resource budget

For a simplified explicit statement assume each LN has ||γ||∞≤Γ, ||b||≤B₀ and ε≥ε₀>0; every listed matrix operator norm and affine bias norm is ≤W with W≥1; d is the fixed residual width, each layer has at most H heads, and all deterministic score biases have |bias|≤T. Define

B = √d Γ+B₀,   G = Γ/√ε₀,   R = W(B+1),   S = R²+T,
F = 1+W²G,
A = F [1+H W²G(4R²+1)],
B_step = F H W exp(2S)(4R³+2R).

Here √dh≥1 and the bounded differences in §2 were relaxed by their saturation bounds. Aℓ≤A and Bℓ≤Bstep. For an affine two-logit readout with matrix norm ≤W and no final LN, Rout≤W/2. Therefore risk≤r<1/32 is possible only if

 N(1/2−16r) ≤ (W/2) L B_step A^(L−1).

This remains a necessary condition when these public caps depend on N; it does not say they are tight. For fixed d,H,L,W,Γ,ε₀,B₀ and fixed r, if only the permitted score-bias cap T is allowed to grow, the displayed inequality forces S≥(1/2)ln N−O(1). This is a bound on a conservative score cap, not a proof that each realized logit or softmax temperature must grow at that rate; alternative growth in other norms, heads, depth or regularization constants changes the tradeoff.

6. Comparison and boundary

The reviewed no-LN construction uses fixed width and depth and paid unbounded positions (1,i,i²), with first-block unshifted score terms O(N³) and matching gap N; its four-block standard-output extension additionally pays log i. It lies outside this pre-LN score restriction, so this is a structural model separation, not a same-architecture, equal-score-budget compute separation. The full-network risk bound says nothing about post-LN's unnormalized first attention, local masks, zero ε, token-position preprocessing, training, or conventional floating point. Prior art already covers the basic bounded-attention influence mechanism; source-level and quantitative claims require direct independent proof and broader literature review before any novelty claim.
