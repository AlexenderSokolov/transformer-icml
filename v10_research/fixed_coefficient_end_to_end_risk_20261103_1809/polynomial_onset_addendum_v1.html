Claim

For any integer q≥1 and the same source, exact-real graph and fixed coefficient error η=1/(2q), if

N ≥ [10⁶(q+1)]⁴,

then the actual-terminal expected TV risk exceeds 3/32. Thus a sufficient onset length is O(q⁴)=O(η⁻⁴). This is an intentionally enormous sufficient threshold, not a lower bound on when drift first matters, an optimum exponent, or a uniform statement for arbitrary perturbations.

Proof

Set t=N1/4. The condition implies t≥10⁶(q+1), in particular t≥10⁶ and q≤t. Reuse the companion's R=2⌈16ln(N+2)⌉, K=Iq+(2q+1)(R+8)+10, d(r)=(3/4)⌊r/2⌋ and Δ.

For N≥16, ln(N+2)≤4(N+2)1/4≤(24/5)t, so R≤160t. Also Iq=⌈4q ln(8q)⌉+2≤32q²+3, using ln(8q)≤8q. As 2q+1≤3q and q≤t,

K ≤32q²+3+3q(160t+8)+10 ≤550qt.

Therefore K≤N=t⁴, so the nontrivial companion bound applies. Its construction gives d(R)≤(N+2)−4≤t⁻¹⁶, and hence

Δ ≤550q/t³ + 1/t¹⁶ + 60/t^(3/2) + 3/t⁸.

For the terminal-mixing term put D=2q+1≤3q≤3t. Since ln(3/4)≤−1/4 and ⌊⌊N/D⌋/2⌋≥N/(2D)−1,

d(⌊N/D⌋) ≤ exp[1/4−N/(8D)] ≤ exp[1/4−t³/24] ≤ t⁻²,

where the last inequality holds for t≥8: t³/24−1/4−2ln t is positive at 8 and increasing afterwards.

Finally e−N≤1/N, √(N+2)≥t² and q≤t. The companion bound becomes

|risk−3/16| ≤ 5760/t^(3/2) + 88169/t² + 160/t⁴ + 288/t⁸ + 96/t¹⁶.

At t≥10⁶ the right side is less than 1/100, which is in turn less than 3/32. Thus risk>3/16−3/32=3/32. Every inequality here is a conservative analytic consequence of the earlier bound; the huge constant is not a computational experiment or practical prediction. ∎
