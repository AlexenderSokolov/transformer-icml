Finite-N bound for the fixed-coefficient drift example

3 November 2026. This candidate supplements the fixed-source asymptotic counterexample, SHA-256 7e41058add6b41818414502fd83bc4959da6f30ae57c1be3c4af6fc3f8689821, and uses the exact same source, four-block graph, one-head perturbation and output. It is deliberately conservative and awaits independent analytic review. No test, enumeration, simulation or source overwrite was used.

1. Explicit bound and scope

For q∈{1,2,…}, put η=1/(2q), D=2q+1, λ=2q/D, and d(t)=(3/4)⌊t/2⌋ for nonnegative integer t. For any N define

I_q = ⌈4q ln(8q)⌉+2,
R_N = 2⌈16 ln(N+2)⌉,
K_(q,N) = I_q + D(R_N+8)+10,
Δ_(q,N) = K_(q,N)/N + d(R_N)
          + √[20(R_N+12)/N + 8d(R_N)].

If N≥Kq,N, the unconditional same-history terminal expected Bernoulli-TV risk LN,q of the perturbed model satisfies

|L_(N,q) − 3/16| ≤ min{1,
    96 Δ_(q,N)
    + 32[3+2K_(q,N)+2N e^(−N)]/N
    + 168/√(N+2) + d(⌊N/D⌋)}.                         (2)

For N<Kq,N, the trivial bound |LN,q−3/16|≤1 applies. The constants are not optimized, and the bound is not asserted for all η. It includes the unperturbed second copy head, early fractional gates, both BOS tokens, all anchors, the actual terminal query, and the final ordinary vocabulary softmax.

2. Copy margin and explicit early cutoff

As in the companion, the perturbed head's center is λ(i−1), its unique nearest integer j(i) has a gap ≥i/(2q), and all other keys have lower score. Hence its off-target mass is at most i exp[−i/(2q)]. The elementary inequality t≤exp(t/2) for t≥0 gives

i exp[−i/(2q)] ≤ 2q exp[−i/(4q)] < 1/4   for i≥I_q.

The original fixed ReLU snap is therefore exact at all i≥Iq, and the second head remains exact on valid data positions. For data position k, the snapped perturbed lag-one symbol is Xf(k), with

f(k)=round[λ(k+1)]−2,    k−2−f(k)≥(k+1)/D−3/2.

The odd denominator D prevents ties. For k≥Kq,N, f(k) is a data index, the copy and valid-position gates are exact, and the singleton Xf(k) is at least RN pair-chain steps from the local late block Xk−2:k. All earlier keys are treated as exceptional without making assumptions about their fractional gate values.

3. Mean and covariance with overlapping time indices

For a,b∈{0,1} use only exact late keys K≤k≤N:

J_ab=Σ 1{X_(k−2)=a, X_f(k)=b},
C_ab=Σ 1{X_(k−2)=a, X_f(k)=b} X_k,
r₀=5/8, r₁=3/8.

Let Zk be either summand. It lies in [0,1]. The source's pair chain is stationary with uniform pair law and has d(t) as a uniform TV mixing bound. Its target late-block mean is μ=1/4 for J and μ=ra/4 for C. By mixing and the deterministic cutoff, |E Zk−μ|≤d(R), and dropping the first K−1 terms contributes at most K/N.

For each k, call ℓ bad if one of |k−ℓ|, |f(k)−f(ℓ)|, |f(k)−ℓ| or |k−f(ℓ)| is at most R+8. Here f is nondecreasing and each integer has at most two preimages because λ≥2/3. Thus each k has at most 12R+102 ≤20(R+12) bad ℓ. Covariance is at most 1 for those pairs. For other pairs, the two early singleton blocks and two late length-three blocks are separated by at least R in the underlying pair chain. Successively replacing at most three transition bridges by the stationary law gives total-variation distance at most 3d(R) from the product of the four stationary block laws. Applying the same bound to each two-block mean yields |Cov(Zk,Zℓ)|≤5d(R), safely bounded by 8d(R). Consequently

Var[(1/N)Σ_(k=K)^N Z_k] ≤ 20(R+12)/N + 8d(R),
E|J_ab/N−1/4| ≤ Δ,    E|C_ab/N−r_a/4| ≤ Δ.         (3)

This four-block TV argument applies whatever the order of early and late blocks, and pays all overlapping/nearby index pairs as bad. It does not assume that the nonstationary sequence Zk, the query or random occupation counts are iid.

4. Four anchors and true terminal risk

Scale block-3 anchor weights by e−N. For a fixed anchor, the exact late matches contribute count J and successor sum C, and the two BOS positions contribute denominator 2 and numerator 1. All keys before K have a gate between 0 and 1, giving total scaled weight at most K; late nonmatches have total scaled weight at most N e−N. On event J≥N/8, the anchor error from ra is at most

8(|C/N−r_a/4|+|J/N−1/4|)
   + 8[3+2K+2N e^(−N)]/N.

By Markov's inequality and (3), P(J<N/8)≤8Δ; the anchor error is otherwise ≤1. Hence each anchor's expected error is at most 24Δ+8[3+2K+2Ne−N]/N. Summing over all four anchors bounds the expected maximum error by 96Δ+32[3+2K+2Ne−N]/N. For N≥K, the actual terminal query is exactly the onehot anchor for (Xf(N),XN), so the same maximum controls its selected scalar pN. The final Gaussian/log-position vocabulary-softmax step differs from pN by at most 168/√(N+2), uniformly on every input.

Finally, N−f(N)≥⌊N/D⌋. By stationary pair-chain mixing, the joint law of Xf(N) and the true terminal pair differs in TV by at most d(⌊N/D⌋) from the product of a uniform bit and a uniform pair. The latter's expected |rbit−θpair| is exactly 3/16. A bounded-loss TV comparison and reverse triangle inequality yield (2), including an upper and lower deviation bound. The equation does not identify the actual first length at which the risk becomes large.

5. What the explicit schedule says

Because d(RN)≤(N+2)−4, for each fixed q the right side of (2) is Oq(√[ln N/N]) with explicit constants above. More generally, for any integer sequence qN≥1 satisfying

q_N [ln(q_N+1)+ln(N+2)] / N → 0,

we have Kq_N,N/N→0 and N/(2qN+1)→∞; thus the same bound gives LN,q_N→3/16. For instance, qN=⌊N/(ln(N+2))²⌋ eventually makes the one scalar's error 2/qN tend to zero while this risk remains nonzero. Here the perturbed graph's weight changes with N; it is a triangular sequence, not one fixed network. The expression (2), rather than an unproved asymptotic guess, supplies a conservative computable threshold: any N≥K for which its right side is <3/32 ensures risk >3/32. This sufficient threshold is far from established sharp.

These statements retain the exact-real, fixed-source and special η=1/(2q) assumptions. No general claim about arbitrary coefficient values, finite IEEE arithmetic, the robustness of other architectures or an optimal perturbation scale follows.
