A fixed score-coefficient error can cause nonzero final risk

3 November 2026. An analytic candidate for independent review. No test, enumeration or source overwrite was used. The conclusion concerns one specified positive no-LN graph and one specified family of fixed perturbations, not all Transformers or generic floating point.

Claim. For every integer q≥1, change only the −ij² score coefficient of the first block's lag-one copy head by a fixed relative η=1/(2q)>0. On the same fixed, stationary, all-positive, uniformly fast-mixing binary second-order source defined below, the complete four-block model's actual-terminal expected TV risk tends to 3/16 as N→∞. Thus there are arbitrarily small but fixed single-entry perturbations of this graph with nonzero limiting prediction risk. The perturbation depends on q, not N; this is not a lower bound for every η>0.

1. Exact architecture, perturbation and source

The unperturbed model is the four-block fixed-parameter graph, with its uniform final-softmax bound and the three-block boundary clarification. Positions i=1,2 have BOS values 1/2 and data Xₖ is at i=k+2. The first block has two separate copy heads h=1,2; alter only the coefficient of −ij² in head h=1:

s₁,η(i,j)=2i(i−1)j−(1+η)ij²,   η=1/(2q),   q=1,2,… .

The original Q-coordinate is −4i and the original key-coordinate is j² with score division by 4; multiplying its one scalar weight −4 by (1+η) implements exactly this alteration. Thus the absolute scalar-weight change is 4η=2/q. All other parameters, the h=2 head, BOS values, masks, positional features (1,i,i²,ln i), residuals, gates and ordinary final vocabulary softmax are unchanged. Every graph has fixed parameters for all N. Exact real arithmetic is assumed.

Initialize the binary pair chain at its uniform stationary distribution and use

θ₀₀(1)=3/4,  θ₁₀(1)=1/4,  θ₀₁(1)=θ₁₁(1)=1/2.

Every legal next-symbol probability is in [1/4,3/4], the uniform pair law is stationary, and every two-step pair transition has probability at least 1/16. In particular P² dominates one quarter of the uniform pair law, so the pair chain mixes geometrically. Its genuine terminal target is θ(Xₙ₋₁,Xₙ). Let r₀=5/8 and r₁=3/8; stationarity gives rₐ=P(Xₖ=1 | Xₖ₋₂=a).

2. Deterministic index drift and actual gates

Set λ=1/(1+η)=2q/(2q+1). Completing the square shows the perturbed score is a row constant minus (1+η)i[j−λ(i−1)]². The center has odd denominator 2q+1 and is never halfway between integers. Its nearest integer

j(i)=round(λ(i−1))

is unique; the score gap to every other integer key is at least i/(2q). For all sufficiently large i, j(i) is a valid data position and the total softmax mass outside j(i) is at most i exp[−i/(2q)]<1/4. The original snap T therefore outputs the bit at j(i) exactly. For data index k=i−2 define

f(k)=j(k+2)−2=round(λ(k+1))−2.

There is a deterministic k₀(q) such that, for every data word and k≥k₀, the lag-one snapped bit is Xf(k). The unchanged lag-two head still snaps Xk−2 on all candidate positions k≥3. Since f(k)=λk+Oq(1), the distance k−2−f(k) grows linearly. Early positions k<k₀ can have fractional snaps, but their finite number is independent of N; the original gates always lie in [0,1]. For the actual terminal k=N≥k₀ the query is exactly onehot for (Xf(N),XN). For every late history position k the anchor-context onehot is exactly (Xk−2,Xf(k)). Both assertions follow from the original gate formulas, not from treating approximate bits as exact without the snap-margin bound.

3. Nonstationary-index count law with dependence

For a,b∈{0,1} and N≥k₀, define late counts

J_ab(N)=Σ_(k=k₀)^N 1{X_(k−2)=a, X_f(k)=b},
C_ab(N)=Σ_(k=k₀)^N 1{X_(k−2)=a, X_f(k)=b} X_k.

We prove jointly, in probability,

J_ab(N)/N → 1/4,      C_ab(N)/N → r_a/4.                     (1)

This is not an iid assumption. The lag f(k) is nonstationary and the same symbols can participate in many terms. Let Yₖ=(Xₖ₋₁,Xₖ). Uniform two-step minorization gives a total-variation mixing bound d(t)≤(3/4)⌊t/2⌋ from every pair state. For each k, f(k) is separated from the late block Xk−2:k by Θq(k), so stationarity and mixing give E[J term]→P(X=a)P(X=b)=1/4 and E[C term]→(1/4)ra. Bounded Cesàro convergence gives the corresponding mean limits.

For the variance, choose an integer R and discard the first Oq(R) k for which the early singleton Xf(k) may be within R+2 of Xk−2:k. For each remaining k, call a second index ℓ bad if any of |k−ℓ|, |f(k)−f(ℓ)|, |f(k)−ℓ| or |k−f(ℓ)| is ≤R+3. Since f is nondecreasing, has slope λ>0 and uniformly bounded fibers, there are Oq(R+1) bad ℓ per k. The bad pairs contribute Oq(R/N) to the variance of an N-term average.

For every other pair, the four blocks consisting of the two early singletons and the two bounded-length late blocks are mutually separated by at least R up to a fixed indexing offset. Ordering these blocks in time and applying the pair-chain mixing estimate at each gap, their joint law differs in TV by O(d(R−O(1))) from the product of their stationary block marginals. The covariance of the two bounded summands is consequently O(d(R−O(1))). Thus the normalized variance is Oq(R/N)+O(d(R−O(1))). Taking R=⌊√N⌋ makes it vanish. The finite discarded prefix also vanishes after division by N. The same proof applies to J and C; Chebyshev and a finite union prove (1) simultaneously. No conditional independence of terminal states or empirical counts is used.

4. Complete anchors, terminal query and final softmax

On the high-probability event from (1), all Jab(N) are Θ(N), and Cab/Jab→ra for all four anchors. In block 3, each late exact matching key and both BOS keys has score N, and each late nonmatch has score 0. At most k₀ early data keys have a fractional gate in [0,1], so their total relative weight is at most k₀ after scaling by e−N; nonmatching late keys contribute at most N e−N. Since the value is always in [0,1], these early and soft-match contributions, plus the fixed BOS pseudocount, alter each anchor's empirical ratio by oP(1). Hence every anchor output converges jointly to ra, independent of its second context label b.

The actual terminal query is onehot at (Xf(N),XN), so the unchanged ReLU selection yields scalar pN satisfying

p_N − r_(X_f(N)) → 0 in probability.

The original fourth block is unchanged and pN∈[0,1]. Its Gaussian/log-position lemma applies to every p, giving the deterministic uniform bound |P̃N−pN|≤min{1,168/√(N+2)} for its ordinary binary vocabulary-softmax output. Thus P̃N−rX_f(N)→0 in probability and, by boundedness, also in L1.

5. Actual-terminal expected risk

Since N−f(N) grows linearly, stationarity and geometric mixing imply the joint law of Xf(N) and (XN−1,XN) tends to the product of a uniform bit and a uniform pair. By bounded L1 convergence and the reverse triangle inequality, the true expected Bernoulli-TV risk converges to

(1/8) Σ_(a∈{0,1}) Σ_(u,v∈{0,1}) |r_a−θ_uv|
= (1/8)[(1/8+3/8+1/8+1/8)+(3/8+1/8+1/8+1/8)]
= 3/16.

This is a fixed-source unconditional average over the actual terminal pair, not a local gate error or a worst-case adversarial word. Every perturbation 4η=2/q can be made as small as desired by choosing q large before taking N→∞. The proof does not cover a generic η whose score centers can repeatedly approach half-integers: then the copy snap can be fractional, and downstream query/anchor analysis requires additional work.

6. Prior art and limitations

Liu et al., Proposition 4 and Appendix C.3 analyze position-coefficient drift in a different flip-flop attention task; their discussion also warns that attention errors need not imply final-output errors. Chen et al., Theorem 3.6 and Appendix C.5 relate copier/matching errors to a complete empirical induction-head predictor under their assumptions, rather than establishing this fixed-coefficient long-horizon TV example. Castin et al. analyze smoothness of attention under specified bounded-input conditions; the unbounded positional coordinates here require explicit accounting. This is a targeted non-exhaustive comparison and no novelty claim.

The result changes only one scalar coefficient in one copy head and applies to the declared exact-real graph; it does not imply that all perturbation signs, all architectures, all fixed-length deployments or all sources fail. It does not model training or IEEE floating point. The original exact-integer backend stores that coefficient exactly and is not contradicted by a nonzero adversarial analog perturbation. The earlier norm-based sufficient bound remains a separate conservative result.
