Logit perturbations, finite precision and conditioned soft-matching risk

3 November 2026. This is a precision bridge under explicit error assumptions and a separate finite-integer comparator. It is not a new softmax-stability theorem, an end-to-end floating-point Transformer proof, or a general architecture lower bound. No random or numerical tests were used.

Result. If every realized finite attention logit on the same support, including BOS, changes by at most δ, then the conventional half-ℓ1 total-variation distance between the two exact softmax weight distributions is at most tanh(δ/2)≤δ/2. Fixed simplex-valued attention outputs contract this distance. Separately bounded value, softmax-evaluation, final-rounding and mask-leakage errors add to the conditional risk. This conclusion presumes those bounds; it does not establish them for a particular floating-point implementation.

1. Sharp stability and its prior source

For finite logits ℓ∈ℝ^K and h∈ℝ^K on the same valid set, p=softmax(ℓ), q=softmax(ℓ+h). Define osc(h)=max_i h_i−min_i h_i. Then

TV(p,q) ≤ tanh(osc(h)/4) ≤ tanh(||h||∞/2) ≤ ||h||∞/2.

This follows from Cohen and Fausti, Theorem 5.1: the Hilbert distance of p,q is osc(h), since their common log-normalizer cancels. That source defines its total-variation norm as the full ℓ1 distance; our conventional TV is half ℓ1, hence the factor shown here. A direct softmax formulation appears in this 2026 paper, Appendix A.2. This is established prior work.

For a self-contained proof, let A={i:q_i≥p_i}, t=p(A), m=e^(min h), K=e^(max h). Then q(A)≤Kt/[Kt+m(1−t)] and TV=q(A)−t. Maximizing the resulting bound over t∈[0,1] yields (√K−√m)/(√K+√m)=tanh(osc(h)/4). If h=0 both sides are zero. Equality in the δ-bound is attained with two logits ℓ=(−δ,0), h=(δ,−δ). A common shift of all logits has osc(h)=0 and cannot change softmax, so magnitude alone is not the invariant quantity.

2. Explicit end-to-end error model and risk composition

Use the stationary, reverse-mixing order-two source and actual-terminal conditional risk of the preceding uniform bound. On each history ending at s, let I contain exactly the M valid observed-successor positions plus BOS. Let ideal attention weights p be the softmax on I and v_i∈[0,1] be the ideal next-bit-one values, with BOS value 1/2. Thus Q=Σ_i p_i v_i is the ideal binary prediction. Denote its population bias by B_s, its weighted occupancy by b_s and its fluctuation bound by H_D(Mb_s) as in the companion theorem.

Suppose for every relevant history:

  • every realized valid score satisfies |ℓ̃_i−ℓ_i|≤δ, including BOS, after any shift common to all logits;
  • computed simplex-valued attention weights p̂ on I are within TV τ of the exact softmax of ℓ̃;
  • each computed scalar value ṽ_i lies in [0,1] and |ṽ_i−v_i|≤ν;
  • any additional mass placed on invalid masked positions is at most λ, with their output values also in [0,1];
  • the final computed and clipped binary probability differs from its ideal weighted sum by at most ρ.

Then the triangle inequality and contraction of TV under a [0,1]-valued readout give, pathwise,

|Q_computed−Q| ≤ tanh(δ/2)+τ+ν+λ+ρ.

The invalid-mass term is a separate assumption: the valid entries must retain the stated score and normalization model after conditioning on valid support. An ideal −∞ mask is not a finite number to which the δ-bound directly applies. If numerical softmax returns an unnormalized vector, τ must be established after explicit normalization or replaced by a separate output-error bound.

Taking expectations conditional on the actual endpoint s gives

ℛ_computed,s ≤ min{1, B_s + H_D(Mb_s) + tanh(δ/2)+τ+ν+λ+ρ},
ℛ_computed,s ≥ max{0, B_s − H_D(Mb_s) − tanh(δ/2)−τ−ν−λ−ρ}.

Here B_s=|Σ_(r≠s)π_r w_s(r)(θ_r−θ_s)|/b_s and b_s=Σ_rπ_r w_s(r), with w_s(r)=e^(−β·Hamming(r,s)). These bounds also hold at M=0 if H_D(0)=1 and BOS is the only valid item. The equality of the ideal Q with the reported source-specific estimator, and H_D itself, are inherited assumptions from the separate reverse-edge theorem; neither follows from softmax stability alone.

3. What must be paid or separately proved

β and logitsSubtract 2β from all valid logits. For binary k=2, full/one-mismatch/two-mismatch scores are 0, −β, −2β, and BOS is ln 2. If stored β differs by at most ε_β and ln 2 by ε_c, exact arithmetic on these categories gives δ≤max{2ε_β,ε_c}; all additional score computation error must be added. β is a relative score gap, not a lower bound on invariant raw parameter size.

For one desired key with score A and at most N−1 other keys at score 0, wrong attention mass is (N−1)/(e^A+N−1)≤(N−1)e^(−A); this is not included automatically in δ.

The rare-context bias envelope is at most (1−π_s)e^(−β)/[π_s+(1−π_s)e^(−β)]. To force this particular envelope ≤ε_b∈(0,1), it suffices to choose β≥max{0, ln[(1−π_s)(1−ε_b)/(π_s ε_b)]}. This is an analysis-level choice that uses π_s; it is not a universal source-blind parameter selection. The companion rare family shows an order-e^(−β)/(π_s+e^(−β)) bias for this fixed output family, but does not imply a general network lower bound.

4. A finite-integer comparator, not a Transformer backend

Choose an integer q≥0 and mathematical β=q ln2. For each of the four contexts r, let J_r,C_r be its same-history total and successor-one counts. Given the actual terminal s, aggregate near and far Hamming classes J₁,J₂ and C₁,C₂. Define exact nonnegative integers

num = 2^(2q)(C_s+1) + 2^q C₁ + C₂,
den = 2^(2q)(J_s+2) + 2^q J₁ + J₂.

Then num/den is exactly the ideal BOS soft-matching output at β=q ln2. The 2^q and 2^(2q) operations are integer shifts. Since den≤(M+2)2^(2q), all numerator/denominator integers fit in O(q+log(M+2)) bits. A one-pass scan keeps eight context counts and the last two input bits in O(log(M+2)) working bits for the fixed binary alphabet; the terminal query selects their four classes. Straightforward bit-complexity is O(M log(M+2)) for counter updates, plus O((q+log(M+2))(p_out+1)) for shifts, additions and p_out-bit long division. Including output workspace, O(q+log(M+2)+p_out) bits suffice. Truncating a probability in [0,1] to p_out fractional bits contributes <2^(−p_out) binary-TV error. The result is a direct count-based comparator, not an attention head or a generic exponential implementation.

If an attention implementation stores a dyadic s with |s−ln2|≤2^(−p) and uses stored β̃=q s, the four ideal normalized score categories above differ from their symbolic q ln2 counterparts by at most max{2q,1}2^(−p), before any arithmetic rounding. One may compare its exact-softmax output to the ideal result using §1. Computing symbolic powers of two by the integer comparator does not prove that an arbitrary floating-point softmax, copied contexts or a standard Transformer evaluates those scores with the required error. No irrational constant needs to be embedded in the integer comparator itself: q is a symbolic integer choice of mathematical temperature.

5. Provenance and limits

Stability is prior literature. The conditional risk term requires the separate stationary reverse-edge proof and its mixing assumptions; it does not follow for arbitrary initialization or nonmixing sources. The arithmetic comparator has explicitly different access and computation from the attention model. No hidden end-to-end finite-word guarantee, trained-weight claim, architecture lower bound, formal verification or comprehensive novelty assessment is asserted.
