# Non-destructive gain-one to gain-two compilation patch

Applies to scalar-position-alignment and the paired reset lemma, assuming epsilon is unchanged, shift remains zero, and both LayerNorm gain vectors are constant ones in the source construction. Replace them by constant twos. Every affine map directly consuming a normalized vector must have its linear weight divided by two: all Q, K, V projections, FFN first-layer W1, and final normalized linear readout. Leave all corresponding biases unchanged. Leave attention output projections, FFN second-layer W2, residual identity paths, positional/token embeddings, epsilon and unnormalized initial states unchanged. A zero projection remains zero. The present scalar-position construction has Q=K=0, so the nontrivial consumers are V, W1 and final readout.

For every physical input h, LN_gain2(h)=2 LN_gain1(h). Thus (W/2)LN_gain2(h)+b=W LN_gain1(h)+b exactly. Induction through attention and FFN branches proves identical physical trajectories, logits and outputs, not just equivalent asymptotic bounds. For nonzero attention Q and K, BOTH consumers must be halved; changing gains alone would multiply scores by four. A functional implemented directly from normalized coordinates, such as the displayed reset increment, needs coefficient -1/2 after the change, not -1.

Norm ledger: every changed consumer Frobenius norm is halved; W2 and output projections are unchanged. Each LayerNorm gain norm increases from sqrt(d) to 2sqrt(d), hence its squared contribution increases by 3d per normalization. With two LNs per block, the additional squared gain contribution is 6d per block, before subtracting the reduction of consumer weights. A final LN, if separately charged, adds another 3d. Fixed per-layer bounds remain finite, but any predeclared numerical cap on LN gains or global aggregate norm must be rechecked. The scalar-position dimension is d=14. This patch does not change its exact pairing, epsilon, state bound, depth or precision requirements.

Scope: this is valid for affine consumers of LN output, with all consumers accounted for, and zero LN shift. It is not permission to change an independently constrained embedding, epsilon, or nonzero shift without a new calculation. It preserves old artifact history; publication should add this conversion note rather than silently relabel gain-one experiments as originally gain-two.
