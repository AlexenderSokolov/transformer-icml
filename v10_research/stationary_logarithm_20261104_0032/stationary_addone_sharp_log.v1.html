Sharp stationary rates for full history add one prediction

Main result. For a stationary second-order source on A symbols observed for n transitions, the unchanged full-history add-one predictor at the actual terminal pair satisfies

Expected forward KL ≤ min{log(n+A),
  14000 A³/(n+1) [1+log₊((n+1)/A³)]},    A ≥ 2, n ≥ 1.

The complete proof retains terminal selection, allows zero probabilities, reducibility and periodicity, and assumes no mixing. It combines a one-count row tilt with the target stationary mass and a weighted low-count loss bound. Exact-real five-block and specified integer/dyadic outputs inherit the same stationary order through their established support floors and pathwise approximation bounds.

Rate comparison. With N=n+2 observed symbols, this matches the HJW second-order minimax order A³/N log(N/A³) throughout their stated growing-alphabet range, after a harmless enlargement of its unspecified constant. For fixed A ≥ 2, the rate is Θ_A(log n/n). The sufficient growing-alphabet consistency condition becomes A³/n → 0.

First-order scope. The same statistical argument gives ordinary stationary k-state add-one risk O(k²/N [1+log₊(N/k²)]). For k ≥ 3 this matches HJW Theorem 1. Conditional on independent external review, it is a potential answer to the specific question stated in their 2023 Remark 1. Current literature priority is not established. Binary first-order prediction remains exceptional: its minimax rate is Θ(log log N/N), which this argument does not attain.

Literature status. Sharp stationary terminal KL rates and network output corollaries

Composition and comparison note, 4 November 2026 (UTC). The new statistical proof has two passing independent exact-file analytical audits. The output results below substitute that bound into separately reviewed pathwise construction and arithmetic guarantees. The substantive conclusions remain conditional on independent external review. This is not formal verification, a software execution certificate, or a literature-priority claim.

1. Statistical theorem and indexing

Let A>=2, n>=1, and L=n+2+A. Observe X_1,...,X_{n+2} from a homogeneous second-order source with a stationary initial pair distribution. Its transition rows may contain zeros; the pair chain may be reducible or periodic. Set

Y_i=(X_{i+1},X_{i+2}),                    0<=i<=n,
J_s=sum_{i=0}^{n-1}1{Y_i=s},
C_{s,b}=sum_{i=0}^{n-1}1{Y_i=s,X_{i+3}=b},
B_s(b)=(C_{s,b}+1)/(J_s+A),
W(A,n)=min{log(n+A),
            14000 A^3/(n+1) [1+log_+((n+1)/A^3)]}.

Here log_+(x)=max{log x,0}, and other logarithms are natural. The new theorem gives

E D_KL(p_{Y_n}||B_{Y_n})<=W(A,n).                    (1)

This is the actual terminal pair of the observed word. There is no independent test context, conditional-binomial assumption, or averaging over suffix estimators. Full-history add-one itself is the predictor in (1).

2. Pathwise KL transfer

For probability vectors P,B,Q with B and Q strictly positive, Jensen's inequality gives

D(P||Q)-2D(P||B)
  =sum_b P_b log[B_b^2/(P_b Q_b)]
  <=log(sum_b B_b^2/Q_b)
  =log[1+sum_b (B_b-Q_b)^2/Q_b]
  <=sum_b (B_b-Q_b)^2/Q_b.                          (2)

Zero P_b terms are omitted. If Q_b>=q_min, then the last expression is at most q_min^{-1} sum_b(B_b-Q_b)^2. Since B-Q has zero sum, its positive and negative parts both have mass TV(B,Q), so

sum_b(B_b-Q_b)^2<=2 TV(B,Q)^2.                       (3)

The support floor is essential. TV closeness by itself does not imply a finite forward-KL transfer.

3. Exact-real five-block network

Let R be the ordinary A-way vocabulary softmax output of the unchanged five-block causal attention/ReLU construction embedded in this package. For each fixed A, its finite parameters are fixed independently of n and the unknown source. Retain all its assumptions: the explicit positional features (1,i,i^2,log i), A smoothing BOS tokens, full causal attention support, no LayerNorm, and the stated architecture and gates. There is no claim of a trained network, fixed finite word width, or removal of those positional features.

The inherited all-word bounds are

TV(R,B)<=min{1,(44A+1)/L},
R_b>=1/[1+(A-1)L]>=1/(AL).

Equations (1)-(3) therefore imply

E D_KL(p_{Y_n}||R)
  <=min{log[1+(A-1)L],
         2W(A,n)+2A(44A+1)^2/L}.                   (4)

The network is unchanged. Only its statistical input bound has improved.

The five blocks retain H_heads=A^2+1 allocated heads per block, d_h=4 ceil(sqrt(A+10))^2, width d_model=H_heads d_h=O(A^3), and active head counts (2,0,A^2+1,A,A). A changing alphabet changes this architecture and its vocabulary. The growing-A formulas concern this explicit family, not one network with an unbounded vocabulary.

4. Specified integer and dyadic evaluation

Let Q be the output of the embedded specialized integer/dyadic evaluator, with

s=ceil(log_2(AL)),    b=32s,    H=2^b,    F=H^2.

The algorithm uses exact integer positions and squares, constructed log positions, exact dyadic gates, max-shifted exponential approximation, and mass-preserving normalization. Its inherited pathwise guarantees are

Q is a nonnegative exact H-grid simplex,
TV(Q,B)<=min{1,(46A+2)/L},
Q_b>=1/(4AL).

Consequently (1)-(3) give

E D_KL(p_{Y_n}||Q)
  <=min{log(4AL),
         2W(A,n)+8A(46A+2)^2/L}.                   (5)

This is a theorem about that algorithm and its exact rounding rules. Additional rounding, generic IEEE floating point, a GPU kernel, automatic mixed precision, or an unspecified softmax backend is not certified by (5).

Fractional bits and signed range

The value b counts fractional bits, not the complete signed machine-word width. The evaluator also represents signed raw scores, rowwise maxima before max shifting, integer positions and squares, exact accumulation, and wider temporary products.

  • Raw copy-score magnitudes are O(L^3); narrow-head scores are O(L^2).
  • The broad-head O(L) statement applies at the terminal position only. Earlier selected probability coordinates can be as large as A^2, and earlier broad scores may have a larger polynomial magnitude. They still require only O(log A+log L) magnitude bits.
  • Signed differences, linear accumulations, and row sums receive their corresponding O(log A+log L) magnitude and guard allowance.
  • Since F=H^2, repeated-squaring products on the F grid may need approximately 4b bits before exact division. Other products receive the constant-factor wider temporaries specified by the inherited evaluator.

Overall working integer lengths are O(b+log A+log L)=O(log(AL)), including those wider temporaries. This does not mean b total signed bits or a fixed exponent range. The length-dependent precision schedule is a paid evaluation resource, not a length-dependent learned coefficient.

The inherited conservative schoolbook cost and row-streamed storage are

O(((A^2+2A+3)L^2+L)b^3
   +(A^3 L^2+A^6 L)b^2) bit operations,

O(A^3 L b+b^2+A^6 log(A+2)) bits of storage.

These are analytical resource bounds, not timings or a claim that the algorithm was executed.

5. Zero observed transitions

For n=0, use L=A+2 and the uniform add-one row. Its KL is at most log A. Do not evaluate W(A,n) through the n>=1 theorem. The all-word transfer guarantees instead yield

E D_KL(p_{Y_0}||R)
  <=min{log[1+(A-1)L],
         2log A+2A(44A+1)^2/L},

E D_KL(p_{Y_0}||Q)
  <=min{log(4AL),
         2log A+8A(46A+2)^2/L}.

The inherited evaluator handles this shortest word without introducing a nonexistent data transition.

6. Fixed and growing alphabet comparisons

Both output penalties in (4)-(5) are O(A^3/L) with absolute constants, and L>=n+1. Therefore B, R, and the specified Q all have stationary worst-case upper bounds

O(A^3/(n+1) [1+log_+((n+1)/A^3)]).                  (6)

In particular, A^3/n -> 0 is sufficient for uniform consistency along growing-alphabet sequences. This is stronger than the earlier sufficient condition A^3 log n/n -> 0. No necessity statement is needed here.

Han, Jana and Wu (HJW), published Theorem 22, use N observed symbols and state, for each order r>=2, a minimax rate

Theta_r( A^{r+1}/N * log[N/A^{r+1}] ),
2<=A<=N^{1/(r+1)}/C_r.

For second order, N=n+2 and r=2. The unspecified absolute constant C_2 can harmlessly be enlarged to at least 2. Then N/A^3>=8, so 1+log_+((n+1)/A^3) is an absolute constant multiple of log(N/A^3), and 1/(n+1)<=2/N. Thus (1), (4), and (5) match HJW's minimax order throughout this growing-alphabet domain:

Theta( A^3/(n+2) * log[(n+2)/A^3] ).                (7)

The lower bound applies to each predictor because HJW's infimum covers every measurable probability-vector prediction based on the observed history. It is a worst-case bound over stationary sources, not a pointwise lower bound at every source. For every fixed A>=2 this gives Theta_A(log n/n). HJW's Theorem 24 explicitly covers binary second-order sources.

The inherited arbitrary-initial-pair theorem remains separate, with upper bound O(A^5 log(n+2)/(n+1)). Stationarity is indispensable to the new proof as given; the A^3 sharpened expression is not claimed for arbitrary starts.

7. Ordinary first-order implication and the 2023 open question

The statistical proof uses D context states and A output symbols only through the final sum over targets and symbols. It gives

14000 DA/(n+1) [1+log_+((n+1)/(DA))]                 (8)

whenever the single-row group tilts used in that proof are legal kernels in the model. They are legal for an arbitrary ordinary k-state Markov chain: a row has k possible next states, and scaling one transition probability and its complement preserves the kernel's support.

For an ordinary stationary k-state chain observed as Z_1,...,Z_N, put n=N-1, D=k, and A=k. Its usual full-history add-one predictor uses

N_i=sum_{t=1}^{N-1}1{Z_t=i},
N_{ij}=sum_{t=1}^{N-1}1{Z_t=i,Z_{t+1}=j},
B_i(j)=(N_{ij}+1)/(N_i+k),

and predicts at the actual terminal state Z_N. For N>=2, (8) gives

E D_KL(P(.|Z_N)||B_{Z_N})
  <=min{log(N-1+k),
         14000 k^2/N [1+log_+(N/k^2)]}.             (9)

HJW's Theorem 1 gives minimax order

Theta( k^2/N * log[N/k^2] ),
3<=k<=sqrt(N)/C,

for an absolute constant C. After enlarging C to at least 2, (9) matches that rate throughout the stated domain. No network architecture claim for first-order chains is added here; (9) is a statistical add-one corollary.

HJW's 2023 Remark 1 asks whether the usual full-history add-one estimator, rather than the suffix-averaged estimator used there, achieves that rate. Conditional on independent external review of the present proof, (9) is a potential answer to that stated 2023 question for k>=3. Current literature priority is not established, and these internal analytical audits are not external peer review. No claim is made that the question remains open today or that this proof is the first answer.

Binary first-order chains are different: HJW recall the minimax rate Theta(log log N/N), whereas (9) only gives O(log N/N) for k=2. This work does not attain the binary first-order minimax rate. The binary second-order Theta(log N/N) statement is a distinct result and does not conflict with it.

A stationary-mass and loss-sensitive bound for full-history add-one prediction

Analytical candidate, 4 November 2026 (UTC). Status: awaiting independent exact-file review. No numerical experiments or external uploads were performed. This document makes no literature-priority claim.

1. The result and precise observation model

Let A >= 2 and n >= 1. Observe X_1,...,X_{n+2} from a homogeneous second-order source on [A], with arbitrary transition rows and a stationary initial pair distribution. Zero transition probabilities, reducibility, periodicity, and multiple closed classes are allowed. Write D=A^2 and

Y_i=(X_{i+1},X_{i+2}),                 0 <= i <= n,
J_s=sum_{i=0}^{n-1} 1{Y_i=s},
C_{s,b}=sum_{i=0}^{n-1} 1{Y_i=s,X_{i+3}=b},
B_s(b)=(C_{s,b}+1)/(J_s+A).

Let p_s denote the true next-symbol row and pi the stationary pair distribution. All logarithms are natural, and log_+(x)=max{log x,0} for x>0. The proposed bound is

E D_KL(p_{Y_n} || B_{Y_n})
  <= min{ log(n+A),
          14000 A^3/(n+1) [1+log_+((n+1)/A^3)] }.       (1)

For n=0 the risk is at most log A. The estimator and its actual terminal query are not modified, randomized, conditioned away, or replaced by an independent test context. No mixing assumption is used.

The argument refines the previously reviewed stationary row-tilt proof in two places: retain the target's stationary mass in the deviation coefficient, and use a loss-sensitive positive-log estimate at small row counts. The first removes the unnecessary high-count bands; the second avoids paying A/m at counts m<A.

2. Stationary fixed-target occupation

For a stationary finite-state Markov chain with law rho, a target s, and integer w>=1,

P_rho(Y_n=s, J_s<w) <= w/(n+1).                         (2)

Here J_s counts the n departure positions 0,...,n-1. To prove (2), put

b_l=P_rho(Y_l=s, sum_{i=0}^{l-1}1{Y_i=s}<w).

Stationarity and restriction to a suffix imply b_l>=b_n for every l<=n. On every infinite path, only the first w occurrences of s can satisfy the corresponding condition, so sum_{l=0}^n b_l<=w. This proves (2).

For dyadic m in {1,2,4,...}, define

E_{s,m}={Y_n=s, m<=J_s<2m},
beta_{s,m}=min{2m/(n+1), pi(s)}.

Consequently

P_pi(E_{s,m})<=beta_{s,m},
sum_s beta_{s,m} <= min{2Dm/(n+1),1}.                  (3)

Both bounds retain the actual terminal selection.

3. The stationary-mass refinement of the row tilt

Fix a target s with pi(s)>0 and split its outgoing row into a group of probability p and its complement of probability 1-p, where 0<p<1. For 0<q<1 replace these two masses by q and 1-q while retaining their within-group proportions. Call the resulting kernel Q. Put

r_min=min{q/p,(1-q)/(1-p)},
r_max=max{q/p,(1-q)/(1-p)},
lambda=log[q(1-p)/(p(1-q))].

The positive scaling preserves the transition support, hence all closed irreducible classes. Choose a Q-stationary distribution nu with the same weights on those classes as pi. On every unchanged class keep its original stationary distribution. On the class containing s use its Q-stationary distribution.

Let v_P(z) and v_Q(z) be expected visits to z from time 0 through the departure before the first positive return to s, when starting from s under P and Q respectively. These vectors are finite, satisfy v_P(s)=v_Q(s)=1, and obey

r_min v_P(z) <= v_Q(z) <= r_max v_P(z).                (4)

For z!=s, (4) follows by conditioning on the first departure: until the next hit of s, the transition kernels coincide, and the first-departure distribution was scaled between r_min and r_max. For z=s, it follows from r_min<=1<=r_max. Write L_P=sum_z v_P(z) and L_Q=sum_z v_Q(z). The normalized excursion vector is stationary: shifting the visit sum by one time step replaces its initial s by its terminal s. Therefore, within the class containing s,

pi(z)/nu(z)=(L_Q/L_P) v_P(z)/v_Q(z),
pi(s)/nu(s)=L_Q/L_P in [r_min,r_max].

It follows that on the entire support of pi,

pi(z)/nu(z) <= r_max/r_min=exp(|lambda|),              (5)
pi(z)/nu(z) <= [pi(s)/nu(s)]/r_min.                    (6)

For (6) outside s's class the left side is 1, while the right side is at least 1 because L_Q/L_P>=r_min. This deals explicitly with mixtures of closed classes.

On any original-positive path having j departures from s and k departures in the first group,

log [P_pi(path)/Q_nu(path)]
  =log[pi(Y_0)/nu(Y_0)] - j d(q||p) - (k-jq)lambda,   (7)

where d is binary forward KL. On either shifted one-sided event

k>=jq+1 if q>p;       k<=jq-1 if q<p,                 (8)

the final term in (7) is at most -|lambda|. By (5), the initial ratio is canceled. By (6), it is canceled up to pi(s)/nu(s), because 1/r_min<=exp(|lambda|). Apply the two resulting likelihood bounds separately. Using stationary occupation under Q in the first, and Q_nu(Y_n=s)=nu(s) in the second, gives

P_pi(E_{s,m}, event (8))
  <= min{2m/(n+1),pi(s)} exp[-m d(q||p)]
  = beta_{s,m} exp[-m d(q||p)].                       (9)

This is a statement about the intersection event, not a binomial law conditional on a count or on the terminal state. A different deterministic q and nu may be used for each application. Targets with pi(s)=0 have zero terminal contribution.

For a second-order source, the first group can be the next symbol b and its complement. This changes exactly one legal second-order row, not an arbitrary pair-chain transition.

4. Joint Bernstein bound and the large-count loss estimate

For t>=1 set delta=sqrt(2pt/m)+2t/(3m). For q=p+delta or p-delta in (0,1),

d(q||p) >= delta^2/[2(p+delta/3)] >= t/m.              (10)

For completeness, if Z is a centered Bernoulli(p), then |Z|<=1 and E Z^2<=p. The exponential series gives

log E exp(+-aZ) <= p a^2/[2(1-a/3)],  0<=a<3.

The variational formula for binary KL at a=delta/(p+delta/3) gives the first inequality in (10); substitution gives the second. On m<=j<2m,

j delta <= sqrt(4jpt)+4t/3.

Thus the two sides of (9) imply

P_pi(E_{s,m}, |C_{s,b}-J_s p_s(b)|
      >sqrt(4J_s p_s(b)t)+4t/3+1)
  <=2 beta_{s,m} e^{-t}.                             (11)

If a tilt would reach or cross 0 or 1, its corresponding strict deviation event is impossible. If p_s(b) is 0 or 1, the deviation is identically zero.

For x>=0 and a>0 let h(x,a)=x log(x/a)-x+a, with 0 log 0=0. The following deterministic implication is useful:

|k-jp|<=sqrt(2jpu)+2u/3, u>=1
  implies h((j+1)p,k+1)<=48u(1+log u).                (12)

Here j>=0, p in [0,1], and 0<=k<=j. To verify it put z=jp and a=k+1. The premise and sqrt(2zu)<=z/2+u imply

z<=2k+10u/3,
(k-z)^2<=4zu+8u^2/9<=8ku+128u^2/9,
((j+1)p-a)^2<=16ku+31u^2,
(j+1)p<=2a+10u/3.

The inequality h(x,a)<=(x-a)^2/a gives h<=47u when a>=u. If a<u then x<6u and a>=1, so h<=x log_+x+a<=6u log(6u)+u<=48u(1+log u). This proves (12).

The exact row identity, for sum_b k_b=j, is

sum_b h((j+1)p_b,k_b+1)
  =(j+1)D_KL(p || (k+1)/(j+A))+(A-1)
    -(j+1)log[1+(A-1)/(j+1)]
  >=(j+1)D_KL(p || (k+1)/(j+A)).                     (13)

Fix s,b,m and put Z=h((J_s+1)p_s(b),C_{s,b}+1)/(J_s+1). Off the event in (11), the deviation is bounded by sqrt(8J_s p_s(b)t)+8t/3, so (12) with u=4t yields on E_{s,m}

Z<=576t(1+log t)/m=:f_m(t).

Together with (3) and (11), layer-cake integration gives

E[1_{E_{s,m}} Z]
  <=f_m(1) beta_{s,m}
      + integral_1^infty f_m'(t) 2 beta_{s,m}e^{-t}dt
  <=[576+3456/e] beta_{s,m}/m
  <2304 beta_{s,m}/m.                               (14)

The integral uses log t<=t-1, so integral_1^infty(2+log t)e^{-t}dt<=3/e, and e>2 suffices for the final strict inequality. Summing (14) over b and using (13) proves

E[1_{E_{s,m}} D_KL(p_s||B_s)]
  <=2304 A beta_{s,m}/m.                             (15)

5. A low-count estimate that does not pay A/m

The exact alternative KL decomposition is

D_KL(p || (k+1)/(j+A))
  =log[(j+A)/(j+1)]
      +sum_b p_b log[(j+1)p_b/(k_b+1)].               (16)

Terms with p_b=0 are defined to be zero. For j in [m,2m), the first term is at most log(1+A/m). Define

ell_b=log_+[(j+1)p_b/(k_b+1)].

If 0<p_b<1, then

ell_b>log 4
  implies k_b+1<(j+1)p_b/4<=jp_b/2,
  implies k_b<j(p_b/2)-1.                            (17)

The second inequality uses j>=1. Apply (9) with q=p_b/2. Binary KL satisfies

d(p_b/2||p_b)>=p_b/8.                                (18)

Indeed, its second derivative with respect to its first argument is 1/[t(1-t)]. On [p_b/2,p_b] this is at least 1/p_b; Taylor's integral formula at p_b gives (18).

Also, j<2m and integrality give j+1<=2m, so ell_b<=log_+(2mp_b). Combining this pathwise bound, (17), (18), and (3),

E[1_{E_{s,m}} ell_b]
  <= beta_{s,m} [log 4 + log_+(2mp_b)e^{-mp_b/8}]
  <10 beta_{s,m}.                                   (19)

Here log_+(2z)<=2z and sup_{z>=0} 2z e^{-z/8}=16/e<8, while log 4<2. If p_b=1 then k_b=j and ell_b=0, so (19) remains valid; p_b=0 contributes no weighted term. Multiplying by p_b and summing, sum_b p_b=1 gives from (16)

E[1_{E_{s,m}} D_KL(p_s||B_s)]
  <=beta_{s,m} [10+log(1+A/m)].                       (20)

This estimate is for the full row loss. It does not sum an unweighted constant over all A output symbols.

6. Sum count bands and stationary masses

Let

H=DA/(n+1),       T=(n+1)/(2D),       A/T=2H.

Summing (15) or (20) over s and using (3), the contribution of a dyadic band m is at most

min{m/T,1} min{10+log(1+A/m), 2304 A/m}.              (21)

1. Quantifiers, notation and result

Fix a publicly known integer alphabet size A>=2. Observe X_1,...,X_{n+2} from an arbitrary homogeneous second-order source on [A], with n>=1 observed transitions. Its A^2 transition rows and its initial pair distribution are arbitrary and unknown. Zero probabilities, deterministic rows, reducibility and periodicity are allowed. Put N=n+2, L=N+A, Y_i=(X_{i+1},X_{i+2}) for 0<=i<=n, and

J_s = sum_{i=0}^{n-1} 1{Y_i=s},
C_{s,b} = sum_{i=0}^{n-1} 1{Y_i=s, X_{i+3}=b},
J=J_{Y_n},  theta_b=theta_{Y_n}(b),
B_b=(C_{Y_n,b}+1)/(J+A),                 b in [A].

All logarithms are natural, and KL is forward D(P||Q)=sum_b P_b log(P_b/Q_b), with 0 log 0=0. TV is half the L1 distance. Define

t=t_{A,n}=max{A, log[2 A^3 n(n+1)^2]},
c=c_{A,n}=A^4/(n+1)                    for arbitrary initial pairs,
c=c_{A,n}=A^2/(n+1)                    for stationary initial pairs,
a=a_{A,n}=min{32 A t^2, 48 A t(1+log t)},
U=U_{A,n}=c a[1+log(n+A)] + log(n+A)/(n+1)^2,
V=V_{A,n}=min{log(n+A), U}.

Then the actual full-history add-one estimator satisfies

E D(theta||B) <= V.                                      (1)

For each fixed A this is O_A(log^2(n+2) loglog(n+3)/(n+1)), uniformly over the stated arbitrary-initial class. The stationary subclass has the improved A^2 occupation factor. Constants are intentionally loose; this is not an optimal A-dependence or logarithmic rate.

For each fixed A, one fixed finite parameter set, independent of n and of the source, implements a five-block exact-real causal attention/ReLU model whose final output is an ordinary A-way vocabulary softmax R. With the architecture detailed below,

TV(R,B) <= min{1,(44A+1)/L},
R_b >= 1/[1+(A-1)L] >= 1/(AL)  for every b,
E D(theta||R) <= 2V + 2A(44A+1)^2/L.                    (2)

Also E D(theta||R)<=log[1+(A-1)L], so this deterministic cap can be taken in (2). The first two claims hold pathwise for every word, including n=0/N=2. For n=0, the statistical logarithmic expression in (1) is replaced by the elementary bound log A.

The five blocks have H=A^2+1 allocated heads each, head dimension d_h=4 ceil(sqrt(A+10))^2, and model width d_model=H d_h=O(A^3). Active head counts are (2,0,A^2+1,A,A). Inputs explicitly include the source-independent positional features (1,i,i^2,log i), A BOS tokens and symbol onehots. The model has no LayerNorm and uses ordinary full causal masks. There is no externally supplied count division, log of a probability, position-times-state feature, or horizon-dependent learned coefficient.

2. Terminal occupation, including arbitrary starts

The pair process Y is a homogeneous chain on D=A^2 states. The following argument holds for any D-state homogeneous chain. Fix an integer w>=1 and write

b_m(s)=P_s(number of earlier visits to Y_m before time m < w),
B_m=max_s b_m(s).

For m>=ell, earlier full-history visits dominate the visits within the last ell departures. Conditioning on Y_{m-ell} and using homogeneity gives B_m<=B_ell. This is a monotonicity statement for the worst initial state, not for each fixed b_m(s).

On any deterministic state sequence, at most the first w occurrences of each state have fewer than w earlier visits. Therefore sum_{m=0}^n b_m(s)<=Dw for each initial state s. Since max_s b_m(s)<=sum_s b_m(s),

(n+1)B_n <= sum_{m=0}^n B_m
          <= sum_s sum_{m=0}^n b_m(s) <= D^2 w.

Taking a mixture over the initial state proves

P(J<w) <= min{1, D^2 w/(n+1)}.                          (3)

For stationary initialization the improved D factor follows from a finite-window charging argument. Fix s. Among r=n,...,2n, at most w times have Y_r=s and fewer than w copies of s in the preceding n positions: if w+1 times were marked, the last has all w earlier marked times in its preceding n-window. Each marked event has, by stationarity, the probability of {Y_n=s,J_s<w}. Thus that probability is at most w/(n+1); sum over s. This argument only extends the chain for analysis, not the observations given to the estimator.

In either case, with c defined above, summation by parts gives

E[1/(J+A)]
  = 1/(n+A) + sum_{k=0}^{n-1} P(J<=k)/[(k+A)(k+A+1)]
  <= 1/(n+A) + c sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(k+1)/[(k+A)(k+A+1)]
  <= c[1+log(n+A)].                                     (4)

Here c>=1/(n+A), A>=2, and the summand is at most 1/(k+A). In particular zero visits are included. This reproduces the general occupation interface of the binary v2 proof, with D=A^2; no mixing or stationary minimum is introduced.

3. Categorical row stacks and simultaneous concentration

For each context s in [A]^2, generate n independent categorical variables with probabilities theta_s, independently across rows and independently of the initial pair. At each departure from s, consume its next unused stack entry as the next symbol. This generates exactly the prescribed source: each path constrains exactly its consumed row prefixes and has the initial-pair probability multiplied by its usual transition product.

Let K_{s,b,j} count symbol b in the first j entries of row s. Pathwise C_{s,b}=K_{s,b,J_s}. There is no claim that a stack stays iid after conditioning on J_s, the terminal row or the observed path. Instead, for every deterministic row/symbol/prefix, Bernstein's inequality for its Bernoulli indicator sum yields

|K_{s,b,j}-j theta_s(b)|
   <= sqrt(2j theta_s(b) t) + 2t/3                      (5)

outside probability 2e^(-t). The variance is bounded by j theta_s(b). Union over A^2 rows, A symbols and j=1,...,n yields a single event G_t with

P(G_t^c)<=2 A^3 n e^(-t) <=(n+1)^(-2).                  (6)

Every deterministic-prefix inequality then holds on G_t at the random selected row and random selected count, without conditional independence. Dependence between symbol counts within one categorical stack does not affect the union bound. The j=0 inequality is deterministic.

For completeness the scalar Bernstein bound can be obtained from

E exp(lambda Z) <= exp[lambda^2 E Z^2/(2(1-lambda/3))],
                  0<=lambda<3,

for a centered |Z|<=1 variable, by expanding the exponential, using |E Z^r|<=E Z^2 and r!>=2*3^(r-2), then 1+x<=exp x. Independence, exponential Markov and optimization give tail exp[-u^2/(2(v+u/3))]; setting u=sqrt(2vt)+2t/3 and applying both signs proves (5). No selected random time appears in this calculation.

4. Forward KL at each simultaneous good prefix

Fix any row and deterministic prefix length j>=0, write its true probabilities p_b, counts k_b, and q_b=(k_b+1)/(j+A). On G_t, t>=A>=2 and (5) hold. For each coordinate set z=jp_b, a_b=k_b+1, x_b=(j+A)p_b. The elementary inequality sqrt(2zt)<=z/2+t gives

z<=2k_b+10t/3,
(k_b-z)^2<=4zt+8t^2/9<=8k_b t+128t^2/9.

As |A p_b-1|<=A-1<=t,

(x_b-a_b)^2
  <= 2(k_b-z)^2+2(A-1)^2
  <=16k_b t+31t^2.                                     (7)

The harmless integer constant 31 exceeds 256/9+2. The same calculation covers j=0 directly.

First, KL<=chi-square and (7) imply

D(p||q)
  <=sum_b (p_b-q_b)^2/q_b
  =sum_b (x_b-a_b)^2/[(j+A)a_b]
  <=32 A t^2/(j+A).                                    (8)

For the sharper bound put h(x,a)=x log(x/a)-x+a. Since h(x,a)<=(x-a)^2/a, if a_b>=t, (7) gives h(x_b,a_b)<=47t. If a_b<t, then

x_b<=2a_b+10t/3+(A-2)<7t,

and a_b>=1 yields

h(x_b,a_b)<=x_b log_+ x_b+a_b
            <=7t log(7t)+t
            <=15t+7t log t
            <=48t(1+log t).                            (9)

The large-a case obeys the same final bound. Both vectors x and a have total mass j+A, so the exact identity

(j+A)D(p||q)=sum_b h(x_b,a_b)

gives

D(p||q)<=48 A t(1+log t)/(j+A).                         (10)

On every path, including the complement of G_t, q_b>=1/(j+A), hence D(p||q)<=log(j+A)<=log(n+A). Combine (8) and (10), then use (4) without conditioning and (6):

E D(theta||B)
  <=a E[1/(J+A)] + log(n+A)P(G_t^c)
  <=U.

The deterministic cap proves (1). The A in max{A,...} is important: blindly replacing the binary numerator by A symbols without controlling the smoothing displacement A p_b-1 would not justify the stated constants.

6. Block 4: A bounded-error rescaling heads

For each symbol b use one head

Q_b=kappa(p_b+1/2+z,-z), K_j=(j,j^2), V_j=j.

At terminal, scores differ by a query-only constant from -(j-mu_b)^2/L, with

mu_b=1/2+L(1/4+p_b/2).

The head output y_b is its discrete-Gaussian mean on 1,...,L. The inherited interior mean bound gives |y_b-mu_b|<10. Here is its analytical justification. For f(x)=(x-mu)e^(-(x-mu)^2/L), total variation on the real line equals 4 sqrt(L/(2e)). Unit-cell quadrature therefore has error at most 2 sqrt(L/(2e)). Both cell-boundary distances from mu to the ends of [1/2,L+1/2] are at least L/4, so the integral has absolute value at most (L/2)e^(-L/16). There are at least floor(sqrt L)>=sqrt L/2 supported integers within distance sqrt L of mu, giving normalizer at least sqrt L/(2e). Consequently

|y_b-mu_b|<=2sqrt(2e)+e sqrt L e^(-L/16)
             <=2sqrt(2e)+sqrt(8e)<10.

The last maximum follows analytically at L=8. Here L>=4. A 2A-unit FFN writes

x_b=ReLU(2y_b-i/2-1)-ReLU(2y_b-3i/2-1).

At terminal this is projection of 2y_b-L/2-1 onto [0,L]. Projection cannot increase the distance to Lp_b, so

0<=x_b<=L, |x_b-Lp_b|<=20.                             (12)

The x_b need not sum to L. The subsequent normalization proof deliberately does not assume that they do.

7. Block 5: A narrow-Gaussian logits and ordinary softmax

For each b use a head

Q_b=kappa(2x_b,-1), K_j=(j,j^2), V_j=log j.

Its scores differ from -(j-x_b)^2 by a query-only constant. The head output ell_b is the expectation of log J under these weights on 1,...,L. Block 5's FFN is zero. The final fixed linear readout supplies logits ell_1,...,ell_A to ordinary vocabulary softmax:

G_b=exp(ell_b), S=sum_b G_b, R_b=G_b/S.

For any center 0<=mu<=L, the constant-variance geometric-mean lemma gives

|exp(E log J)-mu|<=24, 1<=exp(E log J)<=L.              (13)

For completeness, a supported integer lies within distance one of mu, so the weight normalizer is at least e^(-1). The unimodal lattice bound sum_{j in Z}e^(-(j-mu)^2/2)<=1+sqrt(2pi), together with |s|e^(-s^2)<=e^(-s^2/2), implies E|J-mu|<=e(1+sqrt(2pi))<10. Jensen gives exp(E log J)<=mu+10. For mu<=2 this and the lower bound 1 suffice. For mu>2, the negative log deficit on mu/2<=J<mu is at most 20/mu. The remainder is at most

e e^(-mu^2/4) sum_{1<=j<mu/2}log(mu/j)
  <= e mu e^(-mu^2/4)<=4/mu.

The sum is bounded by its decreasing-function integral from 0 to mu/2, which is less than mu. Hence E log(J/mu)>=-24/mu, giving exp(E log J)>=mu exp(-24/mu)>=mu-24. This proves (13).

Combining (12)-(13), |G_b-Lp_b|<=44 for every b. Normalization is handled without a large-L condition:

||R-p||_1
  <= ||G/S-G/L||_1 + ||G/L-p||_1
  = |1-S/L| + sum_b |G_b/L-p_b|
  <= 2 sum_b |G_b/L-p_b| <=88A/L.

Thus TV(R,p)<=44A/L. Also G_b>=1 and each other G_a<=L give the exact support floor

R_b>=1/[1+(A-1)L].                                     (14)

For N>=2, N^2e^(-N)<=4e^(-2) and Ne^(-N)<=2e^(-2), so

L Ne^(-N)/A
  = (N^2/A+N)e^(-N)
  <=(4/A+2)e^(-2)<=4/e^2<1.

Together with (11), this proves the pathwise approximation in (2). No complement-coordinate trick specific to binary output is used.

8. Multiclass KL transfer

For distributions T,P,Q with P,Q strictly positive, the entropy variational inequality gives

D(T||Q)<=2D(T||P)+log sum_b P_b^2/Q_b
        <=2D(T||P)+sum_b (P_b-Q_b)^2/Q_b.              (15)

One proof is to use E_T f<=D(T||P)+log E_P exp f with f=log(P/Q), then sum P^2/Q=1+sum(P-Q)^2/Q and log(1+u)<=u. True probabilities T_b may be zero.

Apply this pointwise with P=B,Q=R. Their positivity holds at every history, including zero visits. For a zero-sum vector delta=B-R, its positive and negative parts each sum to TV(B,R), hence sum_b delta_b^2<=2TV(B,R)^2. By (14),

sum_b (B_b-R_b)^2/R_b
  <=AL sum_b (B_b-R_b)^2
  <=2AL TV(B,R)^2
  <=2A(44A+1)^2/L.                                    (16)

Taking expectation in (15) and using (1) proves (2). An additive probability approximation by itself would not justify this step; the explicit floor (14) is essential. No terminal-state independence or conversion of a TV risk through reverse Pinsker is asserted.

  • There are exactly five residual attention/ReLU blocks with H=A^2+1 allocated heads per block and d_h=4ceil(sqrt(A+10))^2 per head, as in the prior finite-A model. Active heads by block are (2,0,A^2+1,A,A), totaling A^2+2A+3. Their values have dimension at most A<=d_h.
  • Reserve disjoint slots for four position features; A token coordinates; 2A raw copies; 2A snapped copies; three flags; A^2 candidate and A^2 query coordinates; A^3 row-head outputs; A selected probabilities; scalar z; A y coordinates; A x coordinates; A final logits.   • Active FFN hidden widths are at most (4A+5,2A^2,A^3,2A,0). A common width max{4A+5,2A^2,A^3,2A} suffices. In particular, saying the maximum is always A^3 would be inaccurate at A=2. The asymptotic FFN width remains O(A^3).
  • All masks permit exactly j<=i. Every key/value is from the observed prefix; the terminal symbol is a legal key, not the unobserved next symbol. BOS smoothing and early invalid candidates are explicitly accounted for.
  • Dense parameter storage has O(A^6) entries. With the displayed integer kappa and fixed rational coefficients, coefficient descriptions use O(log(A+2)) bits. This statement concerns fixed weights, not exact-real log positions or evaluated softmax values.
  • Positions (1,i,i^2,log i) are paid source-independent inputs. Integer position i and its square require O(log L) bits per exact integer, or paid generation; exact log i is a real-number assumption. Intermediate x and y grow as O(L); positional magnitudes reach O(L^2); raw copy scores reach O(L^3), row matching gaps are N, rescaling scores are O_A(L), and narrow-head scores reach O(L^2). Charging head dimensions/vector aggregation and dense projections/FFNs gives a conservative O(A^3 L^2+A^6 L) exact-real arithmetic-operation bound, in addition to positional generation and the chosen cost of exp/log evaluation. Row-streamed activations and dense weights use O(A^3 L+A^6) real slots; materializing every attention matrix adds O(A^2 L^2) slots.
  • No finite-bit bound is proved for the new two-block output. The prior four-block specialized backend does not automatically control rescaling error multiplied by L, narrow-Gaussian score error, or final probability support under output rounding. Generic IEEE arithmetic, constant word length, fixed exponent range, training attainability and optimal compute are not certified.

10. Prior results, scope and remaining gaps

Han, Jana and Wu, Optimal prediction of Markov chains with and without spectral gap, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 69(6), 2023, pp. 3920-3959, DOI 10.1109/TIT.2023.3239508, treat stationary higher-order same-trajectory prediction. Their fixed-order finite-alphabet minimax theorem includes the A^3 complexity for order two in its stated regime. Their Remark 1 distinguishes full-history add-one from suffix-averaged add-one; their higher-order upper proof uses the stationary redundancy-to-prediction reduction. Therefore those optimal stationary minimax guarantees are not silently assigned to the full-history predictor implemented here, or to arbitrary initialization. Sources: published article, author preprint, Sections 5 and 6. The locally available published PDF was inspected; a fresh fetch of the published URL failed, while the author preprint was accessible.

For each fixed A, (1)-(2) establish a rigorous estimator-specific O_A(log^2 n loglog n/n) upper and an O_A(1/n) output-approximation penalty. They do not establish the optimal O_A(log n/n) rate for full-history add-one. Uniform O(1/n) over the full stationary second-order class is ruled out already by its binary subfamily, so that is not a target claimed here.

The displayed inequalities are valid for every finite A,n, but the fixed-A asymptotic notation must not be read uniformly over growing A. In the regime A<=O(log(n+2)), the loose arbitrary-start statistical factor is of order A^5 times logarithmic factors divided by n, and the stationary version has A^3 instead. Outside that regime t>=A explicitly records the larger smoothing cost. No optimal alphabet-growth threshold follows.

The prior finite-A construction already supplies A^2 row heads, categorical BOS smoothing, exact terminal selection and A-way broad-Gaussian readout. This note keeps its first three blocks and replaces the readout using the binary narrow-Gaussian rescaling mechanism applied independently to A coordinates, followed by a multiclass normalization and support argument. Conditional n-gram, position-copy and smoothed-attention mechanisms have prior literature, including the sources cited in the archived finite-A note. No novelty, exhaustive prior-art clearance, Transformer-wide lower bound or training claim is made.

1. Statistical theorem

The theorem is valid for every finite integer A>=2 and n>=1, with all four quantities t,c,a,U exactly as defined in Section 1. The separate n=0 statement is valid. The finite-parameter inequalities do not require A to be fixed as n varies, but their displayed asymptotic O_A rate is correctly restricted to fixed A.

Occupation and reciprocal moment

The pair process has D=A^2 states. The suffix argument proves monotonicity of the maximum over starts, not of each initial-state probability. Summing pathwise first-w flags separately over all D initial states gives D^2 w, and therefore the arbitrary-start constant A^4/(n+1). The stationary finite-window argument gives D rather than D^2, hence A^2/(n+1). Both arguments allow zero transition probabilities, reducibility, periodicity, and arbitrary off-support starts when stationarity is not imposed.

The summation-by-parts identity in Section 2 includes J=0. Its harmonic estimate is valid for A>=2. One can also directly bound its summand by 1/(k+A+1), obtaining a harmonic sum at most log((n+A)/A); the looser displayed c[1+log(n+A)] follows. No unprovided minimum stationary mass, mixing rate, or independent query is used.

Simultaneous categorical stacks

There are A^2 row stacks, A indicator coordinates per stack, and n deterministic positive prefix lengths. A two-sided Bernstein bound costs 2e^-t per coordinate-prefix event, giving precisely 2A^3 n e^-t. The choice t>=log[2A^3 n(n+1)^2] makes the failure probability at most (n+1)^-2.

The stack construction generates the original source law and permits pathwise evaluation at its actual terminal row and data-dependent count. No assertion is made that counts remain binomial after conditioning on J, on the terminal row, or on a chosen path. Correlation among coordinates of one categorical stack is harmless because the proof uses a union bound, not independence among those coordinates.

All-A smoothing displacement and exact KL

For a symbol coordinate, z=jp and e=(j+A)p-(k+1) satisfy

z<=2k+10t/3,
(k-z)^2<=8kt+128t^2/9,
e^2<=16kt+31t^2.

The last step uses |Ap-1|<=A-1<=t. This is why t>=A is substantive, particularly for growing A. The constants 31, 32, and 48 all check algebraically.

4. Explicit max-shifted integer exponential

All scores used below are exact H-grid dyadics. For each row, compute its maximum exactly on its actual causal support. A nonnegative shifted gap is d=X/H with integer X>=0. The following deterministic integer routine approximates exp(-d):

  • If X>bH, return 0.
  • Otherwise set Z_0=F-X. For r=0,...,b-1 set Z_{r+1}=floor(Z_r^2/F).
  • Return w_tilde=floor(Z_b/H)/H.

The zero-gap output is exactly one. All outputs lie in [0,1]. Since H>=2b, the retained case has d/H<=1/2. The elementary inequality

-u-u^2 <= log(1-u) <= -u,  0<=u<=1/2,

shows that the difference between exp(-d) and (1-d/H)^H is at most d^2/H<=b^2/H. Each square-and-floor contributes at most 1/F absolute error, and x->x^2 is 2-Lipschitz on [0,1]. After b steps the total such error is less than 2^b/F=1/H. The final conversion adds less than 1/H. In the truncated case, exp(-d)<exp(-b)<2^(-b)=1/H. Hence uniformly

|w_tilde-exp(-d)| <= epsilon_w := (b^2+2)/H.      (P6)

No exponential of a positive O(L^2) or O(L^3) argument is formed. A row always has a retained, exact weight-one maximizer, so its numerical denominator is at least one.

For m supported keys, let the returned weights be Z_j/H, and S=sum_j Z_j. Compute a_j=floor(H Z_j/S), R=H-sum_j a_j, and add one to any deterministically chosen R distinct coordinates, for example the first R supported keys. Here 0<=R<m. Dividing the resulting integers by H produces an exact nonnegative simplex alpha_tilde, each coordinate within 1/H of the exact normalization of the numerical weights. Largest-remainder sorting is unnecessary for this error bound. Normalization of the weight errors costs TV at most m epsilon_w; this follows from the exact weight sum being at least one and the standard two-term normalization identity. Thus

TV(alpha_tilde, softmax(scores))
   <= m epsilon_w + m/(2H)
   <= m epsilon_g,
epsilon_g := (b^2+3)/H.                           (P7)

This explicitly charges m<=i<=L for attention and m=A for the final vocabulary softmax. It does not replace full causal support by a sparse or future-looking set.

5. Constructed log-position routine

Exact log i is not supplied to the numerical evaluator. Here is a source-independent integer construction. For j>=1 write j=2^k u, with integer k=floor(log2 j) and 1<=u<2; finding k uses the integer bit length. Put r=(j-2^k)/(j+2^k), so 0<=r<1/3. For log 2 use r=1/3. The identity

log u = 2 sum_{t>=0} r^(2t+1)/(2t+1)

follows by integrating the geometric series for 2/(1-r^2). Use T=b terms, F-grid truncation r_tilde=floor(Fr)/F, and q_tilde=floor(F r_tilde^2)/F. Form c_t=floor(F/(2t+1))/F. Evaluate by Horner's rule from h_T=0:

h_t = c_t + floor(F q_tilde h_{t+1})/F,
   t=T-1,...,0,
ell_tilde(r) = 2 floor(F r_tilde h_0)/F.

All these expressions are integer products and divisions on F-grid representations. The exact finite Horner values are at most 9/8. We have |q_tilde-r^2|<2/F; the Horner error recurrence is bounded by

e_t <= (17/4)/F + e_{t+1}/9,

so e_t<5/F. The final multiplication and factor two cost less than 8/F in total. The omitted tail is at most 3^(-2b)<H^(-3). Therefore

|ell_tilde(r)-log u| < 8/F+H^(-3) < 9/F.

The same bound holds for log 2. Compute k ell_tilde(1/3)+ell_tilde(r), and round to the nearest H-grid point to obtain lambda_j. Since k<=s and H>=18(s+1),

|lambda_j-log j| <= 9(s+1)/H^2 + 1/(2H) <= 1/H.  (P8)

8. Exact failure modes that a generic finite-bit claim must exclude

  1. Final zero destroys forward KL. For a constant-symbol word, a different symbol has B_b=1/(n+A), and the reference's exact readout gives R_b=O_A(1/L). Fixed-grid nearest rounding eventually makes that coordinate zero. Choose an iid source giving that symbol strictly positive true probability: the constant-word event still has positive probability, so conditional KL on that event and expected KL are infinite. Small TV error does not repair this.

  2. Coarse rounding of z can destroy even TV. If a fixed fractional grid floors 1/L to zero, block 4 uses score (p_b+1/2)j. Its mean is at least L-1/(exp(1/2)-1)>L-2. For L>=10, the clipping FFN therefore sets every x_b=L, so all block-5 logits are equal and final softmax is uniform. Constant-symbol histories have B approaching a point mass, hence TV tends to 1-1/A rather than O_A(1/L). This failure occurs even with every remaining computation exact.

  3. Large raw logits cannot be ignored. Unshifted exponentiation of raw copy and narrow-head logits encounters respectively O(L^3) and O(L^2) positive magnitudes. An unspecified finite exponent range cannot support an all-length theorem. Max shifting, an exact weight-one anchor, and explicit tail control avoid this; simply saying “softmax is stable” does not.

  4. The port exponents are sufficient, not sharp. In particular p accuracy O(L^-2) and z accuracy O(L^-3) were chosen to make a global sup-score proof short. Local Gaussian sensitivity may permit weaker ports. This note proves an O(log(AL))-bit construction, not a matching necessity theorem for every numerical representation.

Under a common positive dyadic output grid 2^(-b), logarithmically growing fractional precision is necessary for the stated all-history O_A(1/L) approximation plus positive support: the constant-word rare coordinate must be at most C_A/L yet, if positive, is at least 2^(-b), forcing b>=log2 L-O_A(1). This is a statement about that encoding class, not every floating/exponent or symbolic encoding.

3. Primary-source locations and hypotheses

3.1 Model and loss

Published Section V-A, pp. 3941-3942, defines a homogeneous order-m process by a fixed stochastic array with A^m rows and A columns. Its initial law is a distribution on the first m symbols. Stationarity is a condition on this initial law and the fixed transition array.

Published Eq. (103), p. 3942, defines the minimax risk using the forward KL loss at the actual terminal m-symbol context:

R_{A,N,m}
  = inf_{Mhat} sup_M
      E[D_KL(M(. | X_{N-m+1},...,X_N)
               || Mhat(. | X_{N-m+1},...,X_N))].

The observed trajectory has N symbols and is started from a stationary distribution. The estimator is a function of the complete observed trajectory, not merely its final context. Writing it as a collection of estimated rows does not restrict that dependence.

This is the desired terminal expected forward KL. It is neither cumulative prediction loss nor an average over an independently sampled test context. The preprint counterpart is Section 5.1, Eq. (95).

3.2 Higher-order theorem

Published Theorem 22, p. 3942, states: for each m >= 2, there exists C_m > 0 such that, for

2 <= A <= N^(1/(m+1)) / C_m,

A^(m+1)/(C_m N) * log(N/A^(m+1))
  <= R_{A,N,m}
  <= C_m A^(m+1)/N * log(N/A^(m+1)).

The lower bound continues to hold when the sources are required to be reversible. C_m depends only on m, not on A or N. Thus at m=2 the constant C_2 is absolute.

No positive lower bound on transition probabilities, stationary masses, or spectral gap is imposed for this theorem. Its domain is a restriction on the alphabet size relative to sample length. The reversibility here is reversibility of the symbol process; it should not be confused with ordinary reversibility of the overlapping-pair chain.

3.3 Explicit binary theorem

Published Section V-C1, pp. 3944-3945, Theorem 24 states

R_{2,N,2} = Theta(log N/N).

The published proof uses a one-parameter binary second-order family, Eq. (111), with rows ordered 11,21,12,22:

M_p(. | 11) = (1-1/N, 1/N),
M_p(. | 21) = (1/N, 1-1/N),
M_p(. | 12) = (1-p, p),
M_p(. | 22) = (p, 1-p),
0 <= p <= 1.

For each p, the stationary pair law is uniform on {11,12,21,22}. The proof places a uniform prior on p and extracts a harmonic-sum lower bound from selected trajectories; its terminal-risk conclusion is Eq. (117), p. 3945. Thus the binary result is established within the required structured second-order family and with a common stationary initial law.

The preprint counterpart is Section 5.3.1, Theorem 24. The general-alphabet construction in published Section V-C2 / Theorem 26 is explicitly the A >= 3 case and is not needed to justify A=2.

4. Exact indexing and fixed-A threshold

The candidate observes X_1,...,X_{n+2}, with n observed transitions and terminal pair

Y_n = (X_{n+1},X_{n+2}).

HJW use N for the number of observed symbols. The exact substitution is therefore

N = n+2,  m = 2,  k = A.

Then their final context is the candidate's Y_n, and their next symbol is X_{n+3}. Hence, writing R_A^stat(n) for the candidate-indexed stationary minimax risk,

R_A^stat(n)
  >= A^3/[C_2(n+2)] * log[(n+2)/A^3]

whenever

n+2 >= (C_2 A)^3.

For every fixed A, this gives Omega_A(log n/n) as n tends to infinity. For an explicit sufficient large-n comparison, additionally require n+2 >= A^6. Then

log[(n+2)/A^3] >= (1/2) log(n+2).

The absolute constant C_2 is not numerically specified by the theorem statement. No numerical value or all-n guarantee is invented here. The displayed logarithm and domain must be retained in any finite-sample or growing-alphabet restatement.

5. Why a terminal predictor is covered

An arbitrary prediction rule q(X_1,...,X_N) in the A-symbol probability simplex can be represented as a row-valued estimator: put q in the realized terminal row and assign arbitrary probability vectors to the other rows. HJW's infimum over row-valued estimators therefore covers every history-dependent probability-vector predictor used here.

If a predictor has internal randomness, conditional averaging of its output cannot increase forward KL, by convexity in the predicted distribution. Randomization therefore does not evade the minimax lower bound.

In particular, the lower bound applies to the full-history add-one output, and also to a normalized architecture output if a separately verified upper bound is available for that output.

6. Extension to arbitrary initial-pair distributions

Let R_A^all(n) be the minimax risk when both the kernel and the initial-pair distribution are arbitrary. The stationary parameter set is a subset of this larger parameter set. Thus

R_A^all(n) >= R_A^stat(n).

Let B be the full-history add-one predictor. The candidate upper bound gives

sup_{all initial laws and kernels} E D_KL(p_{Y_n} || B)
  <= U_{A,n},

where U_{A,n} = O_A(log(n+2)/(n+1)). Therefore

R_A^stat(n)
  <= R_A^all(n)
  <= sup_all E D_KL(p_{Y_n} || B)
  <= U_{A,n}.

Furthermore,

R_A^stat(n) <= sup_stationary E D_KL(p_{Y_n} || B).

Together these inequalities prove the claimed fixed-A minimax-rate match for both classes, conditional on the upper-bound theorem. The stationary lower bound is sufficient because a worst case in a smaller class is still a valid worst case in the larger class.

This does not imply a lower bound for every prescribed initial distribution, every deterministic starting pair, or every fixed transition kernel. The supremum is taken separately at each sample length, and the hard source may depend on that length, as the displayed binary construction does.

7. Correct direction of alphabet embedding

A lower bound over a larger alphabet does not imply a lower bound for the binary subclass. The valid monotonicity goes in the other direction.

Embed any stationary binary second-order source in [A] by setting probabilities of symbols 3,...,A to zero and supporting the initial pair on {1,2}^2. Define unused rows arbitrarily. This is allowed in the candidate class, which permits zero probabilities and reducibility.

For any A-symbol prediction q and binary-supported true row p, set

z = q_1+q_2,
q' = (q_1/z,q_2/z)  if z>0.

For z>0,

D_KL(p || q) = D_KL(p || q') - log z
            >= D_KL(p || q').

If z=0, the A-symbol loss is infinite, so the inequality is harmless after choosing any binary q'. This pointwise reduction maps every A-symbol estimator to a binary estimator. Taking the supremum over binary sources and then the infimum over estimators yields

R_{A,N,2} >= R_{2,N,2}.

Thus Theorem 24 alone gives a matching order in N for every fixed A>=2. This alternative argument does not recover the sharp A^3 factor of Theorem 22, but it is sufficient for fixed-A optimality in n.

An unrelated shortcut is also invalid: applying a lower bound for arbitrary ordinary A^2-state chains does not automatically lower-bound the structured pair chains coming from an A-symbol second-order source. HJW's actual higher-order theorems avoid that issue.

J_s=sum_{i=0}^{n-1}1{Y_i=s},

K_n=1+floor(log_2 n),
W_{A,n}=A^4/(n+1) [log A+1536 A K_n],

2. A kernel-uniform, fixed-target occupation bound

For every homogeneous Markov chain on D states, every initial distribution mu, every target state s, and integer w>=1,

P_mu(Y_n=s,J_s<w) <= D w/(n+1).                       (2)

For clarity, the bound is uniform over the transition kernel; this is crucial when it is used for an altered kernel below.

Proof: For a chain started at z define

b_m^s(z)=P_z(Y_m=s, sum_{i=0}^{m-1}1{Y_i=s}<w),
B_m^s=max_z b_m^s(z).

If m>=ell, fewer than w earlier copies in the full path implies fewer than w copies in its last ell departures. Conditioning at time m-ell gives B_m^s<=B_ell^s. On each deterministic path only the first w occurrences of s satisfy the flag, so sum_{m=0}^n b_m^s(z)<=w. Therefore

(n+1)B_n^s <=sum_{m=0}^n B_m^s
            <=sum_z sum_{m=0}^n b_m^s(z)<=Dw.

Mixture over the starting state proves (2). Put r=D/(n+1). In particular, for any integer m>=1,

P_mu(Y_n=s, m<=J_s<2m)<=2rm.                          (3)

No stationarity, irreducibility, reversibility or mixing assumption is used.

3. Change one row, rather than conditioning a stack

Fix s,b and p=p_s(b) in (0,1). For any q in (0,1), define an alternative homogeneous second-order source Q with the same initial pair distribution and all rows unchanged except s. In row s let

q_s(b)=q,
q_s(a)=p_s(a)(1-q)/(1-p), a!=b.                       (4)

Thus the alternative remains in the same D-state pair-chain class. Zero probabilities among the other symbols remain zero. The original law P and Q have the same path support. For any observed path of positive probability, put j=J_s and k=C_{s,b}. The exact path-probability ratio is

P(path)/Q(path)
   =(p/q)^k [(1-p)/(1-q)]^(j-k).                     (5)

The initial factor cancels, all unmodified rows cancel, and each departure from s contributes one of the two displayed factors. There is no extra factor from Y_n: the probability of the observed path already includes the terminal state and all n observed transitions.

Let d(q||p) denote binary forward KL. If q>p and k/j>=q, or q<p and k/j<=q, then

log[P(path)/Q(path)]
   =-j d(q||p)
     -(k-jq)log[q(1-p)/(p(1-q))]
   <=-j d(q||p).                                    (6)

Consequently, for E_m={Y_n=s,m<=J_s<2m},

P(E_m, C_{s,b}/J_s>=q)
   <=2rm exp[-m d(q||p)], q>p,
P(E_m, C_{s,b}/J_s<=q)
   <=2rm exp[-m d(q||p)], q<p.                       (7)

To obtain (7), sum (6) over the indicated paths and apply (3) under the altered law Q, not under P. The initial distribution remains the same but is not assumed stationary for Q. This use of the arbitrary-start bound is intentional.

4. A joint occupation-times-deviation tail

For t>=1 let

delta=sqrt(2pt/m)+2t/(3m).

For either q=p+delta or q=p-delta lying in (0,1), the Bernstein binary-KL bound gives

d(q||p)>=delta^2/[2(p+delta/3)]>=t/m.                 (8)

Here is a direct justification of the first inequality. For Z a centered Bernoulli(p) variable, |Z|<=1 and E Z^2<=p. Expansion of the exponential gives, for either sign and 0<=lambda<3,

log E exp(+-lambda Z)
   <=p lambda^2/[2(1-lambda/3)].

Use the variational formula for binary KL and take lambda=delta/(p+delta/3)<3. This gives the first inequality in (8). For the second, put v=t/m and expand delta=sqrt(2pv)+2v/3; then delta^2>=2v(p+delta/3).

On m<=J_s<2m,

J_s delta <=sqrt(4J_s p t)+4t/3.

Thus (7)-(8), with a union over the two directions, give

P(E_m, |C_{s,b}-J_s p|>sqrt(4J_s p t)+4t/3)
   <=4rm exp(-t).                                   (9)

If p+delta>=1, the corresponding strict upper-tail event is impossible; similarly if p-delta<=0 for the lower tail. Such directions do not require an endpoint tilt. If p=0 or p=1, the row count is deterministic given J_s, so the deviation event is empty and (9) holds directly.

Equation (9) does not assert that C_{s,b} is binomial after conditioning on J_s or on the terminal state. It obtains the product factor by a change of the actual transition kernel.
