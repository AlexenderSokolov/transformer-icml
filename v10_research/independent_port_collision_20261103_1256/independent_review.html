Independent review: restricted q/r/h port collision

2026-11-03. Verdict: the deterministic two-history collision and its restricted-port fidelity lower bound are algebraically valid for the claimed parameters. This is not an architecture-wide impossibility, a true-parameter risk lower bound, or a novelty certification. Source comparisons below are restricted to the cited primary passages; no comprehensive priority search is claimed.

Block and filler accounting

Initialize with 00. Appending A=010100 gives the word 00010100; appending B=100100 gives 00100100. In each case the six departures are indexed by positions i=3,...,8, the terminal pair is 00, and row occupancies (00,01,10,11) are (2,2,2,0). Both have two successor ones. For A, successor-one counts by row are (1,0,1,0); for B, (2,0,0,0). Thus replacing an A by B moves one successor-one count from row 10 to row 00 without changing any of the four row counts or the total number of ones.

For k≥0, append F=10(10)^k0, of length 3+2k. Starting from 00, it ends at 00 and contributes row occupancies (1,k+1,k+1,0) and successor-one counts (1,0,k,0). Every block begins at 00, so counts concatenate without overlaps or omissions. For n≥1 and 1≤d≤n, all A versus d B plus n−d A gives the same actual terminal query 00 and m=6n+3+2k departures, N=m+2 symbols, C_all=2n+k+1, J00=2n+1, J01=J10=2n+k+1, J11=0. The row-00 successor counts are C_a=n+1 and C_b=n+d+1. No X_(N+1) is used. Block ordering is immaterial to these counts since each returns to 00.

Exact collision and quantified loss

For γ>0, q=(C_all+γC)/(m+γJ), r=C_all/m and h=J/m. These histories have identical r,h, m, γ and query, but q_b−q_a=γd/(m+γJ). Presenting q′=(q_a+q_b)/2 for both has worst absolute q error e*=γd/[2(m+γJ)] and leaves all other named ports exact. q′∈[0,1]. Therefore for every error budget e≥e* the observed q′,r,h and public data collide. In fact the vector of all four row fractions h_a also collides, not just the selected h; this strengthening does not cover any other residual or path-dependent coordinates.

The exact same-path add-one targets differ by ΔB=d/(J+2). Every decoder depending only on the collided ports and public data gives the same output distribution on both histories. The triangle inequality yields max(|p−B_a|,|p−B_b|)≥ΔB/2 for a deterministic decoder, and max(E|P−B_a|,E|P−B_b|)≥ΔB/2 for arbitrary source-independent random bits. It is not a claim that a fixed one of the two histories fails for every seed. The midpoint predictor attains ΔB/2 on this pair, so the pairwise bound is tight. Both words have positive probability under the same fair i.i.d. binary source, but that fact does not turn a same-prefix fidelity lower bound into a true-θ risk lower bound; for that source the constant prediction 1/2 has zero risk.

For d=n, n→∞, k/n→∞ and fixed γ>0, h→0, J→∞, e*~γh/4 and ΔB/2→1/4. The dependence on γ is explicit: letting γ vary changes the threshold, and large γ or k comparable to n does not justify an unqualified small-e asymptotic. The lower bound only concerns adversarial absolute port errors; it does not show ordinary floating-point rounding attains the adversarial midpoint, nor does it limit a decoder with other history-bearing residual channels.

Primary antecedents and accurate scope

Rajaraman et al., arXiv v1 Theorems 4.4 and 4.5, already state conditional k-gram attention-only constructions: respectively two layers with k heads and dimension (k+2)S+k+1, and ⌈log₂(k+1)⌉ layers with one head per layer, relative position encodings and dimension at most 2k(S+1)+S. At order k=2 and alphabet S=2 these give two layers, with respectively two first-layer heads and dimension 11, or one head per layer and dimension bound 14. The NeurIPS 2024 proceedings version calls these Theorems 2 and 3. The paper's arXiv Remark 4.2 / proceedings Remark 1 explicitly claims O(log T)-bit precision with additive O(1/T) approximation. Its conditional empirical k-gram is unsmoothed and has a zero denominator if there is no historical matching context; the cited proof handles positive denominators and derives ideal copying/matching via limiting temperature. These are not the same statement as exact finite-logit binary-match inversion plus add-one on empty rows, and the precision remark is not this review's adversarial q-port model. Both histories here have J00>0, so the collision itself is not a zero-count example.

D’Angelo et al., Proposition 3.1, already gives a mask-count interpolation for a two-layer disentangled relative-position architecture. At order two it assigns scores by position-wise matching, so one-position matches receive intermediate weights, unlike the candidate's single score for full match versus every nonmatch. The stated formula treats copying ideally: its proof describes first-layer hard copying as a limit. Corollary 4.1 obtains symmetric add-α using BOS and β→∞, with κ=2β+log(α|V|) when the order is 2; this is not a finite-β no-BOS exact add-one identity. Appendix D.3 analyzes a risk-optimal β approximation using a Taylor/mean-substitution heuristic and, for a closed higher-order expression, equal-marginal and independence approximations. Those are approximation assumptions, not a universal adversarial port-precision theorem.

The same words give a precise further comparison with Proposition 3.1's no-BOS positionwise weights. With equal coordinate scores β>0, write t=e^β. The row weights are t²,t,t,1; the identical denominator is W=t²J00+t(J01+J10)+J11. The B blocks move d successor ones from row 10 to row 00, so q_b−q_a=d(t²−t)/W. A common midpoint costs e_D=d(t²−t)/(2W), while all four exact row-occupancy fractions and the terminal query still coincide, and the add-one fidelity lower bound is unchanged. This is a direct algebraic specialization under ideal copies and the same restricted information interface, not a theorem claimed by that paper and not a general-network lower bound.

Exact finite diagnostic and reproducibility

Two deterministic scripts and all generated case records are retained in this folder. The first checks n=1,...,8, k=0,...,8, all 1≤d≤n, and γ∈{1/10,1/2,1,3,10}; the second checks the corresponding positionwise-score specialization with t=1+γ. Each contains 1,620 exact rational records, with no RNG. These finite checks supplement, not replace, the symbolic argument.
