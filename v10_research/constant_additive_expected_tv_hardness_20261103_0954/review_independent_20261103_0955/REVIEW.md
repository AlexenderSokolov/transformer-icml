## Imported theorem and normalization

Previously inspected [Håstad, *Some Optimal Inapproximability Results* (JACM 2001), Theorem 6.5 and Definitions 2.2–2.4](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/max3satl.pdf): the theorem distinguishes satisfiable E3-CNF from (7/8+ε)-satisfiable formulas and includes perfect completeness; the definitions use optimal satisfied-clause fraction and exactly three literal occurrences. Using ε=1/16 gives alpha=15/16. Its equality-defined soundness subclass is included in the at-most-alpha promise. I checked the primary theorem statement, not a new proof of the PCP result or a comprehensive priority search. Clause-by-clause deletion of duplicate same-sign literals and replacement of any complementary pair by an always-true feature preserves every clause's truth value. Keeping every original clause slot, including duplicate clauses and tautologies, preserves m and all assignment satisfaction fractions; no dummy clause changes the gap.

## Exact game, model and expected optimum

1. M is the least power of two ≥max(n²,m²,128), so n,m≤sqrt M and k=n≤sqrt M. P is the least power of two ≥m, so m≤P<2m for every m≥1; in particular m=1 gives m/P=1, not 1/2. Both M,P and the explicit O(Mm) model representation are polynomial in formula size. Zero-score uniform attention and the shared pre-head affine/ReLU identity ReLU(s+x−1)−ReLU(−s+x−1)=sx for s∈{−1,0,1}, x∈{0,1}, recover each normalized clause's literal count without a free token-position product. A tautology uses zero position coefficients and C_j=1; duplicate clauses occupy duplicate model outputs.
2. With S=m−V the number of satisfied original clauses, p=1/M² and q(z)=p+S(z)/(2P), every word satisfies p≤q≤p+1/2≤9/16. The inequality q≥p, including the equality case S=0, gives Bernoulli TV=|q−p|=S/(2P); no monotonicity of the earlier B(h)-surrogate CE is needed. As M,P are powers of two, source, pooling scale and output are exact dyadic with O(log M) scalar widths, provided intermediate widths, explicit masks, position table and bit/evaluation costs are paid. The statement does not transfer to actual softmax, tanh, sigmoid or BLAS without separate analysis.
3. Every assignment can be imposed from every history by replacing only its first n editable bits. This fixed map flips at most k bits, keeps the protected pair and dummy bits, and needs no hidden current theta or future label. Any edited word encodes some assignment; hence no randomized or deterministic legal kernel can beat S*=max_x S(x), and a hardcoded maximizing assignment reaches it on all histories. For every initial pair law mu, R*=S*/(2P). All four source transition rows are Bernoulli(p), and P_theta²(u,v)=Pi(v) for all u,v, so beta=1. A hardcoded maximizer is an existential witness; the reduction does not claim finding it is free.

## Constant-additive thresholds and policy extraction

Let C=m/(2P)>1/4. Yes R*=C; no R*≤(15/16)C. Their gap C/16>1/64. The rational midpoint tau=(31/32)C is at least C/32>1/128 from each promise side. Therefore an estimate with |v−R*|≤1/128 cannot cross the midpoint in either case; its comparison decides the hard promise. This is an NP-hard additive-*value* approximation at a fixed constant, conditional on P≠NP, not a claim that functional-policy decision lies in NP or that an arbitrary policy's true expected value can be efficiently evaluated.

For the separate deterministic-policy guarantee, under stationary start lambda=P(H≠0^(M+2))≤(M+2)/M²≤2/M≤1/64. On a satisfiable formula, if an everywhere-legal deterministic policy with polynomial-size/evaluable description has regret ≤1/128, its satisfied fraction f0 on h0 obeys f0≥1−(1/128)/C−lambda>1−1/32−1/64=61/64>15/16. Directly evaluate it at h0, check legality, and count *original* clauses to decide the promise. Stationary high mass and deterministic polynomial evaluability are essential for this extraction; the value result holds for arbitrary starts. No randomized-policy extraction or opaque policy evaluation is proven.

The rescaled q can give TV up to 1/2, and a yes instance has optimal expected TV C>1/4. Hence the previous vanishing O(1/M) absolute risk does not apply. A classical predictor supplied the same public p can output p on every input with exactly zero TV, paid O(log M)-bit representation; this source-specific comparison does not solve the general unknown-source problem or support a Transformer advantage.
