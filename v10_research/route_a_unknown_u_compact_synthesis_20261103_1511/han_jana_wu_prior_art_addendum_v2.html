Direct overlap

Han, Jana and Wu, Optimal Prediction of Markov Chains With and Without Spectral Gap, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 69(6), 3920–3959 (2023), DOI 10.1109/TIT.2023.3239508: journal PDF, p. 3935, Lemma 16. For finite irreducible reversible chains it bounds second and fourth moments of the transition-count residual Nᵢⱼ−NᵢM(j|i), and the fourth moment of occupancy Nᵢ−(n−1)πᵢ, explicitly conditional on the actual final state X_n=i. Its Appendix B proof, pp. 3954 onward, uses time reversibility to express the conditioned historical quantities in reverse order. Section IV-B equation (66), p. 3935, analyzes the add-one estimator (Nᵢⱼ+1)/(Nᵢ+k). The paper’s discussion on pp. 3922–3923 itself emphasizes conditional-on-terminal empirical-count concentration. Therefore neither terminal-conditioned count analysis nor reversing the history at the actual terminal state is new as a general technique.

Why neither direct implication nor separation is automatic

Here the observed binary order-two process lifts to a four-state pair chain. It is genuinely nonreversible throughout the declared rare family: π₀₀P(00,01)=ut>0, whereas π₀₁P(01,00)=0. Thus Lemma 16’s stated reversible-chain assumption fails. The exact same path still has a valid stationary reversed kernel, whose special 00-row structure and endpoint flow are used in the bound in the source synthesis. The journal paper expressly considers nonreversible phenomena: Remark 2, p. 3922, says its Theorem 3(ii) extends under an appropriate pseudo-spectral-gap condition and distinguishes other parts. Theorem 3(ii) has a large-k condition, so it does not automatically give this fixed-k=4, growing-N result; its higher-order analysis recognizes that a block-chain lift is generally nonreversible. Lemma 31, pp. 3948–3949, supplies a pseudo-spectral-gap bound for a particular higher-order construction under uniform block-stationarity and specified positive transition bounds. The present family has nonuniform (u,w,w,w) stationary law, and a legal transition probability ut/w tends to zero as u→0, so those hypotheses cannot simply be presumed uniform here. This observation does not rule out a different nonreversible theorem or an adaptation of the paper's machinery.

The authors’ primary stated prediction loss is KL and their principal risk averages over the terminal state. Their equation (66) uses k-state add-one smoothing with denominator Nᵢ+k. Applying it literally to the four pair states smooths impossible successors too, whereas the present binary conditional estimator smooths only the two legal next symbols and has denominator J+2. The paper nevertheless contains the closer per-terminal conditional moment bounds described above; it would be inaccurate to describe its entire analysis as merely unconditional. Establishing whether another theorem or support-aware reduction in the paper implies the exact rare-row TV constant/rate would require a full assumption-by-assumption proof, which this targeted comparison has not completed. Conversely, these mismatches do not establish that the present rate or derivation is novel.
