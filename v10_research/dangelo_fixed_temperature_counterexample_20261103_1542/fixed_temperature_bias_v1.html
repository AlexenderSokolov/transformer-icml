Fixed-temperature bias in one soft-matching estimator

Research note, 3 November 2026. This is a direct counterexample for one precisely specified estimator family, not a generic Transformer resource lower bound or a claim of novelty. No sampling, RNG, numerical experiment or external write was used.

Result. For the equal-β idealized mask-count estimator of D’Angelo et al., Proposition 3.1, equation (8), with binary add-one BOS coupling κ=2β+ln 2, a strictly positive stationary binary order-two chain has actual-terminal conditional asymptotic error at least 0.3e−β. The result follows directly from the published equation and standard ergodic averaging. It constrains that specific output map, not networks with other heads, residuals, readouts, parameters, data-dependent temperature or source knowledge.

1. Exact model and legal source

Observe X₁,…,X_N and query its actual terminal pair S_N=(X_{N−1},X_N). Prepend BOS as in the source; k=2 and V={0,1}. For each predecessor state ab let J_ab be the number of i=3,…,N for which (X_{i−2},X_{i−1})=ab, and let C_ab count the same occurrences followed by 1. Fix the stationary order-two source

P(1|00)=3/4,     P(1|01)=P(1|10)=P(1|11)=1/4.

The pair-state transition graph uses only legal shifts (a,b)→(b,c). All legal transition probabilities are positive. The stationary law, in order 00,01,10,11, is π=(3/10,3/10,3/10,1/10): π₀₀P(1|00)=π₁₀P(0|10)=9/40, π₀₁P(1|01)=π₁₁P(0|11)=3/40, and the remaining balance equations follow. It is irreducible and aperiodic, since legal paths connect all states and 00 has a self-loop. In particular P(S_N=00)=3/10 for every N under stationarity.

2. Published estimator specialized to terminal 00

Let β≥0, z=e^(−β)∈(0,1], and choose κ=2β+ln 2, as in the α=1 specialization of Corollary 4.1. After dividing equation (8) by e^(2β), its idealized scalar prediction on S_N=00 is exactly

Q_N(z) = [1+C₀₀ + z(C₀₁+C₁₀) + z²C₁₁]
       / [2+J₀₀ + z(J₀₁+J₁₀) + z²J₁₁].

The weights 1,z,z² are the relative weights for two, one and zero matching coordinates. The +1 and +2 are the BOS contribution. The valid candidate range is i=3,…,N, including i=N; it never uses the unobserved X_{N+1}. This formula assumes the paper’s ideal positional-copy and early-position-suppression limits. Finite β does not remove partial matches. The proof does not claim that a finite-score full network evaluates this ideal formula exactly.

3. Actual-terminal asymptotic error

By the ergodic theorem for this finite irreducible aperiodic chain, almost surely J_ab/N→π_ab and C_ab/N→π_ab P(1|ab). Since J₀₀/N→3/10>0, taking N→∞ at fixed z gives

Q_N(z) → q(z) = [9/40 + (3/20)z + (1/40)z²]
                     / [3/10 + (3/5)z + (1/10)z²].
3/4 − q(z) = (1/2) [(3/5)z + (1/10)z²]
                          / [3/10 + (3/5)z + (1/10)z²].

For 0≤z≤1 the denominator is at most 1, so

3/4 − q(z) ≥ (3/10)z = (3/10)e^(−β).

The conclusion is genuinely conditional on the actual terminal pair, even though the unconditional pathwise ergodic theorem was used: for every δ>0,

P(|Q_N(z)−q(z)|>δ | S_N=00)
  ≤ P(|Q_N(z)−q(z)|>δ) / P(S_N=00) → 0.

All predictions lie in [0,1], so bounded convergence in probability gives

limₙ E[|Q_N(e^(−β))−3/4| | S_N=00]
  = 3/4−q(e^(−β)) ≥ (3/10)e^(−β).

No independence between the query and history is asserted. The finite-sequence formula applies to the same past used by the terminal query.

4. What the necessity does and does not say

For a fixed temperature β, if the above long-history conditional error is required to be at most ε<3/10, necessarily β≥ln(3/(10ε)). The same conclusion holds for a deterministic sequence β_N≥0 under a uniform asymptotic ε guarantee, in the following exact sense. Since all six normalized counts converge and J₀₀/N→3/10, the rational expressions converge uniformly over z∈[0,1]; the denominator is eventually uniformly bounded away from zero. Consequently

E[|Q_N(e^(−β_N))−3/4| | S_N=00] ≥ (3/10)e^(−β_N) − o(1).

Thus limsup error≤ε entails liminf β_N≥ln(3/(10ε)). If a target ε_N itself tends to zero, the displayed asymptotic result implies β_N→∞, but it does not by itself prove a finite-N β_N≥ln(c/ε_N) rate: the unspecified o(1) must first be controlled relative to ε_N.

This is not a lower bound on logit size, temperature or precision for arbitrary Transformers. A different readout, learned source information, another head or residual channel can bypass this exact interpolation map; the counterexample does not rule out any of them. It is also not a computational lower bound: attention and bit-operation costs depend on the declared execution model. A source-specific constant predictor 3/4 would have zero error on this one source, but is not a common predictor for unknown second-order laws. No head-count, depth, width, training or finite-bit necessity follows.

5. Prior-art and comparator context

The result is a specialization and elementary consequence of equation (8), not independent evidence that the mechanism or its finite-temperature smoothing is new. As β→∞ at fixed N, Corollary 4.1 gives binary add-one in the additional ideal copying and suppression limits. Conversely, the above calculation fixes β before taking N→∞, or allows β_N only under the stated uniform comparison. These limits should not be interchanged without specifying the scaling.

A finite-β bias in a fixed interpolator does not by itself show its statistical risk is inferior at every finite N: partial matching can reduce variance, and comparison with direct joint matching must charge all channels, representation, precision, attention and compute under the same model. The source-specific conditional loss differs from the paper’s latent-prior averaged predictive loss. No comprehensive literature search or formal novelty certification is claimed.
