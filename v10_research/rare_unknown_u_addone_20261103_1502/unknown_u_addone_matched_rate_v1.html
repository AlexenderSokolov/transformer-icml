Unknown-u terminal risk and finite-score add-one network

3 November 2026. Candidate proof, not formal verification, external peer review, training theory or novelty certification. No RNG, numerical test, enumeration, old-file overwrite or external write was used. Independent analytic review of the argument was requested; final-artifact review is pending.

Scope: homogeneous binary order-two family, stationary initialization and the actual terminal event S_N=00. The same add-one rule and network are independent of both u and t. The rate is conditional risk for this event, not an unconditional-risk theorem or a lower bound on attention depth, bits or heads. Structural masks are explicit model-R access, not free standard dense-causal attention.

1. Statement and quantifiers

Let N≥2, M=N−2, 0<u<1/4, w=(1−u)/3, and t∈[1/4,3/4]. The pair-state chain S_i=(X_(i−1),X_i) begins at S₂∼π=(u,w,w,w), ordered 00,01,10,11, and has successor-1 table θ=(t,1/2,1−ut/w,1/2). Observe X₁:N; the event S_N=00 has positive probability u. For s define

J_s=Σ_(i=3)^N 1{(X_(i−2),X_(i−1))=s},
C_s=Σ_(i=3)^N 1{(X_(i−2),X_(i−1))=s, X_i=1},
B_s=(C_s+1)/(J_s+2).

For M=0 the sums are empty. The same B_{S_N} depends neither on u nor t. The proposed result is

E_(u,t)[ |B_00−t| | S_N=00 ] ≤ min{1,157/√(1+Mu)}.             (1)

Here binary total variation is absolute difference of success probabilities. The bound holds for every allowed u,t,N simultaneously; the expression in its analysis need not be an input to B. In particular, for N=2, B_00=1/2.

For each fixed u, an existing independently scoped full-history two-point lower gives, for every common rule even if allowed to know u,

max_(t∈{t−,t+}) E_(u,t)[TV(p̂,Bernoulli(t)) | S_N=00]
 ≥ (1−1/√6)/[32√(1+Mu)],
t±=1/2±1/[32√(1+Mu)].                                           (2)

Thus the known-u full-history conditional minimax rate is Θ((1+Mu)^−1/2), and the single u-blind rule B attains the upper rate for all u. Lower (2) allows the entire history and public u, so it also applies to any smaller declared neural class. This does not imply a depth, head or bit lower bound.

2. Reverse chain and exact endpoint flow

Condition on the real event S_N=00. Define Z_j=S_(N−j), j=0,…,M. The reversed chain begins deterministically at Z₀=00 and has rows

R = [ 1−t   0    t    0  ]
    [  h    0   1−h   0  ]
    [  0   1/2   0   1/2 ]
    [  0   1/2   0   1/2 ],    h=ut/w.

Define J=Σ_(j=0)^(M−1)1{Z_j=00}, K=Σ_(j=0)^(M−1)1{Z_j=00,Z_(j+1)=10}, and δ=1−1{S₂=00}. With empty sums at M=0, endpoint flow along the same path gives

J=J_00+δ,   K=C_00+δ,
B_00=(K+1−δ)/(J+2−δ).                                           (3)

The first identity shifts the endpoints in the 00 occupation count. For the second, forward departures from 00 to 01 number C_00, forward arrivals into 00 from 10 number K, and out−in=1{S₂=00}−1{S_N=00}=−δ. This is a pathwise identity, not an iid assumption.

3. Reverse occupation and martingale

Each row of R² puts at least 1/8 on 11, so with ρ=7/8, sup_s ||R^j(s,·)−π||_TV≤ρ^⌊j/2⌋. This uses a two-step common atom and does not assert a two-step π-minorization. Consequently

|E[J | S_N=00]−Mu| ≤ Σ_(j≥0)ρ^⌊j/2⌋ =16.                    (4)

Let I_j=1{Z_j=00}. For i<j, set h=j−i. Under the nonstationary reverse law,

|Cov(I_i,I_j)|
 ≤ E I_i·(|R^h(00,00)−u|+|P(Z_j=00)−u|)
 ≤ 2 E I_i·ρ^⌊h/2⌋.

Here j≥h. Since Σ_(h≥1)ρ^⌊h/2⌋=15, summing the diagonal variances and twice the absolute covariance yields

Var(J | S_N=00) ≤61 E[J | S_N=00] ≤61(Mu+16).                 (5)

Define the reverse martingale difference sum

D=K−tJ=Σ_(j=0)^(M−1)1{Z_j=00}(1{Z_(j+1)=10}−t).
E[D² | S_N=00] = t(1−t)E[J | S_N=00] ≤(Mu+16)/4.             (6)

Martingale differences are valid under the already-conditioned reverse law started at 00. No forward martingale is conditioned on its terminal event; J and D are never assumed independent.

4. Conditional upper proof and end cases

From (3), B_00−t=[D+1−δ−t(2−δ)]/(J+2−δ), and |1−δ−t(2−δ)|≤1. Write x=Mu. If x≥64, (4) gives E J≥3x/4. Chebyshev and (5) give

P(J<x/2 | S_N=00) ≤16·61(x+16)/x² ≤1220/x.                 (7)

On J≥x/2, the denominator in (3) is at least x/2. Cauchy and (6) imply

E[|B_00−t|·1{J≥x/2} | S_N=00]
 ≤ (2/x)·(√(x+16)/2+1)
 ≤ (√5/2)/√x + 2/x.

The complement costs at most one, so the total is at most (√5/2)/√x+1222/x. For x≥64, √5/2<9/8 and 1222/x≤(1222/8)/√x; also 1/√x≤(65/64)/√(1+x). Therefore

(√5/2)/√x+1222/x < (80015/512)/√(1+x) <157/√(1+x).

For x<64, |B_00−t|≤1 and 157/√(1+x)>1, yielding (1). M=0 is included without dividing by x. These constants are sufficient and intentionally loose. A martingale-ratio estimate under the reverse source can improve constants, but no optimal constant is claimed.

5. Finite-score, one-head add-one computation for every actual state

For M≥1, model R grants two first-block singleton-mask heads that copy X_(i−2),X_(i−1) at every history row i=3,…,N while retaining X_i; early rows have harmless self masks. A paid tokenwise Boolean/ReLU FFN forms historical state onehot e_i∈{0,1}⁴ for (X_(i−2),X_(i−1)), and f_i=e_i X_i. Each f_i component can be computed as ReLU((e_i)_r+X_i−1). At the terminal row, a separate onehot q=e_(S_N) is made from the retained (X_(N−1),X_N). These are distinct coordinates even at i=N. History-side keys and values do not receive the terminal query.

The second block uses one head at the terminal row, with mask {3,…,N}, score β⟨q,e_i⟩, β=log(1+M/2), and value (f_i,e_i)∈R⁸. For the usual score division by √d_k with d_k=4, set the query projection to 2βq and the key to e_i. All allowed scores are finite and equal 0 or β. No BOS or synthetic token is used. Writing s=S_N, J=J_s,C=C_s and D=M+(e^β−1)J=(M/2)(J+2), the selected head-coordinate values are

Q_C=e^β C/D=(M+2)C/[M(J+2)],
Q_J=e^β J/D=(M+2)J/[M(J+2)].

The onehot terminal query selects Q_C,Q_J from the two four-vectors. For v∈[0,1] and a bit b, ReLU(v+b−1) equals bv, so one may sum four gated coordinates for each selected quantity. The paid pointwise readout then produces

p = 1/2 + [M/(M+2)](Q_C−Q_J/2) = (C_s+1)/(J_s+2)=B_s.    (8)

Thus the actual terminal prediction is exactly the same-history add-one value for every binary word and every terminal state, including J_s=0. It uses no u, t or hidden source information. If the actual query were a different state, the same network/formula would output that state's add-one statistic on that input. However, for a fixed query s the unselected r≠s head coordinates have unboosted numerator C_r/D or J_r/D; equation (8) is not simultaneously a valid decoder for all four off-query states from those coordinates. For M=0 bypass the empty mask and output 1/2. Emit exact probability vector (1−p,p).

6. Risk and numerical interface of the declared network

In ideal real arithmetic, this single u-blind parameter family exactly realizes B_{S_N}; combining (1), (2) and (8) gives the matching conditional Θ((1+Mu)^−1/2) order in the specified model-R class, with no oracle u in the upper network. This is a statistical lower even when an adversary is granted all of X₁:N and u. It does not prove any lower bound on the number of heads, width, layers, score or bit precision.

Suppose the exact terminal onehot and the selected Q_C,Q_J are available to the readout, while the two selected coordinates each have absolute error at most ε. Clip all eight incoming head coordinates to [0,1] before the exact ReLU selector, so unselected gates remain zero; clipping cannot enlarge error relative to the ideal coordinates. With exact selector, affine readout, final [0,1] projection and complement, the extra TV error is at most

[M/(M+2)](ε+ε/2) ≤ 3ε/2.                                      (9)

For the scalar head-coordinate errors alone, nearest b-bit fixed-point rounding has ε≤2^(−b−1). Because u<1/4, requiring

2^(−b−1) ≤ (2/3)/√(1+M/4)

makes (9) at most 1/√(1+Mu) for all allowed u. This is a sufficient rounded-output port condition, not a bit lower bound or complete finite-word realization. It does not bound errors in score log(1+M/2), the softmax exponential and normalizer, masks, bit copying, token features, terminal selector, coefficient M/(M+2), accumulator, final projection, or complement. Additional numerical errors require their own accounting.

7. Cost, comparators, and prior art

The explicitly specified ideal model-R graph uses two singleton-copy head instances in its first attention block and one matching head in its second; query/key dimension four, value dimension eight, and constant-width tokenwise feature/selection circuitry. It may need a constant number of additional FFN stages or blocks depending on the exact architecture; these must be counted rather than silently treated as free. In a sparse executor the active edges and constant-width feature work are O(N), versus possible O(N²) work in a dense executor. The structural masks, positions and their storage, score gap β=log(1+M/2), value and residual widths, all FFN stages, exact ratio coefficient, numeric dynamic range and bit formats must be paid. No proof compiles this to an ordinary dense-causal decoder with finite scores.

An exact same-history streaming count of J_s,C_s (or all four rows) and a final add-one division is an essential baseline; the graph computes the same statistic, so it cannot claim a statistical advantage over that baseline. Full-history R011 already supplies broad all-state average-risk add-one theory, BCJ001 supplies a paid complete finite-bank result in a different port/edit/certificate model, and FOT003 supplies related exact path flow. D’Angelo et al., Proposition 3.1 already supply finite-score soft mask matching and Corollary 4.1 use a uniform BOS target to realize symmetric add-one in a score limit. The present two-channel affine identity avoids BOS in its stated model but is an elementary construction, not a certified new mechanism. Rajaraman et al. already represent terminal empirical k-grams in shallow attention; smoothing, zero matches, cost and numerical differences need direct comparison. No comprehensive novelty search, training theorem, optimal complexity result or matched-resource separation is claimed.
