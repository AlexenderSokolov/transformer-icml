Prior art: bounded causal attention and terminal prediction

3 November 2026. Targeted original-source audit, not a complete literature search or formal verification. The current proposed fixed-positive-mixing second-order-source prediction lower bound was described in the assignment and was still being developed; no frozen proof is evaluated here. No tests or random computations were run; no existing file was overwritten.

Attribution. Vanishing influence of a single input under bounded soft attention and fixed Lipschitz operations is established prior work, most directly Hahn (2020), Lemma 5. Causal residual Transformer sensitivity and explicit path decompositions also have strong precedent. A fixed-source, actual-terminal expected-prediction lower bound requires an additional source-specific probability argument; this search did not verify a published theorem with exactly all those source and architecture quantifiers. That limited negative finding does not establish novelty.

Michael Hahn, TACL 2020, Theoretical Limitations of Self-Attention in Neural Sequence Models, §3 and Lemma 5 (§6).With fixed layers and heads, bounded activations/scores and Lipschitz position-wise processing, changing one nonfinal symbol changes the final output activation by O(1/n), with constants depending on the model. §3 explicitly permits skip connections in the position-wise map. 2. What a causal proof must add

For full causal attention and uniformly bounded logits |s|≤B, every eligible weight in row j is at most e2B/j, not e2B/n uniformly over all j. If the edited token lies at i≥cn and the two inputs agree earlier, causality makes positions j<i identical. Then every row capable of seeing the change has j≥cn, giving O(1/n) weights. For fixed depth, a recurrence must separately control the changed value, changes to all keys, query changes, attention normalization, FFN, residual paths and output map. At i fixed near the left boundary, sums of 1/j can introduce logarithms; the middle/late restriction is substantive and should not be discarded without a separate proof.

With regularized pre-LN, bounded scale/bias and finite-dimensional fixed weights make normalized vectors and Q/K/V bounded; ε>0 gives a finite Lipschitz constant. The raw residual can contain unbounded position features, but the proof must verify that token substitutions at the changed position have uniformly bounded input difference, and that raw residual or output channels cannot bypass the controlled map. A source-independent positional feature by itself does not bound arbitrary token–position interactions. The proposition needs fixed head count, depth, regularization and operator bounds, or it must expose their dependence in constants.

3. From influence to a Markov-source risk lower

A pointwise stability inequality is not by itself an expected-risk lower bound: two strings differing in one earlier symbol must both have non-negligible probability under the same fixed source; their true next-symbol distributions must differ by a fixed amount; and their final position/readout must be compared without changing its direct token input. For a strictly positive finite order-two source, one may attempt a local change-of-measure or matched-pair argument using bounded ratios of the finitely many affected transition probabilities. The target source's actual terminal context and all conditioning must be specified and verified; positivity/fast mixing alone does not guarantee the relevant two transition rows differ.

There is also a quantifier issue. For each fixed model, an O(Cmodel/n) influence bound can yield a fixed-source asymptotic risk floor if Cmodel is finite. An infimum over a class of models at each finite n requires a uniform bound on the constants across that class; with parameters allowed to grow with n the order of infimum and limit cannot be exchanged. Discrete exact classification by arbitrarily small signed margins is not the same as separated probability outputs or an expected TV/cross-entropy statement.

4. Search boundary

Searches covered bounded soft attention, single-token influence, causal decoder residual pathways, layer normalization and Markov next-token prediction. The cited primary passages were checked; this does not inspect every subsequent paper or prove absence of a direct stochastic lower bound. The currently proposed Markov derivation was not frozen or independently reviewed here. Cite the classic influence results as prior art, state the causal and same-source probability bridge precisely, and avoid priority claims pending broader citation tracing.
