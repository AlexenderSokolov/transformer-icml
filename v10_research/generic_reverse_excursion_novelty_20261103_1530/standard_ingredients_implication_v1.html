Endpoint-conditioned add-one risk from standard Markov ingredients

Source-bound novelty assessment, 3 November 2026. This is an analytic derivation, not a claim that a single cited paper states the theorem below verbatim. No randomness, simulated data, numerical enumeration, or external writes were used. The derivation is not a formal proof verification.

Assessment. The special rare-family upper rate follows with loose constants from elementary time reversal, Markov excursions, martingale maximal inequality, and summable reverse mixing. This substantially weakens a claim to a standalone new statistical upper bound. Han–Jana–Wu already supply closely related terminal-conditioned moments in the reversible setting. This search does not prove that the exact nonreversible statement is absent from the literature, nor does it rule on the novelty of the combined Transformer implementation or its lower bound.

1. A generic conditional row bound

Let P be an irreducible finite-state Markov transition matrix with stationary distribution π, and let X₀,…,X_M be a stationary trajectory, M≥0. Fix state i with πᵢ>0 and any state j, with p=P(i,j). Define K as the number of departures from i and C as the number of i→j departures among the M observed transitions. Write

K = Σₜ₌₀ᴹ⁻¹ 1{Xₜ=i},   C = Σₜ₌₀ᴹ⁻¹ 1{Xₜ=i,Xₜ₊₁=j},   p̂ = (C+1)/(K+2).

The two pseudocounts refer to the binary event “next state j” versus “not j”; this is a binary row estimator if exactly two successors are legal. It remains an estimator of the single coordinate p for a larger state space. Let R(a,b)=π_b P(b,a)/π_a be the time reversal. Assume there are nonincreasing upper bounds d_h on sup_z ||Rʰ(z,·)−π||TV with B=Σ_{h≥1}d_h<∞. Then for x=Mπᵢ,

E[|p̂−p| | X_M=i] ≤ C_B / √(1+x),

where C_B is a finite function of B, independent of πᵢ, M, i, and j. The estimator is independent of the unknown πᵢ and B. For an explicit deliberately loose choice put B₀=max{B,1} and

C_B = max{ √(1+4B₀),  √2 [5+20(1+4B₀)] }.

No reversibility, known πᵢ, or independence between the terminal state and history is assumed. In a binary row, the displayed absolute error equals the total-variation distance of the two Bernoulli laws. For finite d-category rows one may bound each coordinate of (C_j+1)/(K+d) similarly, with constants depending on d; no dimension-free claim follows here.

2. Exact reversed-excursion count and marks

Conditional on the actual event X_M=i, Z_r=X_{M−r} is the R-chain starting at i, through r=M. This follows directly from the stationary joint path probability. Extend Z beyond M on an auxiliary probability space solely for the proof; the estimator observes no extension. Finite irreducibility makes all successive returns to i almost surely finite. Define T₀=0 and T_a=inf{r>T_{a−1}:Z_r=i}. A self-loop is a length-one excursion. Let Y_a=1{Z_{T_a−1}=j}, including the case j=i. Then

K = max{a:T_a≤M} = Σᵣ₌₁ᴹ 1{Zᵣ=i},    C=Σₐ₌₁ᴷ Y_a.

The second identity is pathwise: a forward departure at time t corresponds to the reverse arrival at r=M−t, and its forward successor is Z_{r−1}. The last incomplete reverse excursion contributes neither a departure nor a mark. It is not observed, and no information from it is added to the estimator.

By the strong Markov property, complete reverse excursions are iid. For every finite i-to-i excursion with no internal i, multiplication of reverse transition probabilities telescopes the stationary factors, giving the probability under P of its reversed path. Its last reversed predecessor is therefore distributed as the first P-step from i: Y_a are iid Bernoulli(p), even when j=i. An excursion mark can depend on its own length; the Y_a need not be independent of K, and no conditional-iid claim given K is made.

3. Bounded stopping fixes the random sample size

With the filtration revealing complete excursions, L=K+1=inf{a≥1:T_a>M} is a stopping time because {L≤a}={T_a>M}. It is bounded by M+1, since every excursion lasts at least one step. K itself need not be a stopping time. Let S_a=Σ_{r=1}^a(Y_r−p). Then S is a martingale in the excursion filtration; each centered mark has conditional variance p(1−p)≤1/4. Applying Doob’s L² maximum inequality to the stopped martingale and its bounded-stopping isometry,

E |C−pK| = E|S_K| ≤ E max₀≤a≤L |S_a|
    ≤ 2 √(E S_L²) = 2 √(p(1−p) E L) ≤ √(E K+1).

The extension and the mark of the first incomplete excursion are used only in this upper bound. K=0 is included: S₀=0 and p̂=1/2. There is no conditioning on a chosen value of K.

4. Reverse occupation, including the nonstationary start

Write I_r=1{Z_r=i}, q_r=P_i(Z_r=i). For r≥1, |q_r−πᵢ|≤d_r. Consequently |EK−x|≤B and EK≤x+B. For 1≤r<s, with h=s−r,

Cov(I_r,I_s)=q_r[Rʰ(i,i)−q_s],
|Cov(I_r,I_s)|≤q_r(d_h+d_s)≤2q_r d_h.

The final inequality uses nonincreasing d_h. Therefore

Var K ≤ Σᵣ q_r + 4Σᵣ q_r Σₕ≥₁d_h ≤ (1+4B)(x+B).

This bound uses the actual initial state i, not a stationary reverse start. If x≥4B₀, then EK≥3x/4; Chebyshev yields

P(K<x/2) ≤ 16(1+4B₀)(x+B₀)/x² ≤ 20(1+4B₀)/x.

On K≥x/2, |p̂−p|≤(|C−pK|+1)/(K+2)≤2(|C−pK|+1)/x. On its complement the loss is at most 1. Using the previous maximum bound,

E|p̂−p| ≤ 2[√(x+B₀+1)+1]/x + 20(1+4B₀)/x.

For x≥4B₀≥4 this is bounded by √2[5+20(1+4B₀)]/√(1+x). For x<4B₀ the trivial risk bound 1 gives the other term in C_B. Thus the stated rate holds for all M including M=0, where the output is exactly 1/2. The constants are intentionally not optimized.

5. Specialization to the actual rare terminal

For the already specified binary order-2 family, label the pair states 00,01,10,11, let π=(u,w,w,w), w=(1−u)/3, 0<u<1/4, t∈[1/4,3/4], and use next-bit-one probabilities (t, 1/2, 1−ut/w, 1/2). At terminal state i=00, the forward transition to j=01 has p=t. With M=N−2 observed triples, K and C are exactly the same-history count of preceding 00 and of 001, and πᵢ=u. Conditioning is on the actual terminal pair, not on a separately sampled query. The bound is uniform in unknown u,t because the next calculation has a common mixing constant.

For each state a, P²(a,11)≥1/8. Also P²(11,b)≥(3/8)π_b for all b: the four probabilities are ut/(2w), (1−ut/w)/2, 1/4, 1/4; use t≥1/4, w≤1/3, and ut/w≤3/4. Hence P⁴(a,b)≥(3/64)π_b. Reversal gives R⁴(a,b)=π_b P⁴(b,a)/π_a≥(3/64)π_b. The common-component coupling bound then permits d_h=(61/64)^⌊h/4⌋. Thus B=3+4·(61/3)=253/3. Substitution yields, uniformly over the family,

E[ |(C₀₀+1)/(K₀₀+2) − t|  | S_N=00 ] ≤ C / √(1+(N−2)u).

At N=2, M=0 and the estimator returns 1/2. This bound is a loose consequence, not a claim of improved constants over the earlier 157 bound. It supplies only an upper risk bound. The separately established conditional full-history lower bound and the full-network construction require their own assumptions and proofs.

The decisive overlap assessment is stronger than “no paper contains this exact formula”: the rate can be derived using classical machinery, and HJW already supply essentially the relevant endpoint moments in a neighboring regime. Full literature coverage is not established. In particular, no absence-of-prior-art or formal novelty certification is claimed. A candidate paper would need an independently valuable, carefully compared architectural/resource contribution or a materially stronger result, not this special-family upper rate alone.
