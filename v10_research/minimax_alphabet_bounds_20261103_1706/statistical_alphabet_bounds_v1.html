Statistical history-length bounds for finite-alphabet order-two prediction

3 November 2026. All statements concern information in the observed history and expected total-variation loss, not Transformer depth, heads, computation, training, or precision. They use standard two-point, Assouad, martingale and mixing ideas; no novelty claim or formal verification is made. No random or numerical experiments were used.

Results to independently check. Binary iid sources already give Ω(N−1/2). A paired-sign family gives an explicit Ω(min{1,√(A³/N)}) lower for all A≥2, N≥2, even among full-support stationary chains with uniform two-step mixing. For stationary initialization, a direct simultaneous-stack argument improves the known all-source add-one upper’s A dependence to order A3/2 up to logarithms. With uniformly bounded reverse mixing, add-one also has a matching O(√(A³/N)) upper. The last two claims have narrower initialization/mixing scope than the existing arbitrary-initial-law bound.

1. Quantifiers and binary iid lower

For alphabet size A≥2, a source has a fixed homogeneous order-two table θ and an initial pair law. Observe X₁,…,X_N, N≥2, and let S_N=(X_{N−1},X_N). A possibly randomized estimator maps the entire observed word to a distribution on the alphabet; its loss is TV(q,θ_{S_N})=½||q−θ_{S_N}||₁. The minimax risk takes the infimum over one common estimator, then the supremum over sources; its randomness is independent of the source. The stationary class requires the pair chain to start from a stationary distribution, whereas the larger class allows any initial pair law. Bounds on either class are not per-source lower bounds.

Choose iid Bernoulli sources p_±=1/2±δ with δ=1/(8√N), embedded in any A≥2 alphabet; their true next law is the same in every terminal state. By kl(a||b)≤(a−b)²/[b(1−b)] and δ≤1/8,

KL(P_−^N||P_+^N) ≤ 4/15,   TV(P_−^N,P_+^N)≤√(2/15).

For any output q, TV(q,p_±)≥|q(1)−p_±|. The absolute-loss two-point inequality gives the worst of the two risks at least

(1−√(2/15))/(8√N).

This already lower-bounds the stationary and arbitrary-initial minimax risks. It does not claim that every single source incurs that error. The iid finite-distribution lower-bound method is classical; see Mazzetto and Upfal for the known iid TV rate and its references.

2. A full-support A³ family

Let m=⌊A/2⌋ and η=(1/16)min{1,√(A³/N)}. For h,r∈{1,…,m} and b∈{1,…,A}, choose independent signs σ_{hbr}∈{−1,+1}. Define ξ(2h−1)=+1, ξ(2h)=−1 on the paired symbols. For paired predecessor a in pair h and paired successor c in pair r set

θ_σ(c|a,b)=[1+η σ_{hbr} ξ(a)ξ(c)]/A.

If predecessor a or successor c is unpaired, use 1/A. Each output pair cancels in a row, so θ_σ is stochastic. For fixed b,c, the paired predecessor perturbations cancel and ∑_aθ_σ(c|a,b)=1. Consequently the pair-chain stationary distribution is the same uniform π(a,b)=1/A² for every σ. All legal transitions are positive, including for odd A. The two-step forward kernel satisfies P²(x,y)≥(1−η)²/A²≥π(y)/4; its stationary reversal satisfies the same inequality. Hence both chains mix uniformly, for example with reverse TV bounds d_t=(3/4)^⌊t/2⌋ and Σ_{t≥0}d_t=8.

3. Conditional-path Assouad calculation

Fix a terminal pair s and flip one sign σ_{hbr}, leaving the rest fixed. Because π(s)=1/A² for every σ, conditioning on the actual event S_N=s leaves the uniform sign prior unchanged. The reversed pair trajectory starts at s and uses R=Pᵀ. Only reverse rows (b,2r−1) and (b,2r) change. In either row, the KL divergence between the two one-step laws is

(2η/A) ln[(1+η)/(1−η)] ≤ 8η²/A,    η≤1/2.

For their two-row set U, at reverse time t its probability is at most 2/A²+d_t. The M=N−2 reverse transitions therefore visit U in expectation at most 2N/A²+8. The chain rule, without conditioning on empirical counts, yields

KL(conditional reversed path || flipped conditional path)
 ≤ 16η²N/A³ + 64η²/A ≤ 1/16+1/8=3/16.

The last inequality uses η²N/A³≤1/256, η²≤1/256 and A≥2. Pinsker gives TV≤√(3/32)<1/2, so any test of this sign from the conditional history has average error ≥1/4. Randomized estimators are covered by incorporating their source-independent random seed in the transcript.

On a terminal row with paired first symbol, each wrong successor-pair sign costs at least η/A in output TV; these disjoint pair costs add. The chance that the terminal first symbol is paired is 2m/A, and each such row has m relevant signs. Averaging the conditional tests gives, for every estimator,

sup_source E TV(q,θ_{S_N}) ≥ (2m/A)·m·(η/A)·(1/4)
                            = ηm²/(2A²)
                            ≥ (1/288) min{1,√(A³/N)}.

The last step uses m≥A/3. It applies to even and odd A with full support and uniform mixing; it is an unconditional actual-terminal average lower bound, not a per-terminal-state conditional minimax claim. It is a statistical lower over all history-based estimators, with no architectural restriction.

4. A stationary all-source add-one upper with logarithms

Let Y_t=(X_{t+1},X_{t+2}), t=0,…,M, and J_s count visits to s among Y₀,…,Y_(M−1). For the actual terminal Y_M, J=J_{Y_M}. Assume only stationary initialization; the finite pair chain may be reducible or nonmixing. For a two-sided stationary extension define J_t^(M)=Σ_{r=1}^M1{Y_(t−r)=Y_t} and J_t^(t)=Σ_{r=1}^t1{Y_(t−r)=Y_t}. The former is pointwise at least the latter. Stationarity gives equality of E[1/(J_t^(M)+1)] for all t, not uniformity of the endpoint rank. Hence

(M+1) E[1/(J+1)] ≤ E Σ_(t=0)^M 1/(J_t^(t)+1)
                  = E Σ_s H_(n_s) ≤ A² H_(M+1),

where n_s is the count of state s in Y₀:M and H_n=Σ_{j=1}^n1/j. The equality is a pathwise grouping by the order in which each state appears. No endpoint is treated as a uniformly random position.

Represent the Markov law by independent infinite successor stacks, one iid categorical stack per context. This representation is unconditional; stack labels are not assumed iid after conditioning on J or the terminal. For any stack and prefix k, the empirical distribution has expected TV at most ½√(A/k); changing one draw changes TV by at most 1/k. McDiarmid’s inequality and a union bound over A² contexts and k=1,…,M give, with probability at least 1−δ, simultaneous bounds

TV(emp_(s,k),θ_s) ≤ [½√A + √(½ ln(A²M/δ))]/√k.

Let q be the original full-history add-one predictor. For k=J>0 it is k/(k+A) times the empirical distribution plus A/(k+A) times uniform; at k=0 it is uniform. Since A/(k+A)≤√A/√(k+1) and √k/(k+A)≤1/√(k+1), choosing δ=1/(M+1), Jensen and the harmonic bound give for M≥1

sup_stationary risk(q) ≤ min{1,
 A[ (3/2)√A + √(½ ln(A²M(M+1))) ] √[H_(M+1)/(M+1)]
 + 1/(M+1)}.

This applies to the actual terminal state, all stationary homogeneous tables, all A≥2, and no mixing bound. It improves the power of A in the older arbitrary-initial O(A5/2/√N) bound at the cost of logarithms and a stationary-start restriction. It is an elementary argument, not a claimed new rate. For N=2 the trivial TV≤1 applies.

5. Sharper add-one upper in the fast reverse-mixing subclass

Assume now that each source is stationary and its reverse pair chain has nonincreasing TV mixing bounds with B=Σ_{t≥1}d_t uniformly bounded; put B₀=max{1,B}. For a fixed actual terminal s, let λ=Mπ_s and K=J_s. The reverse occupation bound from the cited elementary excursion argument gives |EK−λ|≤B₀, Var(K)≤(1+4B₀)(λ+B₀), and, when λ≥4B₀, P(K<λ/2)≤20(1+4B₀)/λ.

Extend the reverse chain beyond the observed horizon only in the proof. Forward-successor onehot marks Y_r of its completed return excursions are iid with law θ_s, but are not asserted iid conditional on K. With T=K+1 a bounded stopping time and S_T=Σ_{r≤T}(Y_r−θ_s), optional isometry gives E||S_T||₂²≤E T≤1+λ+B₀. Since ||Y_T−θ_s||₂≤√2, E||S_K||₂≤√(1+λ+B₀)+√2. The add-one numerator has smoothing ℓ1 norm at most 2A. On K≥λ/2,

TV(q_s,θ_s) ≤ [√A||S_K||₂+2A]/λ.

If λ≥max{A,4B₀}, use the previous moment and probability bounds; if λ is smaller, use TV≤1. The following conservative bound holds for all λ≥0:

E[TV(q_s,θ_s) | S_N=s] ≤ min{1,(50+120B₀)√[A/(1+Mπ_s)]}.

Average with the actual terminal weights π_s and use Σ_s√π_s≤A. For M≥1,

sup_(stationary reverse-mixing sum ≤B) risk(q)
 ≤ min{1,(50+120B₀)√(A³/M)}.

Together with §3, this gives a matching Θ_B(min{1,√(A³/N)}) rate on any such class that contains the uniformly mixing witness family. This is an application of classical ingredients; it does not automatically apply to arbitrary initial distributions or unbounded mixing.

6. Strongest inspected prior art and limitations

Han, Jana and Wu, Theorems 5 and 22, already establish stationary order-m actual-terminal minimax KL risk Θ_m((A^(m+1)/N)log(N/A^(m+1))) for m≥2 in their stated range 2≤A≤N^(1/(m+1))/C_m. At m=2, Pinsker and Jensen turn their KL upper into a TV upper O(√[(A³/N)log(N/A³)]) for their predictor. Pinsker does not turn a KL lower into a TV lower, and their theorem does not automatically give the same upper for the unaveraged full-history add-one rule or arbitrary initialization. This is the strongest directly inspected prior overlap and precludes presenting an A³ stationary minimax upper exponent as a new discovery.

Wolfer and Kontorovich study finite-sample transition-matrix estimation under a different TV-type criterion. The iid Assouad and reverse-mixing arguments above are standard-style reductions; the literature search is not exhaustive and absence of a previously stated equivalent TV bound has not been established. No new architecture, computation, precision or training lower bound follows.
