Same input and same non-LN weights: a restricted LayerNorm separation

3 November 2026. A derived, exact-real corollary of the separately reviewed positive decoder and pre-LN risk theorem. The formulation below awaits its own independent analytic review. No experiments, source overwrites, formal verification, novelty, training or hardware conclusions are claimed.

What is and is not equal. The two models use exactly the same input, ordinary causal mask, non-LN weights, dimensional graph and vocabulary-softmax output. One has eight extra frozen LayerNorm operations. This is a separation by a specific insertion of normalization, not equality of FLOPs, floating-point behavior, dynamic range, scores or numerical precision. It is not an impossibility theorem for post-LN, local attention, length-scaled scores or all normalized Transformers.

1. Fixed common interface

For N≥2 data symbols, positions i=1,2 are BOS tokens with scalar token coordinate xᵢ=1/2; positions i=k+2 contain xᵢ=Xₖ∈{0,1}. The common 128-dimensional input residual contains the five coordinates (1,i,i²,ln i,xᵢ), with all other coordinates zero. These are the same token-independent, source-independent positional features and bounded token embedding in both models; ln 1=0. Generating and representing positions is an explicit paid input assumption. The two BOS markers are ordinary causal keys. Both models use exactly four residual attention/FFN blocks, eight allocated heads per block, head dimension 16, causal support j≤i and two output logits followed by ordinary two-class softmax. The attention score is q·k/4 with no extra positional or token-dependent bias; input/score maps have their ordinary fixed affine biases. There is no dropout.

Use the fixed parameters W of the four-block no-LN candidate, including the boundary/precedent addendum and the sharper pathwise-rate addendum. The three nonzero FFNs have at most 9, 8 and 4 hidden ReLU units respectively, and the fourth is zero; pad each to a common width 9 by zero units. Zero-pad the unused head/FFN coordinates exactly as in the source candidate. There are nine active head instances over the four blocks; the other allocated heads have zero value/output maps. The same padding and all non-LN weights W, including the terminal affine readout, are used in the following two graphs.

  • G₀ (no LN): attention and the FFN read the residual directly, each followed by its residual addition. This is the source candidate.
  • G₁ (pre-LN): immediately before each attention and each FFN apply LayerNorm to the whole width-128 residual, with γ=1, offset 0, fixed ε=10⁻⁵; then use exactly the same non-LN operations, W and residual additions as G₀. Thus every Q, K, V and FFN reads only normalized features. There is no final LN. The LayerNorm scale/offset are frozen constants, not extra learned parameters.

Under a convention that registers fixed γ and offset as parameter slots, G₁ nevertheless has extra registered constant slots; the comparison is of the identical non-LN weights and number of independent learned non-LN parameters, not a claim that every framework reports identical total parameter slots.

2. The common source

For ab∈{00,01,10,11}, let θab be the probability of the next symbol being 1 and set

θ₀₀=3/4,  θ₁₀=1/4,  θ₀₁=θ₁₁=1/2.

Its stationary pair distribution is uniform on {00,01,10,11}; each legal one-step symbol probability is at least 1/4, and every pair-to-pair two-step probability is at least 1/16. Hence its pair kernel dominates one quarter of the uniform distribution after two steps, is irreducible and aperiodic, and the source is genuinely second order. Start both comparisons from the same stationary pair law. The terminal target is the same-trajectory θ(Xₙ₋₁,Xₙ) and risk is expected half-L1 TV.

3. Separation and derivation

Let R₀(N),R₁(N) denote the actual-terminal risks of G₀ and G₁ on this source. The positive source candidate and its rate addendum show for every input history

|P₀,N−B_N| ≤ (N/2)e^(−N)+min{1,168/√(N+2)},

where BN is the same-history add-one estimate. Since the fixed source is finite-state and irreducible with every pair visited with positive limiting frequency, the empirical row counts converge almost surely to their true transition probabilities; the finitely many rows converge simultaneously. Thus E TV(BN,θSₙ)→0 by dominated convergence, without needing a uniform-in-source statistical theorem. Consequently R₀(N)→0. The inherited R011 bound can supply an explicit conservative O(N⁻¹/²) rate, but is not needed for this fixed-source convergence.

The pre-LN theorem, read with its input and quantifier and score-interface clarifications, applies to G₁: its eight positive-ε normalizations are positionwise; all nonlinear branches see LN outputs; the raw residual and final affine output are accounted for; there is no input-dependent score bias or local mask. For two same-length words that differ only in their penultimate data bit, their predicted next-symbol probabilities differ by at most CW/N for a finite constant depending on W, ε and the fixed graph, not on N or the word.

Condition on any history prefix before its last two data symbols. The continuations 00 and 10 each occur with conditional probability ≥1/16, and their true next-one probabilities are 3/4 and 1/4. Triangle inequality gives

R₁(N) ≥ max{0,1/32 − C_W/(16N)},   N≥4,
liminf_N R₁(N) ≥ 1/32.

Therefore inserting the specified pre-LN operations into this exact fixed W graph changes convergence on the same fixed, stationary fast-mixing source. More generally, the same lower applies to every fixed-parameter positive-ε pre-LN graph satisfying this interface, even if its fixed weights differ from W; restricting it to the common four-block, width-128, eight-head, width-9 budget remains valid. The fixed-model limit cannot be exchanged with an infimum over unrestricted parameters chosen separately at each N. Under explicit common norm and score budgets, the finite-N capacity ledger gives the separate stated necessary condition.

4. Exact accounting and exclusions

  • Identical: full input coordinates (including paid ln i), token and BOS encoding, sequence length, causal masks, four blocks, width 128, eight dimension-16 heads per block, FFN width 9 after zero padding, all non-LN matrices/biases W and output logits-to-softmax interface.
  • Different: eight frozen positive-ε LayerNorm operations before attention/FFN. They add arithmetic, intermediate values and possibly registered constants. Pre-LN bounds Q/K scores at fixed weights; G₀'s unnormalized copy scores have O(N³) raw terms and an N-growing gap. Therefore their realized logit ranges, intermediate magnitudes, numerical conditioning, precision requirements and runtime are not the same.
  • Exact-real only: positive graph has a separately specified specialized arbitrary-integer numerical backend; this does not turn the negative proof into a theorem about a shared fixed-bit rounding scheme. Non-Lipschitz rounding, underflow, a precision-dependent LN or extra operations require separate analysis.
  • Information: neither model is given θ or future symbols. Fixed weights here are manually constructed, not learned from tasks. The result is on one fixed source and does not give an independent calculation lower bound or establish optimization/training behavior.

5. Prior-art boundary

Hahn (2020), Lemma 5 gives the classical bounded-soft-attention single-symbol influence mechanism. Barbero et al. study causal residual sensitivity under their stated simplifications. Chiang and Cholak show why zero-ε normalization and length-scaled logits change the picture, while Yang et al. discuss hard-attention simulations with length-sensitive resources. D’Angelo et al., Ekbote et al. and He et al. already cover closely related n-gram, positional or smoothing mechanisms in their models. See the targeted source audit for specific theorem scopes. This corollary is an explicit connection of existing ingredients; no literature-priority or independent-paper novelty is asserted.
