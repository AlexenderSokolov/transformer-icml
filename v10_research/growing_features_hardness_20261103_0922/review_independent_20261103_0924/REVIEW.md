## Independent proof checks

1. Standard 3SAT remains NP-hard after removing tautologies and repeated literal occurrences, with empty-clause and empty-formula edge cases mapped in polynomial time to fixed no/yes instances. Enumerate or renumber the n variables that actually occur; then n and m are polynomial in the source encoding. Every remaining clause has one to three distinct variables. The least power of two M≥max(n²,m²,4) is polynomial, P≥m is the next power of two, and n=k≤sqrt M, m≤sqrt M. The history is zero, edits are confined to first M symbols, and final two zeros are protected. Setting the first n bits to any assignment costs at most n; editable dummy flips add cost but do not change any feature. Conversely, every legal word specifies an assignment in those n bits. Thus dummy flips do not create false yes instances.
2. For each clause, s in {-1,0,1} and x in {0,1}, ReLU(s+x−1)−ReLU(−s+x−1)=sx. This uses shared affine/ReLU operations on an additive token-plus-position embedding, not a free multiplication gate. Zero attention scores and an exact mask selecting M editable slots yield an exact uniform 1/M average for m value channels. Adding the negative-literal count C_j to M times head j gives precisely the integer number L_j of true literals. ReLU(1−L_j) is the unsatisfied-clause indicator; V is their sum. Query at the protected terminal position needs the explicitly specified valid mask/routing; other causal queries, if evaluated, need valid masks too.
3. V∈[0,m] and m≤P give q=3/(2M)−V/(2MP)∈[1/M,3/(2M)], and q=Q=3/(2M) iff all clauses are satisfied. A rational comparison of q≥Q has a polynomial-size legal mask witness and requires only integer/dyadic arithmetic. The claimed family is in NP assuming the input's allowed hybrid operations/encoding have polynomial exact evaluation, as this explicit family does; no generic exact exponential/log/sigmoid comparison oracle is asserted. Attacker uses h, public model and B(h), not a hidden source or label.
4. For the zero word the selected row has M counts and no ones, so t=B(h)=1/(M+2). Since q≥1/M>t and q≤3/8<1, derivative dCE(t,q)/dq=(q−t)/(q(1−q)) is positive. The exact rational decision q≥Q is equivalent to crossing CE(t,Q); represent that threshold by (t,Q), not a rational logarithm.
5. For V≥1, Q−q≥1/(2MP). Throughout the interval, q−t≥2/[M(M+2)] and q(1−q)≤1/4, giving derivative ≥8/[M(M+2)]. Integration yields CE(Q)−CE(q)≥4/[M²(M+2)P]. Thus a polynomial-time algorithm returning a legal witness of strictly smaller additive q regret than 1/(2MP), or CE regret than this latter bound, would return a satisfying assignment on every satisfiable input; checking V decides 3SAT. This is inverse-polynomial, not constant-additive hardness, and does not require evaluating logarithms to check the returned witness.
6. Dyadic denominators 1/M and 1/(2MP), coefficients ±1,0, constants M,P,Q and signed partial sums require O(log M) bits with a suitable common fixed-point grid/intermediate range. The dense position matrix needs O(Mm) entries; exact final-query evaluation O(Mm) arithmetic plus bit and I/O costs. Explicit masks/network/input representation are charged. Do not infer exact behavior of BLAS, arbitrary softmax logits, sigmoid or CE hardware.
7. Each feature's sum has range from minus its negative occurrences to plus its positive occurrences and at most four integer values, so the previous DP's product bound can be 4^m. Hardness with growing m and polynomial-size numbers is compatible with the polynomial-time guarantee only for fixed d and bounded efficient local/readout costs; fixed k direct enumeration also remains valid because k=n grows here. The proof does not establish a 4^m lower bound or any formal broad strong-NP-completeness claim.
