Uniform O(1/√L) Gaussian log-position bound

This is an immutable analytic addendum to the four-block candidate, SHA-256 bf7f6c3ab19472d83658a3dc5cf24b90514df96e145b578706504526045c14c8. It tightens the pathwise final-softmax error and retains all of that candidate’s exact-real, paid-ln-position, ordinary-causal-mask and fixed-parameter qualifications. No numerical or random experiment was performed.

1. Uniform geometric-mean approximation

Let L≥4, μ∈[0,L], J∈{1,…,L} have weights proportional to exp[−(j−μ)²/L], and define G=exp(E ln J). The denominator Z obeys Z≥√L/(2e), because at least ⌊√L⌋≥√L/2 integer positions lie within √L of μ. Also,

E|J−μ| ≤ 17√L.

To see this, use |x|e^(−x²/L)≤√L e^(−x²/(2L)) and the lattice-sum bound Σ_(j∈ℤ)e^(−(j−μ)²/(2L))≤1+√(2πL). A proof of the latter: compare all terms away from the two integers bracketing μ with neighboring unit integrals; those two together exceed their shared interval integral by at most 1. Division by Z gives 2e(1/√L+√(2π))√L<17√L for L≥4.

Jensen gives G≤EJ≤μ+17√L, while G≥1. If μ≤2√L, therefore |G−μ|≤17√L. For μ>2√L, split the log deficit into μ/2≤J<μ and J<μ/2. On the first region, ln(μ/J)≤2(μ−J)/μ, so its expectation is ≤34√L/μ. On the second region, each unnormalized weight is at most exp[−μ²/(4L)] and

Σ_(1≤j<μ/2) ln(μ/j) ≤ ∫₀^(μ/2) ln(μ/x) dx < μ.

Its contribution is at most 2e(μ/√L)e^(−μ²/(4L))≤8√L/μ, since max_(x≥0) x²e^(−x²/4)=4/e. Thus E ln(J/μ)≥−42√L/μ and G≥μe^(−42√L/μ)≥μ−42√L. In both cases

|G−μ| ≤ 42√L.

This is uniform at μ=0 and μ=L; the logarithm is taken only of J≥1, never of p=μ/L.

2. Consequence for the two-logit output

In the four-block candidate, μ₁=Lp and μ₀=L(1−p), and the final vocabulary softmax yields G₁/(G₁+G₀). Let d=42/√L. Each G/L differs from p or 1−p by at most d. If d≤1/4, direct algebra gives |G₁/(G₁+G₀)−p|≤d/(1−2d)≤2d; if d>1/4, the error is at most 1≤4d. Therefore, for every p∈[0,1],

|G₁/(G₁+G₀)−p| ≤ min{1,168/√L}.

Combining this with the pathwise earlier add-one approximation yields |P_N−B_N|≤(N/2)e^(−N)+min{1,168/√(N+2)}. The inherited R011 uniform average-risk bound then gives O(N−1/2) overall in the same exact-real model. This removes the √ln N factor in the first candidate’s bound, not the separate requirement to implement and pay for ln j, scores, copy margins and numerical softmax.
