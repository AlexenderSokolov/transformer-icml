Fixed-parameter causal decoder with vocabulary softmax and paid log positions

3 November 2026. Exact-real research candidate for independent proof and architecture review. It explicitly supplies a source-independent logarithmic positional feature; it does not invoke log of a model probability. No random or numerical experiment was used. No finite-word, training or novelty claim is made.

Claim. Extending the separately specified three-block fixed-parameter causal count decoder by one standard causal attention block gives two ordinary vocabulary logits. With paid position encoding (1,i,i²,ln i), two BOS tokens, fixed finite matrices and full causal masks, the resulting standard two-class output softmax approximates the prior block’s probability p uniformly over p∈[0,1], with explicit O(√(ln N/N)) pathwise error. Together with the inherited add-one risk result this yields uniform actual-terminal average-TV consistency over unknown binary homogeneous order-two sources, including arbitrary initial pair laws, in the declared exact-real model. This does not establish a generic floating-point implementation or an optimal rate.

1. Input and first three blocks

For N≥2 set L=N+2. Positions 1,2 are BOS tokens with scalar token value 1/2; position k+2 contains X_k∈{0,1}. Each position i receives exactly the fixed, source-independent positional features (1,i,i²,ln i). No p, state×position product or log probability is supplied by preprocessing. ln i is defined even for the first BOS (ln 1=0). Its generation, range and precision are paid in §6.

Use width 128 and eight heads of dimension 16 per block, so scaled attention scores are Q·K/4. Embed the three-block fixed-parameter decoder unchanged into the first coordinates, zero-extending its matrices; read that candidate with its boundary and prior-art addendum. Its two lag-copy heads, gating block and four final anchor heads produce, at the actual terminal position, a scalar p∈[0,1] with

|p−B_N| ≤ (N/2)e^(−N),
B_N=(C_(S_N)+1)/(J_(S_N)+2),   S_N=(X_(N−1),X_N).

All parameters in this stage are fixed and do not depend on source or N. Add one hidden ReLU unit in block 1 that writes m_i=ReLU(2−i): m₁=1 and m_i=0 for every integer i≥2. Alongside block 3’s four anchor heads, add a fifth head with Q=K=0 and value m_j. At each position i it outputs 1/i under the full causal mask. The terminal residual therefore contains

z=1/L,      p∈[0,1].

This quotient is produced by a standard zero-score softmax normalization, not by a supplied length or division feature. The model input contains unbounded i, i² and ln i, so the length is not hidden. Block 1 now uses two active heads; block 2 has zero attention value maps; block 3 uses five active heads. All unmentioned heads have zero output maps. Causal context copying and the two BOS boundary positions are as explicitly proved in the linked candidate.

2. Fourth block and standard final vocabulary softmax

At the terminal query, the two active fourth-block heads use

head 1: q=(8p,−4z,0,…),      head 0: q=(8(1−p),−4z,0,…),
k_j=(j,j²,0,…),               value_j=ln j.

Their standard scaled-dot-product scores, for all causal keys 1≤j≤L, are respectively

2pj−j²/L = Lp² − (j−Lp)²/L,
2(1−p)j−j²/L = L(1−p)² − (j−L(1−p))²/L.

The first terms are rowwise constants and do not affect softmax. Let J_p and J_(1−p) have the corresponding truncated discrete-Gaussian weights, and set

A=E ln J_p,    B=E ln J_(1−p),    G_p=e^A,    G_(1−p)=e^B.

The fourth-block attention outputs A,B into two residual slots. Its FFN is zero. The final linear-logit readout returns logits (A,B) for symbols (1,0); the ordinary two-class vocabulary softmax therefore predicts

P_N = e^A/(e^A+e^B) = G_p/(G_p+G_(1−p)).

There is no log(p), log(1−p), count division or query-specific positional feature supplied to the model. The extra logarithms are only the explicitly supplied position feature ln j. Parameters are length-independent. The positional score/query products are ordinary bilinear attention products, not precomputed products of i and a state.

3. A pathwise error bound, including p=0 and p=1

For L≥4 and μ∈[0,L], put w_j=e^(−(j−μ)²/L), 1≤j≤L, and Z=Σ_jw_j. At least ⌊√L⌋≥√L/2 integer positions are within √L of μ, even at either boundary, so Z≥√L/(2e). Each unit-distance bin from μ contains at most two integers. For t≥1, splitting the exponent in half gives

P(|J−μ|≥t) ≤ 24 exp(−(t−1)²/(2L)).

Indeed Σ_{k≥0}e^(−k²/(2L))≤1+√(πL/2), and for L≥4 the prefactor after division by Z is at most 4e(1/√L+√(π/2))<24. Set

T_L=1+√[2L ln(24L²)],     d_L=[2T_L+1+ln L]/L.

Then P(|J−μ|≥T_L)≤L⁻². Let G=exp(E ln J). Since 1≤J≤L and ln J≥0, Jensen gives G≤EJ≤μ+T_L+1/L. If μ≤2T_L, the trivial G≥1 bounds μ−G≤2T_L. If μ>2T_L, on the good event J≥μ−T_L>1 and therefore

G ≥ (μ−T_L)^(1−L⁻²) ≥ μ−T_L−(ln L)/L.

Consequently, uniformly for every p∈[0,1],

|G_p/L−p| ≤ d_L,    |G_(1−p)/L−(1−p)| ≤ d_L.

These estimates use only log J for J≥1, never log p. If d_L≤1/4, the denominator (G_p+G_(1−p))/L≥1−2d_L and direct algebra gives |P_N−p|≤d_L/(1−2d_L)≤2d_L. If d_L>1/4, the trivial error bound 1 suffices. Thus for every word and every N≥2,

|P_N−B_N| ≤ (N/2)e^(−N) + min{1,4d_(N+2)}
                    = O(√(ln N/N)).

The bound includes p=0 and p=1; no lower bound on the empirical query count is assumed.

4. Actual-terminal uniform average risk

Taking expectation and then the supremum over sources preserves the pointwise error. The separately reviewed R011 result gives worst-case actual-terminal average-TV risk for add-one at most min{1,2¹³√[32/(N−1)]} for N≥3, over all homogeneous binary order-two tables with arbitrary initial pair law. Every finite pair transition matrix has a stationary distribution and the theorem’s β=0 minorization condition is vacuous. At N=2 use the trivial TV cap. Therefore

sup_(all homogeneous order-two tables and initial laws) risk(P_N)
 ≤ min{1, 2¹³√[32/(N−1)] + (N/2)e^(−N) + 4d_(N+2)} → 0.

This is a worst-case-per-N statement for one source-independent fixed parameter set, not merely fixed-source convergence. The R011 corollary is an inherited result, not reproved here; an accessible release needs its actual proof and review. This rate has a √ln N factor and does not claim the sharper O(N−1/2) rate for the complete vocabulary-softmax decoder.

5. Source-independent, length-independent weights versus position cost

There are four fixed-width blocks, width 128, eight allocated heads per block and nine active head instances: two copy heads, no active attention in block 2, four anchor heads plus one reciprocal-length head in block 3, and two Gaussian/log-position heads in block 4. The copy and match scores grow as in the linked three-block candidate: block 1 has O(N³) raw magnitudes, block 3 score gaps N. Fourth-block scores are O(N) in the exact model. Dense causal evaluation uses Θ(N²) query-key pairs per active head, apart from constant-width FFNs and paid positional setup. The final output is now an ordinary two-class vocabulary softmax of linear logits.

The position generator is a substantive extra assumption, not a free neural log oracle. Exact ln i cannot be stored exactly in a finite bit string; in the exact-real model it is a prescribed source-independent real feature. For a finite approximation, write j=2^k x with k=⌊log₂j⌋ and 1≤x<2, t=(x−1)/(x+1)∈[0,1/3). Then

ln j = k ln 2 + 2Σ_(n≥0) t^(2n+1)/(2n+1),
ln 2 = 2Σ_(n≥0) (1/3)^(2n+1)/(2n+1).

Truncation after K terms has a geometric tail O(9^(−K)); choose K=O(log N+log(1/ρ)) for absolute positional error ρ. For ρ=1/poly(N), exact rational generation and rounding require polylog(N) bit operations and temporary bits per position; the rounded table occupies O(N log N) bits if O(log N) fractional bits are retained. Position i and i² also require O(log N) integer bits each. This is extra source-independent numerical preprocessing, not a claim of a conventional fixed-word positional encoder.

6. Finite-bit bridge is not yet an end-to-end proof

If the third stage supplies p̂∈[0,1] and ẑ with |ẑ−1/L|≤ε_z, and the fourth block computes scores for p̂ with additional absolute error ε_score, each Gaussian head score differs from the ideal-at-p̂ score by at most δ=L²ε_z+ε_score. The sharp softmax TV bound then makes the weighted log-position output differ by at most (ln L)tanh(δ/2), plus separately paid value error ρ and numerical-softmax error τ ln L. A final two-logit softmax adds at most half the maximum logit error, plus its own evaluation/rounding error. The earlier fixed-parameter count decoder’s p error must also be paid. For example, bounds ε_z=O(L−3), ε_score=o(1/ln L), ρ=o(1), τ=o(1/ln L), and vanishing final arithmetic error are sufficient for consistency.

This is a conditional error budget, not a demonstrated generic IEEE or fixed-word implementation. A complete backend must establish all those per-operation bounds, prevent overflow/underflow, pay score magnitudes and summation, and verify that earlier causal-copy gate margins survive. Merely noting that the ideal matrices are fixed does not establish any of that.

7. Prior art and caveat

Ekbote et al., D’Angelo et al. and He et al. already study closely related k-gram, attention smoothing or positional-copy mechanisms. A targeted source comparison records their stated model and limit differences. The logarithmic positional feature and discrete-Gaussian readout here must be compared against further literature; no originality, training, general lower bound or peer-review claim is made. The zero-logit and score-growth assumptions are architectural choices and must not be conflated with bounded activations or bounded logits.
