Uniform conditioned risk for ideal soft context matching

3 November 2026. Analytical derivation for a declared ideal estimator and source class. No random, numerical or exhaustive experiment was used. This is not a novelty claim or a lower bound for unrestricted Transformers.

Finding. For a stationary binary order-two source with a summably uniformly mixing reverse pair chain, the same-history risk conditional on the actual terminal pair s separates into the exact population soft-matching bias and a finite-history fluctuation bound. The fluctuation term depends on weighted stationary occupancy b_s(β)≥π_s, without assuming the observed count denominator is deterministically large. A previously studied uniformly mixing rare-state family shows the dependence on e^(−β)/π_s in the population bias is sharp in order.

1. Assumptions and exact output

Let P be the irreducible finite pair-state transition matrix of a stationary homogeneous binary order-two process, with stationary law π and row next-bit-one probabilities θ_r. Fix terminal pair s with π_s>0; irreducibility makes all four masses positive for the legal shift chain. Observe X₁,…,X_N and define M=N−2≥0 and S_N=(X_{N−1},X_N). The target is θ_s and all risk statements condition on S_N=s. The same rule, with no knowledge of π, P or θ, is used for every source and terminal query.

The reverse kernel is R(x,y)=π_y P(y,x)/π_x. Assume nonincreasing deterministic bounds d_h∈[0,1], d₀=1,

sup_x ||R^h(x,·)−π||TV ≤ d_h,    D=Σₕ₌₀∞ d_h<∞.

Bounds hold source by source, with a uniform constant for any class sharing a common upper bound on D. Stationary initialization and summable reverse mixing are substantive assumptions; no claim is made for arbitrary nonstationary or nonmixing sources.

Fix any deterministic β≥0, possibly depending on M, let z=e^(−β)∈(0,1], and set weights w_s(r)=z^Hamming(r,s), w_s(s)=1. The z=0 hard-match limit can be included by continuity. On the actual history let

W = Σᵢ₌₃ᴺ w_s(Xᵢ₋₂Xᵢ₋₁),
V = Σᵢ₌₃ᴺ w_s(Xᵢ₋₂Xᵢ₋₁) 1{Xᵢ=1},       Q=(V+1)/(W+2).

This is D’Angelo et al., Proposition 3.1, equation (8) for k=2, binary alphabet, equal β and κ=2β+ln 2, after dividing by e^(2β). The +1 and +2 are BOS pseudo-counts, not extra observed transitions. Their circuit proof uses limiting copying and early-position suppression; this result does not claim a fully finite-logit implementation. The last eligible successor is X_N, not X_{N+1}. For M=0 the natural empty-count extension gives Q=1/2; the separate edge case does not assert that the paper states an empty-index convention.

2. Risk and population decomposition

For each s define

b_s = Σ_r π_r w_s(r) ≥ π_s,       a_s = Σ_r π_r w_s(r) θ_r,
q_s = a_s/b_s,                    bias_s=|q_s−θ_s|,
ℛ_s=E[|Q−θ_s| | S_N=s],           x=M b_s.

Set H_D(0)=1. For x>0 let

H_D(x)=min{1, [2√((1+4D)(x+D)) + 2D+3]/x}.

Then for every M≥0 and β≥0,

max{0,bias_s−H_D(Mb_s)} ≤ ℛ_s ≤ min{1,bias_s+H_D(Mb_s)},
 bias_s = |Σ_(r≠s) π_r w_s(r)(θ_r−θ_s)| / b_s
        ≤ (1−π_s)z / [π_s+(1−π_s)z].

For x≫D, H_D(x)=O_D(x−1/2); for a sequence of sources the stated dependence on D must be retained. The x=0 convention is a valid trivial bound. Population bias may be much smaller than its upper envelope because signed differences can cancel; the upper bound is not claimed to be an equality for every source.

3. Reverse-edge proof, including endpoint bias

Conditioning the stationary trajectory on its actual terminal pair gives the reverse chain Z₀=s, Z_i=S_{N−i}, i=1,…,M, with kernel R. For an observed forward transition Z_i→Z_{i−1}, the successor symbol is the final bit of Z_{i−1}. Thus the weighted success and total counts are the sums of the adjacent-edge functions

f_i = w_s(Z_i) 1{lastbit(Z_{i−1})=1},   g_i=w_s(Z_i),
V=Σᵢ₌₁ᴹ f_i,                                W=Σᵢ₌₁ᴹ g_i.

Both are in [0,1] and f_i≤g_i. These are not assumed to be independent, and f_i depends on both adjacent states. Let h_f(x)=Σ_y R(x,y)w_s(y)1{lastbit(x)=1} and h_g(x)=Σ_y R(x,y)w_s(y). Each lies in [0,1]. By π_xR(x,y)=π_yP(y,x), their stationary means are respectively a_s and b_s.

The nonstationary start Z₀=s creates a boundary deviation, paid explicitly: for either edge function,

|E_s f_i−a_s|≤d_(i−1),   |E_s g_i−b_s|≤d_(i−1),
|E_s V−Ma_s|≤D,   |E_s W−Mb_s|≤D,
E_s V≤Mb_s+D,     E_s W≤Mb_s+D.

For 1≤i<j≤M, conditioning on the history through Z_i leaves j−i−1 reverse steps before the starting point of edge j. Hence

|E_s[f_j | Z₀,…,Z_i]−a_s| ≤ d_(j−i−1),
|E_s f_j−a_s| ≤ d_(j−1) ≤ d_(j−i−1),
|Cov_s(f_i,f_j)| ≤ 2 E_s[f_i] d_(j−i−1).

Exactly the same argument holds for g. With D=Σ_{h≥0}d_h, and f_i²≤f_i, g_i²≤g_i,

Var_s(V), Var_s(W) ≤ (1+4D)(Mb_s+D).

Consequently E_s|V−Ma_s| and E_s|W−Mb_s| are each at most √((1+4D)(Mb_s+D))+D. No deterministic lower bound on W is used. Instead, since 0≤Q≤1, the exact identity

Mb_s(Q−q_s) = (V+1−Ma_s) + Q(Mb_s−W−2)

implies E_s|Q−q_s|≤H_D(Mb_s) for M≥1. Reverse triangle and triangle inequalities complete the two-sided risk statement. For M=0 use Q=1/2 and H_D(0)=1 directly.

4. Rare-state dependence cannot be dropped uniformly

Take the stationary family 0<u<1/4, w=(1−u)/3, π=(u,w,w,w) and θ=(1/4,1/2,1−u/(4w),1/2). Query s=00, so π_s=u. Its legal pair chain is irreducible. The stationary weighted occupancy and bias are

b₀₀=u+2wz+wz²,
q₀₀−1/4 = [z(w−u/4)+(w/4)z²]/[u+2wz+wz²]
          ≥ (3/16) z/(u+z).

Indeed w≥1/4 and u≤w give w−u/4≥3/16, while u+2wz+wz²≤u+3wz≤u+z. The upper envelope in §2 has order min{1,z/u}, as does z/(u+z).

The reverse-chain mixing constant remains uniform as u varies. Specifically P²(11,·)=(u/(8w), [1−u/(4w)]/2, 1/4, 1/4). Reversal gives R²(a,11)=wP²(11,a)/π_a≥1/8 for all four states. Thus a common-atom contraction permits d_h=(7/8)^⌊h/2⌋ and D≤16 independently of u. Applying §2 gives, for every M≥0,

ℛ₀₀ ≥ max{0, (3/16)z/(u+z) − H₁₆(M[u+2wz+wz²])}.

For each fixed u>0 and z∈[0,1], the fluctuation term vanishes as M→∞; hence the population-bias envelope’s min{1,z/u} dependence cannot be uniformly replaced by a smaller order on this source family. This is a lower bound for the fixed ideal interpolation output, not a statistical minimax or full-network architecture lower bound. The finite-M expression is only useful when its explicit fluctuation term is smaller than the bias; no uniform sharp statistical lower bound is asserted.

5. Prior art and scope

The exact weighted-count formula and its finite-β partial matching are from D’Angelo et al., Proposition 3.1; Corollary 4.1 gives the BOS add-α hard-match limit, and §4.2 and Lemma 4.2 already discuss partial matches and temperature scaling under a different criterion. The reverse-chain and mixing arguments use classical Markov tools; see Levin, Peres and Wilmer, Markov Chains and Mixing Times. This is a source-specific analytical application, not a claim that either mechanism or the proof tools are new.

The risk is conditional on the actual terminal state, not a separately sampled query. Constants depend on reverse mixing; b_s and π_s are analysis quantities, not information supplied to the estimator. Results do not cover nonstationary initialization, arbitrary nonmixing chains, path-dependent β, full Transformer networks, finite-precision implementations or training. They do not establish that matching bias always dominates statistical noise, nor do they give a matching statistical lower bound.
