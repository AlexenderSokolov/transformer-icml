# Central robust-prediction prior-art audit

Date: 2026-11-03. Targeted theorem-subsumption check, not an exhaustive survey or priority certificate. Local research only; no external writes. Primary-paper downloads/extractions are outside this research directory and must not be bundled for public release.

## Finding

The defensible candidate contribution is the **observation-dependent, same-map budget-adaptation theorem in the precise protected-terminal experiment**, not robust estimation, unknown-contamination adaptation, Markov next-symbol prediction, count-compatible path rearrangement, generic constrained risk, or logarithmic quantization separately. No checked theorem in this pass supplies the full conjunction. This is a statement about the inspected sources, not evidence of priority or a claim that the result cannot be deduced from other work.

A relevant addition to the current bibliography is **Maity–Mitra (AISTATS 2025), Theorem 2**, a genuine robust Markovian mean-estimation result, not an IID-only comparison. Its model and algorithm still differ materially from this experiment. The most important existing subsumption test remains **Jain–Orlitsky–Ravindrakumar (JOR), Lemmas 1 and 3**: their selector can handle a random terminal target pointwise, but requires jointly valid candidate radii, not separate expected-risk guarantees.

The new approximate-statistics and fixed-bank precision results do not erase these boundaries. ACG001 gives a particular gate's perturbation tolerance; PBP001 gives a matching lower bound for twelve specified deterministic rounded rails. Neither is a general neural or encoder limitation.

## Exact object being compared

Local manuscript: `resumption_20261103/recovered_science/positional_manuscript_finite_20261102/main.tex`, with positive, negative and finite-horizon proof sections. Binary order-two source; M departures; hidden arbitrary initial pair law; supplied all-state certificate P²(u,v) >= Pi(v)/M including states outside stationary support. A common offline kernel on words, not given the unknown source parameters, changes at most k of the first M symbols on **every** input/output and protects the final pair. The predictor's random seed is independent. Loss is unconditional expected unsquared TV at the actual protected terminal pair.

The known-budget rate is g_M(k)=max(M^(-1/2),k/M). The same predictor map at a fixed M must serve all k. Exact triple counts plus first/last r symbols incur optimal competitive order 1+log((M+2)/r); the upper only uses the suffix. A whole-word scan can instead compute a half-count gate bit, with counts and endpoint uncharged. Neither result is a lower bound at every k or against all compact statistics.

The load-bearing positive mechanism is the reciprocal-occupation estimate gated by older visits; the lower needs both an occupation-mixture domination and an exact histogram/boundary-preserving legal transport. These are the right places to test claimed specificity.

## Three proposed reductions, and exactly where they stop

### 1. Feed known-budget predictors into a generic selector

At each corrupted observation z, all candidates query the same protected pair, so their target is a well-defined point in the binary probability simplex. JOR's pointwise geometry is available. The missing premise is joint accuracy with sufficiently cheap tails over every candidate above the true budget. Markov's inequality applied to each expected oracle bound incurs failure/radius costs; a union bound does not remove them. A valid alternative proof would need to demonstrate those costs stay O(g_M(k)) uniformly, using only the allowed observation. The accepted count-only lower theorem, if correct, rules out such a construction for that information cut. It does not invalidate JOR.

### 2. Treat row departures as IID data or treat triples as independently contaminated

Independent transition stacks generate the clean chain, but their used prefix lengths and selected terminal row depend on those stacks. Conditioning on these choices is not an independence theorem. Edits also change context membership, neighboring triples and denominators, not just successor values. One raw edit affects at most three triples, which gives an upper sensitivity bound. The converse is false: arbitrary contaminated triple histograms need not be realizable by a nearby word. Therefore a generic contamination lower bound does not certify the project's strict-k lower kernel.

### 3. Replace the bespoke lower by constrained risk

Generic triangle/change-of-measure or Duchi–Ruan comparison can replace a comparison step once actual observation laws have been constructed. The nonstandard burden remains: all proposal/null sources satisfy the same supplied certificate, the same map is good on clean alternatives, histogram and sampled boundaries match exactly, the attack is one legal word kernel rather than a source-indexed prescription, and its budget holds on every path. Count-preserving relocation plus mixture domination are substantive obligations not contained in a generic information inequality. This is the appropriate narrow candidate novelty claim, subject to wider literature review.

## Bounded-reading caution on Bhatt's adaptation argument

This is **not a claim that its theorem is false**. On PDF page 9, §6 (17) chooses the largest prefix of confidence intervals with nonempty intersection; (18) returns that interval's center. On PDF page 17, Appendix A.7 (60) controls simultaneous coverage of valid-budget intervals, and (61) then states coverage for the selected interval. We did not establish that stronger step from the displayed definitions and surrounding bounds. As a purely geometric warning, with target 0, a valid interval [-1,1] and an invalid smaller-radius interval [1/2,3/2] intersect, yet the latter excludes 0. This example is not claimed to be an instance of their M-estimator. JOR-style distance comparisons may justify a weaker bound without that coverage claim. No repaired constants or invalidity assertion is made. The paper remains an explicit prior confidence-adaptation construction; our non-subsumption decision does not depend on this caveat.

## Consequences for ACG001 and PBP001

Both root acceptance receipts were read. ACG001 permits coordinate count error e and output error xi, paying O(e/M)+xi in addition to the robust benchmark. It does not preserve the discontinuous gate bit exactly. Its clipping/ratio perturbation argument is elementary; the application-specific content is compatibility with the accepted terminal occupation/half-count bounds. PBP001 proves Θ(max(M^(-1/2),2^(-h))) only for twelve separately nearest-rounded normalized J/C/D coordinates plus exact endpoint. Its lower already uses clean stationary IID Bernoulli paths, monotone mean curves, a common quantization cell, bounded differences and a two-point comparison. These are standard statistical/quantization methods. Do not promote this to total bit complexity, arbitrary encoders, network precision, training or architectural superiority. The earlier reliability audit already identifies Xu–Raginsky's absolute-loss Bernoulli communication example as a close broader antecedent; that source was not re-audited here.

## Recommended claim wording

“We characterize the uniform unknown-budget competitive price of exact triple counts and prescribed boundary windows for protected-terminal prediction in a binary order-two Markov edit experiment. A positional gate achieves the known-budget rate with a computed supplemental bit; the lower combines a constrained-risk comparison with a legal count-preserving path transport. We additionally analyze approximate positional statistics and one fixed rounded-bank interface.”

Keep the complete assumptions visible. Avoid “first robust Markov prediction,” “unknown contamination makes adaptation impossible,” “counts are insufficient under corruption,” “one bit of raw positional access suffices,” or “optimal precision for neural prediction.” No originality/priority verdict has been established.
