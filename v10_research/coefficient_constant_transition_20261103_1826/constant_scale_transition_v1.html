Constant transition for the specified positive copy-coefficient error

3 November 2026. This exact-real candidate awaits independent analytic review. It studies the same four-block, no-LN graph and fixed stationary binary source as the c=2 result, including that result's moving-target clarification. The new argument below does not extrapolate from the earlier qN regime. No tests, enumeration or old-file overwrite were used.

Claims for fixed c. Put L=N+2 and perturb only the lag-one score coefficient by ηN=c/L. For every fixed 0<c<1/2, all sufficiently large N have exactly the same output on every word as the unperturbed graph. For every fixed 1/2<c<3/2, the actual-terminal expected TV risk on the specified source instead tends to (11c−3)/[16(4c−1)]>0. The threshold endpoints c=1/2 and c=3/2, c depending on N, other signs, coefficients and architectures are not decided here.

1. The source and score

The model has two BOS positions, data Xk at i=k+2, fixed causal masks, and the same score, residual, snap, context gates, four anchors and ordinary final vocabulary softmax as the linked graph. All weights are unchanged except the lag-one Q entry that makes its score

s(i,j)=2i(i−1)j−(1+c/L)ij²,   1≤j≤i≤L.

Thus the perturbed model varies with N and the changed scalar weight differs by 4c/L; no θ, future token or token×position feature is supplied. The source is fixed, initialized at its uniform stationary pair law, with

θ₀₀(1)=3/4,  θ₁₀(1)=1/4,  θ₀₁(1)=θ₁₁(1)=1/2.

All one-step symbol probabilities are ≥1/4, so P²(s,t)≥1/16 for every pair-state s,t; the source is irreducible, aperiodic, uniformly fast mixing and genuinely second order. The target is the true next-symbol distribution at SN=(XN−1,XN), on the same observed trajectory.

2. Fixed c below 1/2: wordwise exact equality eventually

Take 0<c<1/2, r=i−1 and j≠r. Since j,r≤i, |j²−r²|≤2i|j−r|, so

s(i,r)−s(i,j)
 ≥ i(j−r)²[1−2ci/L].

The function f(i)=i−2ci²/L is concave. If L≥max{4,32c,3/(1−2c)}, then f(4)=4−32c/L≥3 and f(L)=L(1−2c)≥3; hence f(i)≥3 for every 4≤i≤L. Summing over integer distances as in the reviewed safe-window proof yields wrong mass ≤2/(e³−1)<1/4 for every required row and every word. The fixed snap exactly recovers each valid lag-one bit; lag two and all other parameters are unchanged.

Valid candidate-context gates, terminal actual-query onehot, anchors, BOS contributions, length marker and final fourth-block logits are consequently identical to the original graph. Raw copy outputs and unused early residual slots can differ but have no route into that terminal output. Therefore for every data word and all such N, Pc,N=P0,N exactly. In particular the original fixed-source TV risk tends to zero. This safe result is eventually wordwise; it does not claim the displayed bound holds at smaller L or at c=1/2.

3. Fixed c in (1/2,3/2): deterministic segmented drift

Now fix 1/2<c<3/2, set D=L+c and r=i−1. The lag-one score is a row constant minus (1+c/L)i(j−[r−ζᵢ])² with ζᵢ=cr/D. For i≤L, ζᵢ∈[0,c), so the only possible nearest integer shifts asymptotically are 0 and 1. The half-integer thresholds correspond to r₁=D/(2c) and r₂=3D/(2c). The first is inside the data range; for fixed c<3/2, r₂−L is positive and of order L.

Let H=⌈100 ln L⌉+10. For all sufficiently large L, r₂−L>H. Discard rows i<H and rows |r−r₁|≤H, in total O(log L), including any exact half-integer ties. For every remaining row, the correct winner has score gap at least H/2. If i<D/(4c), then ζᵢ<1/4, the winning shift is 0 and the gap is at least i/2≥H/2. Otherwise i≥D/(4c); the distance to both half-integer thresholds exceeds H in r coordinates, so the gap is at least i·2cH/D≥H/2. Every other key is no closer than the runner-up. Its total softmax mass is at most L e−H/2≤L⁻⁴⁹<1/4, so the original snap is exact on good data rows.

With data index k=i−2, the original lag-two head supplies Xk−2. The perturbed lag-one head supplies Xk−1 in the first segment and Xk−2 in the second, with respective limiting lengths

α=1/(2c) ∈ (1/3,1),  and  1−α.

Consequently the corresponding context gates are onehot for (Xk−2,Xk−1) and (Xk−2,Xk−2), respectively. At the terminal i=L, ζL=c(L−1)/(L+c)→c∈(1/2,3/2); the nearest shift is 1 with a gap growing linearly in L. Hence for all large N the actual terminal query is onehot for (XN−2,XN).

4. Anchor limits from a single dependent history

Let ra=P(Xₖ=1|Xk−2=a), so r₀=5/8, r₁=3/8. Write Jab and Cab for good matching counts and matching successor-one counts. The finite irreducible stationary pair-chain ergodic theorem applied to the finitely many relevant fixed-window indicators, followed by subtraction of prefix sums at the deterministic boundary, gives almost surely

J_ab/N → α/4 + (1−α)·1{a=b}/2,
C_ab/N → α θ_ab/4 + (1−α)r_a·1{a=b}/2.

This accounts for the actual dependent sequence: it assumes neither iid observations nor an independent query. At most O(log N) excluded keys have arbitrary valid-gate values in [0,1]; the two BOS values and those keys have total scaled weight O(log N) after dividing attention weights by eN. Good nonmatches contribute at most N e−N. Each limiting J is positive because α>0, so the four anchor estimates converge jointly almost surely to

p_00=(5−2α)/[4(2−α)],    p_01=1/2,
p_10=1/4,                   p_11=(3−α)/[4(2−α)].

The actual query chooses pXₙ₋₂,Xₙ. The final fourth-block Gaussian/log-position softmax approximation is uniformly at most 168/√(N+2), and therefore the final probability PN satisfies E|PN−pXₙ₋₂,Xₙ|→0. This compares with a moving random target; it does not assert that the target itself converges.

5. Exact expected terminal risk

Let a=XN−2, b=XN−1, z=XN. Their joint distribution is P(a,b)=1/4 and P(z=1|a,b)=θab. The prediction tends in the moving-target sense above to pa,z, while the true next-one probability is θb,z. Because α∈(1/3,1), one has 1/4<p₀₀<3/4 and p₁₁<1/2. The reverse triangle inequality, stationarity and this fixed finite joint law yield

lim_N E TV(P_N,θ_(X_(N−1),X_N))
 = (1/4) Σ_(a,b) [(1−θ_ab)|p_a0−θ_b0| + θ_ab|p_a1−θ_b1|]
 = (11−6α)/[32(2−α)] = (11c−3)/[16(4c−1)] >0.

For instance c=1 gives 1/6; as c decreases to 1/2 from above, the limit approaches 5/32. The discontinuous comparison of the two fixed-c open intervals does not resolve c=1/2 itself or a boundary layer c=cN approaching 1/2 with N. The pair of statements identifies a critical constant in this restricted positive-c fixed-c sense, not a universal or finite-N sharp robustness theorem.

6. Prior art and exclusions

Liu et al., Appendix C.3, Proposition 4 already analyze positional QK constraints and length-dependent drift in a distinct one-head flip-flop model. Chen et al. and Castin et al. give related copier/output and attention-smoothness bounds under their own assumptions. This note adds only an explicit calculation in the declared graph and fixed Markov source; no novelty or publication claim follows. It does not cover simultaneous errors, other weights or signs, learned parameters, finite precision, general normalization, or all Transformers.
