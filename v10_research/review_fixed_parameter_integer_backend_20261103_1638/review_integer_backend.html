1. Exponential table and row normalization

Let H=2b, F=H² and a=1−1/H. The initial z/F is exactly a. At each squaring, both the exact and rounded quantities lie in [0,1], so the absolute error is at most twice the preceding error plus 1/F. After b squarings the error is at most (2b−1)/F<1/H, and the ideal power is aH. The logarithmic inequalities in the manuscript bound its difference from e−1 by 1/(H−1). Flooring r introduces less than another 1/H, yielding the stated strict 4/H bound for H≥2.

For k≤b, induction on the nonnegative table recurrence gives |T[k]/H−e−k|≤5k/H; for k>b, the zero weight differs by less than 1/H because e−b<2−b. Thus ε=(5b+1)/H uniformly bounds each unnormalized weight error. T[0]=H ensures the approximate denominator is at least one after rescaling, while the exact denominator is also at least one. For x in [0,1], comparing numerator and denominator gives error at most 2Σ|Δw|. Integer floor division contributes less than 1/H and preserves [0,1]. Both the 2x representation of the BOS half-values and the row-specific max shift are exact.

With s=⌈log₂ L⌉≥2 and b=16s, 80s+1≤28s, L≤2s, and therefore Lε≤2−7s≤L−7; 1/H≤L−7. The quoted 3L−7 row bound follows, for the same values and logits.

2. Gates and three-block composition

The block-1 score formula is exactly the stated scaled dot product. At i≥4 for lag one and i≥5 for lag two, the exact wrong-attention mass is at most 2/(e⁴−1)<2/15. The numerical row error is at most 3/4⁷<1/15. The sum is below 1/5 and thus below the 1/4 snapping threshold. T(a) consequently maps the intended binary predecessor to its exact bit. At all earlier positions a∈[0,1] and T(a)∈[0,1].

The positional gates give bj=1 at precisely the two BOS positions, and vj=1 precisely at j≥5. Nonnegative mismatch makes every g zero at invalid positions; at valid positions the exact snapped bits make g a binary one-hot context indicator. Thus all block-3 logits are exact integers, even though early t coordinates may be fractional. At the final position, t₁ and the current bit give a one-hot q, so the last ReLU sum selects exactly one [0,1] head. Applying the row bound to that head and the source's pathwise comparison yields |Q̃−BN|≤(N/2)e−N+3L−7. Direct output (Q̃,1−Q̃) is a valid probability pair. The absence of accumulated score error depends on the exact gate margins and on the special algorithm's use of exact integer scores.

3. Boundary N=2

L=4; lag one at the terminal copies the first data bit with the established margin, while v=0 for all positions, so there are no valid triples. The terminal selector encodes (X₁,X₂). Both BOS positions have matching score N and values 1/2; the two data keys have zero score. W≥H and B₂=1/2. The output need not equal 1/2 exactly, but the stated bound applies. The addendum correctly restricts direct use of R011 to N≥3 and uses TV≤1 for N=2. No independent validation of R011 is claimed.

4. Resource accounting and interface

The magnitude of a block-1 score is bounded by the manuscript's conservative 3L³, hence a shifted gap by 6L³. Block-3 scores are between 0 and L−2. Positions, scores, counters and gaps need O(log L) bits. Fixed-point values need O(b) bits, table squaring uses operands O(b) bits and products O(b) bits, and row sums and scaled numerators need O(b+log L) bits. Since b=16⌈log₂L⌉, all are O(log L) bits. Grade-school multiplication/division costs O((log L)²) bit operations per operation; O(log L) table updates cost O((log L)³). Two scans per row, six dense causal active heads, and the remaining gates/positions give O(L²(log L)²+(log L)³) bit operations. The length-O(log L) table stores O((log L)²) bits. Streaming residual/position arrays use O(L log L) bits; scores need not be materialized quadratically. A sequential table scan per lookup also fits this bound, avoiding reliance on an uncharged large random-access table.

Recommended wording correction: “Six active full-causal heads require Θ(L²) score/weight visits” should say that the specified straightforward dense evaluation makes Θ(L²) visits. It is not a proven lower bound for all algorithms: this review does not rule out exploiting special logits or computing only the required outputs more cheaply. The stated upper bound itself is supported for the dense evaluator. Count zero heads as part of the model graph, even when their zero contributions are skipped.

The backend is deterministic, arbitrary-precision fixed-point integer arithmetic with a deliberately truncated exponential table. It is not a standard IEEE floating-point or generic library softmax implementation, a fixed-word guarantee, or a conventional final vocabulary-softmax decoder. The manuscript states these limitations appropriately. Its inherited risk corollary remains conditional on R011 and its assumptions.
