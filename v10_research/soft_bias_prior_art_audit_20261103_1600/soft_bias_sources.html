Source audit: soft matching, Markov regression and terminal risk

3 November 2026. Targeted source-by-source comparison, not an exhaustive search or a novelty determination. Primary-source versions and assumptions below matter.

Summary. The finite-temperature masked-count output, partial-context smoothing and logarithmic temperature calibration have close direct antecedents. Normalized softmax attention as kernel smoothing, the bias–variance/occupancy perspective for Markov-dependent designs, and terminal-conditioned transition-count moment bounds also predate this application. The reviewed bridge combines these ingredients for a particular ideal BOS output, reverse-chain condition on the actual terminal pair, explicit finite-history bound and a rare-state family. This comparison does not certify that combination as new, nor imply a full-network temperature lower bound.

Scope of the compared result: stationary binary order-two chain with π_s>0 and summably mixing reverse pair chain; actual observed terminal state S_N=s; weights w_s(r)=exp(−β·Hamming(s,r)), deterministic β≥0; M=N−2 observed transitions; Q=(V+1)/(W+2); population a=Σπ_rw_s(r)θ_r, b=Σπ_rw_s(r), q=a/b. Its stated conditional error separates |q−θ_s| from H_D(Mb), where D sums reverse-mixing bounds. For a specified rare family π₀₀=u, it exhibits population bias Ω(z/(u+z)), z=e^(−β). The source-specific calculation is not a minimax or general-network theorem.

1. Masked interpolation and BOS: direct prior formula

D’Angelo et al., Induction Heads Interpolate N-Grams, arXiv v1, Proposition 3.1 and equation (8) derive mask-weighted next-token counts for their constructed induction-head circuit. Specializing to binary k=2, equal matching scores β, and κ=2β+ln2, then dividing numerator and denominator by e^(2β), gives exactly the studied Hamming weights and the +1/+2 BOS counts. Their positional-copy and early-suppression limits remain part of the interpretation; finite β alone does not make the entire ideal circuit finite-logit.

Corollary 4.1 treats add-α via BOS in the hard-match limit. Lemma 4.1, Corollary 4.2 and equations (14)–(15) already give the interpolation and structured partial-match pseudo-count viewpoint. Lemma 4.2 and Appendix D.3 approximately calibrate expected no-BOS pseudo-counts; the displayed β is logarithmic in sequence length when alphabet, order and positive prior parameters are fixed. Its prior-averaged calibration is not the same as conditioning on a realized terminal state or the rare-u source-specific rate. Neither interpolation nor logarithmic temperature scaling should be called a new mechanism.

2. Softmax and kernel regression: established overlap

Goel and Bartlett, Can a Transformer Represent a Kalman Filter?, 2024, Theorem 1 and equation (3) explicitly represent a Gaussian Nadaraya–Watson smoother with a softmax self-attention block using a nonlinear embedding. The general identity behind the present case is more elementary: exponentiated scores normalize nonnegative kernel weights and form a weighted average of values. On binary pairs, exp(−β Hamming) is a finite-state kernel; the BOS term is equivalent to the prescribed pseudocount contribution. Thus the kernel-smoother interpretation and population weighted-mean bias are standard algebra, not new statistical principles. Goel–Bartlett is a representability statement for its declared embedding; it does not by itself give this trajectory-conditioned risk bound.

3. Markov-dependent kernel regression: close statistical neighbor

Trottner, Covariate shift in nonparametric regression with Markovian design, §3, Theorem 4.1, Theorem 4.3 and Proposition 5.1 studies Nadaraya–Watson estimates under Markov dependence. Theorem 4.1 bounds expected integrated squared error by a Hölder smoothing-bias term plus a sample-size/occupancy similarity term, using pseudo-spectral-gap assumptions; Proposition 5.1 specializes to finite chains. Section 3 separately bounds a reciprocal empirical occupation sum for a nonnegative function. Theorem 4.3 goes beyond an independent test sample and analyzes expected squared error at a point from the same target chain, with stated mixing and noise conditions. It would be inaccurate to describe Markov-kernel prior work as considering only iid data or independent test points.

Difference, not a claim of novelty: the cited Trottner estimator uses a uniform-ball kernel and its regression model assumes conditionally independent centered errors given the covariate chains; Theorem 4.3 also imposes its stated independent-noise form and mixing conditions. The response in a transition sample is part of the next state and overlapping trajectory. Its displayed risks are integrated/generalization or same-chain query MSE, not the reviewed fixed-event E[|Q−θ_s| | S_N=s] for the same edge history with a soft Hamming kernel and BOS terms. A direct application therefore requires verifying a mapping, not merely replacing squared loss by TV. Theorem 4.3 deserves specific citation because its same-chain query is substantially closer than independent-test-only comparisons suggest.

4. Terminal-conditioned Markov prediction

Han, Jana and Wu, Optimal prediction of Markov chains with and without spectral gap, arXiv v2, Lemma 16(i), (iii) already establish second-moment control for N_{ij}−N_i P(j|i) and fourth-moment control for state occupation, conditional on the actual final state X_n=i, for finite irreducible reversible chains with spectral-gap dependence. Their next-symbol prediction and add-one analysis is closer to the same-trajectory target than independent-query results. Their main prediction risk is averaged KL, and the cited lemma is not directly a theorem for the nonreversible pair-chain, soft Hamming weighted numerator/denominator and conditional binary TV bound. Their §5.3.3 also treats higher-order chains with separate assumptions; it should not be collapsed into the reversible lemma.

Levin, Peres and Wilmer, Markov Chains and Mixing Times provides standard time reversal and total-variation mixing/contraction tools. Starting the reverse chain at the observed terminal state, accounting for the boundary shift and applying covariance decay to adjacent-edge functions is a reasonable combination of classical tools, not evidence by itself of a novel principle. The exact application needs its own proof because the successor bit lives in the later forward state, the edge terms overlap, and the random soft count can be small.

5. What the explicit bridge does and does not show

The ratio q=a/b is a ratio of stationary weighted expectations, not E[V/W]. Its signed bias identity is ordinary weighted-average algebra; b≥π_s and mismatch weights ≤z yield the envelope (1−π_s)z/[π_s+(1−π_s)z]. A source family with no cancellation can make this envelope order-sharp. For the specified family, direct stationary-flow algebra gives b=u+2wz+wz² and bias at least (3/16)z/(u+z), while a reverse common-atom argument supplies a u-uniform mixing bound. These computations exhibit the rare-mass effect for this one ideal output map; the temperature sensitivity does not forbid a different estimator or extra network resources.

The reviewed proof then bounds the finite-history difference from q using the reverse edge covariance, an explicit start-up term D, and a ratio identity with population denominator Mb rather than a deterministic lower bound on W. This is a self-contained bridge under its hypotheses. The sources inspected above do not immediately furnish the exact displayed constants and same-event result with all those features in one quoted theorem; that observation is limited to this search and cannot support a general novelty assertion.

6. Search and limitations

Targeted searches covered softmax/Nadaraya–Watson representations, Markov-design kernel regression, same-chain prediction, terminal-conditioned transition counts, and temperature/rare-state attention. The primary papers above were inspected at the named propositions or sections, not just abstracts. This is not a systematic bibliography: related results may exist under dependent regression, time reversal, rare-event conditioning, nonparametric transition estimation, context trees, occupancy inequalities, or finite-alphabet smoothing. No claim is made that an exact equivalent is absent. The classification is a source audit, not formal peer review, and the referenced calculations require their own proof and hypotheses.
