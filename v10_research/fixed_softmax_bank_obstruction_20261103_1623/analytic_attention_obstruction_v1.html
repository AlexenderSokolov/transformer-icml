One extra softmax layer after a normalized histogram: a scoped obstruction

3 November 2026. This note investigates a specific fixed-parameter, position-homogeneous extension of a count-bank readout. It is not a general Transformer impossibility theorem or a claim of literature priority. No random or numerical experiment was used.

Finding. Even if a fixed finite extra softmax layer directly attends to all M historical candidates, a model of the precise type below cannot have its actual-terminal average binary-TV risk tend to zero for every source in the specified stationary, uniformly mixing order-two family. The proof uses real-analytic continuation of each finite ReLU branch. It does not cover position-dependent token features, scores that depend on N, unbounded length features, a division oracle, or general standard causal Transformers.

1. Source family, input and computation

Let X₁,…,X_N be observed, M=N−2, and condition on or average over the actual final pair S_N=(X_{N−1},X_N). For 0<u<1/4 and u/4<v<3u/4 set w=(1−u)/3, π=(u,w,w,w), and θ=(v/u,1/2,1−v/w,1/2). The source is stationary, irreducible and aperiodic. A compact rectangle such as 1/10<u<1/5, u/4<v<3u/4 has π_min≥1/10 and uniform reverse mixing sum at most 16. For the proof we use the larger connected wedge

Ω={(u,v):0<u<1/4,  u/4<v<3u/4}.

Let p_{r,y} be the empirical fraction of candidate triples of type (predecessor pair r, successor bit y), with the usual finite BOS/pseudo-count fractions and the actual terminal query also supplied to the final readout. As M→∞, these converge to the population frequencies

00: (u−v,v),    01: (w/2,w/2),
10: (v,w−v),   11: (w/2,w/2).

Every coordinate is affine in (u,v). The model is granted these contextualized triple types and the global normalized count bank; forming them from causal raw tokens, copying positions and broadcasting the bank to historical tokens are extra operations, not claimed free. We grant that access to avoid hiding information from the lower-bound model.

The permitted additional attention stage has any fixed finite number of heads and reads all M candidates. At the final query, the score and value assigned to a candidate may depend on its one of eight (r,y) types, the normalized bank and terminal query via fixed finite affine maps, bilinear dot products, ReLUs and standard fixed-size softmax modules. All candidates of the same type receive the same score and value: there is no position-varying or N-dependent input to this stage. A fixed number of special tokens, including BOS, may be present, with finite scores and values computed in the same way. The final output may use the bank, attention outputs and terminal query in any further fixed finite affine/ReLU/fixed-size-softmax network, optionally ending in a binary softmax or clipping to [0,1]. All parameters are finite, chosen once independently of source and N. No explicit division/rounding oracle, unbounded N or position channel, N-dependent parameters, hard context mask, or other sample-dependent residual is supplied.

This is a deliberately restricted, partly strengthened interface: it allows an extra head to see all history and even grants a global histogram broadcast, but forbids distinct positional features within a type. It is not an unrestricted two-layer causal Transformer. A numerical implementation is included only if its output approaches this fixed ideal-real computation with vanishing error on the relevant bounded inputs; that property is not proved here.

2. Limit of a homogeneous softmax head

Fix terminal query 00 and a source (u,v). By ergodicity, each empirical type fraction converges almost surely to its population probability p_a(u,v), and the fixed-size network inputs converge. For a head with type score ℓ_a and scalar value V_a, the finite-M output is, with a finite collection of BOS/special terms,

[Σ_a n_a exp(ℓ_a) V_a + special numerator]
 / [Σ_a n_a exp(ℓ_a) + special denominator].

After dividing by M, special-token terms vanish because their scores and values remain finite as the bank converges. The data term tends to

A(u,v) = [Σ_a p_a(u,v) exp(ℓ_a(b(u,v))) V_a(b(u,v))]
         / [Σ_a p_a(u,v) exp(ℓ_a(b(u,v)))].

Here b(u,v) is the population bank, and multiple heads or vector values are handled coordinatewise. The denominator is positive on Ω, because p_a≥0, Σp_a=1 and every finite exponential is positive. The limiting output for fixed terminal 00 is continuous in (u,v). This is the same history and actual terminal; no independent query is introduced.

3. Finite analytic branches rule out the ratio

A finite ReLU network has finitely many complete activation patterns. Freeze a pattern. On that branch, fixed-size softmax, exponentials, affine maps and products of query/key coordinates are real-analytic in (u,v). The denominator of every fixed-size softmax is strictly positive for real inputs. The additional historical-attention denominator is strictly positive on Ω for the reason above, even after arbitrary ReLU branches are frozen. At (u,v)=(0,0), the 00 types have zero mass but the other six types have positive total mass; with finite branch logits the denominator is again strictly positive. Thus each branch’s limiting output extends analytically to an open connected domain containing Ω and a neighborhood of the origin. For several finite heads take the intersection of their positive-denominator domains and its connected component containing Ω; positivity holds throughout Ω and at the origin.

The closed sign-pattern domains cover Ω. Finitely many closed sets cannot cover an open set without at least one having nonempty interior. On such an interior the model equals one analytic branch F_σ. Suppose, for contradiction, that its 00 prediction equaled the true target v/u for every source in Ω. Then the real-analytic function

G_σ(u,v)=uF_σ(u,v)−v

vanishes on a nonempty open set, hence by the real-analytic identity theorem vanishes on its entire connected analytic domain. That domain includes a neighborhood of (0,0), but ∂G_σ/∂v at (0,0) is −1. This is impossible. See Mityagin, on zero sets of real-analytic functions for a primary source on the identity principle and stronger zero-set statement. The same conclusion holds for an optional final sigmoid/softmax, which remains analytic on each branch.

Therefore every one fixed model has a source with limiting prediction gap Δ>0. The witness may depend on its parameters. By continuity, the gap is positive on an open parameter neighborhood, so a source prior with strictly positive density over the wedge gives positive limiting source-averaged loss for that fixed model. No parameter-uniform numerical lower constant over all widths or all weights is claimed.

4. Actual-terminal average risk

For the fixed witness, let Q_M(00) be the model’s output when evaluated with 00 as its terminal query; its almost-sure limit is F(u,v). Boundedness and dominated convergence give E|Q_M(00)−F(u,v)|→0. Since P(S_N=00)=u for every N, even though S_N depends on the history,

|E[1{S_N=00}|Q_M(00)−v/u|]−u|F(u,v)−v/u||
   ≤ E|Q_M(00)−F(u,v)| → 0.

The actual model’s unconditional loss includes this 00 term, so its limiting average TV risk is at least uΔ>0. Conditional 00 risk tends to Δ. Integrating against a positive-density source prior is justified by bounded convergence. This is a probabilistic statement, not merely an adversarial collection of vanishing-frequency input words.

5. Decision boundary and prior art

A later attention head that distinguishes individual positions or whose logits grow with N is outside the proof; so are causally computed token representations with position-dependent within-type variation, unbounded positional/length features, exact division or rounding, hard masks, and parameters retrained or rescaled with N. The compact-causal v3 construction uses N-dependent parameters, full causal positional computation and a specified specialized backend, so this obstruction does not contradict it. Neither does it contradict the four-anchor count recovery, which explicitly uses ratios and count rounding. An unrestricted continuous reciprocal readout can also recover the target on classes with a positive occupancy lower bound.

Goel and Bartlett, Theorem 1, already connects softmax attention with kernel smoothing; D’Angelo et al. analyze the mask-weighted estimator. Finite ReLU regions and the real-analytic identity theorem are classical. This is a scoped consequence of those tools, not a claim to have discovered the general stability or expressivity principles. Further literature search and an independent review are needed before stronger interpretation.
