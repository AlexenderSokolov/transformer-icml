# Fixed-half tail and exact gate geometry

3 November 2026. Post-hoc analysis of the already-frozen calibration, all 4,104 words, and existing checkpoints. No fitting, new samples, parameter selection, or external writes. The findings are descriptive and proof-based; they do not establish a general SGD, minimax or source-risk result.

## Conclusions

1. **No loss of target information in the fixed-half features, on any valid M=64 word.** For integer counts, J≤64 and 0≤D≤J. Thus `J>0 and 512D≥J` is equivalent to `D≥1`. The six unnormalized count coordinates are `(a0+b0,a1+b1,a0,a1,b0,b1)`, where a0,a1 and b0,b1 are the first-half and second-half counts of the selected terminal pair followed by 0 or 1. They determine `J=a0+a1+b0+b1`, `C=a1+b1`, `D=a0+a1`, and terminal coordinate F. Therefore `Q=1/8+(3/4)(C/J if D≥1 else F)` is exactly determined. J=0 occurs only on the second branch. Any two valid words with the identical full feature vector have the same Q. The existing independent decoder is not trained and is not the finite tanh readout.
2. **A representation-only existence result is possible at arbitrarily large width, not at the studied width of 32.** The four half-counts each range 0–32, with each half summing to ≤32; together with two endpoint bits, there are at most N=4×561²=1,258,884 possible feature vectors. The affine readout can form the injective integer score `t=4[(((33 a0+a1)33+b0)33+b1)]+2F+G`, where `(F,G)` are the endpoint bits. Let the N scores of actual valid feature vectors be sorted `t_1<...<t_N`, and let `m_i=(t_i+t_(i+1))/2`. Use a constant output bias and N−1 hidden units `tanh(K(t−m_i))`. At the N feature vectors, the first column of the evaluation matrix is all ones, while column i+1 tends to −1 in rows 1,...,i and +1 in rows i+1,...,N as K→∞. Successively subtracting the previous original row from each later row therefore gives a first row beginning with 1 and, below it, the diagonal block 2I. Its determinant has magnitude `2^(N−1)`. Continuity of the determinant implies invertibility for all sufficiently large finite K. Solving the resulting linear system for output coefficients interpolates the desired transformed logits `log((Q−eps)/(1−eps−Q))`, eps=1/66². The existing floor-plus-sigmoid then returns Q exactly on all valid words. This is merely a finite-set, real-arithmetic existence proof, with at most 1,258,883 hidden units; it has no useful weight, precision, stability, efficiency, 32-unit capacity, or learnability guarantee.
3. **Coverage is limited, but it is not the only demonstrated difficulty.** For the common worst validation word, `(J,C,D,U)=(2,1,1,10)`, Q=0.5; there is no training trajectory with this statistic or with its full feature vector. The scaled checkpoints predict 0.933922, 0.901289, 0.923181. In each seed, 10 of the 13–14 validation words with absolute error >0.1 have a `(J,C,D,U)` statistic absent from both the prior-training sample and four collision words. That is a post-hoc association, not a causal estimate.
4. **The finite fit also underfits sampled cases.** The three final scaled checkpoints have respectively 15, 12, and 15 training-prior trajectories with error >0.1 and worst training errors 0.615896, 0.578985, and 0.613527. The same worst training trajectory (index 1699, J=6,C=1,D=0,U=11,Q=0.875) was drawn 52 times in the actual minibatch stream, but final predictions are 0.259104, 0.296015, and 0.261473. A nearest same-endpoint training feature to the shared worst validation case, index 797, has `(J,C,D,U)=(3,1,1,10)` and Q=0.375, is drawn 63 times, and has final predictions 0.638993, 0.639820 and 0.667379. Some high-error validation trajectories even share all features with training examples. Hence absence of a training feature alone cannot explain every failure, and seeing a word repeatedly did not guarantee its accurate fit.
5. **The average criterion permits rare large errors by construction.** One prior-training trajectory has objective mass `0.5/2048=1/4096`; one supplied collision trajectory has `0.5/4=1/8`, a ratio of 512. This is per listed trajectory, not per unique feature if duplicate prior words or features occur. The KL contributions of the single worst validation case to the validation mean are 0.699495/2048, 0.516596/2048, and 0.629967/2048, about 0.000252–0.000342, consistent with small overall means. The 13–14 errors above 0.1 contribute 66.8%–73.0% of the total empirical validation KL while remaining a small part of its mean.
6. **Aggregate saturation does not identify the cause of the worst case.** At the final checkpoint the common worst validation word has respectively 0, 0 and 1 of 32 hidden activations with absolute value >0.99. This does not rule out other forms of saturation, coupling, or poor gradients, but shows that the large aggregate prior saturation cannot by itself explain this particular failure.

The strongest supported account is a finite weighted-average, finite-budget fit that leaves some rare feature configurations badly fitted, alongside inadequate observed coverage of some validation statistics. The matched-scaling result in the prior report is direct evidence of optimizer/parameterization sensitivity for its average and collision screen. Here neither held-out failure nor training underfit establishes that width 32 cannot represent the targets, that a different objective or optimizer will succeed, that coverage is the unique cause, or which factor dominates counterfactually. No causal ablation has been run.

## Reproduction and verification

Run from workspace root with the recorded environment:

    OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python v10_research/fixed_half_tail_geometry_20261103/analyze.py > v10_research/fixed_half_tail_geometry_20261103/run.log
    OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python v10_research/fixed_half_tail_geometry_20261103/verify.py > v10_research/fixed_half_tail_geometry_20261103/verify.log

`RESULTS.json` contains all reported counts, top-20 tails, exact observed features, selected training matches, sampled-index counts, and checkpoint trajectories. `verify.py` independently reconstructs literal half counts, the exact gate, the Q target and final checkpoint predictions for all 4,104 words without importing the prior statistic/feature code. It gets zero target discrepancy, no contradictory Q among 3,530 distinct frozen feature vectors, and reproduces all final predictions to absolute tolerance 1e−14. These finite tests corroborate but do not replace the all-word proof. `interactive_probe_error.py` and its log retain an early diagnostic which printed first-seed results then raised `UnboundLocalError`; it was superseded by the complete `analyze.py`, not silently omitted. `SOURCE_BINDINGS.json` identifies consulted files and analysis artifacts by SHA256. Original calibration files remain unchanged. Only stored seeds and checkpoints were read, and no new seeds or checkpoints were created.
