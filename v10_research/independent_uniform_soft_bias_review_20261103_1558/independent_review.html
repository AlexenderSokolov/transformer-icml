Disposition: no mathematical blocker found within the stated scope. The source is stationary, the reverse pair chain has the stated summable mixing bound, the conditioning is on the actual terminal state, and β is deterministic. The output is the specified idealized BOS interpolator, not an unrestricted Transformer.

Checks

  • Indices and ideal BOS. Z₀=S_N and Zᵢ=S_{N−i} for 1≤i≤M=N−2 cover the observed forward transitions from (X₁,X₂) to (X₂,X₃) through the transition ending at (X_{N−1},X_N). In the reverse edge Zᵢ→Zᵢ₋₁ the forward successor is lastbit(Zᵢ₋₁), not lastbit(Zᵢ). Thus the displayed V and W are exact. The binary BOS contributes +1 and +2 after rescaling, not extra data. M=0 is explicitly an empty-count extension Q=1/2, with H_D(0)=1, rather than a claim about the cited paper's indexing convention.
  • Boundary and adjacent edges. For h_f(x)=Σ_yR(x,y)w(y)1{lastbit(x)=1}, h_g(x)=Σ_yR(x,y)w(y), stationarity and reverse flow give πh_f=a_s and πh_g=b_s. Their ranges are [0,1], so initialization at s costs at most d_{i−1} per edge and at most D in the sum of means. For j>i, conditional on the reverse history through Zᵢ, there are j−i−1 full reverse steps before applying h for edge j. Both the conditional mean and unconditional mean differ from the stationary mean by at most d_{j−i−1} (using monotonicity for the latter), giving |Cov(fᵢ,fⱼ)|≤2 E[fᵢ] d_{j−i−1}, likewise g. Summing and using fᵢ²≤fᵢ, gᵢ²≤gᵢ, and EΣfᵢ≤EΣgᵢ≤Mb_s+D gives (1+4D)(Mb_s+D). This argument makes no conditional-iid or terminal-independence claim.
  • Population and random denominator. b_s≥π_s>0 and a_s/b_s is the ratio of stationary weighted expectations, not the expectation of the random ratio. The exact identity Mb_s(Q−a_s/b_s)=(V+1−Ma_s)+Q(Mb_s−W−2) uses the deterministic population occupancy, so it does not assume W is bounded below. Since Q and a_s/b_s lie in [0,1], the minimum with 1 is valid. The two D boundary contributions, +1 and +2 BOS terms, and the square-root variance terms yield the stated H_D for M≥1. Its convention at zero gives valid trivial two-sided bounds for M=0.
  • Soft bias. The signed difference a_s−b_sθ_s is the sum over other states of π_rw_s(r)(θ_r−θ_s). Since a nonmatching binary pair has Hamming distance at least 1, the mismatched weighted mass is at most (1−π_s)z; monotonicity of v/(π_s+v) gives the stated envelope. No equality is claimed when signed contributions cancel.
  • Rare family and mixing. For π=(u,w,w,w), w=(1−u)/3, the displayed bias numerator is z(w−u/4)+(w/4)z² and its denominator is u+2wz+wz². Given 0<u<1/4, w≥1/4 and u≤w, the numerator is at least 3z/16; denominator is at most u+3wz≤u+z. Multiplying the row P²(11,·) by w/π_a for each destination a verifies R²(a,11)≥1/8. Therefore δ(R²)≤7/8 and sup_x||Rʰ(x,·)−π||≤(7/8)^⌊h/2⌋, whose sum is 16. The finite-M lower bound follows from the general result. Its utility requires its fluctuation term to be smaller than the bias; the limiting sharpness claim concerns the population envelope, not a finite-M minimax risk.
  • Scope and comparison. The published masked-count formula and the finite-temperature interpolation and calibration discussion predate this calculation. The result gives no architecture-wide lower bound, finite-bit implementation guarantee, source-independent statistical lower bound, or training claim. It does not establish novelty or comprehensive literature coverage.
