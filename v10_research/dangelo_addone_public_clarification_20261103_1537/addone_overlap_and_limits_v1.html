Add-one prediction and D’Angelo et al.: exact overlap and limits

Version and reading scope: Francesco D’Angelo, Oğuz Kaan Yüksel, Swathi Shree Narashiman and Nicolas Flammarion, Induction Heads Interpolate N-Grams, arXiv:2607.02800v1, ICML 2026. Directly checked §§2.1–2.2, 3.1–3.2, 4.1 and Appendix D.1. This note makes no exhaustive prior-art or novelty claim.

Correction to any weaker prior-art description: the paper does not merely suggest smoothing or an analogous weighted estimator. Its Corollary 4.1 explicitly gives the same binary add-one prediction, for every input sequence in its stated valid-context setting, as a hard-match limit of its two-layer construction. The construction has idealized copying and early-position suppression limits. The distinction of the current finite-resource candidate is not invention of add-one, BOS pseudo-counts, or idealized two-layer add-one representation.

1. Exact alignment of contexts, counts and endpoints

Take binary input X₁,…,X_N, N≥3, and prepend a BOS symbol: x₁=BOS and x_{i+1}=X_i for 1≤i≤N. In the paper’s notation T=N+1, order k=2, vocabulary V={0,1}, and with BOS k′=k+2=4. Its terminal query u_T=(x_{T−1},x_T) is exactly (X_{N−1},X_N). A candidate output at source index s∈{4,…,T} maps to i=s−1∈{3,…,N}; its preceding context is (x_{s−2},x_{s−1})=(X_{i−2},X_{i−1}) and its successor is x_s=X_i. The candidate at s=T is valid: its context ends before X_N, so it does not leak the next token X_{N+1}.

J = Σᵢ₌₃ᴺ 1{(Xᵢ₋₂,Xᵢ₋₁)=(X_N₋₁,X_N)},
C_m = Σᵢ₌₃ᴺ 1{(Xᵢ₋₂,Xᵢ₋₁)=(X_N₋₁,X_N), Xᵢ=m}.

Thus the paper’s full-mask counts N_[2]^(T)=J and N_[2]^(T)(m)=C_m exactly. In particular, C₀+C₁=J. These are counts from the same observed history and its actual terminal query, not an independent test context. The N=2 case has no valid observed triple and the add-one rule is 1/2; because the paper’s interval notation explicitly discusses valid candidates from k′ to T, we do not infer its edge-case coverage at T<k′ without an additional convention.

Source: Definition 3.1 and Proposition 3.1, and §2.2 for context ordering.

2. The two-layer overlap and its distinct limits

Proposition 3.1 states a two-layer disentangled causal transformer with relative-position encoding, k first-layer heads and one second-layer head, yielding its weighted mask-count estimator. Set β₁=β₂=β, α=1, |V|=2 and κ=2β+ln 2. Write n_r(m) for the number of valid candidate successors equal to m with exactly r matching coordinates, r=0,1,2; n_r=Σ_m n_r(m). The formula is

Q_β(m) = [e^(2β) + n₀(m) + e^β n₁(m) + e^(2β)n₂(m)]
         / [2e^(2β) + n₀ + e^β n₁ + e^(2β)n₂].

Divide by e^(2β) and fix the finite sequence. Corollary 4.1 and Appendix D.1 take β→∞ with κ coupled as above, giving

lim Q_β(m) = [1+n₂(m)]/[2+n₂] = [1+C_m]/[2+J].

The denominator remains positive even when J=0. The smoothing limit is therefore not the unsmoothed maximum-likelihood rule and not just an approximation in notation. It matches all binary outputs, not solely the 00 state.

However, the paper’s §3.2.1 describes exact token copying by δ⁽¹⁾→∞, and §3.2.2 suppresses early positions by δ₁⁽²⁾→∞. The β→∞ limit is a further limit after adopting that idealized copying/suppression mechanism. It is not justified to say all these limits are finite real logits or to replace them by a single unquantified simultaneous limit. Nor does finite β alone compute exact add-one: partial matches generally remain. The statement of Proposition 3.1 is phrased as an exact estimator, while its displayed proof uses the stated limiting steps; this note reports the relationship without declaring the theorem false.

Sources: §3.2.1 copying; §3.2.2 matching and suppression; Corollary 4.1; Appendix D.1.

3. Finite β already approximates add-one in the idealized count formula

It would also be misleading to claim that the prior formula has no finite-β quantitative consequence. In the idealized estimator above, define v=e^(−β)n₁+e^(−2β)n₀ and v_m=e^(−β)n₁(m)+e^(−2β)n₀(m). For β≥0, 0≤v_m≤v≤(N−2)e^(−β). The output is a convex mixture of the exact add-one distribution and a partial-match distribution, with mismatch mass v/(J+2+v). Hence, uniformly over the history and m,

|Q_β(m) − (1+C_m)/(2+J)| ≤ v/(J+2+v) ≤ (N−2)e^(−β)/(J+2) ≤ (N−2)e^(−β)/2.

Thus β≥ln((N−2)/(2η)) when that quantity is positive suffices for coordinate error at most η in the ideal count formula. This elementary deduction is not a finite-copy, finite-suppression, or finite-arithmetic guarantee for the actual circuit. Source: the displayed equation (8) in Proposition 3.1.

5. Source-notation caution

In §3.2.2, the displayed denominator and numerator use an indicator of whether the BOS score is positive, while equations (8) and (10) and the immediately preceding piecewise score give BOS weight e^κ whenever BOS is present. A finite BOS score can be nonpositive while its exponential remains positive; the indicator appears inconsistent with those displays. For the add-one specialization above with β≥0, κ=2β+ln 2>0, so this issue does not alter the displayed β→∞ comparison. It is a notation/derivation caveat, not a claim that the published corollary is false.
