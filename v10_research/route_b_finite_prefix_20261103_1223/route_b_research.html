Route B: finite-prefix conditioning and visible-channel counterexample

Research note, 3 November 2026. Status: exact source mapping, pathwise algebra and a restricted-channel lower bound; not formal verification, a novelty certificate, a full-network lower bound, or a completed resource-optimal theory. The provisional model is v10_research/t1t2_shared_model_20261103/shared_model_interface_v0_1.html. We do not bind this review to the original overwritten audit bytes.

1. Common model and finite random prefix

Let X be binary and S_t=(X_{t−1},X_t). Take N≥3, M=N−2, t=2,…,N−1, J_s=Σ1{S_t=s}, C_s=Σ1{S_t=s}X_{t+1}, u_s=J_s/M and terminal query S_N. State order is 00,01,10,11. All statistics and the query use the same trajectory. No independence of S_N and the prefix is assumed. A supplied query state uses signs (a,b)∈{−1,+1}² and a candidate (c,d) similarly. Let

K_st=exp(β₁ac+β₂bd), z=Ku,
A=diag(z)⁻¹ K diag(u), q=A p̂,
p̂_t=C_t/J_t when J_t>0; zero-count terms contribute zero.

All z_s are positive because M≥1. K is the Kronecker product of the two matrices [[exp β_i,exp(−β_i)],[exp(−β_i),exp β_i]]. Its singular values are products of 2 cosh β_i and 2|sinh β_i|. Therefore for β₁β₂≠0, rank(A) equals the number of occupied states, regardless of occupancy or trajectory dependence. If all four J_s>0, A is invertible. If either β_i=0 or any state is unoccupied, full four-state inversion fails. This does not settle whether the one query-relevant coordinate is identifiable in a given singular instance.

2. Exact occupation-weighted geometry

Assume u_s>0, β₁β₂≠0, and define μ_s=u_s z_s/(uᵀKu). Then μ_s A_st=μ_t A_ts. In L²(μ), A is represented by the symmetric matrix

B=diag(sqrt(u/z)) K diag(sqrt(u/z)).
σ_min,μ(A) = σ_min(B)
 ≥ 4|sinh β₁ sinh β₂| min_s(u_s/z_s),
||A||_{2,μ} ≤ 1.

The lower bound follows by applying the inverse norm inequality to the symmetric diagonal congruence; it is valid even if either β is negative. Markov contraction gives the upper bound via Jensen and stationarity of μ. These are deterministic conditional on the realized prefix. They do not require independent contexts. If π is any strictly positive weighting, including the stationary πθ, put R_s=μ_s/π_s. The same linear map in L²(π) has

σ_min,π(A) ≥ σ_min,μ(A) sqrt(min_s R_s / max_s R_s),
||A||_{2,π} ≤ sqrt(max_s R_s / min_s R_s).

Here σ_min,π means the smallest singular value after conjugation by diag(sqrt π). Usually μ≠π even if u=π, since z_s varies. The bounds become weak when an occupation is small or a β_i is close to zero; they are not a universal interaction-information lower bound. A π-weighted norm is a pathwise vector norm, not automatically the actual terminal-query risk, because S_N and u, C are dependent.

3. Finite-precision recovery with four query rows

Suppose a circuit actually exports all four q_s and the occupancy vector u. This is more information and work than a single terminal-query head. Set κ=|coth β₁ coth β₂|. For exact arithmetic v=u⊙p̂=K⁻¹ diag(Ku) q. Let ũ be a probability vector, q̃∈[0,1]⁴, and suppose ||ũ−u||∞≤ε_u≤λ/2, ||q̃−q||∞≤ε_q and min_s u_s≥λ>0. Define ṽ=K⁻¹diag(Kũ)q̃ and p̃_s=clip_[0,1](ṽ_s/ũ_s). Since ||K⁻¹||∞||K||∞=κ and each ũ_s≥λ/2,

||p̃−p̂||∞ ≤ (2/λ)[κ(ε_u+ε_q)+ε_u].

This is an explicit interface error bound. The ε terms must include score, softmax, hidden-value, accumulation and output rounding; proving them for model D or a particular model R circuit is a separate obligation. The rational inverse, multiplication, clamping and parameter precision must be charged. If λ fails, use a specified fallback; the event and its impact must be controlled. This four-row construction makes the conditioning/information distinction concrete, but is not a resource-matched one-terminal-row recovery theorem.

4. Finite path collision and a restricted-channel risk bound

Consider an explicitly restricted interface consisting only of S_N, M, u, r=Σ C_s/M, and two scalar attention outputs: one scores only the older coordinate of S_N against the corresponding historical coordinate, the other scores only the recent coordinate, each with its own arbitrary finite β. Both values are the successor bit. No history-sensitive vector value, extra head, position channel, or intermediate residual is visible.

N=8 sequenceS₈J by 00,01,10,11C by 00,01,10,11successor sums by older / recent coordinate
0001101111(2,2,1,1)(1,2,1,0)(3,1) / (2,2)
0010011111(2,2,1,1)(2,1,0,1)(3,1) / (2,2)

Both have r=4/6, so every interface component is identical for every choice of the two finite βs, but C_11 is 0 versus 1. In fact q_old=(3e^(−β₁)+e^β₁)/(4e^(−β₁)+2e^β₁) and q_recent=2/3 in each case. This is a finite legal binary path collision, not an i.i.d. four-state sample construction.

One can obtain a nonzero, fixed-N lower bound without claiming a full-network result. Take prior probability 1/2 on each stationary Markov kernel θ⁻ and θ⁺ with θ_00=θ_01=θ_10=1/2 and θ_11⁻=1/4, θ_11⁺=3/4. Their stationary π_00 values are respectively 3/11 and 1/5. The probability of the first sequence under θ⁻ is 9/1408, while the second sequence under θ⁺ has probability 3/640. Both lead to the identical restricted output but have targets 1/4 and 3/4. For any restricted predictor f, the equal-prior expected terminal TV risk therefore satisfies

(R₈(θ⁻,f)+R₈(θ⁺,f))/2 ≥ (1/4) min(9/1408,3/640) = 3/2560.

Proof: retain just those two path-world terms and use |f−1/4|+|f−3/4|≥1/2. This is an N=8 two-prior result, not a nonasymptotic rate, not a lower bound for θ outside this prior, and not a general Transformer separation. The stationary initial probabilities are included; no posterior independence claim is used.

5. Joint-feature bypass, and accounting

In reference model R, at each historical row i=3,…,N, use two singleton-copy heads to obtain X_{i−2},X_{i−1} (with original X_i retained). At the terminal position preserve X_{N−1},X_N in separate query coordinates. A local ReLU FFN can form e_s=1{(X_{i−2},X_{i−1})=s} and e_s X_i for all four states: binary AND is ReLU(x+y−1), applied after complementing bits where needed. One uniform head with permitted structural mask {3,…,N} and eight value coordinates exports (J_s/M,C_s/M)_s. The full history-side values do not know the future query. Local query gating selects the desired state. No BOS is assumed.

With α=1/2 this joint bank gives (C_11+1/2)/(J_11+1): 1/4 and 3/4 on the two displayed paths. The joint bank also contains the marginal features needed to reproduce the restricted heads, subject to charging arithmetic. A single extra vector-valued head, FFN, residual, or position pathway may therefore bypass the purported loss. In fact these two paths already differ in X_{N−2}, visible in the copying residual; the collision is valid only for the declared restricted port. The two exact matches above do not establish lower overall Bayes risk, a strict matched-FLOP separation, or an architecture-wide impossibility.

Both paths use the same X₁:₈. At the second aggregation layer, a comparison allowing a joint-feature FFN can give both constructions the same eight output coordinates: four joint occupation and four joint successor coordinates; pad unused heads if enforcing equal H. The restricted two heads alone use two scalar outputs, while the joint method needs state-feature computation and vector values. Conversely, the four-row inversion requires four scalar softmax heads plus an occupancy head and inverse/readout. Compare all value dimensions, scalar operations, FFN neurons, head count, score and hidden precision, exponential/normalization, mask and position storage, and readout rather than describing them as equal FLOPs. A model D compilation requires finite-logit copy leakage and costs; model R’s structural masks are not free in D.

A finite-size exact-grid readout can be built without assuming free real division: for integer counts J,C∈{0,…,M}, create plateaus ψ_j(z)=ReLU(2−4|z−j|)−ReLU(1−4|z−j|), which equal 1 when |z−j|≤1/4 and 0 when distance ≥1/2. With M times the averaged head outputs known within 1/4, these plateaus identify the integer counts. For j≥1 and c≤j combine ψ_j(J),ψ_c(C) with ReLU(ψ_j+ψ_c−1) and sum coefficients c/j; for j=0 use an explicit fallback. A selector on the terminal state yields the requested output. This construction uses O(M²) count-pair gates and stored coefficients per state and a constant number of tokenwise ReLU stages; dense implementation or applying the same FFN at every token can cost substantially more than O(M²). Approximating coefficients within ε gives output error at most ε after the plateaus correctly decode; finite score/accumulator errors must ensure each count average error <1/(4M). The construction is a feasibility upper bound with high cost, not optimality or a bound for the standard decoder D.

6. Sampling risk is a separate term

For the Regime II stationary family, the two-step kernel has a common 11 atom of mass at least 1/8, so |P^h(s,s)−π_s|≤(7/8)^⌊h/2⌋ and Var J_s≤31 Mπ_s. If D_s=C_s−θ_s J_s, the martingale-difference identity gives E D_s²≤Mπ_s/4, independent of a terminal-query independence assumption. Chebyshev gives P(J_s<Mπ_s/2)≤124/(Mπ_s). For α>0 use p̂^α_s=(C_s+α)/(J_s+2α); for α=0 use C_s/J_s when J_s>0 and any probability fallback at zero. Then

R_N(θ,p̂^α) ≤ 4/√M + 8α/M + Σ_s min{π_s,124/(Mπ_s)}.

Proof: on J_s≥Mπ_s/2, Cauchy gives E[1{S_N=s}|D_s|]≤π_s√M/2, and the smoothing contribution is ≤2α/M. On the complement, risk is ≤1 and its probability with S_N=s is at most the minimum shown. Sum over four states. This deliberately loose check is a statistical reference bound, not a new contribution or sharper substitute for existing internal v10 occupation results. It does not cover score or arithmetic error, and a bound on a recovered vector cannot be multiplied by π as if S_N were independent of the prefix.

7. Source comparison and research limits

D’Angelo et al., Proposition 3.1, already give exponentiated partial-match weights and explicitly use limiting copy/mask construction in §3.2. For signs, βac=2β1{a=c}−β, so their aligned-match coefficients are 2β₁,2β₂; the common offset cancels. Their Appendix E has additional cross-lag equality comparisons under stated symmetry/copying assumptions. The product Walsh eigenvalues in uniform occupation are a direct algebraic property, not claimed new. The pathwise weighted geometry and finite-path port counterexample above are derived here but have not passed a comprehensive literature novelty check; compare prior internal v10 results as well as Akman et al. before any publication claim.
