Prior art: terminal prediction risk over growing alphabets

3 November 2026. Targeted primary-source comparison, not an exhaustive literature review or independent proof certification. No tests, programs, simulations or random operations were run; no previous file was overwritten.

Most significant overlap. Han–Jana–Wu (2023), Theorem 5, already obtains the higher-order stationary minimax KL rate in alphabet size and sample size. Its upper bound yields a corresponding terminal-averaged TV upper bound by Pinsker and Jensen. Their §VI-A also directly addresses the same-trajectory terminal loss with squared TV. Neither result, as stated, is the proposed unsquared-TV lower bound for the order-two fast-mixing family. A lower bound for KL must not be reversed into a lower bound for TV.

1. Target and review scope

The target manuscript, as described in the accompanying research summary, claims an unsquared TV lower bound (1/288) min{1, √(A³/N)} for A,N≥2, using a stationary, uniform-pair, full-support order-two family with rapid forward and reverse two-step mixing. The estimator is allowed the entire observed trajectory and loss is evaluated at its actual terminal pair. Its add-one upper bound OB(√(A³/N)), clipped by the TV cap, is stated for stationary chains satisfying a bounded reverse-mixing sum B; a general stationary upper bound retains a logarithm. This comparison takes those manuscript details from that summary; the local manuscript itself was not independently read under this no-program workflow, and its constants, assumptions and proof are not independently certified here.

TV means half the ℓ1 distance. The observable trajectory, true terminal context and estimator are statistically dependent. A bound for a fresh independent query, a fixed row, π-weighted matrix estimation, a maximum-row matrix norm, time-averaged regret, squared TV, or KL is not automatically the same result.

3. Direction of valid conversions

For distributions P,Q, TV(P,Q)≤√(D(P‖Q)/2). Consequently E TV≤√(E D/2), so an existing KL upper bound produces a TV upper bound under the same estimator and source quantifiers. There is no converse of the form D≥c implying TV≥√(c/2): rare events can make KL large while TV is small. Similarly, Jensen gives E TV≤√(E TV²), a useful upper bound, not the reverse lower bound. Since TV≤1, E TV≥E TV² is valid but retains the smaller squared-loss scale. A TV minimax lower requires a direct TV/ℓ1 separation, testing or Bayes argument with the actual terminal event and valid source family.

4. Interpretation and unresolved questions

The literature already has the same order-m parameter count Am+1 in stationary terminal-prediction KL and has direct first-order squared-TV terminal risk and fast-mixing matrix-ℓ1 lower constructions. The target's fast-mixing, stationary order-two, unsquared TV lower with its particular all-alphabet quantifiers was not identified in the examined results. This bounded negative finding is not a novelty determination. It remains necessary to inspect the target's proof, constants, endpoint indexing and saturation regime, to trace work citing these papers, and to search separately for prior reverse-chain concentration arguments and unsquared last-step TV rates. Mixing, reversibility, stationary vs worst-case initialization, and m-dependent constants should remain explicit in any theorem statement.
