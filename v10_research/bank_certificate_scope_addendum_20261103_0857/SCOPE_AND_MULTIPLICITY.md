# Additive scope and multiplicity clarification

3 November 2026. This note supplements, without altering, `conditional_bank_interval_certificate_20261103_0854/RESEARCH_NOTE.md` and its retained code and outputs.

The executable demonstration uses exactly **one active hidden unit** and the three explicitly enumerated words for M=2, k=1. Its exact-rational transcendental routines establish the assertions for that specific readout and input set. The suggested signed 9-by-32 affine/interval propagation is a mathematical method description: a generic signed 9-by-32 interval evaluator was not implemented, tested, or benchmarked here. No complexity or bit-cost bound for general rational endpoint propagation is established. In particular, large intermediate numerators and denominators, high precision, extreme arguments, wide intervals, and cancellation can materially affect running time or feasibility. This note does not establish or measure a practical speedup.

A minimum-edit-cost count-bank DP supplies a reachable bank and a legal witness. Its retained minimum cost alone **does not establish that the bank has exactly one legal word**: distinct prefixes or complete words can merge into the same bank and higher-cost legal words may have been discarded. A unique floating maximizer cannot be inferred merely because the bank has one retained DP representative. Uniqueness requires separate evidence that no other legal word shares the bank, or a separate strict upper bound on all such alternatives. In the specific M=2 example, singleton status is independently established by listing the complete legal set 0000, 1000, 0100 and observing their three distinct banks, not by claiming the minimum-cost DP counted multiplicity.

One possible way to establish multiplicity in a larger instance is an additional DP retaining each **exact** edit cost 0 through k and, at each complete state, the number of distinct prefixes capped at 2. Initialize from distinct permitted first pairs; append each permitted symbol once; sum incoming capped counts with saturation at 2; then sum over all terminal states with the same bank and costs <=k, again capped at 2. Each legal word determines a unique sequence of appended bits, so distinct words are not double-counted. A result of 1 certifies a singleton if the state and transitions faithfully cover all legal words. This is a proposed extension, not an implemented or benchmarked algorithm, and its complexity and storage overhead must be analyzed for the chosen DP state. The existing minimum-cost DP cannot simply be assumed to provide these counts.

The float-ranking statements remain conditional on independent, uniform actual-backend error premises and on the previously stated batch context. This clarification does not certify a NumPy/BLAS implementation, a checkpoint attack, or a backend maximum. No random operations or model executions were used for this note.
