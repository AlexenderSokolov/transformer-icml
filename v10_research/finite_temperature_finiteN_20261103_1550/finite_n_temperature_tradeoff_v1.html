Finite-history error for an ideal soft-matching estimator

3 November 2026. This is an elementary finite-N specialization of a published mask-count formula and standard Markov mixing estimates, not a generic Transformer resource theorem or a novelty claim. No random or numerical experiment was used.

Result. For the fixed stationary binary order-two source and the idealized equal-temperature BOS estimator defined below, let M=N−2≥1, z_M=e^(−β_M) with deterministic β_M≥0, and condition on the actual final pair 00. Its expected binary-TV error R_M obeys

max{0, (7/20)z_M − 45/√M} ≤ R_M ≤ min{1, z_M + 45/√M}.

Consequently R_M=O(M−1/2) if and only if z_M=O(M−1/2), equivalently β_M≥(1/2)ln M−O(1). The score gap here is between a full match and a one-coordinate mismatch in this fixed interpolation family. This is not a general depth, head, precision, training, raw parameter magnitude or computing-time lower bound.

1. Source, task and estimator

Let (X_i) have stationary pair law π=(3/10,3/10,3/10,1/10), in state order 00,01,10,11, and next-bit-one probabilities (3/4,1/4,1/4,1/4). The pair-chain transition matrix and its square are

P = (1/4) [[1,3,0,0], [0,0,3,1], [3,1,0,0], [0,0,3,1]],
P²= (1/16)[[1,3,9,3], [9,3,3,1], [3,9,3,1], [9,3,3,1]].

The displayed π is stationary; the chain is irreducible and aperiodic. For the actual terminal pair S_N=(X_{N−1},X_N), P(S_N=00)=3/10. Target P(X_{N+1}=1 | S_N=00)=3/4. Binary-TV loss is |Q_N−3/4|.

On a history ending in 00, assign each valid predecessor pair s its match weight w_z(00)=1, w_z(01)=w_z(10)=z and w_z(11)=z². Let Y_t=(X_{t+1},X_{t+2}), t=0,…,M, so Y_M=S_N. Use all M=N−2 real transitions, including the one ending at X_N:

A = Σₜ₌₀ᴹ⁻¹ w_z(Y_t) 1{X_(t+3)=1},    B = Σₜ₌₀ᴹ⁻¹ w_z(Y_t),
Q_N = (A+1)/(B+2).

The unobserved X_{N+1} is not used. On histories ending in other pairs, the usual estimator uses that pair as query; only its conditional 00 risk is analyzed here. The formula is the binary α=1 specialization of D’Angelo et al., equation (8), with κ=2β+ln2, after dividing by e^(2β). The published circuit derivation uses ideal positional copying and early-position suppression; finite β here does not mean all constituent logits are finite.

2. Population bias

Stationarity gives a=EA/M=(9+6z+z²)/40 and b=EB/M=(3+6z+z²)/10≥3/10. Define q(z)=a/b, so

q(z)=(3+z)²/[4(3+6z+z²)],
3/4−q(z)= z(6+z)/[2(3+6z+z²)].

For 0≤z≤1, direct comparison of polynomials yields

(7/20)z ≤ 3/4−q(z) ≤ z.

This is the partial-match bias of this fixed source and output map. It is neither a worst-case bias over sources nor an assertion that borrowing information is always harmful at finite M.

3. Concentration despite the actual-terminal condition

For a Markov matrix K let δ(K)=sup_(x,y)||K(x,·)−K(y,·)||TV. Comparing the rows of the displayed P² gives δ(P²)=1/2. By contraction, δ(P^h)≤2^(−⌊h/2⌋), h≥0. Each summand in A or B is in [0,1] and is a function of one transition. For two such summands separated by h≥1, the Markov property and the range bound imply |Cov|≤δ(P^(h−1)). Therefore, without independence of overlapping transitions,

Var(A), Var(B) ≤ M/4 + 2M Σₕ₌₁∞ 2^(−⌊(h−1)/2⌋)
                  = (33/4)M.

The expectation and variance just stated are under the stationary source. Let E={Y_M=00}, with P(E)=3/10. Cauchy–Schwarz, applied before dividing by P(E), gives

E[|A−Ma| | E] ≤ √(Var(A)/P(E)) ≤ √((55/2)M),
E[|B−Mb| | E] ≤ √((55/2)M).

These inequalities explicitly allow the terminal event to depend on the same trajectory. There is no independent test query and no iid sample assumption.

Since 0≤Q_N≤1 and b≥3/10, the exact identity

Mb(Q_N−q) = (A+1−Ma) + Q_N(Mb−B−2)

implies

E[|Q_N−q(z)| | E]
 ≤ [2√((55/2)M)+3]/[(3/10)M]
 = (20/3)√(55/2)/√M + 10/M ≤ 45/√M,    M≥1.

The bound depends only on the range of the weights, so it holds uniformly for every deterministic z=z_M∈[0,1]. By triangle and reverse-triangle inequalities, it gives the finite-N two-sided risk display at the beginning. The lower bound may be zero when the stochastic term dominates; this proof does not claim a separate Ω(M−1/2) variance lower bound.

4. Consequence for a prescribed statistical rate

If z_M≤C/√M for all sufficiently large M, then R_M≤(C+45)/√M. Conversely, if R_M≤C′/√M for all sufficiently large M, the lower bound implies

z_M≤(20/7)(C′+45)/√M,
β_M≥(1/2)ln M − ln[(20/7)(C′+45)].

Thus z_M=O(M−1/2) is necessary and sufficient for this source-specific rate in the specified one-output interpolator. It is a relative match-score condition, invariant to adding a common constant to all attention logits. β_M may be chosen as a deterministic function of M. A trajectory-dependent β would require a separate formulation and is not covered by the stated necessity.

5. Prior-art relationship and limitations

D’Angelo et al., Proposition 3.1 already gives the mask-weighted estimator, and Corollary 4.1 gives BOS add-α smoothing in the hard-match limit. Their §4.2 and Lemma 4.2 and Appendix D.3 already discuss temperature and approximate pseudo-count calibration; at fixed alphabet, order and prior parameters their displayed approximation scales logarithmically with sequence length. That approximation concerns a different no-BOS/prior-averaged smoothing criterion, not this fixed-source actual-terminal conditional-TV requirement. Therefore neither soft interpolation nor logarithmic temperature scaling should be claimed as a new mechanism.

The present argument is a direct finite-sample consequence of the published formula plus standard mixing; its literature coverage is not exhaustive. It does not show the specific β achieves the best finite-M risk, that log scaling is necessary for another architecture or output port, or that extra heads, residuals, trainable readouts and source-specific predictors cannot bypass this restriction. The source-specific constant predictor 3/4 has zero error on this one chain, although it is not a single valid solution for all unknown chains. All resource comparisons must charge the architecture and access model explicitly.
