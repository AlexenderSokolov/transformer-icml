1. Architecture and timing

At integer positions m_i=ReLU(2−i) is 1 only at the first BOS. The added zero-score head in block 3 sees all i causal keys uniformly, so outputs z_i=1/i. The original third-block FFN produces p in its terminal residual after the attention stage; both p and z=1/L are present at the input to block 4. This timing is consistent with attention followed by FFN in each block.

Width 128 and eight 16-dimensional heads give the advertised √16=4 attention denominator. The two fourth-block queries q=(8p,−4z) and (8(1−p),−4z), with keys (j,j²), give scores 2pj−j²/L and 2(1−p)j−j²/L. Completing the square yields the specified discrete Gaussian laws. The source-independent feature ln j is provided at each causal key, not ln p or ln(1−p). Two BOS positions give L=N+2 and j runs from 1 to L, so every supplied logarithm is finite. Values A,B are written to residual slots and a final linear readout gives logits (A,B) for classes (1,0), followed by the actual ordinary vocabulary softmax.

The first three blocks embed into the wider model with zero extensions and disjoint new slots. Active head count 2+0+5+2=9; the extra block-1 ReLU for m needs capacity but no cross-token input feature. The model uses unbounded absolute positions and exact-real ln j, fixed learned matrices and length-dependent causal support. No hidden query-specific log feature is supplied.

2. Discrete Gaussian normalization and tail

For μ∈[0,L], at least ⌊√L⌋ indices in {1,…,L} lie at distance at most √L, including at the endpoints. For L≥4, ⌊√L⌋≥√L/2 and Z≥√L/(2e). Split exp(−d²/L) into two halves and bound one half on the tail; partition distances into half-open unit bins, each containing at most two integer positions. The result is

P(|J−μ|≥t) ≤ 4e(1/√L+√(π/2)) exp(−(t−1)²/(2L))
                < 24 exp(−(t−1)²/(2L)),       t≥1.

The text uses an inequality weaker than necessary in the exponent; its constant 24 is valid. With T_L=1+√(2L ln(24L²)), the tail is at most L⁻². No independence assumption enters this deterministic row-wise calculation.

3. Geometric mean, endpoints and output normalization

Let G=exp(E ln J). Jensen gives G≤EJ≤μ+T_L+L⁻¹, because J≤L. For μ≤2T_L, G≥1 implies μ−G≤2T_L. For μ>2T_L, the good event has J≥μ−T_L>1; the outside contribution to E ln J is nonnegative. Thus G≥(μ−T_L)^(1−L⁻²)≥μ−T_L−(ln L)/L. Both one-sided deviations are covered by Ld_L=2T_L+1+ln L.

These bounds apply for every μ∈[0,L], including μ=0 and μ=L, because only ln J with J≥1 occurs. For a=G_p/L and b=G_(1−p)/L, |a−p|≤d_L and |b−(1−p)|≤d_L. If d_L≤1/4, the denominator a+b≥1−2d_L and

|a/(a+b)−p| ≤ [(1−p)|a−p|+p|b−(1−p)|]/(a+b)
                 ≤ d_L/(1−2d_L) ≤ 2d_L.

Otherwise the probability difference is at most 1. Hence the stated looser bound min{1,4d_L} holds for all L≥4 and all p∈[0,1]. Combining pathwise with the original (N/2)e⁻ᴺ error is valid and gives O(√(ln N/N)).

4. Terminal conditioning and finite precision

The inequality is deterministic for each word, so taking expectation over any source and initial law, then a supremum for each N, preserves it without assuming the final state independent of the history. The add-one risk bound is inherited from R011, not established by this review; the source and its proof/review must accompany any public deployment of that corollary. For N=2 the terminal pair exists, no historical triple exists, and the risk is safely bounded by TV≤1. R011 is only invoked at N≥3.

Conditioned on an already-computed p̂∈[0,1], the p̂-dependent part of the Gaussian score is exact in the ideal reference; replacing z=1/L by ẑ contributes at most L²ε_z and additional arithmetic contributes ε_score. Softmax TV sensitivity with a uniform logit error δ gives tanh(δ/2); a value in [0,ln L] then changes by at most (ln L)tanh(δ/2), plus paid value and softmax-implementation errors. For two logits, the logistic derivative gives final output error at most one half of the maximum absolute logit error. The earlier p̂ error must separately be compared with B_N. The proposed asymptotic error budgets therefore suffice if actually achieved; they do not construct or test a finite-bit implementation, including the final exponential.

5. Limits

The proof assumes exact-real attention, ln-position inputs and exp/log evaluation; fixed weights do not imply bounded score magnitudes, a fixed-word implementation, a learned solution or standard hardware correctness. Computing ln j to inverse-polynomial absolute error by the given arctanh series is possible in polylogarithmic bit resources, but that alone does not prove the end-to-end execution budget. The result is a particular four-block model and a uniform source-risk statement, not a general Transformer theorem or a priority/novelty determination.
