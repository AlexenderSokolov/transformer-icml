## Proof, complexity, and scope

- For any prefix, each increment is either included or absent, so each coordinate lies in [sum min(0,l_tj), sum max(0,u_tj)]. The zero is essential: for fixed increments +1,-1 the full sum is 0 but the intermediate value is 1. All-formal-input bounds are sufficient but may be loose. Integer ranges include both endpoints.
- At a fixed layer, q, last r bits and the accumulated bank contain all modified-prefix information allowed to affect future increments, transitions, constraints and terminal utility. Public clean h and its initial/protected bits may enter freely: h is fixed and need not be stored as dynamic state. Initial or ending bits of z can enter only if protected/derivable from w, h, q or the bank; arbitrary initial z bits must be encoded into q or bank. This sufficiency condition is central. Larger prefix cost is dominated only for at-most-k; pruning above k is valid because mismatches are nonnegative. Exactly-k and two simultaneous resource budgets need additional state or frontier.
- Positions 0,...,M-1 branch, and M,M+1 are forced to clean terminal values while still updating feature sums/state. Same key has same achievable continuation/score, hence all reachable terminal keys and minimum-cost witnesses survive, even though not every word does. Terminal output can depend on q_T,w_T, the bank and fixed public h; it cannot depend on a discarded prefix identity or the mismatch cost unless retained.
- With T=M+2 and S=2^r Q product R_j, there are at most 2TS trial transitions, O(S) terminal scores, and O(S) rolling cost cells; a full layerwise predecessor table costs O(TS log(S+1)) bits, a trace O(T). Repeated prefix recomputation permits O(T²S) trial transitions and O(S) cost cells, plus output. These are unit-cost operation counts: per transition must pay local rules, legality, d integer additions and dense indices (width roughly log Q+r+sum log R_j), plus their bit costs and bank increments' encodings; readout evaluation/comparison is charged separately. Cost cells need log(k+2) bits (unreachable included); read-only input, temporary state, indexing/arithmetic, table initialization and T-bit output are excluded from the small rolling bound as stated. Full traceback assumes deterministic tie handling or preserved predecessor pointers.
- If |g_j|≤W_j then R_j≤2TW_j+1. For fixed d,r and polynomial Q, W and transition/comparator bit costs, the method is polynomial; b=O(log M) matters for aligned integer numerators, not just floating significands. Dyadic denominators/exponent alignment must itself be affordable, particularly when exponents are binary encoded. Large binary weights can induce exponentially wide numeric range despite short representation. d and r are exponential/product factors; no assertion that constant heads alone guarantees efficiency.
- For each normalized head, independently accumulate sum a and each sum av, after exact product and alignment; the ratio includes the known power-of-two scale difference. Denominator positivity must hold for every legal output. Vector head needs p_h+1 banks. These local a and v may depend on declared state/window/index/public h, not an unencoded whole modified prefix. Masked positions use zero weight. Explicit dyadic unnormalized weights are an assumption, not a claim that exp of arbitrary dyadic softmax logits is dyadic. Exact rational readout or separately justified comparison oracle is required; exponentials/logarithms and numerical floating reductions are not automatically covered.
- A one-feature weighted subset-sum encoding can have exponentially large binary-encoded weights, so the O(MA) range factor is pseudopolynomial and consistent with the prior weak NP-hardness construction. The reduction imposes k≤sqrt M via padding, but imposes no polynomial bound on numerical weight size. No implication for approximate hardness or trained pilot. Four-counter fixed-half interface recovers d=4, r=2 and M^5 transition/M^4 state bounds, subject to the earlier explicit exact-real vs float-code correction.

## Deterministic checks and provenance

No random operation, sampling, model initialization, source modification or repository publication was performed. The independent `independent_check.py` uses a different deterministic signed three-coordinate four-state rule and a separate two-head rational rule; for r=0 and r=2, all M=1,...,5, all 2^(M+2) clean words and all budgets 0,...,M, it compares complete brute-force legal-mask terminal-key/minimum-cost maps to a separately coded DP, checks each returned witness including both protected symbols, and compares exact rational maxima. Heads independently reconstruct values from literal word weights, including denominators and differing scales. Result: **5,120 cases, 72,800 enumerated legal mask records, 55,792 reachable terminal keys, PASS**. Every derived mask/word/key and DP key/cost/witness for these checks is preserved in `full_records.jsonl.gz`; test source and original stdout are alongside this review. This is finite corroboration, not a proof or an original author-run record.

Author `check_dp.py` was rerun to a fresh local log, byte-identical to retained author stdout: 1,280 signed-state cases / 17,672 terminal keys and 512 normalized-head cases / 2,348 terminal keys. Author proof/test/log source hashes before and after our checks are identical and recorded in separate files; final correction and diff were independently hashed after they appeared. These hashes are integrity bindings, not enforced immutable filesystem seals. The author program uses no RNG and writes only stdout. ## Literature

The candidate appropriately disclaims novelty of standard DP. The publisher's primary metadata for [Gurski, Rehs and Rethmann, *Knapsack Problems: A Parameterized Point of View*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2018.12.019) identifies the named authors, title and multidimensional-knapsack scope; full publisher page failed to open, so I did not independently verify its specific fixed-dimension bound from full text. [Dutta and Rajasree](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11020v2) explicitly attributes the O(nt)-time, O(t)-space subset-sum DP to Bellman (1957); that is a later research paper, not itself the original Bellman source. [Bellman's 1957 *Dynamic Programming* book](https://books.google.com/books/about/Dynamic_Programming.html?id=wdtoPwAACAAJ) is an identified primary reference; the exact relevant passage was not inspected. These references support prior-art positioning, not the task-specific theorem or any broader novelty claim.
