# Exact attack DP for a fixed number of bounded-grid position features

2026-11-03. Candidate theorem and finite deterministic diagnostics, not independently reviewed. This is a direct task-specific finite-state/dynamic-programming construction, not a novel general knapsack algorithm, a result on arbitrary Transformers, or a finite-precision floating-backend guarantee. Prior frozen work was read only. No random operations, model initialization or sampling were performed.

## 1. Scope and parameterized bound

Fix integers M>=1, k in [0,M], d>=1 and r>=0, a public clean binary history h of length T=M+2, and a public terminal pair u=(h_M,h_(M+1)). A legal output z differs from h in at most k positions in {0,...,M-1} and has z_M,z_(M+1)=u. During a left-to-right scan, let w_t denote the previous r bits (zeros pad the nonexistent negative indices), and q_t one of Q>=1 finite-state labels, initially fixed. At position t, the allowed candidate bit x=z_t, its local window, q_t, the fixed index t and the entire *observed* h determine:

- d integer increments g_j(t,q_t,w_t,x;h), j=1,...,d;
- a deterministic next state q_(t+1)=delta(t,q_t,w_t,x;h).

The d-bank is B_j(z)=sum_(t=0)^(T-1) g_j(t,q_t,w_t,z_t;h). It represents dyadic feature F_j(z)=2^(-p_j) B_j(z), with public integer p_j>=0 and one common denominator per coordinate; the same denominator is used across positions after exact alignment. An exact deterministic terminal readout U(B_1,...,B_d,q_T,w_T;h) can depend on h, protected endpoint, and fixed public model parameters. It must not receive hidden source parameters or future labels if it is to define a source-independent attack. An arbitrary rational readout is allowed when its evaluation and comparisons are exact; no efficient or bounded-bit implementation is implicit.

For each (t,j), let l_tj <= g_j <= u_tj over all formal local inputs, not just reachable inputs. Define

  L_j=sum_t min(0,l_tj), H_j=sum_t max(0,u_tj), R_j=H_j-L_j+1,
  S=2^r Q product_j R_j.

Including zero in every term is necessary because intermediate prefix sums need not lie between the final sums when signed increments cancel. These ranges may be loose; at every layer, each bank coordinate of every prefix lies in [L_j,H_j]. In a dense-state unit-cost model, the exact maximum and one legal witness can be obtained with at most O(T S) two-branch transitions and O(S) terminal readout/comparison operations, plus initialization and T-bit output. Include O(d) bounded-integer additions/index operations per transition, cost of obtaining g and delta, and readout/comparison costs. Rolling storage is O(S log(k+2)) bits for costs/reachability (including an unreachable marker) plus the read-only input and indexing machinery. A full dense predecessor record can name the previous state and bit in O(log(S+1)) bits; retaining all layers costs O(T S log(S+1)) bits and a single traceback takes O(T) records. A rolling-memory alternative reruns the relevant prefix at each traceback step, requiring O(T^2 S) transitions, O(S log(k+2)) working bits plus O(T) output bits; its data access, arithmetic and tie handling must be deterministic and paid. These are upper bounds, not measured performance.

If |g_j|<=W_j for all local inputs, R_j<=2T W_j+1; if the aligned numerator magnitude is at most 2^b uniformly, then S<=2^r Q (2T 2^b+1)^d. With d,r fixed, Q polynomial in M, all W_j polynomial in M, local transition and exact-readout/comparison time polynomial in the explicit input size, and all associated grid exponents/encodings polynomially manageable, this is a polynomial-time method. In particular b=O(log M) is sufficient **only when b bounds the aligned integer numerators**, not merely the number of bits in an unaligned floating significand. Growing d gives a product/exponential-in-d bound. Binary weights with b=Theta(M^c) can make the range bound exponential in input length; pseudopolynomial means polynomial in their numerical magnitudes, not their binary representation. An exactly comparable readout can itself be computationally expensive even when S is small. Unbounded r or Q also changes the complexity.

## 2. Recurrence and correctness

Initialize q=q_0, w=0^r, B=0 and minimum mismatch cost 0. At step t<T, if t<M, branch x=0 or 1; if t>=M, require x=h_t. Add 1{x!=h_t} when t<M (zero otherwise), evaluate increments, update the finite state and last-r-bit window, and discard any path with cost>k. For each resulting key (q_(t+1),w_(t+1),B_1,...,B_d), retain the least cost and a predecessor for one such prefix.

The induction invariant is that the retained value is the minimum legal prefix Hamming cost for that exact key. If two prefixes share it, every common suffix encounters the same future local inputs, evolves through the same automaton labels, produces the same added bank increments and mismatch costs against the fixed public h, and reaches the same allowed terminal readout. Therefore the larger-cost prefix is dominated for a budget of at most k. Nonnegative mismatch increments justify pruning costs above k. Every legal full word appears as one path, and every retained terminal key has a legal witness, so evaluating U on the reachable terminal keys and selecting its greatest exact value is exact.

This is not valid for exactly k edits, unless the actual cost is retained in the key (or a different correct recurrence is supplied). An additional additive nonnegative weighted history-dependent cost c(t,q,w,x;h) can replace the Hamming cost if its own budget is the intended threat model; to impose both Hamming and weighted constraints generally requires retaining an additional resource dimension or Pareto frontier. Arbitrary dependence on the *modified* full prefix cannot be silently handled by q: the stated q or w must actually make future increments and costs Markovian. Clean h is fixed read-only public information and may influence time-varying weights or costs. State restrictions may be imposed by marking transitions illegal or accepting only specified terminal q.

## 3. Multiple normalized attention heads

Consider H heads, with p_h scalar value channels for head h. At position t let nonnegative dyadic a_(h,t)(q_t,w_t,z_t;h) be its explicitly represented unnormalized weight and v_(h,t,c)(q_t,w_t,z_t;h) a signed dyadic value. Assume their dependence is only through the declared local window, finite state, fixed t and h, and that a strictly positive denominator is guaranteed for each legal z. Give head h the exact normalized outputs

  O_(h,c)(z)= [sum_t a_(h,t)(...) v_(h,t,c)(...)] / [sum_t a_(h,t)(...)].

For each head keep one denominator sum and p_h numerator sums: d=sum_h(p_h+1). Multiply the dyadic local values exactly first, then align per-coordinate denominators; account for the resulting numerator bit widths and exponent spans in b. All O_(h,c) are exact ratios of retained integer sums with known dyadic scales, hence any rational terminal readout of these head outputs is covered if zero denominators are excluded and its exact cost is charged. A head may have a fixed public mask by taking a=0 on masked positions. Fixed public positional attention and a bounded finite-state/local token-dependent rational normalization are therefore special cases. A fixed number of scalar heads gives d=2H; vector-valued heads cost p_h+1 each, not merely two counters.

This statement assumes dyadic unnormalized weights are themselves explicit permitted coefficients, not that exponentiating arbitrary finite-precision softmax scores gives dyadics. Standard softmax may generate irrational exponentials; its exact semantics requires a different symbolic/comparison analysis. The result does not cover arbitrary data-dependent/global attention scores, query interactions depending on the whole edited history, multi-layer feedback, layer normalization, hidden full history, arbitrary feature maps, or a NumPy/BLAS float execution whose reduction order changes the bank. Fixed number of heads is not enough unless the resulting sufficient additive feature count, integer ranges, finite-state size and exact readout complexity are all controlled.

## 4. Reconciliation and limits

The fixed-half selected-row bank is a special case with d=4,r=2,Q=1, 0/1 position-dependent triple increments, and bounds O(M^5) transitions / O(M^4) rolling states as in `../four_counter_dp_20261103/RESEARCH_NOTE.md` and its independent review. The accepted correction `../four_counter_dp_20261103/ADDITIVE_CORRECTION.md` distinguishes mathematical rational inputs from its existing float-converting implementation; this new theorem is likewise an exact-integer/rational statement and is not a repair of that implementation.

The earlier `../position_weighted_attack_hardness_20261103_0903/RESEARCH_NOTE.citation_corrected.md` reduction uses one additive weighted sum, but permits binary-encoded weights of size A exponential in their encoding length. Here d=1 and the range factor can be O(M A); a pseudo-polynomial DP and weak NP-hardness are consistent. Padding its instance to M>=n² gives k<=sqrt(M), but does not bound the weight magnitudes by poly(M). Fixed k also allows direct mask enumeration in sum_(j<=k) binom(M,j) times actual model-evaluation cost. Neither the pseudo-polynomial upper bound nor prior reduction implies hardness for approximate attack or a lower bound for trained models. Exact numerical comparison of sigmoid/log based CE requires a proved monotonic reduction or separately justified exact-comparison oracle.

The method is a finite-state version of familiar subset-sum / multidimensional knapsack dynamic programming. Bellman's classic pseudo-polynomial subset-sum bound is discussed in recent research https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11020. For a fixed number of dimensions, see Gurski, Rehs and Rethmann, *Knapsack Problems: A Parameterized Point of View*, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2018.12.019. No novelty is claimed for multidimensional table states, minimum-resource dominance, or pseudo-polynomial complexity. The useful contribution is the explicit edit-window/protected-endpoint/feature/precision interface and comparison to the project’s finite-position and weak-hardness cases; independent review remains necessary.

## 5. Deterministic diagnostic

`check_dp.py` exhaustively compares the complete terminal-key/minimum-edit map, legal witnesses and a nontrivial exact rational readout against independently enumerated legal masks for M=1,...,5, all clean histories and budgets, with two signed position- and clean-history-dependent features, r=2 and a three-state automaton. It separately checks normalized two-head dyadic numerator/denominator reconstruction on small cases, including protected endpoints. The saved stdout `check_dp.log` is an original deterministic run; these checks are finite corroboration, not a proof, backend certification or benchmark.
