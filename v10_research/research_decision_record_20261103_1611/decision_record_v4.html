Research decision record: context matching, risk and resource accounting

3 November 2026; version 4. Decision aid for binary order-two prediction on the same observed history and its actual terminal query. “Reviewed” below means an independent language-model mathematical/source review in the stated scope, not formal verification or external peer review. No paper-novelty or training-success conclusion is asserted.

Decision. Do not frame add-one estimation, idealized two-layer BOS smoothing, general softmax stability, logarithmic temperature scaling, or the special-family statistical upper rate as standalone new discoveries. The strongest surviving direction is a precisely matched standard-causal architecture/precision question that allows richer values and residual bypasses and charges the full computation. Current evidence does not establish a full-network lower bound or a publication-ready separation.

A content fingerprint identifies bytes, not accessibility; inherited results must be accompanied by their proofs and reviews before any package is called self-contained.

3. Prior art and claim reductions

  • D’Angelo et al., Proposition 3.1 and Corollary 4.1 already give soft-mask matching and limiting BOS add-α, including binary add-one. Their Lemma 4.2 already discusses logarithmic length-dependent temperature under a different smoothing criterion. Exact indexing and limit comparison.
  • Han, Jana and Wu, Lemma 16 already gives terminal-conditioned transition-count moments under reversibility; the generic nonreversible implication here uses standard arguments and is not a novelty certification.
  • Trottner, Theorems 4.1 and 4.3, gives Markov-design Nadaraya–Watson risk bounds and, in Theorem 4.3, average squared prediction error for a query on the same chain under explicit noise and mixing assumptions. This is close prior on the bias/occupancy theme, not directly a binary next-state loss conditioned on one actual terminal state: in particular the theorem’s independent-noise condition cannot be silently imposed on successor labels that also influence the subsequent state path. Soft-matching source audit and exact theorem scope.
  • Goel and Bartlett, Theorem 1, explicitly represents a Gaussian Nadaraya–Watson estimator using a softmax attention block. This is a substantial prior representation, not by itself a conditional next-state TV risk or the specified finite-bit implementation.
  • Cohen and Fausti, Theorem 5.1 is prior sharp Hilbert-distance/TV stability; their TV norm is full ℓ1. Blanchard, Higham and Higham analyze numerical softmax/log-sum-exp rather than treating exact softmax as ordinary floating-point evaluation.
  • Weiss et al. and Behrens et al. already study attention-based histograms and counting. The existing research-program finite-bank and count-flow antecedents further reduce any internal novelty claim; cited fingerprints alone do not make their proofs publicly accessible.
  • Rajaraman et al., §6 has lower bounds under different order, alphabet, accuracy or attention-pattern restrictions; those results cannot simply be read as a binary order-two average-risk full-network lower.

The preceding links establish source overlap within inspected sections and versions, not an exhaustive search. An independent LLM review is useful error checking, not journal peer review or a proof assistant certificate. No external novelty gate or publication decision is asserted to have been passed.

4. Most useful next question

Resolve one fully specified standard-causal model, including its bypass channels. Fix constants μ>0 and D₀<∞. Consider stationary binary order-two chains with every pair-state probability ≥μ and reverse mixing sum ≤D₀, and measure expected binary TV conditional on each actual terminal s. Use a length-uniform two-block causal decoder with one attention head per block and fixed width at most 32; allow full residual/value channels and standard tokenwise ReLU feed-forward operations, with no external division, rounding oracle, precomputed count bank or input reread. Give positions through an explicitly charged (1,i,i²) representation, and require the input-dependent component of each match score to have range at most a fixed B independent of M. The length-dependent parameters in the compact-causal v3 construction do not resolve this fixed-parameter question. Parameters here are fixed independently of the unknown source and sequence length; integer range and p=O(log(M+2)) fractional bits, masks, softmax evaluation, intermediate rounding and dense or sparse work must be specified.

Question: can such a declared model attain uniform O((M+1)−1/2) conditional risk for all s and sources in that class, or can a lower bound rule it out even when every allowed value and residual channel participates? A positive answer needs an explicit construction with end-to-end numeric error and operation accounting; a negative answer needs an information/representation argument for the entire allowed model, not one head port. Compare the same information and memory with the one-pass count baseline. Before proving either direction, formalize how the fixed-B input-dependent score range composes with positional terms and ReLU/residual paths so it cannot exclude or smuggle in the proposed counterexamples by definition.

This is a question, not a theorem or an assertion that the restricted problem will be novel. If the model proves too weak or the precision semantics prohibit a meaningful construction, record the obstruction and adjust the model transparently rather than claim a general Transformer separation.
