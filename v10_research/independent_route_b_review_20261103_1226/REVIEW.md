## Derivations and attacks

1. **Finite-prefix operator and zero occupation.** For each s, z_s=Σ_t K_st u_t>0 because N≥3, M>0, u is a probability vector, and K is strictly positive. Thus A_st=K_st u_t/z_s and q=A p̂; undefined p̂_t for u_t=0 can be assigned any value because its column vanishes. For β₁β₂≠0 each 2×2 factor has nonzero determinant, so K invertible and its occupied columns linearly independent: rank A=number of occupied states. With β_i=0, rank K≤2, so full inversion is impossible even with all states occupied. A rank-deficient vector map can nevertheless identify a particular linear functional; no blanket coordinate non-identifiability follows. This is pathwise and does not assume independence of query or prefix.
2. **Weighted reversible geometry, signs and π-norms.** For full support μ_s=u_s z_s/(uᵀKu), and μ_s A_st=u_s K_st u_t/(uᵀKu)=μ_t A_ts. Conjugating by diag(√μ) gives B=D K D with D=diag(√(u/z)), symmetric even when a β is negative. The eigenvalues of K are products of 2 cosh β_i and 2 sinh β_i; its singular values use absolute values, so σ_min(K)=4|sinh β₁ sinh β₂|. Since σ_min(DKD)≥σ_min(D)^2σ_min(K), the proposed lower bound follows without assuming K is positive semidefinite. Jensen and stationarity give L²(μ) norm≤1. For strictly positive π, min R‖x‖²_π≤‖x‖²_μ≤max R‖x‖²_π with R=μ/π; applying this to inputs and outputs gives both displayed √(min/max) and √(max/min) bounds. These pathwise norms do not themselves average terminal risk.
