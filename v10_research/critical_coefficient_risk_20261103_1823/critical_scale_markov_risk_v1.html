At a fixed multiple of 1/N, coefficient drift can still cause risk

3 November 2026. Exact-real analytic candidate awaiting independent review. This proof does not extrapolate the earlier qN=o(N/log N) argument, use tests or enumeration, or claim a general Transformer threshold.

Claim. In the same four-block graph and fixed stationary positive fast-mixing binary source as the fixed-η counterexample, change only the lag-one head's −ij² coefficient to −(1+ηN)ij² with ηN=2/(N+2). Then the actual-terminal expected TV risk tends to 583/3072. Here the scalar weight change is 4ηN=8/(N+2), so the perturbed weights depend on N and approach the original weights.

1. Setup and deterministic position regimes

Let L=N+2, D=L+2, i=k+2 for data position k, and r=i−1=k+1. The source has uniform stationary pair law and next-one probabilities θ₀₀=3/4, θ₁₀=1/4, θ₀₁=θ₁₁=1/2. The exact-real graph, two BOS tokens, all residuals, lag-two head, masks, FFNs and final vocabulary softmax are unchanged; only one lag-one Q-projection scalar is multiplied by 1+ηN.

The perturbed lag-one score is a row constant minus (1+2/L)i(j−[r−ζᵢ])², where ζᵢ=2r/D lies in [0,2). Its nearest integer key is r−d when ζᵢ is nearest integer d∈{0,1,2}. Thus the switching locations in r are D/4 and 3D/4. These depend on N; exact half-integer ties are not broken by assumption but placed in the exceptional set below.

Set H=⌈100 ln L⌉+10. Discard historical rows with i<H, |r−D/4|≤H or |r−3D/4|≤H. There are O(log L) such rows. For each remaining i≤L, the winning key is unique and its gap to the second-best key is at least H/2. Indeed, if i<D/8, then ζᵢ<1/4, d=0 and the score gap is at least i/2≥H/2. If i≥D/8, distance greater than H from both switching points gives gap at least i·4H/D≥H/2. This remains true even when the unconstrained real center is near the causal boundary, because comparing against the nearest integer in the larger integer line only makes the bound conservative. All other keys have no larger score than the second-best.

The total off-target mass is at most L e−H/2≤L⁻⁴⁹<1/4. The original snap therefore exactly reads Xk−1−d on every remaining data row, while the unchanged lag-two head reads Xk−2. For all sufficiently large N the selected key is a data position. The resulting exact anchor label is

d=0: (X_(k−2), X_(k−1)),       asymptotic fraction 1/4;
d=1: (X_(k−2), X_(k−2)),       asymptotic fraction 1/2;
d=2: (X_(k−2), X_(k−3)),       asymptotic fraction 1/4.

These fractions follow directly from the deterministic switch locations. At the terminal i=L, ζL=2−6/(L+2); for all sufficiently large N it has unique nearest integer d=2, the snap is exact, and the actual query onehot is (XN−3, XN). The finitely many small N are irrelevant to the asymptotic claim; the N=2 boundary remains that of the original graph, not a premise of this result.

2. Four limiting anchor estimates

Write ra=P(Xₖ=1|Xk−2=a), so r₀=5/8, r₁=3/8. Also let

t_(b,a)=P(X_k=1 | X_(k−3)=b, X_(k−2)=a)
       =(1−θ_ba) θ_a0+θ_ba θ_a1.

The relevant t values are t₀₀=9/16, t₁₀=11/16 and t₀₁=t₁₁=3/8. For each anchor (a,b), the limiting fraction of keys and fraction of successor-one keys are

J_ab/N → 1/8 + (1/4)·1{a=b},
C_ab/N → (θ_ab+t_ba)/16 + (r_a/4)·1{a=b}.

These follow by considering the three deterministic blocks above. In the first and third blocks the relevant ordered pair is uniform with probability 1/4; in the middle block only diagonal anchors occur, with probability 1/2. For each of the finitely many fixed-offset indicator functions, the stationary finite irreducible pair-chain ergodic theorem gives almost-sure convergence on prefix averages. Differences of prefix sums give the corresponding deterministic-interval averages, with lengths tending to 1/4, 1/2 and 1/4. Removing O(log N) rows changes each normalized count by o(1); no iid or independent terminal-query assumption is made.

Each limiting denominator is positive, so the normalized block-3 anchor outputs converge almost surely to

The two BOS positions contribute fixed pseudocounts. The O(log N) exceptional keys have gate values in [0,1], so after scaling by e−N they have total weight O(log N); all good nonmatches contribute at most N e−N. Since every good anchor has Θ(N) matching keys, these terms and the fixed BOS contribution vanish relative to the denominator. For the terminal query, the unchanged ReLU selector therefore outputs pXₙ₋₃,Xₙ+o(1) almost surely. Its scalar lies in [0,1]. The unchanged fourth-block Gaussian/log-position estimate makes the final vocabulary-softmax probability differ by at most 168/√(N+2), uniformly over inputs, so it has the same limit in L1.

3. Actual-terminal risk

Let a=XN−3, b=XN−1, c=XN. The true next-one probability is θbc, and the selected limiting prediction is pac. Under the stationary pair law, summing over XN−2 gives the following joint probabilities for c=0:

(a,b)(0,0)(0,1)(1,0)(1,1)
P(a,b,c=0)7/6410/649/646/64

For c=1, P(a=0,c=1)=15/64 and P(a=1,c=1)=17/64, while both θ₀₁ and θ₁₁ equal 1/2. For c=0 the four absolute errors in the table's order are 11/96, 37/96, 42/96 and 6/96. For c=1 the errors are 3/32 when a=0 and 5/48 when a=1. Therefore

lim_N E TV(Q_N,θ_(X_(N−1),X_N))
 = (7·11+10·37+9·42+6·6)/(64·96)
   + (15/64)(3/32)+(17/64)(5/48)
 = 583/3072 >0.

The true source and stationary initialization are fixed. The model weights depend on N only through the displayed one coefficient ηN. Bounded convergence justifies passing the four-anchor convergence and uniform fourth-block bound to expected TV; the joint probabilities above are those of the true terminal window, not an independent-query approximation.

4. What is and is not matched

The independently reviewed safe-window calculation gives exact wordwise equality with the unperturbed output whenever |ηN|≤1/[8(N+2)]. This example gives failure at positive ηN=2/(N+2), so both boundaries have Θ(1/N) scale up to constants for this one coefficient and graph. It does not identify the critical constant, assert monotonicity or failure for every η outside the safe range, cover all signs at failure, or establish a threshold for other architectures, general perturbations or IEEE arithmetic. If one scalar Q weight is counted, the respective sufficient/error magnitudes are 4 times these η values.

Liu et al., Appendix C.3, Proposition 4 already study position-QK perturbation and failure at large length in a different one-head flip-flop setting. Chen et al. relate copier and matching errors to full empirical predictor error under their assumptions. Neither prior is represented here as a generic general-network lower bound, and their mechanisms cannot be claimed new merely because the present same-source actual-terminal risk is explicit. Broader prior-art search remains open.
