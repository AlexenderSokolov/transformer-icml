Fixed alphabet terminal KL at logarithmic rate

Composition note, 4 November 2026. This note combines the frozen, analytically reviewed row-tilting theorem with the separately reviewed five-block exact-real construction and its specialized integer/dyadic evaluator. It does not change any of those source files. The proof statements and complete analytical reviews are reproduced in the accompanying offline HTML.

Scope and main conclusion

Fix a publicly known alphabet size A>=2. Observe X_1,...,X_{n+2} from an arbitrary homogeneous second-order source on [A]. Its transition rows and initial pair distribution are arbitrary and unknown. Zeros, deterministic rows, reducibility and periodicity are permitted. The target is the true next-symbol row at the actual terminal context Y_n=(X_{n+1},X_{n+2}); there is no fresh independently sampled context. All logarithms in KL and risk bounds are natural.

Let B be ordinary full-history add-one at that terminal context, let L=n+2+A, and for n>=1 define

K_n = 1+floor(log_2 n),
V_{A,n} = min{log(n+A),
              A^4[log A+1536 A K_n]/(n+1)}.

The frozen row-tilting proof gives

E D_KL(p_{Y_n}||B) <= V_{A,n}.

For every fixed A, this is O_A(log(n+2)/(n+1)), uniformly over the stated kernels and initial pair distributions. Its leading alphabet factor is A^5. It is not a claim of sharp growing-alphabet dependence or of an optimal constant. The proof evaluates the actual full-history estimator; it does not replace it by a minimax-existence or suffix-averaged estimator.

Exact real five block corollary

Use the unchanged five-block causal attention/ReLU model in the complete exact-real prerequisite. For each fixed A, its learned coefficients are fixed independently of history length and source. Inputs and resource assumptions remain exactly those specified there, including the positional features (1,i,i^2,log i), full causal attention, A smoothing BOS tokens, and ordinary final A-way vocabulary softmax. Let R be its exact-real terminal output.

The prerequisite proves, for every word including n=0,

TV(R,B) <= min{1,(44A+1)/L},
R_b >= 1/[1+(A-1)L] >= 1/(AL).

Hence for n>=1,

E D_KL(p_{Y_n}||R)
  <= min{log[1+(A-1)L],
         2V_{A,n}+2A(44A+1)^2/L}.

The only substitution in the statistical transfer is the new upper bound for E D_KL(p_{Y_n}||B). Its pathwise approximation and support bounds do not depend on which statistical proof is used. For fixed A, the statistical rate is O_A(log(n+2)/(n+1)), with a separate O_A(1/(n+1)) output-approximation penalty. This is an exact-real theorem, not a finite-bit assertion.

Specialized finite bit corollary

Separately use the exact integer/dyadic evaluator specified in the complete precision prerequisite. It is an explicit numerical evaluation scheme for the same five-block model, with max-shifted exponential approximation, constructed log positions, exact dyadic gates, and mass-preserving normalization. Let Q be its numerical terminal output, and choose

s = ceil(log_2(AL)),
b = 32s,  H = 2^b,  F = H^2.

The prerequisite proves, for every word including n=0,

Q is an exact nonnegative H-grid simplex,
TV(Q,B) <= min{1,(46A+2)/L},
Q_b >= 1/(4AL),
D_KL(p_{Y_n}||Q)
  <= 2D_KL(p_{Y_n}||B)+8A(46A+2)^2/L.

Consequently, for n>=1,

E D_KL(p_{Y_n}||Q)
  <= min{log(4AL),
         2V_{A,n}+8A(46A+2)^2/L}.

This follows by substituting the same add-one statistical theorem into a pointwise finite-evaluator inequality. It does not infer forward KL from TV alone; the explicit positive support floor is essential. For fixed A the rate remains O_A(log(n+2)/(n+1)).

The value b counts fractional bits, not the entire signed word. Integer positions, score magnitude, accumulation and constant-factor wider products are charged separately, with total working integer lengths O(log(AL)). The exact-real model's learned parameters remain fixed; arithmetic precision is a length-dependent resource. The inherited conservative complete causal evaluation cost is

O(((A^2+2A+3)L^2+L)b^3
   +(A^3 L^2+A^6 L)b^2) bit operations,

with row-streamed storage

O(A^3 L b+b^2+A^6 log(A+2)) bits.

This corollary is not a guarantee for generic IEEE, fixed word length, fixed exponent range, GPU kernels, or automatic mixed precision. It is not a matching lower bound on all numerical representations, an optimal compute theorem, a training claim, or an executed implementation certificate.

The zero transition case

For n=0, B is uniform and D_KL(p_{Y_0}||B)<=log A pointwise. Do not evaluate K_n or V_{A,n} at zero. The two output bounds above instead use

E D_KL(p_{Y_0}||R)
  <= min{log[1+(A-1)L],
         2log A+2A(44A+1)^2/L},
E D_KL(p_{Y_0}||Q)
  <= min{log(4AL),
         2log A+8A(46A+2)^2/L},
L=A+2.

These boundary formulas use the unchanged all-history pathwise guarantees.

What the sharpening does and does not establish

The decisive joint tail applies a kernel-uniform occupation estimate under an explicitly altered transition law. It is not a multiplication of two unrelated marginal bounds and does not condition selected row counts to be binomial. The initial pair law is retained under the alternative kernel; stationarity under that kernel is neither assumed nor needed.

The arbitrary-start occupation constant must be used after tilting. This argument therefore does not inherit the better stationary alphabet factor from the older log-squared proof. The older proofs and their rate statements remain preserved as historical source text; this composition note supersedes only the statistical upper bound used in these two corollaries.

For fixed A and order two, these three upper bounds match a primary-source stationary minimax lower bound in their dependence on sample length. The precise implication is stated separately below. It does not establish sharp A dependence or resolve every parameter regime of the ordinary full-history add-one question. The binary first-order minimax rate is different, so the displayed O(log n/n) rate is not asserted to be optimal there.

The attached prior-art audit is a bounded search record dated 3 November 2026, not a novelty certificate or exhaustive exclusion of earlier results. Classical occupation, Chernoff, random-count and Markov change-of-measure tools are explicitly distinguished from this particular combination. Matching fixed alphabet minimax lower bound

The separate primary-source audit checks the actual-terminal forward-KL loss in Han, Jana and Wu's published Section V-A, Eq. (103), and the second-order lower bound in Theorems 22 and 24. Their observed-symbol length is N=n+2, so the terminal pair and next-symbol target coincide exactly with those used here. Theorem 22 gives

R_A^stat(n)
  >= A^3/[C_2(n+2)] log[(n+2)/A^3],
provided n+2 >= (C_2 A)^3,

where C_2 is an absolute constant supplied by that theorem, not assigned a numerical value here. For every fixed A this is Omega_A(log n/n) as n tends to infinity. Theorem 24 explicitly covers binary second-order sources. Binary first-order behavior is not substituted for that result.

The stationary source class is a subset of the class allowing arbitrary initial-pair laws. Its minimax lower bound therefore also lower-bounds minimax risk over that larger class. Every history-dependent probability-vector output, including B, R and Q, is covered by the minimax infimum. Combining that lower bound with the corresponding uniform upper bounds above shows that each of B, R and Q is minimax-rate optimal in n for each fixed A>=2, both over the stationary class and over the unrestricted-initial-pair class:

worst-case expected forward KL = Theta_A(log n/n),
as n tends to infinity for fixed A.

This is a worst-case, fixed-alphabet asymptotic result. It is not a lower bound at each individual source, for each prescribed initial distribution, or at every sample length. It does not close the gap between the A^5 upper factor here and the A^3 stationary minimax factor. For Q it still applies only to the specialized evaluator and its paid precision schedule.

Candidate: terminal KL through row-tilted occupation bounds

Analytical candidate, 3 November 2026. This file must receive independent exact-file review before acceptance. It preserves all previous frozen notes. No numerical experiment, simulation, training, external upload or public repository mutation was performed. No literature-priority claim is made.

1. Statement

Let A>=2, n>=1, and observe X_1,...,X_{n+2} from a homogeneous second-order source on [A]. All transition rows and the initial pair distribution are arbitrary. Zero probabilities, deterministic rows, reducibility and periodicity are allowed. Define D=A^2, Y_i=(X_{i+1},X_{i+2}) for 0<=i<=n, and

J_s=sum_{i=0}^{n-1}1{Y_i=s},
C_{s,b}=sum_{i=0}^{n-1}1{Y_i=s,X_{i+3}=b},
B_s(b)=(C_{s,b}+1)/(J_s+A),
J=J_{Y_n}.

Write p_s(b) for the true row probabilities, use natural logarithms, and evaluate forward KL at the actual terminal context. Let

K_n=1+floor(log_2 n),
W_{A,n}=A^4/(n+1) [log A+1536 A K_n],
V_{A,n}=min{log(n+A), W_{A,n}}.

The candidate theorem is

E D_KL(p_{Y_n}||B_{Y_n}) <= V_{A,n}.                 (1)

Thus the actual full-history add-one predictor has O_A(log(n+2)/(n+1)) risk for each fixed A, uniformly over arbitrary initial pair distributions. The result also applies to stationary initial pairs, but it does not retain the better stationary alphabet factor of the earlier log-squared bound. For n=0 use log A.

The leading alphabet factor here is A^5, not the A^3 complexity in sharp stationary higher-order minimax results. This candidate does not assert optimal growing-alphabet dependence, a new estimator, or a solution of every parameter regime of the full-history add-one question in prior work.

2. A kernel-uniform, fixed-target occupation bound

For every homogeneous Markov chain on D states, every initial distribution mu, every target state s, and integer w>=1,

P_mu(Y_n=s,J_s<w) <= D w/(n+1).                       (2)

For clarity, the bound is uniform over the transition kernel; this is crucial when it is used for an altered kernel below.

Proof: For a chain started at z define

b_m^s(z)=P_z(Y_m=s, sum_{i=0}^{m-1}1{Y_i=s}<w),
B_m^s=max_z b_m^s(z).

If m>=ell, fewer than w earlier copies in the full path implies fewer than w copies in its last ell departures. Conditioning at time m-ell gives B_m^s<=B_ell^s. On each deterministic path only the first w occurrences of s satisfy the flag, so sum_{m=0}^n b_m^s(z)<=w. Therefore

(n+1)B_n^s <=sum_{m=0}^n B_m^s
            <=sum_z sum_{m=0}^n b_m^s(z)<=Dw.

Mixture over the starting state proves (2). Put r=D/(n+1). In particular, for any integer m>=1,

P_mu(Y_n=s, m<=J_s<2m)<=2rm.                          (3)

No stationarity, irreducibility, reversibility or mixing assumption is used.

3. Change one row, rather than conditioning a stack

Fix s,b and p=p_s(b) in (0,1). For any q in (0,1), define an alternative homogeneous second-order source Q with the same initial pair distribution and all rows unchanged except s. In row s let

q_s(b)=q,
q_s(a)=p_s(a)(1-q)/(1-p), a!=b.                       (4)

Thus the alternative remains in the same D-state pair-chain class. Zero probabilities among the other symbols remain zero. The original law P and Q have the same path support. For any observed path of positive probability, put j=J_s and k=C_{s,b}. The exact path-probability ratio is

P(path)/Q(path)
   =(p/q)^k [(1-p)/(1-q)]^(j-k).                     (5)

The initial factor cancels, all unmodified rows cancel, and each departure from s contributes one of the two displayed factors. There is no extra factor from Y_n: the probability of the observed path already includes the terminal state and all n observed transitions.

Let d(q||p) denote binary forward KL. If q>p and k/j>=q, or q<p and k/j<=q, then

log[P(path)/Q(path)]
   =-j d(q||p)
     -(k-jq)log[q(1-p)/(p(1-q))]
   <=-j d(q||p).                                    (6)

Consequently, for E_m={Y_n=s,m<=J_s<2m},

P(E_m, C_{s,b}/J_s>=q)
   <=2rm exp[-m d(q||p)], q>p,
P(E_m, C_{s,b}/J_s<=q)
   <=2rm exp[-m d(q||p)], q<p.                       (7)

To obtain (7), sum (6) over the indicated paths and apply (3) under the altered law Q, not under P. The initial distribution remains the same but is not assumed stationary for Q. This use of the arbitrary-start bound is intentional.

4. A joint occupation-times-deviation tail

For t>=1 let

delta=sqrt(2pt/m)+2t/(3m).

For either q=p+delta or q=p-delta lying in (0,1), the Bernstein binary-KL bound gives

d(q||p)>=delta^2/[2(p+delta/3)]>=t/m.                 (8)

Here is a direct justification of the first inequality. For Z a centered Bernoulli(p) variable, |Z|<=1 and E Z^2<=p. Expansion of the exponential gives, for either sign and 0<=lambda<3,

log E exp(+-lambda Z)
   <=p lambda^2/[2(1-lambda/3)].

Use the variational formula for binary KL and take lambda=delta/(p+delta/3)<3. This gives the first inequality in (8). For the second, put v=t/m and expand delta=sqrt(2pv)+2v/3; then delta^2>=2v(p+delta/3).

On m<=J_s<2m,

J_s delta <=sqrt(4J_s p t)+4t/3.

Thus (7)-(8), with a union over the two directions, give

P(E_m, |C_{s,b}-J_s p|>sqrt(4J_s p t)+4t/3)
   <=4rm exp(-t).                                   (9)

If p+delta>=1, the corresponding strict upper-tail event is impossible; similarly if p-delta<=0 for the lower tail. Such directions do not require an endpoint tilt. If p=0 or p=1, the row count is deterministic given J_s, so the deviation event is empty and (9) holds directly.

Equation (9) does not assert that C_{s,b} is binomial after conditioning on J_s or on the terminal state. It obtains the product factor by a change of the actual transition kernel. A different deterministic alternative kernel may be used for each threshold t; tail integration below requires separate probability bounds, not one common simultaneous alternative law.

5. Scalar forward-KL control

For x>=0 and a>0 set

h(x,a)=x log(x/a)-x+a,

with 0 log 0=0. Suppose j>=0, p in [0,1], 0<=k<=j, u>=1, and

|k-jp|<=sqrt(2jp u)+2u/3.                             (10)

Then

h((j+1)p,k+1)<=48u(1+log u).                         (11)

Proof: Let z=jp, a=k+1, x=z+p. From sqrt(2zu)<=z/2+u,

z<=2k+10u/3,
(k-z)^2<=4zu+8u^2/9<=8ku+128u^2/9.

Since |p-1|<=1 and u>=1,

(x-a)^2<=16ku+31u^2,
x<=2a+10u/3.

The inequality h(x,a)<=(x-a)^2/a shows h<=47u when a>=u. If a<u then x<6u and a>=1, giving

h(x,a)<=x log_+x+a<=6u log(6u)+u
         <=48u(1+log u).

This proves (11), including p=0, p=1 and j=0.

For a full A-symbol row with counts summing to j, let q_b=(k_b+1)/(j+A). The exact scaling identity is

sum_b h((j+1)p_b,k_b+1)
   =(j+1)D_KL(p||q)+(A-1)
       -(j+1)log[1+(A-1)/(j+1)]
   >=(j+1)D_KL(p||q),                                (12)

where the last step uses log(1+v)<=v. Thus no A-dependent lower bound on u is needed.

6. Integrate one coordinate on one dyadic band

Fix s,b,m. Define the nonnegative random variable

Z=h((J_s+1)p_s(b),C_{s,b}+1)/(J_s+1).

Take u=2t in (10). The threshold in (10) becomes exactly the one in (9). By (11), for t>=1 and on E_m outside that deviation event,

Z<=96t[1+log(2t)]/(J_s+1)
   <=192t(1+log t)/m=:f_m(t).                        (13)

The second inequality uses 1+log 2<=2 and J_s+1>=m. Therefore

P(E_m,Z>f_m(t))<=4rm exp(-t), t>=1.                  (14)

Since f_m is strictly increasing, layer-cake integration and (3) give

E[1{E_m}Z]
   <=f_m(1)P(E_m)
        +integral_1^infty f_m'(t)P(E_m,Z>f_m(t))dt
   <=384r+768r integral_1^infty(2+log t)exp(-t)dt
   <=384r+2304r/e
   <1536r.                                          (15)

For the penultimate step use log t<=t-1, so the integral is at most integral_1^infty(1+t)e^(-t)dt=3/e. The final inequality uses e>2. No union over coordinates is used in (15); nonnegative scalar expectations are summed only after integration.

7. Sum bands, rows and symbols

The disjoint dyadic bands m=1,2,4,...,2^floor(log_2 n) cover J_s=1,...,n. For a selected terminal row with positive count, (12) bounds KL by the sum of the A nonnegative scalar Z terms. Summing (15) over K_n bands, D target rows and A symbols yields

E[1{J>=1}D_KL(p_{Y_n}||B_{Y_n})]
   <=1536 D A K_n r
   =1536 D^2 A K_n/(n+1).                           (16)

On J=0 the add-one estimator is uniform, so its loss is at most log A. Equation (2) with w=1, summed over s, gives P(J=0)<=D^2/(n+1). Thus

E[1{J=0}D_KL(p_{Y_n}||B_{Y_n})]
   <=D^2 log A/(n+1).                               (17)

Combine (16)-(17), substitute D=A^2, and use the deterministic pathwise cap log(n+A). This proves the candidate bound (1).

8. Transfer to the existing five-block output

The independently reviewed five-block exact-real construction is unchanged. Its pathwise approximation and support bounds, for L=n+2+A, are

TV(R,B)<=min{1,(44A+1)/L},
R_b>=1/[1+(A-1)L]>=1/(AL).

The existing entropy-variational transfer consequently gives

E D_KL(p_{Y_n}||R)
   <=2V_{A,n}+2A(44A+1)^2/L.                         (18)

The deterministic cap log[1+(A-1)L] may also be applied. If (1) is accepted after independent review, this is an O_A(log n/n) statistical rate with an O_A(1/n) exact-real output-approximation penalty. No finite-precision theorem or architecture change is asserted.

9. Relation to the log-squared proof and prior work

The separate log-squared note correctly shows that a terminal occupation marginal and a self-normalized error marginal alone do not imply log n/n: their adverse correlation can cost a further logarithm. The present argument does not use that invalid inference. It applies the occupation bound under an explicitly altered transition kernel and obtains the joint probability bound (9).

For a general D-state ordinary chain with A=D possible next states, the proof gives O(D^3 log n/n) for fixed D. This is not the optimal D^2 dependence in the stationary minimax theorem of Han, Jana and Wu. Their Remark 1 asks about optimality of usual full-history add-one in the setting of their stated theorem; the present candidate must not be described as settling the complete growing-state question. In the stationary binary first-order case the optimal minimax rate is different, so this candidate also does not assert minimax optimality there.

For fixed A and order two, the candidate upper rate matches the order in n of the stationary minimax lower bound quoted in the existing fixed-alphabet research. Matching the rate in n does not imply optimal alphabet dependence, and no priority assertion is made. Literature status after the cited 2023 paper has not been exhaustively established here.

1. Kernel-uniform target occupation

The fixed-target occupation bound is correct for every D-state homogeneous kernel and every initial distribution. For target s, suffix domination makes the maximum over initial states nonincreasing in time. On a deterministic path only its first w occurrences of s can carry the flag. The charging argument, summed separately over all D starting states, gives Dw/(n+1).

It follows that the probability of E_m={Y_n=s,m<=J_s<2m} is at most 2rm, where r=D/(n+1). The fact that this upper bound may exceed one does not invalidate later uses. Its uniformity in the transition kernel is established, not assumed.

2. Exact row-tilting identity

For fixed s,b and p in (0,1), changing the b-coordinate to q in (0,1) and scaling all other coordinates by (1-q)/(1-p) gives a legal homogeneous second-order source. Existing zero coordinates remain zero, and the two path laws have the same support. Keeping the initial pair distribution unchanged cancels its likelihood factor.

Every observed departure from s contributes exactly one factor. All other rows cancel. Thus on every positive-probability observed path,

P(path)/Q(path)=(p/q)^k[(1-p)/(1-q)]^(j-k).

The terminal state introduces no extra factor: the observed likelihood already contains all n transitions, and the departure from the terminal state is not observed or counted.

The logarithmic identity in (6) has the correct sign. When q>p, the coefficient multiplying -(k-jq) is positive, so k/j>=q makes the correction nonpositive. When q<p, both signs reverse and k/j<=q has the same effect. On the relevant event the likelihood ratio is therefore at most exp[-j d(q||p)]<=exp[-m d(q||p)].

Applying the occupation bound to Q gives (7). The proof deliberately keeps the original initial law even when that law was stationary for P. It does not assume stationarity for Q. Reusing the improved stationary occupation constant at that step would be invalid; the candidate correctly does not do so.

3. Joint deviation bound

For delta=sqrt(2pt/m)+2t/(3m), the binary-KL lower bound in (8) follows from the displayed centered Bernoulli moment bound for either sign. Substitution of lambda=delta/(p+delta/3) is admissible for p>0. Its objective value is delta^2/[2(p+delta/3)]. Expanding delta verifies that this is at least t/m.

On the band J_s<2m,

J_s sqrt(2pt/m)<=sqrt(4J_spt),
J_s(2t/(3m))<=4t/3.

Hence exceeding the candidate's random-count threshold implies exceeding the fixed empirical-frequency threshold delta. The two row-tilted tail bounds sum to 4rm exp(-t).

If a proposed q is outside (0,1), the corresponding strict tail is empty. In particular no absolute-continuity assertion at a zero-probability endpoint is silently required. When p=0 or p=1 the observed row count of symbol b is deterministic given J_s, and the deviation event is empty directly.

Different deterministic thresholds may use different tilted kernels. Tail integration only needs a probability bound at each threshold; it does not require one alternative law to represent all thresholds simultaneously. The concentration result is a joint bound for occupancy and error, not a product of two unrelated marginal estimates.

4. Scalar forward-KL bound and smoothing

The scalar implication (10)-(11) was checked term by term. The mean/count comparison gives z<=2k+10u/3 and the stated squared error. Since x=(j+1)p, the smoothing displacement p-1 is at most one; no u>=A assumption is necessary.

For a=k+1>=u, h(x,a)<=(x-a)^2/a gives 47u. For a<u, x<6u and a>=1 give 6u log(6u)+u, which is bounded by 48u(1+log u). The proof includes x=0 and j=0.

At terminal this is projection of 2y_b-L/2-1 onto [0,L]. Projection cannot increase the distance to Lp_b, so

0<=x_b<=L, |x_b-Lp_b|<=20.                             (12)

The x_b need not sum to L. The subsequent normalization proof deliberately does not assume that they do.

7. Block 5: A narrow-Gaussian logits and ordinary softmax

For each b use a head

Q_b=kappa(2x_b,-1), K_j=(j,j^2), V_j=log j.

Its scores differ from -(j-x_b)^2 by a query-only constant. The head output ell_b is the expectation of log J under these weights on 1,...,L. Block 5's FFN is zero. The final fixed linear readout supplies logits ell_1,...,ell_A to ordinary vocabulary softmax:

G_b=exp(ell_b), S=sum_b G_b, R_b=G_b/S.

For any center 0<=mu<=L, the constant-variance geometric-mean lemma gives

|exp(E log J)-mu|<=24, 1<=exp(E log J)<=L.              (13)

For completeness, a supported integer lies within distance one of mu, so the weight normalizer is at least e^(-1). The unimodal lattice bound sum_{j in Z}e^(-(j-mu)^2/2)<=1+sqrt(2pi), together with |s|e^(-s^2)<=e^(-s^2/2), implies E|J-mu|<=e(1+sqrt(2pi))<10. Jensen gives exp(E log J)<=mu+10. For mu<=2 this and the lower bound 1 suffice. For mu>2, the negative log deficit on mu/2<=J<mu is at most 20/mu. The remainder is at most

e e^(-mu^2/4) sum_{1<=j<mu/2}log(mu/j)
  <= e mu e^(-mu^2/4)<=4/mu.

The sum is bounded by its decreasing-function integral from 0 to mu/2, which is less than mu. Hence E log(J/mu)>=-24/mu, giving exp(E log J)>=mu exp(-24/mu)>=mu-24. This proves (13).

Combining (12)-(13), |G_b-Lp_b|<=44 for every b. Normalization is handled without a large-L condition:

||R-p||_1
  <= ||G/S-G/L||_1 + ||G/L-p||_1
  = |1-S/L| + sum_b |G_b/L-p_b|
  <= 2 sum_b |G_b/L-p_b| <=88A/L.

Thus TV(R,p)<=44A/L. Also G_b>=1 and each other G_a<=L give the exact support floor

R_b>=1/[1+(A-1)L].                                     (14)

For N>=2, N^2e^(-N)<=4e^(-2) and Ne^(-N)<=2e^(-2), so

L Ne^(-N)/A
  = (N^2/A+N)e^(-N)
  <=(4/A+2)e^(-2)<=4/e^2<1.

Together with (11), this proves the pathwise approximation in (2). No complement-coordinate trick specific to binary output is used.

8. Multiclass KL transfer

For distributions T,P,Q with P,Q strictly positive, the entropy variational inequality gives

D(T||Q)<=2D(T||P)+log sum_b P_b^2/Q_b
        <=2D(T||P)+sum_b (P_b-Q_b)^2/Q_b.              (15)

One proof is to use E_T f<=D(T||P)+log E_P exp f with f=log(P/Q), then sum P^2/Q=1+sum(P-Q)^2/Q and log(1+u)<=u. True probabilities T_b may be zero.

Apply this pointwise with P=B,Q=R. Their positivity holds at every history, including zero visits. For a zero-sum vector delta=B-R, its positive and negative parts each sum to TV(B,R), hence sum_b delta_b^2<=2TV(B,R)^2. By (14),

sum_b (B_b-R_b)^2/R_b
  <=AL sum_b (B_b-R_b)^2
  <=2AL TV(B,R)^2
  <=2A(44A+1)^2/L.                                    (16)

Taking expectation in (15) and using (1) proves (2). An additive probability approximation by itself would not justify this step; the explicit floor (14) is essential. No terminal-state independence or conversion of a TV risk through reverse Pinsker is asserted.

E D(theta||Q) <= min{log(4AL), 2V + 8A(46A+2)^2/L}.

s = ceil(log2(AL)),  b = 32s,  H = 2^b,  F = H^2. (P3)

2. Softmax perturbation and gate ports

For any two score vectors u,v on the same finite support,

TV(softmax(u),softmax(v)) <= ||u-v||_infinity.      (P4)

The same statement holds after subtracting arbitrary rowwise constants. To prove it, interpolate u+t(v-u). The derivative of coordinate alpha_j is alpha_j(r_j-E_alpha r), whose total absolute sum is at most 2||r||_infinity. Integrate and divide by two. Therefore the expectations of any value in an interval of length R differ by at most R||u-v||_infinity. This bound is independent of key count; numerical approximation of each unnormalized weight must separately pay that count.

Require exact integer positions and squares, exact full causal supports j<=i, exact token onehots, and exact dyadic ReLU/linear operations. Copy-head outputs must lie in [0,1] coordinatewise and approximate their exact outputs within 1/32 per coordinate. At every valid data lag used for candidate or terminal gates, i>=A+2>=4. The wrong copy mass is less than

2/(exp(4)-1) < 1/16.

The inequality exp(4)>33 follows already from the first five terms of its positive series. Thus every incorrect copy coordinate stays below 1/4 and the correct coordinate stays above 3/4. The exact two-hinge map snaps the needed copies to exact onehots. At invalid candidates, v=0 and the snapped coordinates remain in [0,1], so their g gates are exactly zero. 3. Conditional numerical ports

  1. The block-3 selected output p_tilde is a simplex satisfying

    ||p_tilde-p||_infinity <= 1/(64L^2),
|z_tilde-1/L| <= 1/(64L^3).

  2. Block 4 evaluates its displayed scores from p_tilde,z_tilde, with additional absolute score error at most 1/(64L).   4. Let R_star be exact vocabulary softmax of the numerical block-5 logits ell_tilde. The final evaluated output Q is a simplex with

    |Q_b-R_star_b| <= 1/(8AL)  for every b.

Proof of (P1). Relative to the exact broad-Gaussian head at p,z=1/L, each block-4 score changes by at most

L/(64L^2) + L^2/(64L^3) + 1/(64L) = 3/(64L).

|x_tilde_b-Lp_b| <= 20+1/8 < 21.                 (P5)

1 <= exp(ell_b^0) <= L,
|exp(ell_b^0)-x_tilde_b| <= 24.

(log L)/(64L(1+log L)) + 1/(64L) + 1/(64L)
  <= eta := 3/(64L).

|exp(ell_tilde_b)-exp(ell_b^0)| <= 2L eta < 1.

4. Explicit max-shifted integer exponential

All scores used below are exact H-grid dyadics. For each row, compute its maximum exactly on its actual causal support. A nonnegative shifted gap is d=X/H with integer X>=0. The following deterministic integer routine approximates exp(-d):

  • If X>bH, return 0.
  • Otherwise set Z_0=F-X. For r=0,...,b-1 set Z_{r+1}=floor(Z_r^2/F).
  • Return w_tilde=floor(Z_b/H)/H.

The zero-gap output is exactly one. All outputs lie in [0,1]. Since H>=2b, the retained case has d/H<=1/2. The elementary inequality

-u-u^2 <= log(1-u) <= -u,  0<=u<=1/2,

shows that the difference between exp(-d) and (1-d/H)^H is at most d^2/H<=b^2/H. Each square-and-floor contributes at most 1/F absolute error, and x->x^2 is 2-Lipschitz on [0,1]. After b steps the total such error is less than 2^b/F=1/H. The final conversion adds less than 1/H. In the truncated case, exp(-d)<exp(-b)<2^(-b)=1/H. Hence uniformly

|w_tilde-exp(-d)| <= epsilon_w := (b^2+2)/H.      (P6)

No exponential of a positive O(L^2) or O(L^3) argument is formed. A row always has a retained, exact weight-one maximizer, so its numerical denominator is at least one.

For m supported keys, let the returned weights be Z_j/H, and S=sum_j Z_j. Compute a_j=floor(H Z_j/S), R=H-sum_j a_j, and add one to any deterministically chosen R distinct coordinates, for example the first R supported keys. Here 0<=R<m. Dividing the resulting integers by H produces an exact nonnegative simplex alpha_tilde, each coordinate within 1/H of the exact normalization of the numerical weights. Largest-remainder sorting is unnecessary for this error bound. Normalization of the weight errors costs TV at most m epsilon_w; this follows from the exact weight sum being at least one and the standard two-term normalization identity. Thus

TV(alpha_tilde, softmax(scores))
   <= m epsilon_w + m/(2H)
   <= m epsilon_g,
epsilon_g := (b^2+3)/H.                           (P7)

This explicitly charges m<=i<=L for attention and m=A for the final vocabulary softmax. It does not replace full causal support by a sparse or future-looking set.

5. Constructed log-position routine

3. Primary-source locations and hypotheses

3.1 Model and loss

Published Section V-A, pp. 3941-3942, defines a homogeneous order-m process by a fixed stochastic array with A^m rows and A columns. Its initial law is a distribution on the first m symbols. Stationarity is a condition on this initial law and the fixed transition array.

Published Eq. (103), p. 3942, defines the minimax risk using the forward KL loss at the actual terminal m-symbol context:

R_{A,N,m}
  = inf_{Mhat} sup_M
      E[D_KL(M(. | X_{N-m+1},...,X_N)
               || Mhat(. | X_{N-m+1},...,X_N))].

The observed trajectory has N symbols and is started from a stationary distribution. The estimator is a function of the complete observed trajectory, not merely its final context. Writing it as a collection of estimated rows does not restrict that dependence.

This is the desired terminal expected forward KL. It is neither cumulative prediction loss nor an average over an independently sampled test context. The preprint counterpart is Section 5.1, Eq. (95).

3.2 Higher-order theorem

Published Theorem 22, p. 3942, states: for each m >= 2, there exists C_m > 0 such that, for

2 <= A <= N^(1/(m+1)) / C_m,

A^(m+1)/(C_m N) * log(N/A^(m+1))
  <= R_{A,N,m}
  <= C_m A^(m+1)/N * log(N/A^(m+1)).

The lower bound continues to hold when the sources are required to be reversible. C_m depends only on m, not on A or N. Thus at m=2 the constant C_2 is absolute.

No positive lower bound on transition probabilities, stationary masses, or spectral gap is imposed for this theorem. Its domain is a restriction on the alphabet size relative to sample length. The reversibility here is reversibility of the symbol process; it should not be confused with ordinary reversibility of the overlapping-pair chain.

3.3 Explicit binary theorem

R_{2,N,2} = Theta(log N/N).

M_p(. | 11) = (1-1/N, 1/N),
M_p(. | 21) = (1/N, 1-1/N),
M_p(. | 12) = (1-p, p),
M_p(. | 22) = (p, 1-p),
0 <= p <= 1.

For each p, the stationary pair law is uniform on {11,12,21,22}. The proof places a uniform prior on p and extracts a harmonic-sum lower bound from selected trajectories; its terminal-risk conclusion is Eq. (117), p. 3945. 4. Exact indexing and fixed-A threshold

The candidate observes X_1,...,X_{n+2}, with n observed transitions and terminal pair

Y_n = (X_{n+1},X_{n+2}).

HJW use N for the number of observed symbols. The exact substitution is therefore

N = n+2,  m = 2,  k = A.

Then their final context is the candidate's Y_n, and their next symbol is X_{n+3}. Hence, writing R_A^stat(n) for the candidate-indexed stationary minimax risk,

R_A^stat(n)
  >= A^3/[C_2(n+2)] * log[(n+2)/A^3]

whenever

n+2 >= (C_2 A)^3.

For every fixed A, this gives Omega_A(log n/n) as n tends to infinity. For an explicit sufficient large-n comparison, additionally require n+2 >= A^6. Then

log[(n+2)/A^3] >= (1/2) log(n+2).

The absolute constant C_2 is not numerically specified by the theorem statement. No numerical value or all-n guarantee is invented here. The displayed logarithm and domain must be retained in any finite-sample or growing-alphabet restatement.

5. Why a terminal predictor is covered

An arbitrary prediction rule q(X_1,...,X_N) in the A-symbol probability simplex can be represented as a row-valued estimator: put q in the realized terminal row and assign arbitrary probability vectors to the other rows. HJW's infimum over row-valued estimators therefore covers every history-dependent probability-vector predictor used here.

If a predictor has internal randomness, conditional averaging of its output cannot increase forward KL, by convexity in the predicted distribution. Randomization therefore does not evade the minimax lower bound.

In particular, the lower bound applies to the full-history add-one output, and also to a normalized architecture output if a separately verified upper bound is available for that output.

6. Extension to arbitrary initial-pair distributions

Let R_A^all(n) be the minimax risk when both the kernel and the initial-pair distribution are arbitrary. The stationary parameter set is a subset of this larger parameter set. Thus

R_A^all(n) >= R_A^stat(n).

Let B be the full-history add-one predictor. The candidate upper bound gives

sup_{all initial laws and kernels} E D_KL(p_{Y_n} || B)
  <= U_{A,n},

where U_{A,n} = O_A(log(n+2)/(n+1)). Therefore

R_A^stat(n)
  <= R_A^all(n)
  <= sup_all E D_KL(p_{Y_n} || B)
  <= U_{A,n}.

Furthermore,

R_A^stat(n) <= sup_stationary E D_KL(p_{Y_n} || B).

Together these inequalities prove the claimed fixed-A minimax-rate match for both classes, conditional on the upper-bound theorem. The stationary lower bound is sufficient because a worst case in a smaller class is still a valid worst case in the larger class.

This does not imply a lower bound for every prescribed initial distribution, every deterministic starting pair, or every fixed transition kernel. The supremum is taken separately at each sample length, and the hard source may depend on that length, as the displayed binary construction does.

7. Correct direction of alphabet embedding

A lower bound over a larger alphabet does not imply a lower bound for the binary subclass. The valid monotonicity goes in the other direction.

Embed any stationary binary second-order source in [A] by setting probabilities of symbols 3,...,A to zero and supporting the initial pair on {1,2}^2. Define unused rows arbitrarily. This is allowed in the candidate class, which permits zero probabilities and reducibility.

For any A-symbol prediction q and binary-supported true row p, set

z = q_1+q_2,
q' = (q_1/z,q_2/z)  if z>0.

For z>0,

D_KL(p || q) = D_KL(p || q') - log z
            >= D_KL(p || q').

If z=0, the A-symbol loss is infinite, so the inequality is harmless after choosing any binary q'. This pointwise reduction maps every A-symbol estimator to a binary estimator. Taking the supremum over binary sources and then the infimum over estimators yields

R_{A,N,2} >= R_{2,N,2}.

Thus Theorem 24 alone gives a matching order in N for every fixed A>=2. This alternative argument does not recover the sharp A^3 factor of Theorem 22, but it is sufficient for fixed-A optimality in n.

An unrelated shortcut is also invalid: applying a lower bound for arbitrary ordinary A^2-state chains does not automatically lower-bound the structured pair chains coming from an A-symbol second-order source. HJW's actual higher-order theorems avoid that issue.

Full-history terminal KL: removing the extra log-log factor

Analytical note, 3 November 2026. This is a separate refinement of the fixed-alphabet terminal KL proof. It does not modify the previously frozen proof or its deliverable. No numerical experiment, simulation, training, external upload, or public repository mutation was performed. Independent review is separate from this derivation.

Result and scope

Let A>=2 and n>=1. Observe a homogeneous second-order source X_1,...,X_{n+2} on [A], with arbitrary transition probabilities and arbitrary initial pair distribution. Deterministic rows, zero probabilities, reducibility and periodicity are allowed. Put D=A^2 and Y_i=(X_{i+1},X_{i+2}), 0<=i<=n. For every context s define

J_s=sum_{i=0}^{n-1}1{Y_i=s},
C_{s,b}=sum_{i=0}^{n-1}1{Y_i=s,X_{i+3}=b},
B_s(b)=(C_{s,b}+1)/(J_s+A),
J=J_{Y_n}.

Write p_s(b) for the true row probabilities. The loss is the forward KL divergence D_KL(p_{Y_n}||B_{Y_n}) at the actual terminal context, with natural logarithms. No independent query or suffix-averaged substitute is used.

Define

t=max{1, log[2 A^3 n(n+1)^2]},
c=A^4/(n+1) for arbitrary initial pairs,
c=A^2/(n+1) for stationary initial pairs,
U_new=c{(24At+log 2)[1+log(n+1)]
             + A(16t+1)log(32t)}
             + log(n+A)/(n+1)^2,
V_new=min{log(n+A), U_new}.

Then

E D_KL(p_{Y_n}||B_{Y_n}) <= V_new.                    (1)

In particular, for each fixed A the risk is O_A(log^2(n+2)/(n+1)), uniformly over all sources and initial pair distributions stated above. This removes the extra log-log factor from the previously reviewed O_A(log^2 n loglog n/n) bound. It does not establish O_A(log n/n), optimal alphabet dependence, or a matching lower bound for this estimator. At n=0 the elementary bound log A applies.

The same argument applies to a finite-state homogeneous chain with D contexts and A possible emitted outcomes per context, provided the outcome and next-state mechanism admits the standard independent row-stack representation. For an ordinary D-state chain, take A=D. The present second-order source has D=A^2.

1. Fixed-target terminal occupation

Fix a target state s and integer w>=1. For a chain started at z let

b_m^s(z)=P_z(Y_m=s, sum_{i=0}^{m-1}1{Y_i=s}<w),
B_m^s=max_z b_m^s(z).

For m>=ell, having fewer than w full-history copies of s implies having fewer than w copies in the last ell departures. Conditioning on Y_{m-ell} and using homogeneity yields B_m^s<=B_ell^s. On a deterministic path only the first w occurrences of s can satisfy this flag, so sum_{m=0}^n b_m^s(z)<=w for every z. Consequently

(n+1)B_n^s <= sum_{m=0}^n B_m^s
            <= sum_z sum_{m=0}^n b_m^s(z) <= Dw.

Taking any mixture over the initial state proves

P(Y_n=s,J_s<w) <= r w,
r=D/(n+1).                                           (2)

Under stationary initialization one instead has r=1/(n+1). Indeed, among r'=n,...,2n at most w times can satisfy Y_{r'}=s and have fewer than w copies of s in their preceding n positions: if w+1 were marked, the last marked time has all w earlier marks inside that window. Stationarity makes each marked event have the probability on the left of (2). This proves the improved bound.

In both cases c=Dr. Summing (2) over target states gives P(J<w)<=cw. Summation by parts then gives

E[1/(J+1)]
  =1/(n+1)+sum_{k=0}^{n-1}P(J<=k)/[(k+1)(k+2)]
  <=1/(n+1)+c sum_{k=0}^{n-1}1/(k+2)
  <=c[1+log(n+1)].                                    (3)

The per-target form (2), rather than only its sum, is essential below because the true row probabilities vary with the terminal state.

2. One simultaneous deterministic-prefix event

Generate n independent categorical entries for each of the A^2 rows, independently across rows and independently of the initial pair. At a departure from s consume its next unused entry. The resulting source has exactly the required transition law. Let K_{s,b,j} count symbol b in the first j entries of row s. Then C_{s,b}=K_{s,b,J_s} pathwise.

Bernstein's inequality and a union bound over A^2 rows, A symbols and n deterministic prefix lengths give an event G with

|K_{s,b,j}-jp_s(b)| <= sqrt(2jp_s(b)t)+2t/3             (4)

for all those prefixes, and

P(G^c)<=2A^3 n exp(-t)<=(n+1)^(-2).                    (5)

At j=0 inequality (4) is deterministic. No binomial or independence assertion is made after conditioning on J_s, on Y_n, or on the observed path. Evaluation at the selected row and selected prefix uses only simultaneous pathwise validity.

3. A scale choice that removes smoothing dependence from t

Fix any row and deterministic prefix, and abbreviate j>=0, p_b=p_s(b), k_b=K_{s,b,j}, q_b=(k_b+1)/(j+A). Set

h(x,a)=x log(x/a)-x+a,
x_b=(j+1)p_b, a_b=k_b+1, z_b=jp_b.

Use 0 log 0=0. Since sum_b x_b=j+1 and sum_b a_b=j+A,

sum_b h(x_b,a_b)
   =(j+1)D_KL(p||q)+(A-1)
                -(j+1)log[1+(A-1)/(j+1)]
   >=(j+1)D_KL(p||q),                                 (6)

by log(1+u)<=u. Thus this rescaling, unlike x_b=(j+A)p_b, gives only the displacement p_b-1, whose absolute value is at most one. It needs t>=1, not t>=A.

4. Split by the true coordinate mean

For one coordinate suppress b. Under (4), write delta=k-z. Then

delta^2<=4zt+8t^2/9,
(x-a)^2<=2delta^2+2<=8zt+(34/9)t^2.                  (7)

Here x=z+p, a=k+1, and t>=1. The elementary inequality h(x,a)<=(x-a)^2/a holds for x>=0,a>0, including x=0 by continuity.

If z>=16t, then

a>=z-sqrt(2zt)-2t/3 >=z/2,

because sqrt(2t/z)+2t/(3z)<=1/sqrt(8)+1/24<1/2. Hence

h(x,a)<=2[8+34/144]t<17t.                             (8)

If z<16t, then

a<=16t+sqrt(32)t+2t/3+1<=24t.

(z+p)log_+(z+p)<=z log_+(2z)+p log_+(2p),             (9)

with zero terms interpreted as zero. For z+p>1 this follows from (z+p)log(z+p)<=z log(2z)+p log(2p), and replacing negative logarithms by zero only increases the right side. For z+p<=1 it is immediate. Thus

h(x,a)<=24t+z log_+(2z)+p log_+(2p).                  (10)

Combine (8) and (10), sum the coordinates, and use

sum_b p_b log_+(2p_b)<=log 2.

Equations (6)-(10) yield the pointwise inequality

D_KL(p||q)
  <=(24At+log 2)/(j+1)
    +sum_b p_b log_+(2jp_b)1{jp_b<16t}.               (11)

The factor jp_b/(j+1) from (6) was upper bounded by p_b. The argument also covers j=0, p_b=0 and p_b=1.

Unlike a uniform bound of order t log t/(j+A), (11) charges the log t factor only where that particular true coordinate mean is small.

5. Integrate the small-mean term before summing the rows

Fix s,b and put p=p_s(b), T=16t. If p=0 the corresponding term is zero. If p>0, the log is bounded above by log(2T) on {J_s p<T}. With w=ceil(T/p), this event implies J_s<w, including the case T/p is an integer. Equation (2) therefore gives

E[1{Y_n=s}p log_+(2J_s p)1{J_s p<T}]
  <=p log(2T) P(Y_n=s,J_s<T/p)
  <=p log(2T) r ceil(T/p)
  <=r(T+p)log(2T)
  <=r(T+1)log(2T).                                   (12)

The use of (2) remains valid when the ceiling exceeds n, because its upper bound need not be below one. Summing (12) over the D rows and A symbols gives at most

c A(16t+1)log(32t).                                  (13)

This sum does not extract a random terminal-row probability from an expectation. Each p in (12) is fixed first; only then are rows summed.

On G, apply (11) to the selected terminal row and count. Use (3), (12)-(13) without conditioning on G. On every path, q_b>=1/(j+A), so D_KL(p||q)<=log(j+A)<=log(n+A). The complement G^c therefore costs at most log(n+A)/(n+1)^2. This proves (1).

6. Transfer to the reviewed five-block output

The five-block exact-real output construction in the prior fixed-alphabet note is unchanged. Its proved pathwise bounds, with L=n+2+A, are

TV(R,B)<=min{1,(44A+1)/L},
R_b>=1/[1+(A-1)L]>=1/(AL).

Its entropy-variational KL transfer consequently gives

E D_KL(p_{Y_n}||R)
   <=2V_new+2A(44A+1)^2/L.                            (14)

The deterministic cap log[1+(A-1)L] may also be taken. Thus the same output has O_A(log^2 n/n) statistical risk and an O_A(1/n) implementation penalty. No additional architecture, training, finite-precision, or computational claim is made here.

7. What this does not establish

The remaining harmonic occupation factor and the logarithmic confidence level give log^2 n/n. Merely replacing the confidence event by an exponential-tail self-normalized bound and separately using the occupation envelope does not justify log n/n. A simple abstract counterexample to that inference is J uniform on {1,...,n} and W=log(n/J):

P(J<w)<=w/n,
P(W>u)<=exp(-u), u>=0,
E[W/J]=(1/n)sum_{j=1}^n log(n/j)/j
       =(log n)^2/(2n)+O(log n/n).

This is a barrier for arguments using only those two marginal bounds. It is not a Markov-source lower bound and does not show the present log^2 rate is optimal. Achieving O_A(log n/n) would require additional control of how the row-estimation error and terminal occupation depend on each other, or a different argument.

Han, Jana and Wu, "Optimal prediction of Markov chains with and without spectral gap," IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 69(6), 2023, 3920-3959, Remark 1, explicitly distinguish suffix-averaged add-one from full-history add-one and leave the latter's optimality open in their stated setting. The present O_A(log^2 n/n) result does not resolve that question, and does not transfer their stationary minimax theorem to arbitrary initialization. It also does not claim that their 2023 question remains unresolved in all later literature. 1. Main finding

2. Target-specific occupation and reciprocal moment

For a fixed target s, the suffix argument proves that the maximum over initial states B_m^s is nonincreasing in m. It does not require monotonicity for each individual initial state. The flag Y_m=s with fewer than w earlier copies of s can occur at most w times on a deterministic trajectory. Summing separately over the D starting states and then using max<=sum gives

P(Y_n=s,J_s<w) <= Dw/(n+1)

under arbitrary initialization. The stationary n-window flag argument gives w/(n+1). Both proofs were checked, including the integer endpoints of the preceding n-position window.

Thus r=D/(n+1) or 1/(n+1), while c=Dr. Summing over the target states recovers the corresponding actual-terminal occupation bound. The summation-by-parts identity for 1/(J+1) includes J=0 and gives exactly c[1+log(n+1)]. No minimum stationary mass, mixing assumption, or positive transition lower bound is introduced.

3. Confidence event and terminal selection

There are A^2 categorical row stacks, A symbol indicators per stack, and n positive deterministic prefix lengths. The two-sided Bernstein union bound is therefore 2A^3 n exp(-t). The displayed t makes this at most (n+1)^-2. The j=0 inequality is deterministic.

Each row stack is unconditionally iid at each deterministic prefix. The original homogeneous source is obtained by consuming the next unused entry at each departure. On the simultaneous event, every row/prefix estimate can be evaluated pathwise at the actual terminal row and its count. Neither count conditioning nor terminal-selection independence is needed. 4. Rescaling and scalar constants

With x_b=(j+1)p_b and a_b=k_b+1, direct expansion gives

On the Bernstein event, delta^2<=4zt+8t^2/9 and |p-1|<=1 give

For z>=16t the lower bound a>=z/2 holds. The resulting coefficient is 16+17/36, strictly smaller than 17. The large-mean branch therefore has the claimed 17t upper bound.

For z<16t, the count bound gives a<=24t, including the +1 pseudocount because t>=1. The inequality

(z+p)log_+(z+p)<=z log_+(2z)+p log_+(2p)

is valid. It also follows from convexity of f(u)=u log_+u and f(z+p)<=one-half f(2z)+one-half f(2p), with f(0)=0. Since a>=1, dropping the nonpositive terms in h proves the stated small-mean bound.

5. Low-mean expectation and row dependence

For each fixed row s and symbol b, p=p_s(b) is a fixed parameter. If p=0 the term is zero. Otherwise the strict event J_s p<T is exactly represented by J_s<ceil(T/p), including when T/p is an integer. The target-specific occupation bound therefore gives

E[1{Y_n=s} p log_+(2J_s p) 1{J_s p<T}]
   <=r(T+p)log(2T)
   <=r(T+1)log(2T).

The ceiling need not be at most n: the occupation upper bound may exceed one and still be valid. There is no division by zero, and arbitrarily small positive p causes no failure.

Only after obtaining this bound for a fixed p does the proof sum over D rows and A symbols. This gives DAr(T+1)log(2T)=cA(T+1)log(2T), with the exact displayed T=16t. In particular it does not pull the probability of the randomly selected terminal row outside an expectation.

All terms used to dominate the good-event loss are nonnegative, so dropping the good-event indicator before applying the unconditional occupation bounds is legitimate. On its complement the deterministic log(n+A) loss cap gives the stated failure contribution. This proves the entire U_new expression and its cap V_new.

For fixed A, t=O_A(log(n+2)). The rare term t log(32t) is no larger than a constant times t^2 for t>=1, while the main term is t times a harmonic logarithm. Thus the claimed O_A(log^2 n/n) order follows. The explicit bound, rather than this fixed-A asymptotic notation, must be used when A grows.
