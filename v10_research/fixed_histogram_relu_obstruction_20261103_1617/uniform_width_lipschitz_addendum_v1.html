Uniform finite-width bound for a fixed histogram interface

This is an immutable addendum to the fixed-readout note, SHA-256 b4e518e2d69970ea2ca00509f894edff7d191d9684a0f8ae39ce1b97e092f4c8. It strengthens that note only for the same normalized-bank interface and scalar clipped ReLU output. It is not a general causal Transformer lower bound or a novelty claim. No random or numerical experiment was used.

1. Explicit source rectangle and model

Use the stationary order-two family in the original note, with u=π₀₀, v=uθ₀₀ and w=(1−u)/3. Draw (u,v) uniformly from the rectangle

I=[1/8,1/6],    J=[1/20,1/18].

This rectangle lies strictly in the original parameter wedge u/4<v<3u/4. Every source has π_min≥1/8 and reverse mixing sum ≤16. Its actual 00-target is f_v(u)=v/u, while the population input to the normalized eight-coordinate histogram bank is affine in (u,v).

The readout on the bank and query 00 is a scalar ReLU network with at most d hidden layers, each of width at most W, followed by an optional scalar clip to [0,1]. All its inputs are the normalized bank, fixed 00 query encoding, and optionally a BOS coordinate converging to 0. No separate unbounded N or position signal, extra attention, division, input reread, or precomputed nonlinear ratio is allowed. Let K be its ℓ1-Lipschitz constant on the bounded bank domain. The model may have arbitrary real weights and residual affine connections within this finite architecture.

2. A uniform-in-weights population gap

For any fixed v, the bank is affine as a function of u. Restricting a width-W, depth-d ReLU network to that line gives at most (W+1)^d affine segments: on each existing segment, the next W preactivations contribute at most W cuts. A final clip can split each segment into at most three. Put R=3(W+1)^d; this also safely covers fewer layers/units.

On I, f_v″(u)=2v/u³≥m=108/5. If g is any affine function on an interval of length h, the centered second difference with offset h/4 is at least m h²/16 on the middle half. Integrating it and applying the triangle inequality to its three shifted terms yields

∫interval |f_v(u)−g(u)| du ≥ m h³/128.

If a piecewise-affine function has at most R pieces of lengths h_j on I, summing and using Σh_j³≥|I|³/R² gives

∫_I |f_v(u)−F(bank(u,v),00)| du ≥ m |I|³/(128R²).

Under the uniform prior on I×J and the stationary actual-terminal event P(S_N=00)=u≥1/8, the resulting limiting prior-averaged TV risk for every fixed readout of this architecture is at least

(1/8) · m |I|²/(128R²) = 3/(81920 R²) > 0.

The lower bound is uniform over the readout weights, but is a bound on each fixed readout’s limiting prior risk. The original terminal-conditioning argument applies: the summary converges while the terminal indicator need not, and stationarity plus boundedness supplies the factor u.

3. Length-dependent readouts under a uniform stability restriction

The preceding result alone does not exchange a limit with an infimum over readouts whose weights can change with M. However, the explicit finite-M bank estimate in the original note gives, for M≥1,

g(M)=[8√(129M/4)+12]/(M+1),
prior-averaged risk ≥ 3/(81920 R²) − K g(M).

Consequently, if a sequence of possibly M-dependent readouts has the same width/depth bounds and a uniform bank-domain ℓ1-Lipschitz bound K≤K₀, its prior-averaged risk remains bounded away from zero for sufficiently large M. This additional case follows directly from the finite-M inequality, not from an unjustified interchange of limits. Without a uniform Lipschitz or other compactness control, no conclusion is claimed here for length-dependent weights. A final binary softmax after ReLU is covered by the original note for each fixed readout, but its uniform-width quantitative constant is not claimed in this addendum.

4. Limits

This is a restriction on one normalized context-count bank followed by a bounded-complexity clipped ReLU readout. A continuous reciprocal, extra attention/softmax stage, unbounded positional or length signals, an explicit division/rounding oracle, or another input-dependent representation changes the model. The statistical target remains the actual terminal pair and the prior is only a proof device; it is not assumed to be known or used by the predictor. This result does not establish impossibility for standard Transformers in general.
