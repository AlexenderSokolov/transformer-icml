# Independent review: supervised fixed-half conditioning calibration

3 November 2026. Scope: design, sealed protocol, source, tests, all retained checkpoints and outputs, plus an independent rerun. This is a review of one fixed-M supervised calibration, not an assessment of a Transformer or a robustness theorem. No author data was changed or published.

## Verdict

The observed conditioning contrast is reproducible and is implemented as described. With the explicit Q teacher, four collision words weighted as half the objective, three matched initializations and the fixed 2,000-step Adam schedule, the count-scaled half readout passed the declared finite-fit screen in all three fits; the normalized half readout did not pass any. Because the representations and initial functions match at M=64, this is evidence that this particular scaling materially changes the optimization trajectory and finite-budget outcome. It does not establish that normalized features cannot fit, that scaling is the unique causal mechanism, or that the original next-token/edit objective would learn Q or be robust.

## Checks and numerical evidence

- Independently reconstructed transitions, full triple histograms, J, C, D, Q and selected features from each saved word without calling the author's statistic, feature or decoder functions. All 4,104 stored targets agree exactly. Collision words have J=(1,1,1,1), C=(0,0,1,1), D=(1,0,1,0), and Q=(1/8,7/8,7/8,1/8). The uncomplemented histogram is (0,1,0,2,1,0,2,58); each early/late pair has the same full histogram and endpoint pair. The boundary words reproduce those four feature classes; they are indexing checks, not held-out feature generalization.
- Independently regenerated both PCG64 prior-word arrays from the declared separate seeds, including draw order, and regenerated all minibatch indices. Exact agreement. Each step draws 64 indices from the fixed empirical 2,048-word training set and 64 from the fixed four-word collision stratum, with replacement. Thus gradients are unbiased for the stated half-and-half empirical objective, conditional on the frozen sets. The collision words are reused in evaluation; their scores are not independent out-of-sample collision scores.
- Training and validation are independent draws, not guaranteed disjoint strings: the retained sets contain 2,022 and 2,029 unique words respectively, with 9 distinct words in both. Such coincident words do not make independently sampled trajectories dependent, but “fresh validation” should not be described as word-disjoint. None of the four prescribed collision words appears in either prior set.
- The count-only features agree within each early/late pair while the two targets are 1/8 and 7/8. For each pair, average CE is minimized at q=1/2, giving collision mean KL at least ln(2)−H(1/8)=0.3163770193035 nats; its contribution to the declared balanced objective is at least half that. The measured control collision KL ranges from 0.3173840 to 0.3176302. This is a four-point information floor, not a bound on all words or source risks.
- With S=diag(64,64,32,32,32,32,1,1,1), the scaled model uses xS and initializes S⁻¹W, so xS(S⁻¹W)=xW for every x. At this fixed horizon S is invertible, giving the same readout function class. All three saved initial effective first-layer matrices match exactly; biases and subsequent weights match by construction. Adam acts on raw parameters and is not invariant to this transformation. The retained gradient tests check every one of 353 readout coordinates in all three conditions; their maximum central-difference error is 4.72e-9. These are numerical checks, not a global proof.
- Independently reconstructed inputs and forward predictions from the stored weights for all 36 checkpoints and all four datasets. Maximum discrepancy across reproduced reported prior mean/SE, collision mean/max error, boundary max error and saved final predictions was 1.67e-16. All 53 output hashes match. Independent replay reproduced frozen data and final prediction arrays exactly; it reused the same implementation and environment, so it is reproducibility evidence, not a fully independent training implementation.
- Final count-scaled validation mean prior KL was 0.001352–0.001640 and its maximum error on the four **trained collision words** was 0.000502–0.000930. Normalized validation mean prior KL was 0.023812–0.026374 and collision maximum error 0.08904–0.13878. Thus the positive screen (mean KL ≤0.01 and collision max error ≤0.05 in each of three fits) passes only for the scaled condition.

## Interpretation and reporting cautions

1. **Finite screen, not uniform approximation.** Count-scaled validation predictions still have maximum individual absolute error 0.401–0.434, with 13–14 of 2,048 prior-validation trajectories exceeding 0.1 absolute error per fit. Mean KL passing and fitting four selected collision words do not imply an all-word gate approximation. Prior hidden-unit saturation |tanh|>0.99 is 69.8%–75.8% for the scaled fits versus 9.5%–12.3% for normalized fits; this is a diagnostic, not by itself a failure or proof of a mechanism.
2. **Explicit target, changed objective.** The teacher is the deterministic, fully supervised smoothed gate Q. It is not the clean posterior B, an observed next-token label, or the original edit-training objective. The collision stratum deliberately gives rare gate decisions half the objective weight, vastly different from natural-prior sampling. Consequently this experiment does not show that natural next-token training discovers the gate, and it evaluates no adversarial attacks or actual-source risk.
3. **Shared data and dependence.** The three initializations share a single frozen prior training sample, a single validation sample and exactly the same minibatch stream. They are three initializations conditional on those samples, not three independent data or experiment replications. A pointwise validation SE does not assess initialization/data variation or inference after selecting conditions.
4. **Paired finite comparison only.** The count-scaled and normalized representations and initial functions coincide, but their Adam parameterizations change the effective update sizes, and the resulting intermediate trajectories and saturation differ. This supports a parameterization-sensitive optimization explanation for this exact setup; it does not isolate feature scale from all other optimizer dynamics or establish what would happen with another optimizer, tuning, budget or horizon.
5. **No feature discovery or upstream attribution.** Half/suffix pools and the Q teacher are supplied; the exact decoder is a nontrainable feature/teacher consistency check, not a learned model. The original pilot's failure under another loss does not establish that conditioning was its sole cause. No minimax, out-of-distribution, adversarial, or length-generalization claim follows. Local pre-run hashes support auditability but are not an externally trusted timestamp or preregistration.

## Code trace

- `calibration.py:11-30` constructs literal collision words and independent word-based Q; `calibration.py:32-46` builds fixed features and exact decoder. `calibration.py:50-69` applies the invertible scaling, matched initialization, forward pass and analytical readout gradients.
- `run.py:38-43` draws separate prior sets and shared minibatch indices; `run.py:46-55` validates the decoder and initialization; `run.py:62-79` trains and scores each fixed readout. The same collision words appear in training and reported collision validation.
- `test_calibration.py` contains all-parameter finite-difference, collision/floor, matched initialization and fixed-pooling tests. Independent calculations and exact numerical logs are in this review directory.

Accept as a reproducible, narrowly scoped positive conditioning calibration, while preserving the limits above and reporting the individual validation failures alongside mean loss.
