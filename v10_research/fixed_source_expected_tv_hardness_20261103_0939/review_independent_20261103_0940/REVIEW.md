## Independent proof review

1. The prior 3SAT construction sets M to the least power of two ≥max(n²,m²,4), k=n≤sqrt M, P the least power of two ≥m, Q=3/(2M) and Δ=1/(2MP). Its model maps every binary word to q=Q−V/(2MP)∈[1/M,Q], with V the number of violated clauses determined solely by the first n editable bits. Positions, masks, width, dyadic and bit encodings are explicit and polynomial in the formula size. If phi is satisfiable, choose a satisfying assignment x* existentially and define A(h) to replace *only* h[0:n] by x*, leaving all other bits unchanged. This one deterministic map is executable in polynomial time given x*, changes at most n=k bits on every history, protects the last pair, and uses no theta, p or future label. It does not give the reduction an algorithm to find x*. Every output has V=0, q=Q.
2. For p=1/M² in every transition row, the actual next-bit probability is p regardless of H and of the arbitrary initial-pair law mu; P²(u,v)=Pi(v) for all four u,v and Pi=(1−p,p) tensor itself, yielding the all-state beta=1 certificate. q≥1/M>p, so true TV=q−p on every possible word, not the earlier B(H)-target surrogate. All legal actions have TV≤Q−p. If satisfiable, the single policy above attains Q−p everywhere, hence R*=Q−p for any mu. If unsatisfiable, every possible output has V≥1 and TV≤Q−p−Δ regardless of h, kernel randomization, edited terminal or dummy bits; hence R*≤Q−p−Δ. This proves the expected gap directly, without needing any positive-probability special history or commuting an unjustified hidden-source maximum.
3. The public midpoint τ=Q−p−Δ/2 is a polynomial-bit rational, and the above cases lie on opposite sides. An exact expected-value algorithm or a rational output v with |v−R*|<Δ/2 would distinguish 3SAT instances, so this is NP-hard and inverse-polynomial additive-value hardness under P≠NP. The supremum includes all admissible history-only kernels, even ones not efficiently searchable, and a value oracle is not the same thing as a returned unevaluated policy. No upper bound or NP membership of the general policy decision is established.
4. Under the *stationary* start, h0=0^(M+2) has probability π0=(1−1/M²)^(M+2)≥1−(M+2)/M²≥5/8 for M≥4. In a yes-instance R*=Q−p. For a deterministic globally legal policy, if its output at h0 violates a clause, the pointwise deficit there is at least Δ; deficits elsewhere are nonnegative, hence expected regret ≥π0Δ≥5Δ/8. An algorithm returning a polynomial-description, polynomial-evaluable deterministic legal map with expected regret strictly <Δ/2 must therefore output a satisfying assignment at h0; evaluating it and checking that assignment decides 3SAT. The proof does not say that an arbitrary randomized policy can be decoded in one evaluation, nor that a hardcoded assignment is efficiently obtainable. Under arbitrary mu the probability of h0 can be zero, so this extraction argument cannot be generalized without additional mass assumptions.
5. Every TV lies in [1/M−p,Q−p], so absolute TV is O(1/M) but TV/p≥M−1. Identity is legal and achieves ≥(1/M−p)/(Q−p)=(2M−2)/(3M−2)≥3/5 of even R* for all initial laws. No constant-factor inapproximability follows. The source-specific classical predictor that is told the same public p and always outputs Bernoulli(p) has exactly zero TV, needs O(log M) bits for this dyadic p, and is unaffected by edits or initial law. This baseline is known-source and does not solve the general unknown-source problem or show a Transformer advantage. The separate classical addendum is correct.
