A fixed-source risk obstruction for a specified pre-LN model

3 November 2026. This is an analytic candidate, separate from the single-copy-path comparison; independent review is required. It concerns one explicitly restricted standard causal architecture, not all models using normalization. No tests, enumeration, training claims, or old-file edits.

1. Model and quantifiers

Take any fixed finite depth, width and head count. Its fixed-parameter pre-LN blocks have (i) causal self-attention whose Q, K, V at each position are fixed affine maps only of that position's LayerNorm output, followed by a fixed affine output projection and residual addition, and (ii) a positionwise fixed finite Lipschitz FFN fed only by LayerNorm, followed by residual addition. LayerNorm uses fixed learned affine parameters and fixed ε>0. Attention at position i sees all i positions 1,…,i, with no other masking, unbounded score bias, length-dependent temperature, attention weights, or parameters. The sequence input is a token embedding plus any token-independent position vector, which may be unbounded and may depend on sequence length. Earlier inputs do not encode other tokens. Fixed token embeddings are finite. Special tokens may be prepended in a fixed number, with total visible prefix length linear in the N data tokens.

The final output probability is produced by a fixed affine vocabulary-logit map and softmax, or by another fixed globally Lipschitz probability readout. Any final LayerNorm uses fixed ε>0. No direct bypass reads previous tokens other than through the specified causal blocks. The statement covers arbitrary fixed weights and a fixed source; constants may depend on the network but not N or the word. It does not cover unbounded relative-position biases, fixed-width local attention masks, ε=0, token-dependent preprocessed side channels, weights/depth varying with N, or the first attention of post-LN fed unnormalized position features.

2. Last-two-position sensitivity

Consider two length-N data words agreeing except at position N−1. Their last token and all position inputs agree. By causality, at each layer all representations before N−1 agree. Write δℓ for their last-position representation difference in Euclidean norm after ℓ complete blocks, with δ₀=0. We claim that fixed finite constants aℓ,bℓ exist such that

δℓ+1 ≤ aℓ δℓ + bℓ/N,     hence δL ≤ C/N.

For any input x, the norm of LayerNorm is bounded by √d||γ||∞+||b||₂, irrespective of the magnitude of its residual or position coordinates. Hence, for fixed affine maps, all per-head Q,K,V norms and all unmasked logits lie in fixed bounded sets, uniformly in N and in both words. In particular every one of the N+O(1) causal-key weights is at most C₀/N. Also, positive ε gives a global LayerNorm Lipschitz constant ||γ||∞/√ε.

For one head at the final query, compare in two steps. First change the last-position query, its own key and its own value, leaving the N−1 key/value unchanged. Their changes are O(δℓ), as are the score changes uniformly across keys; softmax changes in TV by at most a constant times the maximum score perturbation, and all values are bounded. Second change the N−1 key and value. All other keys and values remain identical; the changed key receives O(1/N) mass both before and after this change, so the output changes by O(1/N). Earlier causal positions remain exactly identical. Combining the finitely many heads and fixed output projection gives an attention residual difference at most cδℓ+c′/N. The normalized FFN plus residual multiplies this bound by another fixed constant. This proves the recurrence. It does not require the residual at N−1 itself to be bounded: the normalized key and value are bounded. The terminal residual position can be unbounded but cancels between the two inputs, and all uses of it in attention or the FFN are normalized.

Any final fixed affine vocabulary logits followed by softmax have globally bounded output sensitivity to their input difference, even when the common raw logits are unbounded. Therefore the two next-token probabilities assigned to symbol 1 differ by at most C′/N for a fixed network-dependent C′. The same conclusion holds for the other specified fixed Lipschitz readouts.

3. One fixed fast-mixing second-order source

Let binary θab denote the next-symbol-one probability and set

θ₀₀=3/4,   θ₁₀=1/4,   θ₀₁=1/2,   θ₁₁=1/2.

All single-symbol transition probabilities lie in [1/4,3/4]. The pair chain is irreducible and aperiodic; in fact every two-step pair probability is ≥1/16, so P² dominates one quarter of the uniform measure on four pair states. Start from its stationary law, or indeed from any initial pair law. This is a genuinely order-two source: the next law at current symbol 0 depends on its predecessor.

Fix any observed prefix X₁:₍N₋₂₎, N≥4. The conditional probabilities of the last-two-symbol continuations 00 and 10 are each at least (1/4)²=1/16. Call the network's symbol-one predictions on the two words q₀,q₁. Their respective actual terminal target probabilities are 3/4 and 1/4, and §2 gives |q₀−q₁|≤C′/N. The conditional expectation of TV loss over all continuations is at least

(1/16)(|q₀−3/4|+|q₁−1/4|) ≥ (1/16)(1/2−C′/N).

The inequality is the triangle inequality; the remaining two continuations only add nonnegative loss. Averaging prefixes yields liminfN→∞ E TV(QN,θS_N) ≥ 1/32 for every fixed network in this class, even on this one stationary, fast-mixing source. The output is queried at the true terminal pair in the same trajectory; no independent query or independence of visits is assumed. The fixed model weights cannot depend on N. This is not a minimax lower over arbitrary model families with N-dependent weights, nor a result about training.

4. Source overlap and limitations

Hahn (2020), Lemma 5 studies the O(1/N) influence mechanism for bounded-input fixed soft-attention networks. The argument above spells out a causal last-two-position specialization and accommodates unbounded token-independent residual positions because every nonlinear branch first sees positive-ε LayerNorm; it should not be called novel without a broader source audit. Chiang and Cholak (2022), Propositions 2–3 illustrate why zero-ε normalization or length-dependent attention scores require a different analysis. The no-LN fixed-parameter constructive candidate, finite-window architectures, score-bias approaches, different value/input channels, post-LN first attention and N-dependent networks lie outside the theorem.
