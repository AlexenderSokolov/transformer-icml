LayerNorm at the copy interface: exact scope

3 November 2026. This note analyzes modifications of the three-block, fixed-parameter candidate, which explicitly has no LayerNorm. Its four-block vocabulary-softmax extension uses the same initial copy mechanism and adds paid log-position inputs. The arguments below are pathwise and source-independent; none is a general impossibility result for all normalized Transformers, a training result, or a floating-point analysis. No experiment, enumeration, or old-file edit was performed.

1. Normalization convention and its basic bounds

For a d-vector x, let P=I−11ᵀ/d and Nε(x)=γ⊙[Px/√(||Px||²/d+ε)]+b, with fixed learned γ,b and ε≥0. If ε=0, N is defined only at nonzero variance. Its normalized part has Euclidean norm at most √d, hence ||Nε(x)||≤B:=√d||γ||∞+||b||₂ independently of ||x||. Fixed Q,K affine maps of N have norms at most Q̄=||WQ||B+||bQ|| and K̄=||WK||B+||bK||, so every unmasked scaled dot-product score has |score|≤C=Q̄K̄/√dh. This assumes no extra unbounded relative-position score bias, temperature depending on N, or access to an unnormalized score bypass. The fixed affine LayerNorm bias b is unrelated to an attention inverse temperature.

For ε>0, the Jacobian bound ||DNε||≤||γ||∞/√ε gives the global perturbation bound ||Nε(x+e)−Nε(x)||≤||γ||∞||e||/√ε. This is only the local module's input-output sensitivity; it does not automatically pay perturbations in Q/K/V matrices, residual paths, masks, precision, or an entire network. If ε=0, there is no global Lipschitz bound near zero variance.

2. No normalization, pre-LN, and normalized copy-only

No normalization. The candidate supplies (1,i,i²), and its first head score is i(i−h)²−i(j−i+h)². Thus its target lag h has a growing gap ≥i against every non-target, yielding exponentially small copy leakage. Its matching head later has gap N. Neither follows when the score inputs are normalized.

Pre-LN, or copy-path-only LayerNorm before Q/K. Consider the candidate's first-layer full-causal h-lag head at row i>h. Apply LayerNorm to each local residual before the fixed affine Q and K projections; allow the V projection either to remain the original scalar token value in [0,1], or to be any fixed affine projection of LayerNorm with bounded norm V̄. The unmasked set contains all i prefix keys. As each score lies in [−C,C], the weight of every individual key is at most e2C/i, regardless of i or its token value. In particular the original −i(j−i+h)² gaps cannot be produced from these normalized Q/K channels with fixed parameters.

Take two histories that differ only at the desired lag position j=i−h, with terminal token fixed. At this first layer the query and all other keys and values are identical. Their other-key conditional softmax average μ is therefore the same, while the changed-key weights are p,p′≤e2C/i. If o=(1−p)μ+pv and o′=(1−p′)μ+p′v′ with ||μ||,||v||,||v′||≤V̄, then ||o−o′||≤2V̄(p+p′)≤4V̄e2C/i. For the original scalar 0/1 values, |o−o′|≤p+p′≤2e2C/i. Thus for all sufficiently large i the same copy head cannot place both outputs within 1/4 of their respective target bits, and the original fixed snap T cannot implement both correct values. Any fixed finite-Lipschitz postprocessing that receives only this head difference plus unchanged features also cannot recover the bit with a fixed positive margin as i→∞.

This is an obstruction to the specified copy implementation, not to the whole pre-LN model. In pre-LN, the unnormalized input remains on the residual stream. Other heads, layers, a different value bank, a local hard window, position-dependent bias, reintroduced positions, or readout can change the computation and must be charged and analyzed separately. No claim is made that this single-key perturbation implies a nonvanishing expected prediction risk for the complete network.

3. Post-LN: a different failure mechanism

In a post-LN block, the first attention calculation occurs before the normalization, so the original unnormalized quadratic-position score can still select the desired lag. It is incorrect to reuse the pre-LN bounded-score proof at that step.

For the original block-1 wiring, however, the residual vector before the first post-attention LayerNorm contains the unmodified coordinate i², a separate coordinate i, constant and token coordinates, and two head-output coordinates in [0,1]. Compare the same two histories as in §2. The terminal local input is unchanged; only the two bounded copy-head output slots can differ, so the pre-LN residual difference has Euclidean norm at most √2. Denote by esq the coordinate vector for i². Since the other coordinates are O(i),

||P u_i|| ≥ i² ||P e_sq|| − O(i) ≥ c i²  for all large i,

for some c>0 independent of the word, and the same lower bound up to constants holds along the line segment between the two residual vectors. Differentiating Nε along that segment gives ||Nε(u_i)−Nε(u′_i)||=O(i⁻²), with fixed d,γ,ε. If the subsequent FFN and residual operate only on this normalized state and ε>0, they have a fixed Lipschitz constant; a second post-FFN LayerNorm also has the fixed global ε-bound above. The original block's final representations for the two histories therefore differ O(i⁻²), so they cannot directly contain the two distinct exact copied bits with a length-independent margin.

This conclusion assumes the original separate-coordinate residual layout, bounded head outputs, fixed LayerNorm parameters and no unnormalized side channel or reintroduced position at the FFN. It is a statement about retaining a copied bit through this particular block, not a lower bound for a complete post-LN Transformer: later attention can gather information at other positions, and alternative encodings or architectures can behave differently. With ε=0, the later normalization is not globally Lipschitz and cannot be disposed of by this argument.

4. Prior results and open endpoint

Hahn (2020), Lemma 5 derives one-symbol influence bounds under fixed-parameter bounded-input/Lipschitz soft-attention assumptions; its normalization and attention/mask assumptions must be checked before any transfer to unbounded positional inputs or every causal intermediate row. Chiang and Cholak (2022), Propositions 2–3 show that zero-ε post-LN can amplify vanishing signals and that length-dependent logarithmic score scaling changes copy/classification behavior, respectively, in their specified encoder tasks. These are reasons not to infer a universal normalized-network impossibility. Veličković et al., Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 2.2 give bounded-logit attention dilution in their settings; verify their input assumptions rather than substituting this candidate's unbounded positions.

Open: construct or rule out actual-terminal risk consistency for a fully specified pre-LN or post-LN causal model with paid position and residual channels, ε, scoring biases, parameter precision and output interface fixed. The copy-interface failure alone neither proves this broader impossibility nor excludes useful normalized constructions.
