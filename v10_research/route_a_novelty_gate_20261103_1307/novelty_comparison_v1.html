Verdict: novelty not established; not a standalone publication-ready contribution on current evidence. Exact candidate, translated before comparison

At a fixed homogeneous stationary rare-family source, u=π(00)∈(0,1/4), w=(1−u)/3, N observed symbols, m=N−2 and v=mu. The same random trajectory supplies both the historical counts and terminal event SN=00. For direct full-state equality scores 0 or β≥0, γ=eβ−1, q=(T+γC)/(m+γJ), with J and C the 00 row count and 1-successor count, and T the pooled 1 count. Its target is the true fixed-source θ00=t, not merely C/J, a Bayes predictor or a training loss.

Candidate eq. (2) bounds the deviation between E[|q−t| | SN=00] and |2w−t|/(1+γu) by an explicit D₀(m,u,δ), for v≥60; D₀ is uniform over β, conservative and can be greater than one. At t=1/2 the bias factor is c(u)=(1−4u)/6, which vanishes as u approaches 1/4. The necessary/sufficient q-only conditions use [c(u)/(ε±D₀)−1]+, with ε>D₀ for the sufficient condition. Any simplified 1/(uε) statement requires a range such as u≤1/8 and ε,D₀ small relative to c(u).

The unknown-u addendum uses a triangular array of stationary sources. For 0<a<1, βm≤(1−a)log m and um=m−1+a/2 give m um→∞ and direct-head conditioned risk→1/6; βm=log(1+m) suffices for vanishing risk under the displayed stronger-than-mu→∞ condition. These are not statements for a single changing-parameter path, not necessary for arbitrary networks, and not an optimal N–L frontier.

How much of the bound is mathematical novelty?

  1. Population bias: q's mixture formula plus the ergodic theorem gives the fixed-u limit |2w−t|/(1+γu); conditioning on an event of fixed positive probability is straightforward. The direct-head counterexample for fixed β is a simple consequence, not a new attention mechanism.
  2. Finite prefix: D₀ combines elementary stationary mixing covariance, reverse-chain occupancy, an exponential martingale and a union bound before dividing by u. It carefully avoids the false terminal-independence assumption, and explicitly handles triangular u and β. These quantifiers are useful, but the constants are loose; its proof is a routine bridge, and no source-specific comparison establishes a non-obvious conceptual or rate improvement.
  3. Two-sided threshold: the formula c(u)/(1+γu) plus D₀ provides a necessary-and-sufficient bracket up to D₀ for the deliberately uncorrected q output. Izzo supplies a near-identical sufficient attention-to-frequency scale; neither this restricted lower nor its difference from the inspected sources implies a lower bound for a network retaining q,r,u or raw/residual channels.
  4. Resource/accounting: changing β or adding a correction changes score precision, heads, nonlinear decoding and access. Their costs are not fully solved. The fixed-β corrected port can avoid score growth in ideal arithmetic, subject to numerical sensitivity; a direct same-history counter or Rajaraman's existing constructions are strong baselines. No N–L separation is proved.

Assessment of the T1/T2 contribution claim

Keep the bound as a candidate lemma or an illustrative subcase, not as a claimed novel main theorem. The closest defensible description is: “a conservative nonasymptotic conditional-TV estimate for a specified finite-score head and specified rare family, with the terminal query drawn from the same path.” Even this is a description, not an originality claim. A stronger route should establish a genuinely nontrivial integrated risk/resource relation, a matching theorem for a meaningful complete architecture, or a clearly justified computational separation against all same-information pathways; first inspect the broader literature and pre-existing project results.

Scientific scope: the same prefix and actual terminal query remain the object; q/r/u is not declared novel, exact or numerically free, and hidden θ or future tokens are not given to the predictor. T3 and training are not used as substitutes for the T1/T2 result.
