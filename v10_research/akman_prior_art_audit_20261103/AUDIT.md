## Resource and model translation: crucial

Paper N = latent Markov order, paper M = test window length, paper T = depth M+1, paper L = input length M, and K-tilde = number of training windows. The T1/T2 target uses N = length of the *same observed in-context history*, L = depth, k=2 fixed, θ unknown at inference. These are not interchangeable. Paper takes finite latent alphabet S, compact observation space, observation kernel Q and conditional independent emissions. Its next observation predictive law P_r is conditional on a source distribution and full observed history; training compresses source-specific predictive information into weights. It need not estimate an unknown task-specific θ from the inference prefix as the T1/T2 target requires. §1.1 p4 explicitly distinguishes fully observed kernel-agnostic k-gram prompt statistics from its own distribution encoded in weights. Theorem 27 fixes r: independent training windows have the same law as the test window, even for nonstationary source; weights can consequently depend on r and on the training data law. Inference is readout of M quantized observations; no query-time oracle receives θ, but source-specific offline training/lookup construction is permitted.

Assumption 3 (p8): emission density ψ(y|z) ≥ q_min>0 for all y,z, ψ Lipschitz in y uniformly in z, and each observation quantization cell has µ-mass at least µ_min^s>0. For literal identity-channel observation of a nontrivial binary latent state, the cross-state density is zero. Thus principal results do not *directly* apply to that representation of the exact fully observed binary order-2 experiment. This is not a proof that no equivalent latent representation exists for any special observed process, nor that a modified proof is impossible. Strong positivity also gives every length-M quantized observation word probability at least (q_min µ_min^s)^M; do not equate that with a source-uniform rare-row guarantee for Regime II of the T1/T2 target.

## Statement/proof inventory and overlap

* Defs. 1–4, Assumption 3, Prop. 5 (pp7–9; proof Appendix B.2 pp38–39): source-dependent full-history, M-window, quantized-input and quantized-output targets are distinct. Prop. 5 controls input-quantization TV by 2M Lψ/(q_min s). It is an approximation of a known predictive conditional law, not statistical estimation from an unknown-kernel in-context path.
* Assumption 6 and Prop. 7 (§2.1 pp9–10; B.3): stationary source and stated ergodic condition; entropy gap Δ_M=H_M−H∞ yields sup_r E TV(P_r,P_r^M)≤sqrt(Δ_M/2)→0. No generic numerical convergence rate follows. Strong overlap with a broad claim 'next-token risk tends to zero as memory grows', but the source information and algorithm are different from the T1/T2 target.
* Def. 8, Prop. 9 (§2.2.1 p11; B.4): TV truncation bound α^M, α=(1−δ(κ))(2−δ(Q)); useful geometric decay requires α<1. δ is the *overlap/minorization* convention (1 means complete overlap), not the contraction coefficient itself. Finite bound remains formally an upper bound even when α≥1, but then no decay is implied.
* Prop. 10 and Remark 11 (§2.2.2 pp12–13; B.5 pp40–42): explicit order-k latent chain, non-overlapping k-block transition T and observation Q; E TV≤α_block^{floor(M/k)}, α_block=(1−δ(T))(2−δ(Q)). The paper explicitly discusses sliding-window lift's structural zero one-step overlap; the two-step/blocked-lift observation is already prior art. This is hidden-state filter forgetting conditioned on fixed data-generating law, not convergence of a source-universal in-context empirical estimator. Explicit assumption A1 excludes direct identity-channel instantiation above.
* Thm. 12, Cor. 13 (pp14; proofs B.6–7 p42): combine Prop.10 with Prop.5: α_block^{floor(M/k)}+2MLψ/(q_min s); for α_block<1 and sufficiently fine s obtain ε-accuracy with M≥k ceil(log(2/ε)/log(1/α_block)). This is a concrete context/approximation sufficiency result, not a lower bound or proof longer statistical context substitutes for a network layer. Memory truncation already disappears once an exactly observed known order-2 source's last two symbols are known; unknown θ estimation is a distinct question.
* Assumption 15 (§3.1 p15) requires recoverable position retained through transitions. §4 (pp21–25) uses M-dimensional one-hot positions, token-id coordinate, M gather coordinates and R(s) logits, so d=2M+R(s)+1. M layers each copy a prescribed position into the final readout via one attention head; FFN realizes identity, all positions can attend all M positions in the paper's explicit formula. Lemmas 16–18 prove invariant position/token coordinates and weights e^{βc}/(e^{βc}+M−1) at chosen index. Lemma 20 gives soft-vs-hard gather error ≤2M(M−1)R(s)e^{−βc}; Cor.21 hard outputs distinguish all windows. Lemmas 23–24 add ReLU interpolation of up to R(s)^M window outputs with width R(s)^M−1, and an exponentially growing Lipschitz upper bound. Paper’s attention is not itself the T1/T2 target’s content-based joint-context matching estimator. The final position sees only observed prompt; do not assume the whole construction or optimal training theorem transfers verbatim to the specified fully causal architecture merely because its readout is last.
* Thm.25 (p25; proof C.7 pp45–46): an explicit depth M+1, one-head/layer, source-dependent interpolator approximates each quantized M-window predictive distribution uniformly to ε when β exceeds its displayed logarithmic threshold involving Lφ; output probabilities have a positive lower bound. It is not a fixed-small-depth theorem: d grows with M, interpolator width grows as R(s)^M−1, and its log-target table depends on the source/window conditional distribution. Source uses positive emission probabilities to take logarithms. At M=1 the printed β formula contains log(M−1); the M=1 boundary requires a separate convention/argument. These are sufficient bounds only; no depth/width/precision optimality is established by this construction.
* §3.2–3.5 and Appendix A, pp16–20,37: raw K-tilde examples are grouped by distinct prompts, y^k is the empirical successor law, n_k their multiplicity, K the number of unique prompts. Eq.(15) is ordinary empirical cross-entropy: sum_k (n_k/K-tilde) CE(y^k, prediction). A finite state/action/measure grid and exact exhaustive backward dynamic programming give an open-loop layer-weight sequence, not a result about practical SGD, computationally efficient optimization, or learned source-universal ICL. Section 5 applies state/measure quantization after each layer; sample target and output are distinct; softmax cross-entropy has irreducible entropy, and E(γ) subtracts it to produce empirical excess KL. The final bound is expected unsquared TV, not necessarily an excess log-loss guarantee on held-out data.
* Thm.26 (§5.1 p27; proof D.3 pp58–59): a *single stationary ergodic training trajectory* produces overlapping windows; independent test trajectory. Almost surely a nested limit with K-tilde→∞ first, then grids/β→∞, then s→∞, then M→∞ yields zero expected TV, uniformly in r≥M. Do not recast this as a joint rate, finite-K dependent-path theorem, or theorem that query-time single-path statistics are read in context. See Assumption 6 issue below.
* Thm.27 (§5.2 pp27–28; proof D.2 pp55–57) *already gives* high-probability finite-sample TV risk and explicit architecture terms. It assumes K-tilde independent copies of the length-(M+1) train window and an independent test sequence. K-tilde ≥8(q_min µ_min^s)^{−M}[M log R(s)+log(3/δ)]; with probability ≥1−δ, expected test TV ≤ α_block^{floor(M/k)} +(1+2M/q_min)Lψ/s +(1+√2)√[R(s)^M Cδ/(q_min µ_min^s K-tilde)] +η(n,m,β)/√2, Cδ=R(s)log2+M log R(s)+log(3/δ). η contains exponentially accurate positional selection, state-grid error, and weight-grid error. MX and MU are bounded but may grow: Eq.(23) imposes MU≥2R(s)^M and appropriate log(1/(q_min µ_min^s)). Aligning grids, η≤1 and explicit positivity are required. Do not claim that 'nonasymptotic TV', 'sample-complexity + architecture decomposition', 'finite precision', or 'a depth-window upper bound' are absent from Akman. Its finite sample is *offline independent training* rather than dependent inference-prefix sample count, its resource error is sufficient not a minimax lower bound, and quantization levels are not a complete inference bit/FLOP complexity account.
* Supporting Lemmas 28–31 (§B), 32–35 (§D): quantization reconstruction, noisy likelihood regularity, (claimed) stationarity/ergodicity transfer, valid block lifting, aligning integer grids, score and interpolation quantization. Lemma 36 (pp52–54) assumes independent windows to apply binomial Chernoff plus conditional multinomial bounds; it covers all R(s)^M windows and then sums errors. Lemma 37 (pp54–55) uses softmax TV/logit stability and positivity to translate errors into KL. D.2 uses triangle inequality, occupancy bound, comparator construction, Pinsker/Jensen and exact optimizer's empirical excess-KL dominance (Eq.42). D.3 instead uses Birkhoff for finite window indicators, which requires process-level ergodicity, and a countable intersection to handle limits.
* §5.3 p28 replaces geometric filter contraction with uniform-in-prior filter stability and observation Lipschitz with a continuity modulus for asymptotic behavior, still under A1/A3. This extension is an author argument, not a new numbered theorem; do not assume geometric mixing is the only route or overlook it when comparing asymptotics.

## Points for independent proof/errata review (not a conclusion that results are false)

1. Eq.(15) p18 uses weights n_k/K-tilde on *unique* prompt representatives; §3.2 p16 explicitly defines K as number of unique prompts and n_k as their multiplicity. Eq.(16) p20 prints 1/K sum of a hatted CE, and Appendix A Algorithm 1 p37 prints 1/K sum of CE over K unique prompts (with a hatted-label step at line 7). Section 5 p26 asserts that its Eq.(16) optimizer minimizes the Eq.(15) weighted V_CE; D.2 Eq.(42) p57 relies on precisely that weighted excess-loss optimality. Deterministic dynamics ensure optimization of the stated DP terminal cost, but do not in themselves equate weighted and unweighted costs for unequal n_k. A reweighted DP terminal loss could be a plausible repair, but it changes the displayed algorithm and may require tracking label quantization errors. Check whether hatted CE hides weights (no such definition identified), whether a common minimizer exists under the actual constrained model, or whether the discrepancy is typographic. This review does not prove a counterexample to the theorem. On 2026-11-03 official submission history lists only v1; no v2 correction was found there. No independent author clarification was obtained.
2. Assumption 6 p9 displays only time-average convergence for all functions f:S→R of one state, and calls this ergodicity. If that displayed condition is the entire formal assumption, it is strictly weaker than shift-ergodicity: a stationary 50:50 mixture of two irreducible symmetric binary chains with different persistence but common uniform marginal has the displayed property on each component yet is not shift-ergodic. Adding independent bit-flip noise with 0<ε<1/2 supplies full positive emission density and preserves distinguishable window laws. Lemma 30/B.1 and D.3 invoke ergodicity for window indicators. The standard stronger meaning of 'stationary ergodic process' would exclude this example, so this is a scope/statement ambiguity requiring independent review, not an assertion that the result under standard ergodicity is false. Neither proposed concern justifies discounting an author theorem as prior art.
3. Lemma24(ii) p25 cites 'Assumptions 3-(A2,4)' although Assumption 3 enumerates A1–A3, and refers to targets as probability vectors, while its proof and Thm.25 proof use log-targets. Likely editorial defects but check exact proof dependency before relying on constants.

## Compared directly to current local v10 work

- The T1/T2 target calls for one source-independent inference computation receiving one unknown-θ order-2 dependent word, targeting θ of its actual terminal pair, and separating source estimation from network implementation and L,d,H,b. This is *not the identical experiment* of Thm.27's independent offline source-specific training, or Thm.26's independent training/test trajectories. That difference is substantive, not a novelty certificate: check D’Angelo/Rajaraman/other relevant sources too.
- `finite_positional_attention_20261103/CANDIDATE.md` explicitly describes a paid finite causal/Boolean-ReLU hybrid with structural singleton masks, one prefix-derived count bank, actual-terminal risk and source-independent parameters; unlike Akman's arbitrary window table it pays position acquisition and links explicit source-path statistics to risk. Its restricted hybrid architecture and imported lemmas must be kept explicit; its candidate status and author wording were not upgraded by this audit. Both papers use positional information, attention copying and ReLU computation, so these broad techniques are not new.
- `positional_bank_precision_lower_20261103/CANDIDATE.md` asserts a lower/upper rate for a very specific rounded 12-coordinate bank cut without raw bypass; Akman's grid-dependent sufficient η does not itself supply that bank-specific minimax lower. This comparison is limited to the inspected named claim, not a claim that such a lower has never appeared elsewhere.
- `clean_training_objective_20261103/CANDIDATE.md` is an explicitly source-mixture population-logloss Bayesian predictor with posterior from *the same observed word* and a separate robust test. Akman's empirical offline CE/DP theorem does not identify that mixed-task posterior or imply its attack-robustness comparison. Conversely Akman already uses the entropy/excess-KL decomposition and optimal cross-entropy training in its own model, so neither generic point is a new claim.
- `causal_attention/CF001_finite_causal_risk_frontier_v1.md` is a fixed-depth, fixed-budget, quantized encoder-class candidate with a declared observation cut; Akman's construction gives different growing-depth/width upper bounds and does not automatically settle that candidate's lower. Candidate status and all cited imports require separate verification. `conditional_channel.../CANDIDATE.md` concerns explicit adversarial conditional channel uncertainty and a different experiment, outside this paper's audited propositions; no global novelty conclusion follows.

## Bottom line for gate G0

Freeze the overbroad claims: general convergence, HMM/order-k filter forgetting, block-lift zero-overlap observation, depth M+1 positional gather-and-interpolate construction, finite-sample TV plus architecture/quantization upper bounds, CE optimal training by exhaustive finite DP, are prior art. Potentially distinct but not certified: uniformly source-independent unknown-kernel ICL from a single dependent prefix, actual-terminal vs rare-state conditional risk, content-soft-matching recovery with all pathways paid, and restricted-architecture or explicit-bank *lower* bounds. The finite-sample, loss, training and positional terms in this paper must appear in the comparison, not merely in a bibliography. Compare directly with its exact theorem quantifiers and identify any repairs needed around Eq.15/16 before treating its displayed bound as settled.

### Diagnostic for issue 1 (elementary, model-independent)
As an illustration of why determinism alone is insufficient, consider two unique prompts whose labels are 0 and 1, with multiplicities 9 and 1, and a deliberately tied constant Bernoulli prediction q. The unweighted CE is −(log(1−q)+log q)/2, uniquely minimized at q=1/2; multiplicity-weighted CE is −0.9 log(1−q)−0.1 log q, uniquely minimized at q=0.1. This is *not* asserted to be a counterexample within Akman's specific quantized Transformer class. It only disproves the generic inference that deterministic dynamics make these differently weighted optimization objectives equivalent. The weights and label rounding must be checked in the actual model or a corrected algorithm.
