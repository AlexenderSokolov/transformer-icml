Independent review: rare-boundary and same-port addenda

2026-11-03. Both addenda are mathematically consistent with the conditional moment and sensitivity bounds in the bound v2 source, subject to the scope below. This is not formal verification or a literature-wide novelty review.

Rare-boundary lower

Fix 0<u₀<1/4, finite β>0 and nonnegative finite coordinate budgets. If Lq·λ(u₀)≤1−η for η∈(0,1), then for every admissible decoder and m with mu₀≥60, v2 at the single source frequency u=u₀ gives

sup_{u∈[u₀,1/4), t∈{1/4,3/4}} R_{u,t}
≥ η/4 − Lq·G(u₀,m) − Lr·A(m) − Lh·u₀·b(u₀,m).

With δ=1/m, the error vanishes independently of the admissible decoder. Thus taking infimum over Fm and then liminf in m preserves the lower η/4. This is a worst-case statement because u₀ is in the allowed interval; it does not assert the same lower at each u>u₀. The supremum is the appropriate operation on the half-open u interval.

Same-port upper and comparison

For public u₀, λ(h)=γh/(1+γh), and Λ=1/λ(u₀), the stated decoder clips its affine-in-ports expression to [1/4,3/4]. At population points, monotonicity gives Λλ(u)≥1, so the clip yields the true endpoint t exactly. The (q,r,h) coordinate sensitivity bounds are (Λ,Λ,Λγ/2). The v2 moment bounds give the stated finite-m risk upper, decaying uniformly as Oβ,u₀(√(log m/m)) for fixed β,u₀, exact ports/output and δ=1/m.

Choosing the low-class budgets Lr≥Λ and Lh≥Λγ/2 admits the upper comparator under the same r/h budgets; its q sensitivity Λ strictly exceeds Lq≤(1−η)Λ. Both procedures use the same declared q/r/h-only cut and public u₀. This comparison covers only t=1/4 and t=3/4. It uses exact-real division and clipping and supplies no finite-ReLU, depth, bit-complexity, full-network, or novelty theorem.

For randomized lower-bound decoders, the seed must have the same law and be independent of each full path, or have the same conditional law given SN=00 under the two endpoint sources, for each fixed u. Common seed marginals alone do not suffice. The upper comparator is deterministic. Port/output errors are included in the finite upper; the asymptotic separation assumes exact ports/output.
