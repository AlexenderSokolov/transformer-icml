Verdict: valid after an explicit seed-conditioning qualification

For deterministic decoders, the finite-risk lower bound, sharp population comparators, FFN norm implication and displayed fluctuation controls check out under the stated hypotheses. For randomized decoders, the source text and its separate clarification should explicitly require that the decoder seed is independent of the entire sampled path under each source, or at least that its conditional law given the terminal event is the same under the two sources. Merely saying that the seed has a common source-independent marginal distribution is not enough after conditioning.

Randomized-decoder quantifier

For every seed ω, let L₋(ω)=|Fω(p₋)−1/4| and L₊(ω)=|Fω(p₊)−3/4|. Their sum is at least 1/2−Λqλ/2. Average this sum with respect to the common conditional seed law, then use max(E L₋, E L₊) ≥ (E L₋+E L₊)/2. Subtract the common fluctuation, port-error, and output-error allowance from each risk. The two path laws need no coupling. Taking the expectation of max(L₋,L₊) and interchanging expectation with max would be invalid.

The qualification matters: abstractly, let the terminal event E have probability u under each source and let a binary seed have marginal probability 1/2 of being one under both. Arrange P(seed=1|E,t₋)=0 and P(seed=1|E,t₊)=1, with complement probabilities 1/[2(1−u)] and (1/2−u)/(1−u), respectively. These are valid for 0<u<1/4. The same seed-indexed constant decoder Fₛ=1/4+s/2 has zero port sensitivities and zero conditional risk under both sources, contradicting the positive lower bound. This example uses a source-dependent seed/path coupling; independent algorithmic randomness excludes it.

Conditional moment controls

  • The reversed states S_N through S_3 supply X_N through X_3, exactly the m successor bits in r. The two-step common atom of mass at least 1/8 gives contraction factor ρ=7/8; the sums from zero and one are 16 and 15. Thus the conditional mean bias is at most 16/m.
  • Using the source's conservative absolute covariance bound 2ρ⌊d/2⌋, the conditional variance is at most (m/4+60m)/m²=60.25/m≤61/m. Hence A=16/m+√(61/m) is valid. The cited reverse-chain occupancy and unconditioned forward exponential-martingale bounds lead to the displayed ub and δ+1220/v controls; conditioning is handled by division by P(S_N=00)=u, not by assuming a conditional forward martingale.
  • On the joint good event, h≥u/2 and |Z|≤√(3vℓ/4). Substitution into the exact q identity gives the report's G, including its denominator 1+x/2 and x factors. On the complement both q and q̄ lie in [0,1], so the exception contribution is at most δ+1220/v. G can be larger than one; the bound remains valid but may be uninformative.

Population and architectural claims

  • F₀ attains [1−Λqλ]₊/4 for fixed known u and β and is independent of the true t; it has zero r and h sensitivities. F₁ attains the same endpoint value without knowing u and has coordinate sensitivities at most (Λq,Λq,Λqγ/2). Its use under separate budgets requires Λr≥Λq and Λh≥Λqγ/2. It is an abstract exact-real comparator, not a demonstrated finite ReLU implementation.
  • For 1-Lipschitz coordinatewise activation, each stated residual block is at most 1+W² Lipschitz in Euclidean norm. With the norm-at-most-one embedding, scalar output norm at most W, and projection, each coordinate sensitivity is at most K=W(1+W²)D. Under the side condition, (4) yields Kλ≥(1−4ε)/2, and λ≤eβu gives the stated logarithmic inequality.
  • For fixed β, u=m−1/4, δ=1/m and K=O(m1/4), the terms in KΔ vanish for exact ports and output. This establishes feasibility of the small-fluctuation side condition, not existence of an accurate decoder meeting the architectural assumptions. The lower remains confined to the declared three-port cut.
