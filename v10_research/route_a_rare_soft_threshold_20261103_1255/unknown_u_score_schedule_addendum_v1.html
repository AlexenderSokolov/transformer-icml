A source-independent score schedule and an adversarial rare-row sequence

2026-11-03. Additive corollary of equation (2) in the unchanged Route A score-threshold report. Status: provisional, independent check requested. It applies only to the uncorrected q-only soft estimator, not to arbitrary heads, residuals, direct counters, smoothing, corrected q/r/h ports or whole transformers. It is not a depth lower bound, a fixed-bit theorem, or a novelty claim.

For each m=N−2, let the score βm≥0 be chosen using m and public metadata, not the hidden u or t. We analyze a sequence of stationary sources from the same rare family. Each source is fixed for its length-m experiment; no stochastic process with parameters changing within one trajectory is assumed. Let the loss be E[|qβ−t| | SN=00].

Sub-logarithmic score can fail despite growing rare-row data

Fix any a∈(0,1). For every such deterministic schedule satisfying βm≤(1−a)log m, choose t=1/2 and um=m−1+a/2 for all sufficiently large m. These are valid sources because um→0 and t is inside [1/4,3/4]. Then

v_m=m u_m=m^(a/2) → ∞,
γ_m u_m=(e^(β_m)−1)u_m ≤ m^(−a/2) → 0,
c(u_m)=(1−4u_m)/6 → 1/6.

Taking δ=1/m, the logarithm in D₀ is O(log m), so D₀→0. Equation (2) therefore gives conditional risk →1/6. The sequence has a growing expected number of rare-row visits; failure is due to this direct head's score-weighted aggregation in this regime, rather than a claim that the path has no information. The network is not given um; the source sequence is an adversarial choice used for analysis.

A source-independent logarithmic score is sufficient under an explicit evidence condition

Take βm=log(1+m), so γm=m and γmum=vm. For any sequence um∈(0,1/4), tm∈[1/4,3/4] satisfying

v_m / log[2m(m+1)/u_m] → ∞,

the bound with δ=1/m has D₀→0 and vm→∞. Since |2wm−tm|≤1, (2) implies the direct-head conditional risk tends to zero. This is a sufficient asymptotic statement under this explicitly stronger-than-mere-v→∞ condition, not an optimality proof or a uniform finite-bit implementation. In particular it does not settle the log-factor gap or show a logarithmic score is necessary for all architectures.

Rajaraman et al. already give two-layer conditional empirical-frequency representations, and D’Angelo et al. already analyze soft n-gram interpolation and approximate β calibration in a different prior/loss experiment; see the same-folder primary-source comparison. This addendum does not claim that general score growth, higher scores, or count consistency was previously unknown.
