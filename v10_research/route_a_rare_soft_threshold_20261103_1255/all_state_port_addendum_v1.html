Collision with all four row occupancies visible

2026-11-03. Additive strengthening of the isolated-port fidelity example in Route A v1. An independent reviewer checked the block identities and preserved rational tests. The exact statement concerns the cut below, not all transformer states or expected risk.

Both length-six blocks A=010100 and B=100100, appended after state 00, have identical row-departure vectors (J00,J01,J10,J11)=(2,2,2,0). Replacing one A block by B transfers one successor-1 count from row 10 to row 00 while preserving the pooled successor count. The common filler F=10(10)k0 adds (1,k+1,k+1,0) to the respective row occupancies. For the two v1 histories, with n blocks and d substitutions, both full occupation vectors are

(2n+1, 2n+k+1, 2n+k+1, 0),

their pooled r and terminal 00 query agree, yet their target add-one estimates differ by d/(2n+3). Thus v1's midpoint collision at q-error γd/[2(m+γJ00)] holds even when the decoder receives all four exact Js/m, not merely J00/m. The cut permits the perturbed q, exact pooled r, all four occupancies, terminal pair and public m,β, together with source-independent decoder randomness. It excludes all additional path-dependent coordinates, input rereads and residuals. Under that declared cut, some word has expected add-one-fidelity error at least d/[2(2n+3)].

Separate calculation for the paper's native two-position scores

For a no-BOS D’Angelo-style k=2 single head with query 00 and equal positional score β>0, the score weights for the four rows are e2β, eβ, eβ, 1. In these words J11=0, and the common denominator is W=e2βJ00+eβ(J01+J10). A-to-B replacement shifts a 1-successor from row 10 to row 00, so d substitutions change the native-score head output by d(e2β−eβ)/W. The same midpoint proof applies at half this output gap even with exact r, all four occupancies and the 00 query. This is an elementary deterministic specialization, not an assertion that the paper proves or fails to prove an architecture-wide lower bound.

These constructed words have positive probability for every admissible fixed u,t, but a mere support example does not bound their mass. In particular, both same-source target probabilities equal t at terminal 00. Therefore neither collision establishes a lower bound on expected true-θ risk or an all-network precision requirement. The raw input or any sufficient residual can avoid this cut restriction. The sample checks do not turn this into a stochastic theorem.
