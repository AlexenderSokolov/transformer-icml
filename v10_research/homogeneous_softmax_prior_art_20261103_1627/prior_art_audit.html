Source audit: fixed homogeneous softmax and conditional frequencies

3 November 2026. Targeted primary-source comparison; not an exhaustive search or novelty certification. No random or numerical experiment was used.

Assessment. Existing work already expresses n-gram estimates as normalized attention, analyzes finite-temperature partial-match bias, extracts counting information from BOS normalization, and proves several sequence-length/counting limitations under specific architectures. The present argument combines a finite-pattern analytic-continuation obstruction with a same-history stationary Markov-risk bridge under a fixed-parameter, within-type homogeneous interface. The inspected sources do not immediately state that exact combined theorem; this limited search cannot establish that it is new. In particular it does not justify a lower bound for general causal Transformers.

Target and correction. The studied model grants a global normalized triple histogram, actual terminal encoding, and finitely many extra heads whose candidate scores and values are identical within each triple type. Parameters, operations and number of heads are fixed as length grows; no position-varying within-type states, hard type masks, unbounded length signals, or division oracle are supplied. The frozen obstruction must be read with its separate region/length clarification: the cited source subset is an open sub-wedge, not a compact rectangle, and the BOS mass can encode length although it tends to zero. The claim is positive limiting risk for some source per fixed model, not a uniform positive gap over all models.

1. Markov context matching: direct softmax ratio antecedent

D’Angelo et al., Induction Heads Interpolate N-Grams, arXiv v1, Proposition 3.1 and equation (8) express the two-layer circuit output as a normalized weighted sum over match-mask counts, with a BOS pseudo-count. Fixed finite matching logits give positive weight to every mask and hence interpolation; their Corollary 4.1 takes a hard-match limit for add-α. Their first layer relies on ideal positional copying and early-position suppression. Finite β does not exactly eliminate nonmatching contexts; making the match sharper, taking β→∞, is not the same fixed finite-parameter homogeneous model. This is direct prior for the output formula, not a proof that an arbitrary extra fixed-softmax network computes v/u on an open family.

Rajaraman et al., Transformers on Markov Data: Constant Depth Suffices, arXiv v1, Theorems 4.4–4.5 and Remark 4.2 construct conditional k-gram representations using lag-copying and relative positions. Their proofs use limiting attention concentration, and their finite-precision remark states a length-dependent approximation qualification; the empirical estimate is specified when the matching count is positive. Their Theorem 6.1 is a one-layer finite-precision worst-case conditional 3-gram lower over a ternary alphabet and error 1/(3T), while Theorems 6.3–6.5 impose attention-pattern assumptions and address induction-head realization. None directly proves the present binary order-two average-risk, all-head fixed homogeneous-interface claim; nor does this obstruction contradict their differently scoped positive constructions.

2. BOS normalization and count recovery

Behrens, Biggio and Zdeborová, Counting in Small Transformers, arXiv v3, Proposition 4.1 and Appendix B.2.2 use a BOS token and softmax attention to encode count information. For each fixed sequence length L and vocabulary size T, their proposition gives a histogram classification construction under d≥T>2 with a single hidden feature. The normalized attention formula depends on the count, with a dedicated readout mapping it to categorical outputs. This is important prior for the fact that BOS normalization can preserve count information, but a per-L classification construction is not a single fixed-dimensional, finite continuous readout computing an exact real conditional-probability ratio for all lengths. It would be incorrect to infer that BOS mass hides length; its vanishing value can still be injective in length.

Shaffy, Dhakal, Shrestha and Ross, Counting with Attention and LayerNorm, arXiv v1, Theorem 1 and Theorem 4 analyze a minimal one-layer, one-head model with two content types and a boundary token, no position encoding, feed-forward sublayer or residual in the principal model. Theorem 1(b) states that an exact finite-parameter linear-regression readout cannot output raw counts for all examples in its bounded-length domain; achieving exact counts after rounding incurs norm growing as Θ(L) at fixed dimension in their no-layernorm setup. Their Theorem 4 shows bounded parameter norms can classify counts at each fixed maximum length, but a dense output keeps one class vector per count and storage grows with L. This concerns raw counts and the particular linear/readout and arithmetic conventions, not the current bounded conditional frequency, multiple heads, global histogram broadcast or source-prior TV loss. It nevertheless shows why length, readout, output dimension and precision must be compared explicitly.

3. Fixed scores and length generalization

Izzo, Nichani and Lee, Quantitative Bounds for Length Generalization in Transformers, arXiv v1, Theorem 5.2 and Appendix B.3 give a simulation-based generalization bound for a declared class of two-layer, infinite-precision, τ-local translation-invariant limit Transformers with specific positional-logit structure. Theorem 5.2 concerns two fixed functions in that class and worst-case comparison between short and arbitrary strings; their k-gram appendix calls its construction heuristic and leaves zero matches arbitrary. Their average-case Theorem 4.2 uses an iid categorical/Dirichlet source setup, while the authors discuss Markov-data extensions as future work. These are close prior on length/frequency structure, not the reviewed stationary order-two same-terminal TV theorem. Their positional assumptions and fixed model class cannot be silently equated with arbitrary positional states.

Hahn, Theoretical Limitations of Self-Attention in Neural Sequence Models, TACL 2020 analyzes fixed-depth/head self-attention limitations for formal-language tasks, under its stated model assumptions. Those sensitivity/sequence-language conclusions are not a theorem about the present conditional-frequency risk or a license to omit assumptions on bounded representations and positions.

4. Analytical mechanism and its boundary

On the source wedge, the eight stationary triple-type masses are nonnegative and sum to one. Finite scores imply a historical-head denominator Σ_a p_a e^{ℓ_a} that remains strictly positive on the wedge and at its origin limit, so each frozen finite ReLU branch has a connected analytic continuation through an origin neighborhood. If it equalled v/u on an open source set, the analytic identity uF−v=0 would extend to that neighborhood, contradicting its derivative in v at the origin. The finite activation-pattern argument and the identity theorem are classical tools; see Mityagin, The Zero Set of a Real Analytic Function. Turning this representational observation into stationary same-history prediction risk uses the terminal event probability and bounded convergence, without independence of the terminal state and counts.

The four-anchor counterexample uses reciprocal normalization and count rounding in its readout, so it is outside this model; a continuous reciprocal on occupancies bounded away from zero is also not excluded from unrestricted computation. A source-specific parameter choice, N-dependent parameters, hard masks, position-dependent representations or extra computational resources change the quantifiers and may bypass the conclusion. The finite-BOS contribution vanishes only because its fixed score/value remain bounded; the BOS coordinate still carries length information before the limit.

5. Search limits

Targeted searches covered attention-based conditional empirical probabilities, BOS/count normalization, fixed-parameter softmax expressivity, sequence-length lower bounds, ReLU/analytic representation and Markov prediction. The linked statements were checked in the named primary versions and sections. The search did not systematically cover all finite-model theory, analytic circuits, ergodic prediction or positional-encoding work. No finding of an identical theorem in the sources inspected is not proof of absence or priority; this remains a provisional comparison pending broader literature review.
