Resource versus prediction error: explicit model boundaries

3 November 2026. Analytic investigation, not formal certification or a novelty claim. We distinguish a sample lower bound, a finite-state output-resolution lower bound, and a query-probe lower bound. The latter two require explicitly declared access models and do not prove Transformer head, depth, width, FLOP or training lower bounds.

1. Same-history experiment and inherited sample bound

For X_1:N from a homogeneous binary order-two source, let S_j=(X_(j-1),X_j), M=N-2 and target θ_(S_N). The actual loss |p−θ_(S_N)| is binary TV. The rare stationary family has public 0<u<1/4, w=(1-u)/3, π=(u,w,w,w), and θ=(t,1/2,1-ut/w,1/2), t in [1/4,3/4]. There is one parameter-independent estimator, which may see the entire path and know u,N.

The cited family audit establishes, for conditional terminal 00 risk,

sup_t E_t[|p−t| | S_N=00] ≥ c / sqrt(1+Mu),
c=(1−1/sqrt(6))/32.

Accordingly conditional error at most ε requires M≥(c²/ε²−1)/u when the right side is positive. As π_00=u for every t, uniform stationary-average risk at most ε requires M≥((uc/ε)²−1)/u when positive. These are statistical history-length requirements even for a full-path estimator with unlimited computation and storage. They do not imply that a Transformer needs additional heads, width, depth, sequential operations, or a particular arithmetic cost.

2. A finite-bit memory lower bound that survives shared randomness

Fix N and u. Consider an estimator that, after consuming the path in any allowed streaming fashion, can expose to its decoder only (i) a history-dependent state of at most B bits, (ii) the actual terminal pair S_N, and (iii) public N,u and a source-independent random tape. The decoder and state update cannot depend on the unknown t. There are no other history-dependent residuals, side channels, exact counts, replay or later rereads of the input; any such information must be included in B or the model changed. The tape may be shared with both update and decoder, need not be stored in B, and may be arbitrarily long. This is a finite-state communication/output-resolution restriction, not a head restriction.

Set K=2^B and condition on S_N=00. Since the source is independent of the random tape and π_00=u for every t, the tape has its original distribution under this conditioning. Fix a complete tape realization r. Despite the fact that the state can be correlated with r, the decoder has at most K possible output values for this fixed r as the B-bit state varies. Call this set A_r. For every t and every conditional path law, the absolute prediction error is at least dist(t,A_r). On an interval I of length L=1/2, at most K real levels have average absolute distance at least L/(4K)=1/(8K): divide I into their Voronoi cells of lengths ℓ_i, use ∫cell |x−a_i|dx≥ℓ_i²/4 and Σℓ_i²≥L²/K. Levels outside I only increase the minimum or can be projected into I.

Integrate the conditional loss uniformly over t∈I, then over r. The pointwise lower bound holds for every r, so correlation between the state and the tape is irrelevant. Taking a supremum over t yields

sup_t E_t[|p−t| | S_N=00] ≥ 1/(8·2^B),
sup_t E_t[|p−θ_(S_N)|] ≥ u/(8·2^B).

Therefore conditional error ≤ε implies B≥log₂(1/(8ε)); stationary-average error ≤ε implies B≥log₂(u/(8ε)); each bound is meaningful only when its right-hand side is positive, and integer B is rounded up. This argument uses only output levels and remains valid with arbitrary source-independent public/shared random tapes and time-dependent rules. It is not claimed new. It does not apply when the decoder also receives history-dependent real-valued heads, exact count ports, residual streams, unrestricted input rereads, or other data not included in B. An exact unbounded-precision real can encode arbitrarily many bits, so a count of real coordinates or abstract words without a precision bound is not B.

3. Adaptive individual-bit observations: a separate computation model

Consider the narrower input-query model in which the terminal pair is given free and an algorithm may adaptively inspect at most k other individual X_i bits, with source-independent randomness. Repeat queries reveal no new information. The i.i.d. Bernoulli(p) sources are a subclass of the binary order-two model. Conditional on terminal pair 00, all other bits remain independent Bernoulli(p), and θ_00=p.

For 0<ε≤1/16, suppose one rule has conditional expected absolute error ≤ε on both p_-=1/2−2ε and p_+=1/2+2ε. The absolute-loss two-point inequality implies TV between conditional transcripts is at least 1/2. Adaptive chain rule gives KL≤k kl(p_-||p_+), because each newly inspected bit is conditionally Bernoulli and choices/repeated reads add no extra KL. The Bernoulli inequality kl(a||b)≤(a-b)²/[b(1-b)] and b∈[1/4,3/4] yield KL≤(256/3)kε². Pinsker gives KL≥1/2, so

k ≥ 3/(512ε²).

This counts individual observations in the specified bit-probe model, not attention heads or serial neural layers. An attention primitive that reads all positions in parallel is outside the unit-cost bit-probe abstraction unless its per-position work is charged; vectorization, masking, memory access, word size and hardware matter. The full-history sample lower bound cannot be relabeled a hardware/FLOP bound without such an explicit reduction.

4. Zero-transition-memory and constant-word comparators

A generic terminal-only baseline outputs 1/2, uses no transition-history memory, and has worst-case binary-TV risk at most 1/2. For the public-u rare family, terminal-pair-only prediction with t₀=1/2 attains exact worst-case stationary-average risk u/2. It still receives the terminal pair: “zero memory” means zero stored transition history, not zero access to the current query.

For arbitrary unknown binary homogeneous order-two tables, one streaming add-one comparator tracks J_ab and C_ab, eight nonnegative counters. On seeing X_i for i≥3, increment J_(X_(i-2),X_(i-1)) and, if X_i=1, increment C for the same pair; then update the two-bit shift register. After X_N the terminal query is S_N and the comparator returns (C_(S_N)+1)/(J_(S_N)+2). This is the same history and causal alignment. It uses O(1) machine words only if each holds Θ(log N) bits, namely O(log N) total bits for the fixed binary alphabet, two stored recent bits, O(N) word updates, and a charged final query selection and rational division. A straightforward bit-operation upper bound is O(N log N), plus the cost of outputting the requested precision. The counts may be output as an exact integer numerator/denominator pair; an actual finite-bit probability and division require a separate precision budget.

The inherited R011 result, at its β=0 endpoint and fixed binary alphabet, gives an O(M^-1/2) worst-case actual-terminal average-TV upper bound for this full-history add-one comparator over a broad homogeneous source class with arbitrary initial law. The R011 result and its independent review are cited, not re-proved here. An unlimited-precision real or integer word could encode a full trajectory, making O(1) words vacuous without a bit/precision definition. Fixed-B and O(log N)-bit models are therefore materially different. Compare masks, positional access, all residual/side channels, width, precision, per-input work, memory and output costs before asserting a same-budget network separation.
